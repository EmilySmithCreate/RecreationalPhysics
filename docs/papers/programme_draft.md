# A phase-changing reality: the programme, and where each piece stands

*Draft for the owner's review, 24 September 2026. Not for publication until she has read it. Every claim says
whose it is: **the author's** (Emily Smith's hypothesis), **measured** (a pre-registered test in the public
repository, with its verdict), **exact** (arithmetic or complete enumeration), or **ours** (the analysis
drafted with Claude, unreviewed by a physicist). Nothing here has yet been read by a physicist (the author's
own bar, S5, is not met).*

---

## The idea, in the author's words

Our spacetime is one settled arrangement of something deeper, X: a specific, relatively stable arrangement
with its own dimensions, curled where ours are open. X was stable for now, not for ever. A push started a
change at one place; it spread as a front, X opened into space, and the change released a lump of energy
that became the Big Bang's matter and radiation. What did not convert remained as a scrap: dark matter.
Energy was conserved across the change. The rules of the two phases are their constants and do not change
from one generation to the next. Inside the space that forms, black holes are regions that re-curl towards
X; one that grows large enough gets the activation energy and "burps" a new space, with the same lump and
the same scrap. Every possible arrangement of what a Big Bang produces therefore gets to exist. The quantum
world is the set of undetectably different versions of an arrangement, all standing at once, until an
interaction makes them distinguishable.

## Where each piece stands

The tests use one published model of emergent geometry (Trugenberger's combinatorial quantum gravity) as a
toy: space is a network, X's stand-in is a torus with one direction curled, and the model's local-term
coefficient λ is a knob. **A toy model can show that a mechanism is possible or impossible inside a class of
models; it cannot show anything about the real universe.**

| Piece | Status | What the model shows | What would count against it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. A stable-for-now arrangement opens sharply into space** | **Measured** (T7, T9, T10, T11; paper 1) | At λ = 1.25 the curled torus waits, starts at one place, runs as one front with the two orders side by side, releases exactly 4(λ − 1) per point, needs a push of exactly 12 at every size, and leaves one small leftover. | A slide instead of a switch; a push that grows with size. Neither happened. |
| **2. It is not a fluke of one setting** | **Running** (T8) | From λ = 1.05 to 1.30 the change is stuck for now, sharp, and releases the exact lump at every size; waiting times follow a formula with nothing fitted. Final verdict pending the last settings. | A floor below which the change stops being sharp. |
| **3. Only a coefficient above 1 gives a lump** | **Exact** | The release per point is 4(λ − 1): zero at λ = 1, and below 1 the change runs the wrong way. The model's author holds λ = 1 as the only physical value; the author holds that our reality has λ above 1, about 1.25. | Nothing in the model can decide between the two authors; it maps what each value gives. |
| **4. The new space must have room for the lump** | **Measured** (T9; T18 across λ) | Sealed, the change completes only if the surroundings can absorb the release; otherwise the new space melts. The room needed grows with the size of the space, and faster than the lump as λ rises (12, 36, 72 stores at λ = 1.10, 1.25, 1.40): a bigger lump is a harder birth. | — (a condition the author's theory must meet: in her theory melting is not real, so the lump must always have somewhere to go). |
| **5. The scrap: fixed size, and more seeds make more** | **Measured** (T10, T17) | One leftover of fixed size per tube, however large, from one seed; with 1, 2, 4, 8 seeds, 1.1, 2.2, 2.5, 3.4 leftovers: more seeds, more scrap, but fewer than one each. At fixed temperature, near λ = 1 the change leaves no scrap at all. | The author's "one scrap per seed" failed beyond two seeds; ours ("one per tube") failed too. |
| **6. The scrap lasts** | **Measured, and against** (T19) | At fixed temperature every leftover anneals away, within 500 to 80,500 sweeps even at the coldest coupling tried; it lasts only in a very cold sealed box (T10). | Already counts against a permanent scrap unless its surroundings stay very cold. |
| **7. Gravity as space's wish to refold** | **Exact, against the simplest version** (O22, O32) | Leftovers exert no force on each other at a distance through energy; and with points interchangeable, counting does not pull identical defects together either (it slightly prefers them exactly opposite). | Already counts against the simplest version. What remains open: a pull when the surroundings are free to rearrange. |
| **8. Black holes as re-curled regions** | **Measured, and against at the size tested** (O20, T21) | A sealed sheet given energy melts rather than folds, with named points and, at 64 points where complete folds exist, with interchangeable points too; at 36 points a fragile lean towards folding. Not yet tested: a local push into cold surroundings. | Already happened at N = 64 for energy spread through the box. |
| **9. Quantum behaviour from counting versions** | **Failed as first stated; counting validated** | Counting the versions of one fixed arrangement cannot violate Bell's inequality (argument, rung 3a); the model's small loop is not a resonator inside the sheet (rung 0b). The closest published relative, Smolin's real-ensemble quantum mechanics, adds exactly the two things counting lacks: versions that interact, and a phase. The next rung passed: with interchangeable points, time spent in each arrangement equals its share of the count (T15 rung 1, validation). | Already failed as stated. What would revive it: an interaction among versions, a new rule the author has not yet decided on. |
| **10. The cycle: new spaces from black holes, every arrangement existing** | **The author's; untestable in the model** | The model has no black holes and no expansion. It can map the ingredients (how stuck X is, the push, the lump, the room, the scrap), not count offspring. | It bears on piece 9: if versions in other spacetimes counted as copies, large objects would behave quantum-mechanically (Smolin's argument); the author's theory must say they do not. |

## What is new here, as far as we have found

We have not found published work that varies the coefficient of the model's local term, or that studies one
ordered arrangement of it turning into another (literature check of 24 September, recorded in the repository).
That is the ground of paper 1.

## What this page does not claim

That the universe is like this. That any piece beyond the first is established. That a physicist has checked
any of it.
