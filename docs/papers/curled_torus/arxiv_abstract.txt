We study activated escape from a curled torus in four-regular bipartite graph energies adjacent to combinatorial quantum gravity (CQG). A coefficient $\lambda$ multiplies the local square-surplus penalty; only $\lambda=1$ is CQG, while our decay experiments use $\lambda>1$. The ideal curled torus lies exactly $4(\lambda-1)$ per vertex above zero-energy states. For a specified Metropolis edge-switch chain, enumerated moves predict mean first-exit times without fitted parameters, consistently with direct measurements. During conversion, most vertices retain curled-like or flat-like square-count signatures, and a connected flat-like region usually dominates at half conversion; multiple early patches occur. Topology checks certify tori for all 120 saved endpoints at $\lambda=1.05$, without establishing macroscopic period growth. At $\lambda=1.25$, energy-conserving graph-plus-reservoir runs escape with a 12-unit budget over $N=48$--192; exhaustive neutral-class enumeration certifies the matching lower bound at those sizes. The product depends on the reservoir's thermal response; full release requires a zero-energy endpoint. Detected-decay waits are a distinct observable, and a registered tail test classifies them as two populations. Read retrospectively from the saved runs, the missed detections, the extreme waits and the fast component are tori that converted inside the detector's 200-sweep resting window; three long waits in one cell remain unexplained. All three registered conversion maps remain inconclusive. These results support a bounded kinetic claim while leaving the conversion mechanism and reservoir equilibration open.
