We study activated escape from a curled torus in four-regular bipartite graph energies adjacent to combinatorial quantum gravity (CQG). A coefficient $\lambda$ multiplies the local square-surplus penalty; only $\lambda=1$ is CQG, while our decay experiments use $\lambda>1$. The ideal curled torus lies exactly $4(\lambda-1)$ per vertex above zero-energy states. For a specified Metropolis edge-switch chain, enumerated moves predict mean first-exit times without fitted parameters, consistently with direct measurements. During conversion, most vertices retain curled-like or flat-like square-count signatures, and a connected flat-like region usually dominates at half conversion; multiple early patches occur. Topology checks certify tori for all 120 saved endpoints at $\lambda=1.05$, without establishing macroscopic period growth. At $\lambda=1.25$, energy-conserving graph-plus-reservoir runs escape with a 12-unit budget over $N=48$--192; exhaustive neutral-class enumeration certifies the matching lower bound at those sizes. The product depends on the reservoir's thermal response; full release requires a zero-energy endpoint. Detected-decay waits are a distinct observable with unresolved long tails: a dedicated registered tail test fails in two cells. All three registered conversion maps remain inconclusive. These results support a bounded kinetic claim while leaving the conversion mechanism and reservoir equilibration open.
