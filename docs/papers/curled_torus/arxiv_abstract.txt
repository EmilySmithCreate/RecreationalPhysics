In combinatorial quantum gravity (CQG) the energy of a four-regular bipartite graph is its total Ollivier curvature: a global term that rewards squares and a local term that removes the reward for a third square on an edge. We multiply the local term by a coefficient $\lambda$. Only $\lambda = 1$ is CQG; every result below is at $\lambda > 1$, in a neighbouring family where the energy is no longer a curvature. There the flat torus is a ground state, and a $4 \times L$ torus, one direction curled to a single square, lies $4(\lambda - 1)$ per vertex above it. At $\lambda = 1.25$ the curled torus is metastable: under the edge-switch dynamics with Metropolis acceptance, the minimum barrier to leave it is a single move, $\Delta E^\ddagger = 12$, offered at a rate we count exactly, so the mean wait follows an Arrhenius law with nothing fitted, and is close to memoryless. The conversion then spreads as a single front: at half conversion 98.8 to 99.8% of vertices belong to one ordered state or the other, and the release is exactly $4(\lambda-1)$ per vertex. Under an energy-conserving version of the dynamics, a seed of 12 units sets off the change at every size from 48 to 192 vertices and nothing smaller does, while the release grows with the system; the change completes only if the reservoir can absorb the release, and leaves about one relic defect of fixed size at every size tested, up to 288 vertices. Mapped from $\lambda = 1.05$ to 1.45 in three pre-registered runs, each inconclusive by the letter of its rules, the torus is metastable up to 1.35; the change keeps its single-front character to 1.30, begins to break up at 1.35, and finishes cleanly near $\lambda = 1$, where the release vanishes. The Arrhenius test and the seed threshold were exploratory; the two-state verdict at $\lambda = 1.25$ followed amendments to its energy gate, three written after the data they judged.
