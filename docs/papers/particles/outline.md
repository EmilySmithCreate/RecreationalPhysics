# Paper 18 (outline, 20 October 2026): Particles as knots in a network: what the toy's relics can and cannot be

*An outline of sections, on the author's request of 20 October ("Start to frame out the sections about particles"). Every
section says which kind of statement it rests on: **exact** (arithmetic or complete enumeration), **measured** (a
pre-registered run, with its verdict), **the author's** (her hypothesis, to be tested, with how), or **ours, unverified**
(an AI-generated reading). Published physics is marked "general knowledge, to verify" until read at its source. At
λ ≠ 1 the toy is our family around the published model, never combinatorial quantum gravity.*

**The one question.** Can the knots a burp leaves behind behave like particles: kinds with fixed masses, conserved
charges, antiparticles, pairs that cancel into light, and an excess of matter? The author's hope (20 October) is that
particles and their masses will also supply the conversion from toy units to physical ones.

## 1. What a particle has to have (the bar)

General knowledge, to verify. A short list the toy is scored against, each with its measured fact:
- **Kinds with fixed masses.** Every electron weighs the same.
- **Conserved charges.** Electric charge, and baryon number at the energies observed.
- **Antiparticles of exactly equal mass and opposite charges.** Meeting, they cancel, their energy coming out mostly as
  light.
- **Stability.** Protons have not been seen to decay.
- **Identical particles.** Swapping two changes nothing observable.
- **Confinement.** Quarks are never seen alone: pulled apart, a tube of force forms between them and snaps into a new
  quark and antiquark.
- **Mass mostly from field energy.** A proton is about 1 % its quarks' masses and 99 % the energy of the field binding
  them.

## 2. The knots the toy already has

Measured and exact (paper 2; O104, O107, O126). Three kinds of scrap, each stuck against every single move, each with a
fixed energy:
- the column (14 units at λ = 1.25; 8 with no curling cost);
- the double column (8 units);
- the knot of two kinds of point (20 to 30 units).

Their number is set at the opening, about one per three seeds (0.29 ± 0.04 per extra seed, T17; 0.35 per natural seed,
T37), not by the cooling. *Ours:* that is the shape of freeze-out, and Kibble's count of defects per region is the
published mechanism to compare it with (Kibble 1976, Zurek 1985; general knowledge, to verify). What this section can
claim: kinds with fixed masses, exactly; stability against single moves, exactly; stability against everything, not
shown.

## 3. Curl and uncurl: two kinds of defect

- **Exact.** The extra square (curl, a link on three squares) and the missing square (uncurl, a link on fewer than two)
  are the toy's two kinds of curvature. Per square at λ = 1.25 the first costs 16(λ − 1) = 4, the second 16. Flat space
  is the floor: both cost energy, as both matter and antimatter carry positive energy.
- **Exact (O22).** A square that drops in flat space leaves two missing squares and no curl: no partner.
- **A sandbox look, 20 October.** Points added in one place leave mostly missing squares and at most a little curl.
- **The author's, to be tested.** Curl is matter and uncurl antimatter. When a square drops, its curl points and its
  uncurl points each close into a string, one of each kind, which later cancel into light. The test: whether any rule
  of the toy makes the two kinds in pairs (section 4).

## 4. Pairs need a conservation law

- **Exact for smooth closed surfaces** (Gauss–Bonnet, textbook). Total curvature is fixed by the number of handles, so
  on a surface of fixed shape positive and negative curvature appear together.
- **Exact (the handle look of 19 October).** The toy's moves can change its topology, so its total curvature is not
  conserved, which is why its defects come alone.
- **Ours, unverified.** A rule that keeps space a surface (moves that never add or remove a handle) would make defects
  come in pairs of opposite sign. The published physics of such pairs is Kosterlitz and Thouless's, with Halperin,
  Nelson and Young (general knowledge, to verify): bound together when cold, freed when hot. That is the author's "they
  merge immediately when cold and stay apart only when hot". The section designs the rule, exact first.

## 5. Strings

- **General knowledge, to verify.** The force between quarks forms a tube that behaves as a string, with a fixed energy
  per length (the string tension); string theory began as a model of it.
- **What the toy has (exact).** The column is a closed loop of four points that still closes a square.
- **The author's, to be tested.** Strings left when curl points meet. The test is whether a loop-like knot in the toy
  has an energy that grows with its length (a tension) and whether pulling two knots apart builds one.

## 6. Matter, antimatter, and the excess

- **The target** (general knowledge, to verify). About 6 × 10⁻¹⁰ protons and neutrons per photon of the cosmic
  background: roughly one extra quark per billion pairs.
- **Sakharov's three conditions** (1967; general knowledge, to verify): a quantity that can change, a difference between
  the two kinds, a moment out of balance.
- **The author's two hypotheses.**
  1. The two kinds' unequal prices (λ − 1 against 1): the same energy makes more curl.
  2. *Withdrawn by the author, 21 October (VISION Update 82).* The excess is inherited: the black hole's stores hold eaten matter, and the part still uncurled when the snap fires
     carries into the new space. With a steady feed, the excess is the snap's duration over the feeding time.
- **Ours, unverified.** Hypothesis 2 puts the excess in black-hole timing, not particle physics. It needs a black hole's
  stores to remember that they hold matter, which standard physics doesn't expect (a black hole keeps only mass, charge
  and spin; general knowledge, to verify). As a share of energy, the target depends on the birth temperature, which is
  known only to lie between about 4 MeV and about 10¹⁶ GeV.
- **The test.** In the toy, the layer form fed unevenly: the spread of the regions' firing times.
- **The author's further hypotheses (21 October), to test, not decided.**
  3. *Withdrawn, 21 October (Update 82).* **Overcurl at the snap:** the stores hold more than the wall when the split fires (the feed keeps arriving during
     the wait), and the part above the wall is what does not cancel.
  4. *Withdrawn with it, 21 October (Update 82), unless she keeps it.* **Uneven stores:** some stores hold more than their share of the wall at the snap; the unevenness is the excess.
  5. **A background that tips the balance:** the network's negative curvature cancels a little more antimatter than
     matter (she notes this runs against her intuition). *Ours:* its published cousin is gravitational baryogenesis
     (Davoudiasl and others, 2004; not read by us, to verify), where an expanding space's changing curvature biases
     matter over antimatter.
  6. **Light into matter under pressure.** *Ours (general knowledge, to verify):* two photons with enough energy make a
     particle and its antiparticle (Breit and Wheeler, 1934), always one of each, so this supplies pairs but needs one of
     the routes above to leave an excess.
- **Exact, ours, and it bears on all of them:** the toy already prices the two kinds differently. Written link by link
  (VISION Update 5), a link on a third square costs 4(λ − 1) and a link on only one square costs 4: one to four at
  λ = 1.25, equal only at λ = 2. So the energy has the "difference between the two kinds" Sakharov's second condition asks
  for, and the snap has his third (a moment out of balance). What it lacks is a rule that says which kind is matter, and a
  conserved count (his first condition read backwards: a count that the snap, and only the snap, can change).
- **Which kind is matter (the author's hypothesis, 21 October, to test):** curl is matter and uncurl antimatter.
  Her reason: the matter that falls into a black hole in excess leaves its uncurl behind in the hole, and the matter
  coming in offsets it, its energy appearing as matter or as the light of its cancelling; concentrated light may be the
  curling energy applied at the edge that fills the stores. The billionth that does not cancel may be surplus curl,
  light turned into matter, a combination, or something else. **What this must face (ours, general knowledge, to
  verify):** matter and antimatter are measured to have the same mass to about a part in 10¹¹ (the proton and
  antiproton compared in a trap), while the toy prices a curl and an uncurl one to four at λ = 1.25. Read literally,
  curl and uncurl as particle and antiparticle predict unequal masses, which is ruled out; the reading survives only if a
  particle's mass is not the raw price of its defect (for a proton most of the mass is field energy, section 7), or if
  each kind is a bound object whose total energies come out equal. This is an exact check to make before anything is
  run on the reading. **Made 21 October (a look, `sandbox/LOG.md`):** the toy's energy is not symmetric under curl and uncurl at any λ (prices 1 : 4 at λ = 1.25, ranges two missing against one extra), and in flat space no defect within two switches carries curl alone: every curl sits inside a larger uncurl. So the raw defects cannot be a particle and its antiparticle of equal mass. **The author's answer (21 October):** the loops are not most of the mass; most is the connecting energy of the whole metastable particle, a secondary force that forms around turning a curled loop (a string) into a particle in three dimensions, with quarks of different spins grouping in specific ways. So the equal-mass test moves to bound objects, which need a binding energy the toy does not have yet (soft space is the candidate, section 9).
- **A count that only the snap can change (ours):** Sakharov's first condition, said plainly. If ordinary moves
  always make or remove curls and uncurls in pairs, the difference (curls minus uncurls) never changes, and no excess
  can ever appear; for an excess to exist, something must change that difference, and for it to survive afterwards,
  nothing else may. Her hypothesis needs the snap to be that something. In the toy, ordinary switches already change the
  difference freely, so the first step is a move set that keeps it (the surface-keeping rule of test 2), and then the
  question whether the snap breaks it.
- *Withdrawn with hypothesis 3, 21 October (Update 82).* **The excess in black-hole time (ours, arithmetic on general-knowledge inputs, to verify; a translation, not a
  match).** In hypothesis 3, with a steady feed, the overcurl is the feed during the delay between the stores reaching
  the wall and the split, so the excess as a share of the wall is (feed rate as a share of the wall) times (the delay).
  For a black hole growing at the fastest steady rate light allows (the Eddington rate, mass e-folding in about 50
  million years for a radiative efficiency of a tenth), a share of a billionth needs a delay of about 50 million years
  times 10⁻⁹, a few weeks of the parent's time. In the toy the same formula reads r × wait / 2a per pool point, so the
  test is the scaling with the feed, not the value: the toy's feeds run far faster than any physical one. The billionth
  is by number (baryons per photon); as a share of energy at birth it depends on the birth temperature (above).
- **Dark matter as made with new links (the author's hypothesis, 21 October), and the web that looks like neurons.**
  She proposes that dark matter's flaws are created as space adds links, in a regular sprinkling, and that the pattern
  then settles as a brain's does: well-used paths thicken into filaments and nodes (galaxies), little-used ones fade
  into empty, flat space, and as space expands between them the nodes pass less to each other, as an ageing brain
  does. **Ours (general knowledge, to verify):** the likeness has been measured: a 2020 comparison of the cosmic web
  and the brain's neuronal network found similar statistics of connections over a range of scales (Vazza and Feletti,
  Frontiers in Physics, 2020; not read by us). In standard cosmology the web forms the way she describes the paths: small
  ripples grow under gravity, matter drains out of the voids onto sheets and filaments and flows along them into the
  nodes, and the voids empty further with time. One measurement bears on "created as links are added": dark matter's
  density thins with the volume exactly as matter's does between the microwave background's time and today, so it was
  not being made as space grew over that time (any steady making would show up as a density that thins more slowly).
  A making early on, before that time, is not ruled out by this. The far-future part matches the standard picture of
  isolated galaxies under accelerating expansion.
- **The daughter's growth (ours, general knowledge, to verify).** With energy conserved across the change, a daughter
  starts with no more than its black hole converted, far less than our universe holds, so it must grow after the burp to
  match its mother. In standard cosmology growth comes from an energy of constant density (inflation), which then turns
  into light and matter (reheating), the excess being made after that; matter itself slows the stretch. So the excess
  routes above have to work in the daughter's hot lump after its growth, or survive the growth, which dilutes everything
  equally and leaves the ratio unchanged.

- **Matter as loops of curved space (the author's, 21 October; VISION Update 81).** The burp's light, with nowhere to
  go, is pressed into matter; matter is tiny loops that are only a relationship, no points, metastable, building quarks
  and then protons. *Ours:* relatives to read before this section is written: Wheeler's geons (1955), Kelvin's vortex
  atoms (1867), Bilson-Thompson's braided ribbons (2005). Exact in the toy: a link joins two different points of two
  kinds, so no link loops on its own point; the nearest object is a small closed cycle. A test: whether any small cycle
  or knot of the toy is a dip that survives inside a sheet with its energy held (paper 2's knots are; the loop of four
  is, but does not ring on its own, T15 rung 0b).
- **The starting quantity (ours, arithmetic, to verify; VISION Update 81).** About 10⁶¹ kg of mass-energy in the patch
  that became our observable universe if the hot era began at 4 MeV, about 10⁷⁹ kg at 10¹⁶ GeV; matter today about
  10⁵⁴ kg.

- **The author's particle (21 October; VISION Update 85): a ring of diagonal links with a twist.** Rings are made of
  squares' diagonals, which join points of one kind (exact: edge links never can); the frozen diagonal length sets the
  ring's length, different lengths are different particles, and matter and antimatter carry opposite twists. *Ours:*
  lengths must come in an allowed set, since particles of one kind are identical; a set of lengths with one twist would
  be the shape of the three generations. Tests (exact, after stage B's brief): ring energy against length; the twist kept
  by every move that does not cut the ring; opposite twists meeting and unwinding.

- **The author's decision and picture (21 October; VISION Update 86).** The particle is a twist carried around a closed
  ring, possibly braided in threes. Matter and antimatter rings meet and become two untwisted rings, photons: light is a
  twist that can be undone, matter one that cannot. Masses are equal because the energy is in the curl, not the length
  (replacing the lengths-as-particles reading of Update 85), so particles differ by braid. *Ours:* a fixed price per
  ring is a new rule (the toy charges per link); photons losing energy by a drain along the path is ruled out (tired
  light), while losing it through the expansion is standard. Benchmarks for bound twists: Manton and Wood's helium.

- **Quarks and dark matter as the cube's diagonals and faces (the author's, 21 October; VISION Update 87).** Three
  diagonal twists braid at once into a quark-like braid, colors as strand orders; dark matter as curl and uncurl loops
  on the cube's faces, paired against the neighbouring wall, heavier, no spin or charge. *Exact:* in the cubic lattice each
  cube owns three face diagonals joining points of one kind and three joining the other kind; body diagonals join
  different kinds; lengths √2, √3, √6, 3, √11. *Ours:* the dark-matter budget is 3.1 eV per photon today, so dark loops
  made one per photon would weigh about 3 eV (hot dark matter, ruled out) unless they too mostly cancel and leave an
  excess (asymmetric dark matter). Counts set beside 5.36 are not results (rule 13).

- **Speed (the author's, 21 October; VISION Update 88).** Light moves one link per tick; matter less, because its twists
  must manoeuvre; dark matter along the edges, matter through the diagonals. *Ours:* Feynman's checkerboard is the
  published form (light-speed hops with turns, mass as the turning rate); it must keep steady motion free of drag, the
  same speed in every direction and at every wavelength to measured precision, and three generations, not a ladder of
  diagonals.

## 7. Mass, and the conversion to physical units

- **General knowledge, to verify.** Matter's mass is mostly bound field energy (section 1).
- **Ours.** A conversion needs one toy quantity tied to one measured number, chosen before its predictions are seen
  (rule 13). The natural candidates are the proton's mass or the string tension, not the quark masses.

## 8. Identical particles and versions

Measured and exact (paper 13; T15): renaming counts, the loop's spectrum, and why counting versions alone cannot beat
Bell. Teased here once; paper 13 carries it.

## 9. Soft space and particles

Built and checked, nothing measured yet (VISION Update 77; ASSUMPTIONS Q26). Stage A puts a field on the points,
made to dip by the knots' curling energy, so knots can now pull through the toy's own wiring. The first registered runs ask whether two knots
drift together and whether the pull grows with their energy. Paper 11 carries the pull; this paper asks what it does
to knots as particles.
