# The owner's notes on paper 1, 14 October 2026, as rules for the other papers

Gathered from her review of *The curled torus burps* (both editions) on 14 October, for applying to the series'
other drafts, outlines and plans. Her words are quoted where she gave them.

## Technical papers (readers: physicists)

1. **Plain wording.** Replace jargon wherever a plainer phrase keeps the meaning; keep a technical term when it is
   the standard name a physicist would look for (detailed balance, Metropolis, microcanonical, canonical, Creutz
   reservoir, minimax barrier, noncontractible loop, false-vacuum decay, Kolmogorov-Smirnov). Examples replaced in
   paper 1: "their ensembles require care" (they do not all simulate the same set of graphs), "identical-model
   validation" (checks of the same model), "precursor" (arrangement), "production sizes" (our sizes), "commitment
   barrier" (barrier to completing the change), "energetic spinodal" (where the barrier vanishes), "transmission
   coefficient"/"renewal" (a correction factor for the exit rate), "equilibrium-conditional proxy" (a proxy valid at
   equilibrium), "invariant measure"/"marginal"/"multiplicity", "operational" (by the stated criteria), "affine",
   "census", "attempt-resolved", "trajectory-dependent".
2. **Tight if every physicist would follow it; clarify if not.** Her rule: "if all readers would understand, keep
   the tighter descriptions. if not, clarify." When a step needs a moment's thought (example: "the hard-core rule
   limits S_e <= 3"), add the one clause that shows it.
3. **Equations readable.** No equation squeezed to fit a column (split it over aligned lines). Another paper's
   formula is set as its own display and marked as theirs ("which Ref. [n] (its Eq. (m)) writes as"), never run
   inline next to our own equation numbers. A rewrite states the identity it uses.
4. **Not longer.** A plain-wording pass should leave the length about the same; trim low-value clauses to pay for
   any clarifying clause.
5. **Version date.** A draft revised today is dated 14 October 2026.
6. **Figures.** She asked why paper 2's technical draft has none. A technical draft whose results are carried only
   by tables should say which figures it will need (do not invent data; figures are drawn from saved results by a
   script).

## Plain-language editions and notes (readers: curious non-physicists)

7. **"Ours" is not a character.** The assistants' predictions are not narrated ("so did we", "our own guess",
   "by the author and by us"). A prediction appears only when it adds to the story; a second guess can be put
   impersonally: "another guess, which the last chapter invites, is ...". The record and the technical paper keep
   every registered prediction.
8. **Lists, not paragraphs,** for things like "who is saying what" (one tag per line).
9. **Say each point once.** A tease for another paper's subject appears once, not in three chapters.
10. **Tighten to the one question.** Strip concepts that belong to another paper and tease them instead (paper 1
    lost: named versus interchangeable dots, the trapped-heat widening test, six- and eight-link directions, the
    random-tangle route, the six-loop price, side asides).
11. **Order follows the paper.** Where a figure or section lists the paper's knobs or ideas, they come in the order
    the paper introduces them, the same in every resource (paper, figures, interactive companion).
12. **Plain numbers.** "In about 26 runs of every 30", not "24 to 28 of 30 per size". Give the share when a claim
    rests on "nearly all" (e.g. about five runs in six finished clean).
13. **A picture where a table makes the reader build one.** Paper 1's "count falls short" table became a figure.
    3D-looking drawings where shape matters, labelled SKETCH where the placement is not data.
14. **Say why it matters.** A concept section can end with where it fits in the larger story and which questions
    it shapes.

## Facts to keep consistent across the series (as of 14 October)

- **Paper 1, plain edition: ten chapters.** 1 the place we start; 2 the toy; 3 the phase before (directions,
  ring and doughnut, the ladder); 4 the obstacle (the 12-unit push); 5 the wait (the lottery; two clocks); 6 the
  change (zipper; six around; the energy); 7 close the lid (sealed boxes; what a cold box leaves); 8 the map; 9 the
  edges; 10 what we have learned. Any "PL, Ch. n" or "Chapter n of the first paper" must use these numbers.
- **What the tube turns into.** Runs that finish clean (zero energy) are rings six around where the tube was four,
  its seam re-glued two along: 9,865 of the map's 11,880 runs finished clean (about five in six), nearly all of
  them one torus six around. Runs that kept a scrap (2,014, about one in six) can hold more than one width: about
  half have a stretch wider than six, and in all 440 that hold both a six-around and a wider stretch a scrap sits
  between them.
- **Paper 2 is "What the change leaves behind"** (renamed from "What the burp leaves behind"). Kinds of scrap:
  the curled loop or column (four dots in a loop that still closes a square; 14 units in its common form, 4 in a
  lighter form; it does not go around the new tube, O126); the double loop or double column (8 units, wired like
  a cube); the twist or knot of two kinds (20 to 30 units). Scraps are **free** (inside one width; the common case,
  about three in five runs that kept a leftover) or **joints** (between a six-around stretch and a wider one; about
  one in five). The registered SEAM test (a far-reaching link) failed; a joint is a seam of another sort.
- **The size-fair clock.** One fair sweep is N/96 chain sweeps, giving every local pair the chances it has at 96
  points. It changes how times compare across sizes, nothing else.
- **Only lambda = 1 is combinatorial quantum gravity**; every other setting is our toy.
