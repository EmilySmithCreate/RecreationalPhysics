We study how a curled torus gives way in four-regular bipartite graph energies next to combinatorial quantum gravity (CQG). A coefficient $\lambda$ multiplies the price of a surplus square; only $\lambda=1$ is CQG, and our decay experiments use $\lambda>1$, where the ideal curled torus lies exactly $4(\lambda-1)$ per vertex above the zero-energy states. For a specified Metropolis edge-switch chain, counting the exits offered per sweep predicts the mean first-exit time with no fitted parameter, consistently with direct measurements. During conversion most vertices keep a curled-like or flat-like square count, and one connected flat-like region usually dominates at half conversion, though several early patches occur. Reconstruction of all 120 original endpoints at $\lambda=1.05$ certifies narrow tori with shortest noncontractible loop six at every tested size; the conversion does not produce two large directions in this sample. At $\lambda=1.25$, energy-conserving runs with a reservoir escape on a 12-unit budget over $N=48$--192, and exhaustive neutral-class enumeration certifies 12 as the lower bound at those sizes, while the available release grows with $N$. What the release produces depends on the reservoir's thermal response; full release requires a zero-energy endpoint. Three earlier registered conversion maps remain inconclusive. A fourth passes its stated criteria for $\lambda=1.05$--1.30 at $N=64$--192 and its frozen predictions at the held-out $N=288$. These results support a bounded kinetic claim and leave the conversion mechanism and reservoir equilibration open.
