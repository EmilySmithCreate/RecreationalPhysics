# Geometric Particle Model — Research Handoff
**Date:** 2026-10-24  
**Status:** Exploratory hypothesis, not a derived physical theory  
**Audience:** Coding / physics research bot

## 0. Executive summary

The working hypothesis is that the difference between empty space, traveling radiation, confined interactions, and persistent particles may emerge from **allowed configurations of paths/links with curvature ("curl"), orientation twist, and closure constraints**. Flat, unexcited links represent available space; excitations propagate along them. A traveling "half-curl" is the founder's candidate geometric representation of a photon. A primitive neutral "one-third twist" is a candidate *substrate or precursor* for quark-like structure (not a statement that physical quarks have no charge). A localized twist combined with a photon-like curl excitation is proposed as an electron-like state. Gluon-like behavior is tentatively associated with confined link excitations that connect/mediate quark configurations. Persistent bound structures arise from geometrically permitted closures and dynamical stability; prohibited arrangements may reorganize or decay.

**Most promising research direction:** test whether one economical set of link/closure rules can *derive*, rather than assign, distinguishable free waves, localized fermionic excitations, color-neutral two- and three-body composites, and their conservation laws. A geometric resemblance to particle properties is not itself evidence of a correct physical model.

**Do not silently assume** that the twist is electric charge, that a half-curl means a literal 180° bend, that photon energy comes in one fixed unit, or that every persistent structure has spin 1/2. All remain unresolved or demonstrably false as universal rules.

## 1. User's stated ideas — preserve faithfully

1. A **flat path/link exists without a photon**; a photon is an excitation traveling along a pre-existing path, not the path itself.
2. A **photon is a traveling half-curl** of energy. The exact mathematical meaning of *half-curl* is unspecified: it could be half a phase cycle, a geometric turning angle, half a closed loop, or a topological winding quantity. Do not select one without testing alternatives.
3. **Twist and curl are distinct.** A one-third twist is **not** an electric charge of ±1/3. An isolated one-third twist was proposed as a neutral building block associated with quarks; the complete configuration is supposed to yield the observed properties.
4. **An electron-like particle** is tentatively: one-third twist + one photon-like curl-energy configuration → stable localized entity with charge −1 and spin 1/2. This is a proposed generative mechanism, not a verified charge/spin equation.
5. Opposite twist configurations **combine/cancel** rather than each twist mechanically unwinding; matter–antimatter annihilation might release energy into traveling waves. The earlier suggestion of paired electron and positron formation should remain under consideration to conserve charge.
6. **Three-part combinations** were proposed as quark confinement structures coordinated by gluon-like interactions, while quark–antiquark pairs (mesons) require an additional two-part color-neutral route.
7. **Gluons are photon-like in some respects**, but remain confined/localized to strong-interaction systems; the user suspects a difference in whether/how they carry the proposed half-curl excitation. **Ambiguity:** the most recent statement about gluons 'holding 1/2 curl like photons' did not unambiguously specify whether they have, lack, or confine it. Keep both options in the hypothesis ledger.
8. A **1×1×4 diagonal/path** was suggested as one candidate link geometry. This is a toy-model candidate, not an established gluon geometry.
9. **Mass may be stored curvature/curl energy**, perhaps with a coupling to twist or spin. The user suspects discrete particle-like mass values, while recognizing not all energy is discretized into identical chunks; postpone the plasma question.
10. The user initially proposed that persistence required spin 1/2 and integer charge. This should be **revised**: persistent configurations must obey conservation laws and lack energetically/quantum-mechanically allowed rapid decay; they need not have spin 1/2. The user's insight about 360° spin-1 rotation behaving like an untwisted/zero net orientation may motivate a topological analogy, but spin 0 and spin 1 remain physically distinct.

## 2. Reality constraints — nonnegotiable benchmarks

Distinguish *observed properties* from hypothetical geometrical explanations.

| Object | Observed spin | Electric charge (in e) | Essential constraint |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Photon | 1 | 0 | Massless electromagnetic excitation; freely propagates in vacuum; helicities ±1; photon energy E = hν, not a universal fixed 'curl quantum' energy. |
| Gluon | 1 | 0 | Massless QCD field excitation with **color** interactions; eight independent gluon degrees of freedom (SU(3) adjoint); no isolated asymptotic gluons under ordinary confinement. |
| Electron | 1/2 | −1 | Color-neutral elementary charged lepton in the Standard Model; rest energy ≈ 0.51099895 MeV; fermionic exchange statistics. |
| Positron | 1/2 | +1 | Electron antiparticle with equal rest mass; total charge conserved in pair creation/annihilation. |
| Up quark | 1/2 | +2/3 | Three color states; does not exist as an isolated observable under ordinary conditions. |
| Down quark | 1/2 | −1/3 | Three color states; same confinement issue. |
| Proton (uud) | 1/2 | +1 | Color-singlet composite; mass mostly from QCD field/kinetic energy rather than sum of quark bare masses. |
| Neutron (udd) | 1/2 | 0 | Free neutron decays (lifetime about 15 minutes); neutrons bound in many nuclei can be stable. |
| Neutral pion π0 | 0 | 0 | Quantum combination of u anti-u and d anti-d; decays chiefly to two photons, lifetime ≈ 8.4×10^-17 s. |
| Charged pion π+ (u anti-d) | 0 | +1 | Primarily decays to μ+ and νμ; cannot decay solely into neutral photons. |
| Helium-4 atom (ground state) | 0 | 0 | Stable counterexample to 'only spin-1/2 configurations persist'. |

**Additional structural constraints:**
- A quark–antiquark **meson** and a three-quark **baryon** are both possible color singlets; 'three twists only' cannot be universal.
- Integer electric charge of observable hadrons is **not** equivalent to color neutrality. Quarks have fractional electric charge; leptons have integer charge; all charge is conserved. A neutral building block + neutral photon cannot yield a lone charged electron without another sector carrying +1.
- Spin is a rotation representation, not literal material rotation. A spin-1/2 state changes sign under a 360° rotation and recovers after 720°. A spin-1 state returns after 360° but is not spin-0. A sketch of a half-turn is not a derivation of fermionic spin or Pauli statistics.
- Free one-photon conversion into e− + e+ in empty vacuum is kinematically forbidden; allowed examples include γ+γ→e−+e+ or conversion near a nucleus that recoils. Threshold total rest energy is ≈1.022 MeV.
- Gluons are confined because of non-Abelian color dynamics in QCD, **not an established absence/presence of geometric half-curl**. A model may try to derive that feature, but must match QCD.
- The Standard Model electron mass comes from its Higgs coupling; proton mass is dominated by QCD dynamics. 'Mass as curl energy' must reproduce these *different* mechanisms or derive an empirically equivalent description.
- Stable magnetic order in iron arises mainly from electron structure and electromagnetic exchange; nuclear binding near iron/nickel is a distinct energy scale. Neither magnetic order nor nuclear stability requires ongoing classical spinning at room temperature; quantum spin persists at absolute zero.
- Persistent **field configurations** need not be particle states. They require a defined quantum spectrum/particle interpretation. Decay rates, not only topological closure, determine lifetimes.

## 3. Minimal mathematical vocabulary to define (no hand-waving)

Separate the following explicitly:
- `path/link`: oriented edge ℓ joining sites (existing even with zero excitations).
- `geometric curvature`: how the path or embedding bends (if the lattice is embedded).
- `gauge curvature / holonomy`: change in an internal phase transported around a closed loop; **not** automatically spatial bending.
- `twist`: orientation/winding/internal degree of freedom, with a precisely specified state space.
- `curl excitation`: a propagating disturbance/wavepacket with defined energy and dispersion; **do not** equate with ordinary vector-calculus curl without a definition.
- `closure`: graph cycle, group product, or topological closure — specify which.
- `electric charge Q`: conserved quantity computed from states; specify its operator/Noether symmetry.
- `color`: independent three-component gauge representation with SU(3) transformation law if making QCD claims.
- `spin`: quantum representation under spatial rotations; calculate the transformation, not infer it from a diagram.
- `mass`: invariant rest energy from E²−p²c² = m²c⁴; distinguish from a traveling photon's nonzero E but zero rest mass.
- `persistence`: stable/metastable solution *plus* permitted transition rates.

**Candidate, NOT a derived model:**

Take a directed graph G=(V,E), unexcited links as the flat background. For an initial electromagnetic analogue put U(1) phases `U_ℓ = exp(i θ_ℓ)` on edges. For a small oriented loop/plaquette p, `P_p = product(U_ℓ)` around the loop, and a simple field-energy penalty is:

`H_EM = κ Σ_p [1 − Re(P_p)]`.

Introduce optional discrete orientation variables `t_ℓ ∈ Z3 = {0,1,2}`, where 1 and 2 are inverse mod 3; **do not** yet call these physical quarks or electric charges. A candidate toy interaction is:

`H = H_EM + J Σ_ℓ f(t_ℓ, neighbors) + λ Σ_p g(t, P_p) + H_dynamics`.

The choices of `f`, `g`, evolution, and what 'half-curl' means must be explicit model variants. Require exact conservation and gauge symmetries, if imposed. A `1×1×4` candidate is an extended path in the graph, not automatically a primitive local link. Test whether changing lattice spacing/orientation destroys the supposed effect.

**Warning:** Z3 orientation bookkeeping alone does **not** implement QCD SU(3); three-valued labels do not give eight gluons, gauge self-interaction, or correct color-singlet states. Adding spinor variables by hand may make a toy model behave like fermions, but would mean spin-1/2 was **assumed rather than explained**. Both are acceptable exploratory controls, but mark them honestly.

## 4. Test program — ordered, falsifiable

### A. Geometry and propagation: simplest toy
**Question:** Can an initially flat network support a propagating 'half-curl' excitation without producing a stable localized particle?

- Operationalize at least three competing definitions of half-curl: (A1) half a phase winding/cycle, (A2) 180° path bending, (A3) half of a closed-loop/holonomy excitation.
- Launch normalized wave packets; compute conserved total energy, propagation speed and dispersion `ω(k)`, localization, and rotational/lattice-direction dependence.
- **Pass:** reproducible propagating massless mode with sensible continuum behavior and no superluminal signal; no charge arises from a neutral pulse alone.
- **Fail/flag:** wave speed chosen by fiat, half-curl undefined, energy not conserved, strong preferred lattice orientation remains without explanation.

### B. Localized composites and cancellation
**Question:** Can discrete twists and wave excitations create distinct stable and unstable bound configurations?

- Enumerate closed graph cycles and `Z3` configurations. Test whether 3 same-orientation units and opposite-orientation pairs form separate dynamical sectors.
- Compare energy minima, barriers, decay channels, and lifetimes from explicit evolution (not hand-labeled stability).
- Probe `twist + traveling excitation` and its charge conjugate as *paired* final states. A neutral initial state must have total `Q=0` at all steps.
- **Pass:** clear conservation laws, multiple state classes, some repeatable stability/decay behavior *without per-particle exceptions*.
- **Fail/flag:** assigning electron charge, mass, or spin by labels rather than deriving observables.

### C. Spin and statistics: critical conceptual gate
**Question:** Can a localized excitation transform as a spinor and obey fermionic exchange statistics?

- Define rotation operators `R(θ)` on the actual model state space and verify `R(2π)|ψ⟩ = −|ψ⟩`, `R(4π)|ψ⟩ = |ψ⟩` for a proposed electron/quark, versus `R(2π)=+1` for bosonic states.
- Check exchange statistics / exclusion as a separate condition; don't accept visual 'half twists' as sufficient.
- Survey quantum spin structures, topological soliton quantization, and the Finkelstein–Rubinstein mechanism. These are **mathematical leads**, not established explanations of elementary electrons.
- **Gate:** no claim of an electron model until both charge and fermionic rotation/statistics are supported mathematically.

### D. Strong interaction / two-body vs three-body
**Question:** Can one unified link rule support quark–antiquark mesons, three-quark baryons, and non-Abelian gauge dynamics?

- First implement a known baseline `U(1)` lattice gauge model and reproduce published qualitative behavior. Next implement or reuse a validated `SU(3)` lattice gauge baseline with link matrices and plaquette/Wilson-loop observables.
- Compare any twist scheme against SU(3): three color states for quarks, eight gluon states, gluon self-interaction, gauge invariance, color singlet `q q q` and `q anti-q` sectors, and confinement signatures.
- **Gate:** a `Z3`-only structure is not a QCD derivation. Avoid conflating geometric twist, electric charge, color, and baryon number.

### E. Pair production, annihilation and decays
**Question:** Can the model predict permitted channels and forbid impossible ones?

Required checks:
1. `e− + e+ → γ + γ` (ordinary two-photon annihilation), conserving total charge, energy-momentum and angular momentum.
2. `γ + γ → e− + e+` above sufficient collision energy; ensure free `γ → e− + e+` is forbidden.
3. `π0 → γγ`, with fast decay rather than persistence just because the state closes.
4. `π+ → μ+ + νμ` dominates; cannot produce photons alone without conserving charge. This eventually requires a weak-interaction sector beyond a minimal EM/strong toy.
5. A free neutron decays while a ground-state helium-4 atom remains stable even with spin 0.

Use known channel eligibility first. Matching actual decay rates is an advanced, separate benchmark.

### F. Mass, scale, magnetism (later-stage)
- Derive a rest-energy spectrum and check the electron's `m_e c² ≈ 0.511 MeV` **without setting that as the answer**; compare proton mass (~938 MeV) and electron/proton ratio.
- Vary candidate universal curvature coupling/constants and test whether relative masses are predicted or require one parameter per particle.
- Explain why photon and gluon are massless in the theory, why a bound proton is massive, and how the electron's known Higgs coupling is reproduced or approximated.
- Only after basic charge/spin/decay checks, investigate stable quantum magnetic order and the distinction from nuclear binding energy near iron/nickel.

## 7. Outstanding clarifications (leave as variants, do not stop work)

1. What does a 'half-curl' mean: 180° spatial bend, half phase winding, half of a closed-loop excitation, or another topological quantity?
2. Is the 1/3 twist an isolated geometrical defect, an orientation state defined modulo 3, or one segment of a threefold loop?
3. Does the 1/3 twist become an actual quark only when it has additional field structure (needed for quark charge, spin 1/2 and color)?
4. What precisely do gluons carry in the proposed picture: no half-curl, bound half-curl, or a different kind of excitation?
5. Is the electron built from a *distinct* 1/3 twist, or from something also capable of appearing in a color-charged quark? If common, why is an electron colorless?
6. What are the positron's geometrical properties, and how does pair creation preserve total charge?
7. Is 'whole-number rule' about total electric charge of isolated color-neutral states, allowed winding of a closed loop, or both? Distinguish them.
8. What physical quantity is 'stored curl energy' (energy density, field strength, integrated curvature, holonomy or something else), and does it yield a Lorentz-invariant rest mass?
9. Is the lattice a numerical regulator/approximation or claimed literal microscopic spacetime? These lead to very different predictions about rotational/Lorentz symmetry.

## 8. Success criteria and stop conditions

**Minimal scientific progress:** one well-defined equation of motion or Hamiltonian, exact conserved quantities, and a nontrivial prediction that isn't preprogrammed. A useful *negative* result is finding that the selected twist/half-curl definition cannot support a fermionic spinor or cannot conserve charge.

**Do not claim a new particle theory** until it reproduces: correct electron/quark charges and spin/statistics, two- and three-quark sectors, eight gluons and confinement, conservation laws and basic permitted/forbidden reactions. Reproducing the complete Standard Model is a far later and much harder bar.

**Reporting format:** (1) what was hypothesized, (2) what was implemented, (3) what is genuinely derived, (4) what was assumed/calibrated, (5) what disagrees with experiment, and (6) next test with the greatest ability to falsify the model.
