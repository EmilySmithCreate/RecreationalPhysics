## 1. How the ratio of about 5 is explained (published)

The idea in one line, [Zurek14] Sec. I: "The connection between the DM and baryon densities arises naturally when the DM
has an asymmetry" and "The DM density is then set by its asymmetry". The relation is [Zurek14] Eq. (2), n_X − n_X̄ ∼
n_b − n_b̄, so that with ρ_DM/ρ_B ≈ 5 the mass is m_X ∼ 5 m_p ≃ 5 GeV. [KLZ09] Introduction: "This mechanism predicts
nDM∼nB, and therefore ΩDM∼(mDM/mB)ΩB." [PV13] Sec. 1: "In most ADM models, the number density of DM particles is
comparable to that of baryons."

So the explanation has two parts, and this matters for the hypothesis: (a) **equal number densities**, which the shared
asymmetry gives; (b) a **mass ratio** that supplies the 5. The general form is [PV13] Eq. (2.14), with r∞ the surviving
fraction of the symmetric part:

    (m_DM / m_p) (η(B_D) / q_DM) / η(B_V) = [(1 − r∞) / (1 + r∞)] Ω_DM / Ω_VM

With complete annihilation (r∞ = 0) and the asymmetries fixed before chemical decoupling, [PV13] Eq. (2.18) gives
m_DM ≃ q_DM × (1.6 to 5) GeV; in another chemical-equilibrium regime (5 to 7) GeV / q_DM (Eq. (2.19), Sec. 2.5). [KLZ09] finds the
number depends on when the transfer decouples: Eq. (2.11) m_X ≃ 2.4 GeV (Ω_DM/Ω_B) ≃ 11 GeV for decoupling above the
electroweak transition, Eq. (2.13) m_X ≃ 13 GeV below it, text after Eq. (2.22) m_X ≃ 6 GeV for the L = 1 model; the paper's
overall range is 5 to 15 GeV (the HTML renders it "55 to 15 GeV", a rendering fault; the abstract page gives 5 to 15).
[Zurek14] Eq. (11), for a sphaleron-transfer model, writes the ratio as a ratio of conserved charges times m_X/m_p.

**Said plainly, and [PV13] says it in Sec. 2.5:** ADM explains why the two *number* densities are alike; it does not
explain why the dark mass is a few GeV, so the coincidence of the two energy densities is moved, not removed. [PV13]
conclusion: the standard WIMP picture "has to accept" the ratio as a coincidence; the few-GeV scale in ADM "strongly suggests that the
microphysics behind the origin of mass in the dark sector is related to SM scales such as the QCD or electroweak
scales" (the conclusion).

## 2. What an ADM mechanism must specify (published; the list is assembled from [PV13] Sec. 2 and [Zurek14] Secs. II to III)

1. **A dark number that is conserved or nearly so**, so that a dark asymmetry can be defined, and a symmetry structure
   saying which combination of visible and dark number is exact and which is broken ([PV13] Sec. 2.1). In the
   baryon-symmetric case B_con = B_V − B_D is conserved and B_bro = B_V + B_D is broken; imposing η(B_con) = 0 gives
   η(B_V) = η(B_D) = η/2 ([PV13] Eqs. (2.3) to (2.5)). The anomaly caveat: B_V must be read as (B − L)_V.
2. **A way to make the asymmetry**: the Sakharov conditions in X-number form, [PV13] Sec. 2.1, "violation of X, C and
   CP through processes that occur out of equilibrium". [MRM12] Sec. 1 maps them onto electroweak baryogenesis:
   "departure from thermodynamic equilibrium is induced by the passage of the rapidly-expanding bubble walls through the
   cosmological plasma"; "violation of baryon number comes from the rapid sphaleron transitions in the symmetric phase";
   "both C- and CP-violating scattering processes are needed at the phase boundaries". The classes [PV13] Sec. 2.2
   lists: out-of-equilibrium decays, Affleck-Dine, **first-order phase transition** ("This is analogous to electroweak
   baryogenesis"; "The movement of the bubble walls creates departures from equilibrium"; "X conservation is violated
   in the symmetric phase via rapid sphaleron transitions"; "The Higgs field driving the phase transition Yukawa couples
   to fermions in a CP violating way"), asymmetric freeze-out, asymmetric freeze-in, spontaneous genesis. [Zurek14]
   sorts the same by where the asymmetry is first made: visible sector, dark sector ("darkogenesis"), or both
   ("cogenesis"), Sec. III; Sec. III.1.3: "Electroweak baryogenesis makes use of a first order phase transition in the
   Higgs sector" and "if this transition is sufficiently first order, the DM asymmetry is frozen in"; Sec. III.2.1, a
   dark sector that "undergoes a first-order symmetry-breaking transition via a dark Higgs".
3. **A transfer and its decoupling**: in [KLZ09] a higher-dimension operator, Eq. (2.1) ΔW_eff = (1/M_i) X̄² L_i H_u,
   or Eq. (2.18) X̄² u d d / M², or Eq. (2.21) X̄² (LH)(LH) / M⁴; the abstract says these "decouple at temperatures above
   the dark matter mass, freezing in a dark matter asymmetry of order the baryon asymmetry". [Zurek14] Sec. II.2: "At
   low temperatures the asymmetry sharing interactions must decouple". The predicted mass depends on whether decoupling
   is above or below the electroweak transition ([KLZ09] Eqs. (2.11), (2.13); [Zurek14] Eqs. (16), (17)).
4. **Annihilation of the symmetric part**, which [PV13] Sec. 2 calls non-optional: "annihilating efficiently the
   symmetric part of DM in ADM models requires an annihilation cross-section" larger than a WIMP's (Sec. 2.3); "A
   minimal possibility for the efficient annihilation of (asymmetric) DM is a dark abelian gauge force" (Sec. 2.4).
   [KLZ09] Sec. 2.1 annihilates to the lightest Higgs-sector pseudoscalar, X̄X → aa, or through light doublets to
   neutrinos and taus.
5. **The dark matter mass** of a few GeV, and a reason for it ([PV13] Sec. 2.5: "the DM mass required to reproduce the
   correct DM abundance depends on the details of the chemical equilibrium").
6. **Stability** of the dark particle, often a discrete remnant of the dark U(1) ([PV13] Sec. 2.1).
7. **No later wash-out**: a number-violating (Majorana-type) mass lets particles oscillate into antiparticles, [Zurek14]
   Sec. VI: "then the DM asymmetry may be washed out via particle-anti-particle oscillations"; "Rapid scatterings prevent
   oscillations from occurring."

For the bubble-wall route specifically, [MRM12] adds the requirements that the transition be "strongly first-order"
(Sec. 2.1; the rule of thumb φ_c/T_c ≳ 1, Eq. (15); otherwise "these transitions will wash out the baryons created by
EWBG"), and that the wall's speed and thickness enter the yield: v_w ≃ 0.05 as a representative value, L_w of order
20/T, and "As v_w increases, other processes have less time to equilibrate before the relevant particle species are
captured by the bubble" (Sec. 3). The asymmetry is made in a three-step picture (Sec. 1): CP-violating scattering off
the moving wall makes an excess in front of it; the excess diffuses into the symmetric phase and biases sphalerons; the
wall then sweeps the baryons into the broken phase where sphalerons are shut off. "Baryon creation in EWBG takes place
in the vicinity of the expanding bubble walls."

The 2022 example, [FP22]: gauge group SU(3)c × SU(2)L × U(1)Y × SU(2)ℓ (Sec. II), the lightest new fermion χ the dark
matter; the first-order transition "provides exactly the out-of-equilibrium dynamics needed", the lepton-number
violation comes from SU(2)ℓ instantons active outside the bubble, and "part of the instanton-generated lepton asymmetry
outside the expanding bubble becomes trapped inside the bubble" (Sec. IV); m_χ ≈ m_p (Ω_DM/Ω_b) |ΔB/Δχ| ≈ 5 GeV (Eq. (30)) with
|ΔB/Δχ| = 84/79 ≈ 1.06, ours from its Eqs. (26) and (27) (|ΔL/Δχ| = 3, ΔB = (28/79)ΔL); the paper does not state the
number; the testable signature is the stochastic gravitational-wave background from the transition
at T* of 1.2 to 2 PeV, peak near 0.19 Hz × (g*/100)^(1/6) × (T*/PeV) × β̃ for sound waves (Eq. (41); Sec. V, Table 1), in reach of Einstein
Telescope, Cosmic Explorer, DECIGO and Big Bang Observer. The origin: [PTV12], "The dark asymmetry can then be equal
and opposite to the usual visible matter asymmetry", "leading to a universe that is symmetric with respect to a
generalised baryon number".

The lineage, as [KLZ09] cites it: "Previous models based on this idea are described in [4–9]", with Nussinov (1985),
Barr, Chivukula and Farhi (1990), Barr (1991), Kaplan (1992) among them; [PV13] has them as [68], [69], [159]. Nussinov's
abstract (INSPIRE paraphrase): if a technibaryon is very long-lived and the early universe made a technibaryon excess
comparable to the baryon asymmetry, stable technibaryons could be the missing mass.

## 3. Known constraints (published)

- **Direct detection at a few GeV.** [KLZ09] Sec. 3: a singlet-Higgs exchange gives "a potential direct detection
  signal several orders of magnitude below current bounds"; the charge-radius case, Eq. (3.5), σ_n ≃ 8 × 10⁻⁴⁴ cm² times factors of order one at the benchmark,
  "is below current bounds, but may give a signal in upcoming experiments". [Zurek14] Sec. VIII.3: light dark forces
  give cross-sections "naturally in the same range observed by DAMA" and the 2013 anomalies "prefer a DM mass in the 7-10
  GeV window", in tension with XENON10 and XENON100; [PV13] Sec. 4.2: "The excesses observed by the various experiments
  are in tension with bounds from XENON100." [Zurek14] Sec. X: "ADM models do not necessarily predict a scattering
  cross-section that is observable in direct detection experiments". (These are 2013 statements; the later null results
  from LUX, XENON1T, LZ and the retraction of most of the anomalies are general knowledge, not in these texts, to
  verify.)
- **Neutron stars and compact stars.** Because ADM does not annihilate, captured dark matter accumulates: [PV13]
  Sec. 4.4, "the DM density in the stars keeps increasing over time". Fermionic ADM collapses only past a
  Chandrasekhar-type limit (Eq. (4.33)); bosonic ADM has no degeneracy pressure ([Zurek14] Sec. VII.1: "If ADM is
  bosonic, however, this degeneracy pressure does not exist"), so a condensate can form a black hole that eats the
  star. [PV13] Sec. 4.4.3 excludes, with no self-interaction, m_DM ≈ 1 MeV to 16 GeV for σ_nχ ≳ 10⁻⁴³ cm²; repulsive
  self-interaction shifts or lifts the bound; [Zurek14] notes "the constraints are lifted due to black hole evaporation
  over most of the mass range".
- **The CMB.** [Zurek14] Sec. VIII.1: "a small relic symmetric component can annihilate and give rise to signals in the
  CMB", which sets a *lower* bound on the annihilation cross-section (about 10⁻²⁵ cm³/s per the tool's reading of
  that section); the symmetric part must be gone by recombination.
- **Dark radiation.** A dark sector light enough to annihilate into leaves radiation; [PV13] Sec. 4.1.1 bounds it
  through ΔN_eff: "While there is no strong evidence for additional radiation, current observations still cannot rule
  out" a substantial fraction of one species.
- **The Sun and dwarfs.** Energy transport, ⁸B neutrino flux, asteroseismology of α Cen B; very low-mass stars from
  captured ADM ([PV13] Sec. 4.4.1; [Zurek14] Secs. VII.2, VII.3).

## 4. Claimed signatures (published)

- The predicted mass window, a few GeV to about 15 GeV, where direct detection must look ([KLZ09] Sec. 5; [PV13]
  Sec. 2.5).
- **Suppressed indirect detection**: [PV13] Sec. 4.3, "the annihilation of DM in the present epoch is suppressed, if
  not completely absent", exponentially in the surviving asymmetry (Eq. (4.22)); the signal returns only if
  oscillations wash out the asymmetry, [Zurek14] Sec. VIII.2, "Thus annihilations may recouple today".
- Higgs decays: [KLZ09] Sec. 4, an invisible width to X X̄ through singlet mixing, or h → 4b, 4τ through light
  pseudoscalars that "can dominate the Higgs width".
- Collider: [Zurek14] Sec. IV.1, "Monojet plus missing energy measurements at the LHC combined with direct detection
  rule out scales" of heavy mediators that would leave the natural window; Z′ decays to the dark sector ([PV13]
  Sec. 4.5, tool summary only).
- For the bubble-wall route, gravitational waves from the transition: [MRM12] Sec. 4.3 says the electroweak signal "is
  typically too low in frequency to be picked up by the LIGO experiment" and detection is "very difficult", and BBO
  the better prospect, in its words; [FP22] moves the transition to the PeV scale so the peak lands near 0.1 to 1 Hz.

What this does to Update 42's sentence (ours): "which would make 5.36 a ratio of two leftovers, as she guesses" is half
right. In ADM the two *numbers* are alike and 5.36 is the *mass* ratio. A hypothesis that makes equal numbers of two
kinds of leftover at a front predicts a ratio of 1 in number and nothing in energy until each kind is given a mass.
That is where Update 149's reading would have to connect to the root-mass option, and it does not yet.

What the constraints of Sec. 3 would say about her dark matter: under her rule of 31 October the one-sided loop
carries no charge and "meets gravity and not light" (Update 149, line (i)), so direct detection is null, which
[Zurek14] Sec. X allows; the neutron-star bound bites only if the object is bosonic and has a nucleon cross-section
above about 10⁻⁴³ cm², which a chargeless object does not; the CMB bound on the symmetric part does not apply if the
sorting is perfect and the symmetric part never exists in our spacetime. None of these is a test the hypothesis can
pass, only ones it does not fail by default.

## 6. The one question for an ADM theorist

Every ADM mechanism we read removes the symmetric part by annihilation inside one universe. The hypothesis instead
separates the two excesses spatially at the front, into regions that later become disconnected (twins). **Is there a
published variant of first-order-transition ADM, or of baryogenesis at bubble walls, in which the two signs of the
asymmetry are segregated by the wall into different domains rather than annihilated, and what do the domain bounds
(CMB and gamma-ray limits on matter-antimatter boundaries) require of such a model?** If the answer is "no such variant
survives the domain bounds unless the domains are causally disconnected", that is a condition the hypothesis then
carries openly, and the toy's version of it is whether a sorting by pole survives the joining of seeds (O100).
