## (a) The mechanism

**Buchert's averaged equations** for irrotational dust over a domain D with volume scale factor a_D ([Buc00] Eqs. (10a)
to (10c), (12d), (13b), transcribed):

- 3 ä_D/a_D + 4πG⟨ϱ⟩_D − Λ = Q_D;
- 3(ȧ_D/a_D)² − 8πG⟨ϱ⟩_D + ½⟨R⟩_D − Λ = −Q_D/2;
- Q_D = (2/3)(⟨θ²⟩_D − ⟨θ⟩_D²) − 2⟨σ²⟩_D: the variance of the local expansion rate θ, minus the shear;
- Ω_k = −⟨R⟩_D/(6H_D²), ⟨R⟩_D the averaged spatial curvature;
- d(a_D⁶ Q_D)/dt + a_D⁴ d(a_D²⟨R⟩_D)/dt = 0, which "displays a universal relation between average scalar curvature and
  'backreaction'".

Abstract: "'backreaction' acts as to produce average curvature in the course of structure formation, even when starting
with space sections that are spatially flat on average." [Buc08] Sec. 1.3.3: "It may appear 'magic' that the
time-derivative of a ... averaged expansion may be positive despite the fact that the time-derivative of the expansion at
all points in D is negative ... it is this correlation (or fluctuation) that adds 'kinematical pressure'." Sec. 3.2.1:
volume acceleration needs "Q_D > 4πG⟨ϱ⟩_D ... This requires that Q_D is positive, i.e. shear fluctuations are superseded
by expansion fluctuations and, what is crucial, that Q_D decays less rapidly than the averaged density".

**Negative curvature.** [Buc08] Sec. 3.2.4: "positive backreaction, capable of mimicking Dark Energy, is fed by the global
'curvature energy reservoir'"; "the averaged curvature changes from an almost negligible value at the CMB epoch to a
cosmologically relevant negative curvature today." Sec. 3.1.3: "Dark Energy cannot be routed back to inhomogeneities on
large scales in Newtonian and quasi-Newtonian models".

**Timescape: two scales, two clocks.** [Wil07b]: walls at "finite infinity" are "assumed to be spatially flat"; "Within
voids the metric is not given by (1) but is negatively curved"; the average curvature "is due to the voids only, which
are assumed to have k_v < 0"; H̄ = f_v H_v + f_w H_w. The clock argument: "the locally defined or bare Hubble parameter,
H̄, can be uniform even though voids appear to expand faster than the bubble walls which surround them, since cosmic
clocks within voids tick faster on account of gravitational energy differences." [Wil07a] Sec. 4.2: "The fact that the
lapse function is generally greater than one is a consequence of the positive gravitational energy associated with
negative spatial curvature." On the late-time tracker the wall rate is "exactly that of an Einstein-de Sitter universe,
but in volume average time not locally measured wall time", voids expand as an empty (Milne) universe, H_w/H_v = 2/3,
and the mean lapse is γ̄ = 1 + ½ f_v. [Wil07a] Sec. 5.5: "in the transition epoch the volume of the less rapidly
decelerating regions increases dramatically, giving rise to apparent acceleration in the volume average."

## (b) Real or apparent; "why now"

**Wiltshire: apparent.** [Wil07a] abstract: "an apparent effect due to quasilocal gravitational energy differences that
arise in the decoupling of bound systems from the global expansion", with matter "obeying the strong energy condition".
[Wil07b]: the volume-average q̄ = 2(1 − f_v)²/(2 + f_v)² "remains positive at all times. Thus a volume-average observer in
freely expanding space detects no cosmic acceleration." A wall observer's q "begins at the Einstein-de Sitter value,
q ≃ ½, for small f_v but then changes sign at epoch when f_v ≃ 0.5867", reaching about −0.043 at f_v ≃ 0.7736 ([Wil07a] Sec. 6.3's
fit starts it at z = 0.909).

**Buchert: the averaged volume itself can accelerate** (his condition above); whether ours does is open ("The present
status of this subject is intermediate", [Buc08] abstract). In timescape's tracker it does not; the acceleration comes
from comparing clocks.

**Why now.** [Wil07a] Sec. 10: "the other coincidence as to why cosmic 'acceleration' should occur at the same epoch when
the largest structures form is naturally solved. Voids are associated with negative spatial curvature ... it is at the
epoch when the gravitational energy gradient changes significantly that apparent cosmic acceleration is seen." [Buc08]
Sec. 3.2.4: curvature evolving with structure "is actually what one needs in order to solve the coincidence problem".

## (c) The critiques and the replies

**[IW06]**, abstract, first word "No.": "If the universe is accurately described by a Newtonianly perturbed FLRW metric,
then the back-reaction of inhomogeneities on the dynamics of the universe is negligible." They grant (Sec. 2) that
this "is merely an assertion". Sec. 3: two disconnected dust
universes, one expanding and one contracting, give "a very simple model where the universe is accelerating according to
the definition eq. (17), but all observers see only deceleration"; in empty Minkowski space a slice of negatively curved
hyperboloids gives 3ä_D/a_D = −⟨R⟩_D > 0, so "'acceleration' as defined by the above averaging procedure can easily arise
as a gauge artifact produced by a suitable choice of time slicing." Their demand: compute the observables.

**[GW11]** is a theorem under assumptions (Sec. II, (i) to (iv)): a family of metrics g_ab(λ) solving Einstein's equation
with matter obeying the weak energy condition, deviations |h_ab| of order λ, first derivatives bounded, and weak limits of
their products existing. Then the effective stress-energy of small-scale inhomogeneities is "traceless and has positive
energy density--corresponding to the presence of gravitational radiation. In particular, nonlinear effects produced by
small scale inhomogeneities cannot mimic the effects of dark energy." Its application to our universe is argued, not
proved (Sec. V).

**Replies.** [Wil07a] Sec. 4: [IW06]'s "seemingly impossible burden" "can be resolved not by departing from an average
FLRW geometry ... but by a careful reinterpretation of the relationship between the parameters of the average homogeneous
geometry and our own observations." [BCE15], abstract "No.": "the proof of the trace-free property of backreaction is
unphysical and the theorem about it fails to be a mathematically general statement; (iii) the scheme that underlies the
trace-free theorem does not involve averaging and therefore does not capture crucial non-local effects". [GW15]: "There
are two cases where our analysis does not apply (and was never intended or claimed to apply)": a metric close to no FLRW
metric, and averaging schemes that build an effective FLRW metric from geodesics ("pseudo-backreaction"); "our results do
not apply to the Buchert formalism". [BCE15] Sec. 7: their definition "is too narrow to address this question."

*Ours:* since 2015 the dispute is over what "backreaction" means; Buchert and timescape sit in the cases the theorem
excludes, and the supernova tests answer [IW06]'s demand for observables.

## (d) Observational status

**[Sei25]**, Pantheon+ (1,690 supernovae), stretch and colour fixed at their SALT2 values: "When considering the entire
Pantheon+ sample, we find very strong evidence (ln B > 5) in favour of timescape over ΛCDM. Furthermore, even restricting
the sample to redshifts beyond any conventional scale of statistical homogeneity, z > 0.075, timescape is preferred over
ΛCDM with ln B > 1." For z_min between 0.023 and 0.054, "moderate to no significant preference"; f_v0 = 0.737 ± 0.029.
Their caveats: the SALT2 assumption "may ... potentially overlook astrophysical systematics"; peculiar-velocity and
Malmquist corrections are omitted because they assume FLRW; the full-sample evidence moved by "ln B ≈ 1.5-2.5" from
[Lane25], whose abstract claims only that timescape "may provide a better overall fit". The very strong figure comes from
supernovae inside the homogeneity scale; beyond it, moderate.

**[Cam24]** (DES five-year sample): supernovae alone weakly prefer timescape, but with BAO "the datasets BAO and
DES-SN5YR are in tension in the Timescape model, and this model is therefore disfavoured relative to Flat-ΛCDM by the AIC
statistic." [Sei25] replies that this BAO treatment uses a ΛCDM calibration of the drag epoch, "which is not the case in
timescape".

## What this means for the owner's rule (ours, unverified)

1. The shape matches and is published. In timescape, negatively curved voids expand faster, bound regions not at all, and
   as voids fill space a wall observer's deceleration turns to apparent acceleration near f_v ≈ 0.59: decelerating, then
   accelerating, timed by structure, within general relativity, with a claimed supernova fit. Under rule 13 that makes it
   a target, not a confirmation.
2. Her rule differs twice. Flat walls grow too (as Einstein-de Sitter), so flatness is not a threshold; only bound systems
   stop. And timescape's acceleration is apparent, from void clocks running faster; the volume average never accelerates.
   A rule that switches growth off near matter is a new law, not general relativity, and must say which it means.
3. [IW06]'s Minkowski example is aimed squarely at curvature rules: averaging over negatively curved slices gives
   "acceleration" in empty space with nothing observable. Such a rule has to be scored on distances and redshifts.
