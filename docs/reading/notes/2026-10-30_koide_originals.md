## Which paper is first, and what it says

- The relation dates from 1982: Y. Koide, "Fermion-boson two-body model of quarks and leptons and Cabibbo mixing", Lett.
  Nuovo Cim. 34 (1982) 201 (DOI 10.1007/BF02817096). The title this project's bibliography gives [Koide83] belongs to Phys.
  Lett. B 120 (1983) 161; the third origin paper is Phys. Rev. D 28 (1983) 252. Koide always cites the three together.
- In those papers it is **not** written as the sum rule. It is a parametrization: m_i = m₀ (z_i + z₀)², with z₁ + z₂ + z₃ = 0
  and z₀ = √((z₁² + z₂² + z₃²)/3) (hep-ph/0506247, Eqs. 1.4 to 1.6; 0706.2534, footnote 4). The explicit form
  Σm = (2/3)(Σ√m)² "was given in Ref. [5]", the 1990 paper.
- The composite picture behind the square root: the mass is a bilinear, (M_e)_ij = m₀ Σ_k G_ik G_jk, "a radiative-mass-
  generation-like mechanism" with couplings G to hypothetical heavy charged leptons E (hep-ph/0506247, Sec. 2, Eq. 2.1,
  "Suggestion (A)"); the lepton is a fermion F (color 3*) bound to a boson H (color 3), grown from his "η–η′ mixing model" in
  which (e, μ, τ) are a 3 of U(3). His own verdict: "such the scenario based on a composite model has not been justified
  from the field theoretical point of view."
- *Exact (the agent's arithmetic, checked by us):* Σ(z_i + z₀)² = Σz_i² + 3z₀² = 2Σz_i², and (Σ(z_i + z₀))² = 9z₀² = 3Σz_i²;
  the ratio is 2/3. So the 2/3 is the content of "z₀ is the rms of three numbers that sum to zero", which in the later
  language is "the singlet part of the VEV equals the octet part" (Foot's 45° between (√m) and (1,1,1)).
- The tau story: the 1982 prediction (1776.97 MeV) was two standard deviations from the then-measured 1784 MeV; the 1983
  PRD paper added a correction δ to fit it (predicting 1786.6); the 1992 remeasurement (1776.99) matched the uncorrected
  prediction and the correction was dropped (Rivero and Gsponer; Koide 1809.00425, Eqs. 0.3 to 0.5).

## The 1990 mechanism (Mod. Phys. Lett. A 5 (1990) 2319; restated in 1809.00425 Sec. 2 and 1711.03221)

A scalar Φ, a nonet of a family U(3), with VEV ⟨Φ⟩ = v₀ diag(z₁, z₂, z₃); the mass matrix M_e = k_e ⟨Φ⟩⟨Φ⟩ (so √m are the
VEVs); the potential V = μ²[ΦΦ] + λ[ΦΦ]² + ε[Φ₈Φ₈][ΦΦ]² with Φ₈ the traceless part (the ε term breaks U(3) to SU(3)).
Minimising gives [ΦΦ] − (2/3)[Φ]² = 0 (Eq. 2.7), "just our mass relation K = 2/3", "independent of the potential parameters
μ and λ". The abstract adds that the rule "is also in good agreement with experiment when applied to the masses of
down-quarks, d, s, and b". Caveat he states later: these are masses "defined in the U(3)-family symmetry limit", not pole
masses (1711.03221).

## What Koide says about the pieces this project cares about

- **The phase 2/9 (gap G2):** his θ_e = 42.7324° is Brannen's δ in another convention (θ_e = π/6 + 2/9; the π/6 is the
  conversion between his sine form and Brannen's cosine form). "Brannen [16] and Rosen [17] have speculated that ... θ_e =
  π/6 + 2/9. ... This is an amazing coincidence. However, at present, there is no reason for the relation (3.9). Too
  adhering to this coincidence will again push the formula (1.5) into a mysterious world, so that it is not recommended
  that we take the relation (3.9) seriously at present" (0706.2534, Sec. 3.1). In eleven of his papers from 2000 to 2018 he
  never derives it; in 1701.01921 he calls searches for mathematical quantities that reproduce the relation "nothing but
  playing of a mathematical puzzle, not physics".
- **Pole against running (a fact G2 and G3 must carry):** K(pole) = (2/3) × (0.999989 ± 0.000014); K(running, μ = m_Z) =
  (2/3) × (1.00189 ± 0.00002). "In general, the 'mass' in the relation derived in a field theoretical model means the
  'running' mass ... Therefore, the charged lepton mass relation K = 2/3 should be never satisfied by pole masses.
  Nevertheless, the relation is excellently satisfied by the pole masses ... This is a serious theoretical problem"
  (1809.00425). Hence "Suggestion (C): the mechanism which leads to the relation must work at a low energy scale".
- **Sumino's answer to that (0812.2090, full):** not a derivation of the relation but a reason it can hold for pole masses:
  a gauged U(3) family symmetry whose one-loop correction to the pole mass has the same logarithmic shape as QED's and
  cancels it if α = α_F/4; the relation imposed on the VEVs then holds "at any scale"; price: a new scale of 10² to 10³ TeV
  and (Koide's objection) an anomalous charge assignment, repaired by Koide and Yamashita 2012 with an inverted
  family-boson mass hierarchy. Koide: the cancellation is "practical ... at a level of the present experimental accuracy";
  a tenfold better tau mass would see the deviation; the model is falsifiable.
- **Quarks and neutrinos (G5, G6):** "the down-quark masses also satisfy the relation" roughly, "the value of b_u/a_u is
  considerably deviated" (hep-ph/0005137); "quarks and neutrinos do not satisfy a relation similar to (1.1)
  straightforwardly" (hep-ph/0605074); "it is hard to see such a symmetry in the quark sector, because the original
  symmetry will be spoiled by the gluon cloud" (0706.2534); his personal view (1701.01921): the relation "holds only in the
  charged lepton sector", "masses and mixings should be investigated based on M_e^{1/2}, not M_e", hierarchies must come
  from VEVs of scalars, and m_e must be nonzero from the start (with m_e = 0 the form gives m_μ/m_τ = 1/(2 + √3)²).
- **What any derivation must face** (hep-ph/0506247, Sec. 2): (a) it is in √m; (b) it is symmetric under exchanging the
  three, so the electron's nonzero mass is essential; (c) it is not invariant under the renormalization group.
- **A second relation of his, for the record:** m_e m_μ m_τ / (√m_e + √m_μ + √m_τ)⁶ = 1/486 = 1/(2 × 3⁵) (1711.03221;
  1809.00425 Eq. 2.8), from a speculated extension of the same potential. Rule 13 applies before it is used for anything.

## What it does for the owner's record

- The published ancestor of "√m is the thing that adds" (G8) is Koide's own: the square root of a mass is a VEV, or a
  coupling to a heavier partner, and the mass is its square. In her parts that is a quantity with a sign, which is what
  Brannen's negative neutrino root uses (companion note).
- The published ancestor of "thirds of a turn" is the three-family symmetry (U(3), later Z3 or S3) of the parametrization;
  the ninths (2/9) have no ancestor, by the author of the relation himself.
- "Gluon cloud" is Koide's reason the quark sector fails; her gluon runs k (G1) are the same object named from the other
  side. Ours, unverified: if the runs set the angle for the leptons, they are present in the quarks too, and the quark
  failure is then a statement about how runs and twists combine, not about the form.
