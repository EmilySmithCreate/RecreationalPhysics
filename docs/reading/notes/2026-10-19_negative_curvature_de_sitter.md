## 1. The curvature, its sign and what kind of curvature it is

- [T22] Sec. III: "we are interested in the emerging manifolds at weak but finite gravity. In this case the graph
  curvature will always be negative." Sec. IV gives the source: "most edges (ij) support a number of squares S_ij less
  than the maximal value 2D−2 ... Since T_ij=0, P_ij=0 (apart from rare defects), the Ollivier-Ricci curvature of the
  ground state ... is negative." So it is a property of the finite-coupling ground state (too few squares), not of
  defects.
- [T22] abstract: "ground-state graphs are discretizations of Riemannian, negative-curvature Cartan-Hadamard
  manifolds". The negative curvature is that of a timeless geometry of Euclidean signature, mapped in Sec. V to "a
  corresponding Lorentzian manifold of the same dimension and positive curvature". It is **not** the curvature of space
  at one time.
- [T22] Eqs. (12) to (14): for D > 2 a finite curvature as N → ∞ needs a coupling that runs with N, f(N) → 0, with "λ ...
  a fixed negative number independent of N"; for D = 2 "the curvature is determined only by the gravitational coupling
  f". Eq. (14), Λ = λ/ℓ_p², then "−Λ can be interpreted as the cosmological constant."
- [T23] abstract: "This cosmological ground state is a graph, appearing on large scales as a Riemannian manifold of
  constant negative curvature."
- [T24] Sec. IV: "Dark energy ... is a simple consequence of ground state curvature at finite coupling." Sec. III: "For an
  infinite number of vertices, we have a transition from infinite curvature at the critical point to a flat Euclidean
  plane at g=0."

## 2. How the sign turns over

An asymptotic equivalence between Brownian motion on the hyperbolic manifold and motion on de Sitter space, followed by
a prescription that reverses two signs. Not an exact analytic continuation, and not de Sitter's open slicing.

- [T22] Sec. V, Eqs. (25) to (34): the upper sheet of a two-sheeted hyperboloid, ds² = dz² + (sinh² Hz / H²) dθ², with
  "the coordinate z can be identified with the diffusion time"; the one-sheeted hyperboloid, ds² = −dz² + (cosh² Hz / H²)
  dθ², is "de Sitter space-time ... with a positive cosmological constant H²"; the two "coincide up to exponentially small
  corrections since both approach the limiting cone". Valid only for z ≫ 1/H; it "cannot be extrapolated back". The
  slicings used are the closed and the flat (Eqs. 35 to 38).
- [T22] Sec. I: "In the equivalent Lorentzian description, the negative curvature of the fundamental CH manifold becomes
  the positive cosmological constant of the associated de-Sitter space-time".
- [T23], after Eq. (20): "The absolute value of the negative curvature becomes the cosmological constant ... The
  prescription for switching from the fundamental to the effective description entails reversing the signs of both the
  time component of the metric and the curvature." And: "asymptotically, the sign of the curvature is ambiguous: 'free
  fall' in constant positive curvature becomes equivalent to Brownian motion in constant negative curvature." On flatness:
  "To make contact with our spatially flat universe we shall also consider the flat slicing of the negative-curvature
  manifold" (Eq. 21).
- [T24] Sec. II: the ballistic part "is equivalent (up to exponentially small corrections) to the geodesics of a de
  Sitter surface of positive curvature of the same absolute value, i.e. to the expansionary drag of a positive
  cosmological constant."
- [T25] Sec. V.1, Eqs. (29) to (33): "a form of Wick equivalence arising between the two signs of curvature"; Sec. VI.1
  lifts it to a (1+3)-dimensional de Sitter bulk, whose curvature "need not coincide with that of the holographic screen";
  Eqs. (45) to (47): "In the Lorentzian picture the curvature changes sign", with +Λ₃ g_μν in Einstein's equations.

## 3. Whether it changes in time

- [T24] Sec. IV: "If we consider the coupling g as a dynamical variable, it is possible to identify 1/g itself with
  universal time ... the curvature decreases from extremely high values towards zero while more and more matter is
  transformed into space-time"; later "the perceived expansion will accelerate again, albeit at a much smaller rate,
  which actually tends to zero as the ground state curvature becomes smaller and smaller at tiny couplings." So in his
  picture dark energy falls as the coupling runs.
- [T22], [T23]: no time law for Λ. [T25] IV.2: the curvature's absolute value "typically decreases" as g decreases.

## 4. Black holes (brief)

[T25] VI.2: a black hole is a random-phase bubble larger than the radius of curvature, "a hyperbolic bubble of
exponentially large curvature with all degrees of freedom on the surface"; entropy proportional to area and temperature
proportional to 1/M (Eqs. 49 to 51), equal to Bekenstein's and Hawking's for g = 1 and su = 1/2; Hawking radiation as the
bubble shrinking; "True black holes are thus only a continuum idealization which does not exist at finite universe
curvature." [T23] conclusion: "There are no singularities, no horizon".

## 5. Standard physics, for comparison (not from these papers)

- De Sitter space can be sliced with negatively curved spatial sections (Spradlin, Strominger and Volovich,
  arXiv:hep-th/0110007, Sec. 2.1(g), Eqs. 25 and 26: ds² = −dτ² + sinh²τ (dψ² + sinh²ψ dΩ²), "surfaces of constant τ are
  d−1-dimensional hyperbolic planes"); those coordinates cover only part of de Sitter space (general knowledge). The
  published model does not use this slicing.
- In the Friedmann equation a spatial-curvature term thins as a⁻² while a cosmological constant stays fixed (Baumann,
  arXiv:0907.5424, Eqs. 21, 26, 31).

## What this corrects in the record (ours)

1. The record's "sign caution" (VISION Update 70; the notebook, 19 October) said the published model's negative
   curvature is **spatial** and de Sitter's is spacetime. That is wrong: his negative curvature is that of a timeless,
   Euclidean-signature geometry, and his prescription turns it into the positive curvature of de Sitter spacetime by
   reversing the signs of time and curvature together; his space is flat or closed. So there is no conflict with the
   observed flatness of space in his reading, and none is inherited by the owner's.
2. The equivalence is asymptotic, valid only far from the beginning, and it is an equivalence of motions (Brownian
   against ballistic), argued, not derived as an exact continuation.
3. "Dark energy" is [T24]'s word; [T22] says "positive cosmological constant".
4. In [T24] the dark energy falls toward zero as the coupling runs, 1/g being his universal time. The owner's Update 70
   freezes the coupling, so in her picture it is a true constant; that is a difference from his, to be stated as hers.
5. The curvature comes from the finite-coupling ground state having too few squares, "apart from rare defects", not
   from defects; the record's "thermal defect density" is the toy's version (missing squares at a coupling above zero),
   and the two should not be described as the same mechanism without that qualifier.
