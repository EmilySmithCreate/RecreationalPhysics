## Plain-language summary

**(a) Does "a separate charge per kind of matter" exist in the literature?** Yes, and it is a large, mainstream family
of ideas. Names to search for: **hidden sector** or **dark sector**; **dark photon**, **hidden photon** or **hidden
U(1)** (a second kind of light, with its own charge); **kinetic mixing** (the one small bridge that lets the two kinds
of light leak into each other, strength written ε); **millicharged particles** (what a dark-charged particle looks like
to us if ε is not zero); **mirror matter** (a whole copy of our particles and forces, meeting ours through gravity and
kinetic mixing only); **atomic dark matter** or **dark atoms**; **dissipative dark matter** and the **dark disk**.
[search summary for Holdom 1986, the dark photon review, Foot's review, Fan–Katz–Randall–Reece; details below.] What we
did **not** find, in the little we could see: anything that ties one such charge to *one direction of space* opening.
The nearest published link between a direction and a charge is Kaluza–Klein, where motion round a curled direction is
electric charge [general knowledge, to verify]. "We have not found" is the right wording; this was not a real search of
the literature.

**(b) What limits it?** Three things, all [search summary or general knowledge, to verify]:
1. **Collisions of clusters and the shapes of halos.** If dark matter pushes on itself too hard, the dark matter of two
   colliding clusters would drag rather than pass through. The Bullet Cluster bounds the self-interaction to about
   σ/m ≲ 1 cm²/g at 1000 km/s [search summary of Tulin and Yu]. A dark charge with its own light acts through a
   long-range force, so this bound limits how strong the dark charge can be.
2. **Cooling.** A charge with its own light lets dark matter radiate, cool and collapse into a disk, as ordinary gas
   does. Most dark matter is observed in round, puffy halos, so **most** of it cannot do this. Fan, Katz, Randall and
   Reece allow only a **small fraction** of dark matter to be dissipative, forming a thin dark disk [search summary].
3. **The early universe.** A hidden sector with its own light adds radiation, which changes how fast the early universe
   expanded; the standard constraint is on the number of light species, N_eff. Mirror models survive this by having the
   mirror sector colder than ours [general knowledge, to verify; the search summaries did not show it].

**(c) Is today's budget the same as the beginning's?** **No, and not close.** From Planck 2018's central values
[search summary: Ω_c h² = 0.120, Ω_b h² = 0.0224, H₀ = 67.4] and the standard rules for how each part thins as space
grows [general knowledge, to verify], [ours, arithmetic]:

| Epoch | Ordinary | Dark matter | Radiation | Dark energy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Today | 4.9 % | 26.4 % | 0.009 % | 68.6 % |
| Dark energy equals matter (z ≈ 0.30, about 3.5 billion years ago, to verify) | 7.9 % | 42.2 % | 0.02 % | 49.9 % |
| Recombination (z ≈ 1090) | 11.9 % | 63.8 % | 24.3 % | 1.3 × 10⁻⁷ % |
| Matter equals radiation (z ≈ 3400) | 7.9 % | 42.1 % | 50.0 % | 2.8 × 10⁻⁹ % |
| Early, z ≈ 4 × 10⁸ (roughly nucleosynthesis) | 0.0001 % | 0.0007 % | ≈ 100 % | 3 × 10⁻²⁹ % |

**The one number that is fixed from early on is dark to ordinary, 5.36**, because both thin the same way (as volume)
once they move slowly, so their ratio does not change unless one is made or destroyed. The dark-energy share is not
fixed at all: it was negligible for almost the whole of history and has passed matter only in the last few billion
years.

**(d) What counts against the owner's picture, stated as prominently as what counts for it.**

- **Against: the 68 % is not a birth share.** Under the standard account, dark energy's density stays the same while
  matter and radiation thin, so its share at recombination was about one part in a billion (1.3 × 10⁻⁹). If the third direction
  released dark energy at the Big Bang in the proportion 68 : 27 : 5, standard cosmology says that share would have to
  have been *today's* by coincidence, or the release would have to be a constant energy per volume that was tiny at the
  start and grew in share only because everything else thinned. The second reading is compatible with her picture ("the
  energy of a direction, held by space itself"); the first is not. A model that fixes 5 : 27 : 68 at birth is ruled out
  by the recombination numbers.
- **Against: a dark charge strong enough to matter is limited.** If the second direction's matter carries its own charge
  *with its own light*, then either the charge is weak (Bullet Cluster, halo shapes) or only a small fraction of dark
  matter carries it (dark disk). All 27 % carrying a strong dark charge with its own light would make dark matter cool
  into disks, which is not what is seen.
- **Against, softer: "does not act on ours" is not free.** In quantum field theory two separate kinds of light
  generically leak into each other through kinetic mixing unless something forbids it [search summary of the dark
  photon review's framing; to verify in the text]. Experiments search for exactly this leak; none has been found, which
  bounds ε to be very small over most masses [general knowledge, to verify].
- **For: the idea has respectable published relatives.** A separate charge for dark matter, not acting on ours, is
  precisely a hidden U(1); mirror matter is the version where a whole copy of our forces exists, and that has a
  60-year history (Lee and Yang 1956; Kobzarev, Okun and Pomeranchuk 1966; Foot) [search summary].
- **For: dark matter to ordinary is fixed from birth** (5.36, above), which is what a picture that makes both in one
  event needs. A ratio set at the moment of creation survives to today untouched.
- **For: "dark energy is the energy of a direction held by space"** fits the one property dark energy is known to have,
  a density that does not thin as space grows [general knowledge, to verify]. The known puzzle that today's share is
  about two thirds *now* (the "why now" coincidence) is not explained by it either.
- **Neutral: one speed for everything.** GW170817 bounds the speed of gravity to equal that of light within about one
  part in 10¹⁵ [search summary], which agrees with her answer (b) of 04:23 ET.

### 1. Dark photon, hidden U(1), kinetic mixing

**[Holdom86]** B. Holdom, "Two U(1)'s and ε charge shifts", Phys. Lett. B 166 (1986) 196. **Not read; not even the
abstract.** Title, journal, volume and page confirmed only by a search summary quoting later papers' reference lists.

- [search summary] Cited throughout the dark photon literature as the paper that introduced kinetic mixing between two
  U(1) gauge fields.
- [general knowledge, to verify] The idea: add a second kind of electromagnetism; a term in the energy mixing the two
  field strengths, with small coefficient ε, lets particles charged only under the hidden one appear to us to carry
  a charge ε times smaller than the electron's (the "charge shift" of the title).

**[FGL20]** M. Fabbrichesi, E. Gabrielli, G. Lanfranchi, "The Dark Photon", arXiv:2005.01515 (2020); also a Springer
book, "The Physics of the Dark Photon: A Primer" (SpringerBriefs in Physics). **Not read (fetch blocked).**

- [search summary] The dark photon is a new gauge boson from a hypothetical dark sector whose particles are neutral
  under the Standard Model's forces, and it can be detected through kinetic mixing with the ordinary photon.
- [general knowledge, to verify; look in its opening sections] Distinguishes a **massless** dark photon (which can be
  rotated so that it does not couple to ordinary charge at all, and dark particles then look millicharged) from a
  **massive** one (which couples to ordinary charge with strength ε e). The review's bounds on ε against mass are what
  "does not act on ours" has to live under.

**[Foot14]** R. Foot, "Mirror dark matter: Cosmology, galaxy structure and direct detection", arXiv:1401.3965 (2014).
**Not read; search summary of the abstract only.**

- [search summary of abstract] Dark matter as a hidden sector interacting with ours mainly through gravity; if it is an
  exact copy of the Standard Model, an enhanced symmetry (space-time parity) pairs each particle with a mirror partner.
- [search summary of abstract] Unbroken, the mirror particles have the same masses and the same internal dynamics as

- [search summary of abstract] The only new interaction is photon–mirror-photon kinetic mixing, whose strength is the

### 3. Limits: self-interaction, cooling, dark disks

**[TY18]** S. Tulin and H.-B. Yu, "Dark Matter Self-interactions and Small Scale Structure", arXiv:1705.02358;
Phys. Rep. 730 (2018) 1–57. **Not read (search summary).**

- [search summary] Covers small-scale structure problems, simulations of self-interacting dark matter, merging systems
  such as the Bullet Cluster, particle models and other probes.
- [search summary] Bullet Cluster: σ_T/m ≲ 1 cm²/g at v ≈ 1000 km/s. Small scales (v ≈ 10 km/s): bounds quoted in
  the literature range over 0.1 to 100 cm²/g. The section numbers were not seen.
- [general knowledge, to verify] A light mediator (such as a dark photon) gives a cross-section that grows at low speed,
  so dwarf galaxies are the sensitive place for a long-range dark charge.

**[FKRR13]** J. Fan, A. Katz, L. Randall, M. Reece, "Double-Disk Dark Matter", arXiv:1303.1521 (2013). Companion:
"A Dark-Disk Universe", arXiv:1303.3271. **Not read (search summary).**

- [search summary] Observations require only that the *majority* of dark matter be cold and collisionless; a
  subdominant fraction could interact strongly and dissipate energy, cooling into a rotating disk as ordinary gas does.
- [general knowledge, to verify] Their dark fraction carries a dark U(1) charge and radiates dark photons; bounds on the
  fraction of order a few per cent come from the Milky Way's disk dynamics. Check the numbers in the text.
- **What stops dark matter forming disks and stars**, in the standard account [general knowledge, to verify]: it cannot
  radiate, so it cannot lose energy and cool. A dark charge with its own light removes that obstacle, which is why the
  dissipative part must be small.

### 4. Kaluza–Klein: a curled direction seen as a charge

**[OW97]** J. M. Overduin and P. S. Wesson, "Kaluza-Klein gravity", Phys. Rep. 283 (1997) 303–378;
arXiv:gr-qc/9805018. **Not read (search summary gives the citation only).**

- [general knowledge, to verify] In five-dimensional gravity with one direction curled into a circle, the mixed
  components of the metric act as the electromagnetic field, and momentum around the circle is conserved and acts as
  electric charge; because the circle is closed the momentum, and so the charge, comes in whole-number steps.
- *Ours, unverified:* this is the only published route we know of from "a direction" to "a charge". Note that it runs
  opposite to the owner's picture: in Kaluza–Klein the charge belongs to a direction that **stays curled**, whereas in
  hers each kind of energy comes from a direction that **opened**. A direction that has opened large no longer
  quantizes a charge in this way.
