# Timescape against the owner's "growth only where space is flat": reading notes (25 October 2026)

**Why this note.** VISION Update 106 (25 October) records the owner's hypothesis about reality: space grows at a front
moving at the speed of light wherever it is flat; matter clumping shrinks the flat share, so the overall expansion slows;
voids taking over grow it, so the expansion speeds up; observers are most likely in between. Update 106 names David
Wiltshire's timescape cosmology as its nearest published quantitative relative, marked "general knowledge, not read by
us". **That marking is out of date:** an assistant agent read the core papers on 20 October
(`docs/reading/notes/2026-10-20_backreaction_timescape.md`, for an earlier form of the same idea). This note does not
repeat that one. It adds the numbers of the founding paper's worked example, the fit papers and the critics not read on
20 October, and answers five questions put against the 25 October form of the hypothesis.

**How it was read, and the caveat on every quotation.** Read by an assistant agent on 25 October 2026 through a page
reader: the arXiv or ar5iv page is converted to text and a small model answers questions against it, quoting. So every
quotation below has passed through a summarizer, and the symbols in it (f_v, H̄, γ̄, q̄) were rebuilt by that model.

## (a) What grows, where, and is the acceleration apparent?

- **Voids expand faster than walls.** [Wil07a] Sec. 4.2: "a larger apparent Hubble rate across voids as opposed to within
  bubble walls"; Sec. 5.1, Eq. (27), h_r ≡ H_w/H_v < 1 with H_w < H̄ < H_v. [Wil07b]: "voids appear to expand faster than
  the bubble walls which surround them, since cosmic clocks within voids tick faster on account of gravitational energy
  differences." On the late-time tracker voids "expand exactly as a Milne universe in volume-average time" [Wil07b].
- **Which regions are flat.** The *walls* are flat; the *voids* are curved. [Wil07a] Sec. 5.1: wall regions "can be
  assumed to be spatially flat"; void regions "are assumed to have negative spatial curvature on average". [Wil07b]:
  "The local average geometry at the boundary of a finite infinity region is assumed to be spatially flat"; "the average
  curvature is due to the voids only". Finite infinity ([Wil07a] Sec. 3.2, Eq. (11)) is the surface round a bound
  region where "the average expansion vanishes, while being positive outside".
- **Clocks.** [Wil07a] abstract: "Within voids γ > 1, representing the quasilocal gravitational energy of expansion";
  Sec. 4.2 bounds the void-to-wall clock ratio by γ < 3/2; Eq. (28), γ̄_w = 1 + (1 − h_r)f_v/h_r.
- **Apparent, not dark energy.** [Wil07a] abstract: "Cosmic acceleration is explained quantitatively, purely in general
  relativity" and "'dark energy' is recognised as a misidentification of those aspects of gravitational energy". [Wil07b]:
  the volume-average deceleration q̄ = 2(1 − f_v)²/(2 + f_v)² "remains positive at all times", so an observer at the volume
  average sees no acceleration; a wall observer, reading wall clocks, does (Eq. (26)). Nothing in the volume average
  accelerates.
- **The void fraction.** f_v is the fraction of the expanding volume in voids ([Wil07a] Eq. (22); [Sei25] Sec. 1). It
  grows: [Wil07a] Eq. (29), ḟ_v = 3f_v(1 − f_v)(1 − h_r)H̄/[h_r + (1 − h_r)f_v] > 0. The late-time tracker, [Wil07b]
  Eq. (19): f_v = 3f_v0 H̄0 t / [3f_v0 H̄0 t + (1 − f_v0)(2 + f_v0)]. "At very late times eventually f_v → 1" ([Wil07a]
  Sec. 5.5). **Present value:** 0.759 in the worked example ([Wil07a] Sec. 6.3: "the void fraction has risen to
  f_v0 = 0.759"); 0.76 (+0.12/−0.09) from [LNW08]; 0.737 ± 0.029 from Pantheon+ supernovae ([Sei25] Sec. 3). The
  observational motivation: "Some 40–50% of the volume … is in voids" ([Wil07a] Sec. 1; [Wil07b], citing Hoyle and
  Vogeley).

## (b) Slowing, then speeding: and when

Yes, as seen by a wall observer. [Wil07b], Eq. (26): the wall observer's q "begins at the Einstein-de Sitter value,
q ≃ ½, for small f_v but then changes sign … when f_v ≃ 0.5867"; the strongest apparent acceleration is q ≃ −0.043 at
f_v ≃ 0.7736, after which q → 0⁻. [Wil07a] Sec. 6.3, for its fitted parameters: "apparent acceleration begins at an epoch
z = 0.909", when the universe is 7.07 Gyr old (wall time) and f_v = 0.587. Today the wall observer has q = −0.0428 and
the volume-average one q̄ = 0.0153: "Both observers register a deceleration parameter close to zero" (Sec. 6.3).
[DHW17] abstract: best-fit q₀ ≈ −0.043.

*Ours, unverified (general knowledge, to verify):* in the standard model the turnover is at z of about 0.6 to 0.7 and
today's q is about −0.5. Timescape's turnover is earlier and its acceleration far weaker; it is a near-coasting model
today, not a strongly accelerating one.

**The late-time limit is coasting.** [Wil07a] Sec. 5.5, Eqs. (65) and (66): as f_v → 1, γ̄ → 3/2, q → 0, h_r → 2/3, and
"ā ∼ t". So timescape ends as the coasting universe Update 106 discussed under reading (b).

## (c) H0 times the age; bare against dressed

- **Two Hubble rates.** The bare (volume-average) rate H̄ and the dressed rate H a wall observer infers. [Wil07b] Eq. (25):
  H0 = (4f_v0² + f_v0 + 4)H̄0 / [2(2 + f_v0)]. Worked example ([Wil07a] Sec. 6.3): dressed H0 = 62.0 km/s/Mpc, bare
  H̄0 = 48.4 km/s/Mpc (ratio 1.28, which Eq. (25) gives at f_v0 = 0.759). [LNW08]: "global average Hubble constant
  H_0 = 61.7 (+1.2/−1.1) km/s/Mpc".
- **Ages.** [Wil07b]: volume-average t0 = (2 + f_v0)/(3H̄0); wall time τ0, Eq. (24),
  τ0 = [2(2 + f_v0)/(9H̄0)] · [1 + (1 − f_v0)/(3f_v0) · ln((2 + f_v0)/(2(1 − f_v0)))]. [Wil07a] Sec. 6.3: "14.7 Gyr old at
  present" in wall time, about 1 Gyr older than the standard model. [LNW08]: 14.7 (+0.7/−0.5) Gyr.
- **H0 times the age.** Neither paper states the product. *Ours, arithmetic from [Wil07b] Eqs. (24), (25):*
  H0 τ0 = [(4f_v0² + f_v0 + 4)/9] · [1 + (1 − f_v0)/(3f_v0) · ln((2 + f_v0)/(2(1 − f_v0)))], which is 2/3 at f_v0 → 0
  (Einstein-de Sitter), **0.930 at f_v0 = 0.759** (and 62.0 km/s/Mpc × 14.7 Gyr = 0.932, a consistency check on the quoted
  numbers), and 0.918 at f_v0 = 0.737. The bare product H̄0 t0 = (2 + f_v0)/3 is 0.92 at f_v0 = 0.759 and tends to 1 as
  f_v → 1. So the number is near 1 but not 1, and it is not a coincidence in timescape: it moves from 2/3 towards 1 as
  voids take over. *(Ours, unverified: whether the dressed product also tends to 1 in the limit needs the limit of
  Eq. (24) taken carefully; not done.)*

## (d) Evidence for and against, as the papers state it

**For.**
- [Wil07a] Sec. 6.3: χ² = 163.2 for 182 supernovae (Gold06), "essentially indistinguishable from the ΛCDM model".
- [LNW08] abstract: a match to the "angular scale of the sound horizon" in the microwave background and to the BAO scale,
  and H0 in agreement with the Sandage et al. value.
- [DHW17] abstract (JLA supernovae): the two models statistically indistinguishable.
- [Lane25] abstract: timescape "may" fit Pantheon+ better, with a caveat that more cosmology-independent data reduction is
  needed.
- [Sei25] abstract: "very strong evidence (ln B > 5) in favour of timescape over ΛCDM" with the whole Pantheon+ sample,
  and ln B > 1 above z = 0.075; Sec. 3 calls the latter "exclusively moderate preference". Its own caveats: peculiar
  velocity corrections omitted (Sec. 1); Malmquist bias not accounted for in one statistic (Sec. 3); the largest
  systematic is "the standardisation of the heterogeneous mix of low-z sample light curves" (Sec. 4); "The discrepancy
  between the results of the full dataset and the subsample suggest the need for further analysis" (Sec. 3). H0 is fixed
  as a nuisance parameter in both models (footnote 7). *Ours, from the 20 October note:* the very strong figure comes
  from supernovae inside the scale where the universe is homogeneous, the part of the sample most exposed to local
  flows; beyond it the preference is moderate.

**Against.**
- [KFL09] Sec. 3.1 and Table 1: with Union and Constitution supernovae the best-fit dressed matter density falls to
  0.08 ± 0.08 and 0.02 (+0.11/−0.02) against 0.33 from Gold; "the FB model is weakly disfavoured" (ln B = −1.38 and
  −1.47); the Gold best fit "is ruled out at 3σ" by Union. Sec. 3.2: the earlier H0 fit used "an arbitrary value of H0";
  Sec. 5: "there is no value of H0 that can produce a consistent cosmology within the FB framework under the currently
  available SNe Ia data". **Reply** [SmW11] abstract: SALT/SALT-II-reduced data favour flat ΛCDM, MLCS2k2-reduced data
  favour timescape; the earlier poor fit is put down to light-curve systematics and to data below the homogeneity scale.
  So the verdict depended on the light-curve fitter in 2011.
- [Cam24], quoted in the 20 October note: DES five-year supernovae alone weakly prefer timescape, but "the datasets BAO
  and DES-SN5YR are in tension in the Timescape model, and this model is therefore disfavoured relative to Flat-ΛCDM by
  the AIC statistic". [Sei25] Sec. 1 replies that the BAO analysis "assumes purely geometric adjustments to the standard
  FLRW pipeline".
- [GW11] abstract, a theorem under stated assumptions: "nonlinear effects produced by small scale inhomogeneities cannot
  mimic the effects of dark energy". The reply that timescape and Buchert's averaging lie outside its assumptions, and
  Green and Wald's own statement that their results "do not apply to the Buchert formalism", are in the 20 October note
  ([BCE15], [GW15]).
- **The microwave background in detail** (the full spectrum of peaks, not one angle) was not found in anything read here;
  [LNW08] uses one angular scale. *Ours:* that is the strongest test timescape has not, in what we read, been put to.
- **A published response to [Sei25]:** not found on 20 October (first search, not a rule-15 note); not searched again today.

## (e) Does timescape resemble "space added at a front moving at light speed, only where space is flat"?

*All of this section is ours, unverified.*

**Where the analogy holds.**
1. **The overall rate follows the make-up of space.** In timescape the measured expansion is an average over regions
   that grow at different rates, and it changes as the share of fast-growing regions (f_v) rises. That is exactly the
   owner's structure: the overall rate is set by how much space is of the growing kind.
2. **Slowing, then speeding, timed by structure.** The wall observer sees deceleration while f_v is small and apparent
   acceleration once f_v passes 0.587 (z ≈ 0.9), and [Wil07a] Sec. 10 (via the 20 October note) offers this as the
   answer to "why now". The owner's "voids taking over" is this.
3. **Bound regions do not grow.** Finite infinity separates regions that do not expand from those that do; her "no new
   points inside atoms, bodies or planets" has this as its published form.
4. **The late limit is coasting** (ā ∼ t), the same R = ct behaviour Update 106 reached under reading (b). Timescape gets
   coasting only as the end state, not at every moment, which is what her joined version asks for.

**Where it fails.**
1. **The flat regions are the slow ones.** In timescape the walls round matter are spatially flat and expand *slower*;
   the voids, which grow faster, are *negatively curved*. "Growth only where space is flat" is backwards against timescape
   if "flat" means zero spatial curvature. It fits only if "flat" in her sense means "empty of matter" (or, in the toy,
   "locally flat cells, no defects"), which is a different property; the record has to say which it means before
   timescape can be cited as support. Walls also grow (as Einstein-de Sitter), so in timescape flatness is not an on/off
   switch for growth.
2. **No front, and nothing moves at c.** Nothing in timescape is added at a moving boundary. Every expanding region grows
   everywhere at once by ordinary general-relativistic expansion; the void fraction rises because voids grow faster
   (Eq. (29)), not because a front sweeps across. The speed of light enters only as the speed of the light that is
   observed. Finite infinity is a boundary round each bound region, not a front moving at c.
3. **No points are added.** Timescape is general relativity with averaging; volume grows, but nothing is created and
   there is no count of building blocks. Her rule is a new law (Update 106: a varying count is on the parked list).
4. **The acceleration is apparent, from clocks.** Timescape's speeding up exists only for observers reading wall clocks;
   the volume average decelerates at all times ([Wil07b]). The owner's version, growth switching on with the flat share,
   would make the *volume* speed up. That is a different and stronger claim, closer to Buchert's averaged acceleration
   (20 October note), and it meets [IW06]'s and [GW11]'s objections head on.
5. **"Observers most likely in between."** Nothing like this is in timescape; it comes from [BHKP07] (Update 106).
   Timescape's observers are simply in walls, because that is where galaxies are.

**What this suggests for the record (ours).** Timescape is the published target her joined version has to meet, by rule
13, not support for it. The toy-level question it sharpens: in the toy, are the regions that would receive new points
(defect-free flat cells) the analogue of timescape's flat walls or of its curved, empty voids? If the latter, her rule
and timescape agree in shape; if the former, they point opposite ways.
