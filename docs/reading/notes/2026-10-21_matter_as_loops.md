# Reading note: matter as closed loops, 21 October 2026

**Why this note exists.** On 21 October 2026 the owner asked: "does any reading describe something like this?" Her
idea, in her words: a curl of space completes into a closed loop with no points ("the curve completing, same type
points canceling, and transforming the hypotenuse relationship from flat space into a circle which stores energy like
mass, the cost of a full curl"); two such loops coming together make two photons; "matter is a loop from 3d and dark
matter is a loop from 2d. that's why we have dark versions of everything but they don't interact." Earlier the same
day she said that most of a particle's mass is the connecting (binding) energy around the loop, not the loop itself,
and that quarks group in specific ways.

**Status of the reading.** Done by an assistant agent on 21 October 2026. Where a paper is marked "read in full" or
"read in part", the assistant read the arXiv or journal text, converted from the PDF to plain text (figures not seen).
Where a paper is marked "read in part (fetch summary)", the assistant read an automated summary of the named sections
of the HTML, not the text itself, and the quotations from it are to be checked. "Abstract only" means exactly that. The
owner has read none of these. Statements marked *general knowledge* are the assistant's background knowledge, not
checked against a source in this session, and are "to verify" before anything is cited. Nothing here is evidence for
the hypothesis (rule 13: a published idea is a target, not a confirmation). None of the keys below are in
`REFERENCES.bib` yet; they are added when a paper is cited.

## Summary: the closest published relatives, in order

1. **Topological solitons: Skyrmions and knotted solitons** (Skyrme 1961; Faddeev and Niemi 1997; Kelvin's vortex atoms
   of 1867 as the ancestor). A particle is a twist in a field that cannot be undone; its count is a whole number (a
   winding number); the mirror twist is its antiparticle; the two can meet and unwind into radiation; and the mass is
   the energy stored in the twisted field around the core. This is the closest match to four of her points at once
   (a closed or wound shape that cannot undo, cancellation with its opposite, release as radiation, mass mostly
   field energy). The difference: the twist is in a field living on ordinary space, not a curl of space itself, and
   the Skyrmion and its opposite unwind into many mesons, not two photons.
2. **Braided ribbons of quantum geometry** (Bilson-Thompson 2005; Bilson-Thompson, Markopoulou and Smolin 2007). The
   first-generation particles are braids of three ribbons of the network that space is made of; an antiparticle is
   the mirror-image braid; a braid and its mirror join into an unbraided state, which is a boson such as the photon;
   three strands is the fewest that can hold a braid; and the three quark colors are the three ways to order the
   strands. This is the closest match to "particles are made of space's own relationships" and to "quarks group in
   specific ways". The difference: mass is not derived (the authors list it as unresolved), and nothing in it makes
   dark matter.
3. **Geons** (Wheeler 1955; Misner and Wheeler 1957). Light running round a ring, held together by the gravity of its
   own energy, is a "body" with mass and no matter in it. This is the closest match to "a circle which stores energy
   like mass" made of nothing but geometry and light. The difference: classical geons turned out to be unstable, and
   the program did not reach elementary particles.
4. **Flux loops** (Jehle 1972; abstract only). Leptons as one closed loop of quantized flux; quarks as loops linked to
   others, two linked loops for a meson and three for a baryon. Close to "quarks group in specific ways"; we have read
   only the abstract.
5. **For the dark half: mirror matter and its relatives** (Lee and Yang 1956; Kobzarev, Okun and Pomeranchuk; Foot;
   Berezhiani; the "manyfold universe" of Arkani-Hamed, Dimopoulos, Dvali and Kaloper). "Dark versions of everything
   that don't interact" is in print almost word for word: a full copy of our particles and forces that meets ours only
   through gravity. What we did not find, in what we read, is the reason she gives (a loop from a lower dimension).
   The observations that limit a full dark copy are in the last section.
6. **Closed strings** (string theory; general knowledge). A particle is a vibrating closed loop; the photon and the
   graviton are modes of strings. Closest in the word "loop"; different in that the loop is the basic object, not a
   curl of space.

## 1. Skyrmions: baryons as twists that cannot be undone

**[Skyrme61]** T. H. R. Skyrme, "A non-linear field theory", Proc. R. Soc. A 260, 127 (1961). **Read:** nothing; the
reference is as cited by [MW08]. A second Skyrme paper, "A unified field theory of mesons and baryons", Nucl. Phys. 31,
556 (1962), is *general knowledge*, not checked. So is the step usually credited to Witten (1983), that in the
low-energy limit of the theory of quarks and gluons the Skyrmion is the baryon.

**[MW08]** N. S. Manton and S. W. Wood, "Light nuclei as quantized Skyrmions: energy spectra and form factors",
arXiv:0809.3501 (no journal reference on the arXiv page). **Read in part (fetch summary):** Secs. 1 to 4 and 7.1. What it
says: the Skyrme model is "a nonlinear theory of pions defined in terms of four fields" constrained to lie on a sphere
(Sec. 2, Eq. 1); static fields "fall into homotopy classes labelled by an integer B, the baryon number" (Sec. 2,
Eqs. 4 and 5), which counts how many times the field wraps space onto its own sphere; the least energy at each B is
identified with "the static Skyrmion mass" (Sec. 2); the B = 4 Skyrmion's mass, 3,679 MeV, is within 2 % of the helium-4
nucleus (Sec. 7.1); the B = 8 Skyrmion is two B = 4 cubes bound together, matching beryllium-8 (Sec. 3).

**[GP98]** T. Gisiger and M. B. Paranjape, "Recent mathematical developments in the Skyrme model", Phys. Rep. 306,
109-211 (1998), arXiv:hep-th/9812148. **Read:** abstract only. A pedagogical review: least-energy shapes at each baryon
number, their dynamics, nucleon-nucleon scattering. Named here as the review to read in full next.

**[HA01]** M. A. Halasz and R. D. Amado, "Skyrmion-anti-Skyrmion annihilation with omega mesons", Phys. Rev. D 63,
054020 (2001), arXiv:hep-ph/0009260. **Read in part (fetch summary):** introduction, Sec. 3.2 and conclusions. What it
says: a Skyrmion (B = +1) and an anti-Skyrmion (B = −1) meet; the baryon number unwinds to zero in about 1.2 fm/c (a
few times 10⁻²⁴ seconds), through "a pointlike singularity" at half-annihilation; the energy leaves as outgoing spherical
waves of pions and omega mesons, about 90 % of it by 6 fm/c, conserved to within 8 % in the simulation. Earlier
simulations with Skyrme's original equations "encountered numerical difficulties".

**How close, and where it differs.**
- *Close (from the sources above):* a particle is a configuration that cannot be smoothly undone; its charge is a whole
  number set by topology; the opposite configuration is the antiparticle; meeting, they cancel the count and release
  the energy as waves; the mass is the energy stored in the field around the core, and nuclei come out as bound
  Skyrmions. Her "most of a particle's mass is the connecting energy" matches the Skyrmion picture, in which there is
  no separate core: the field energy is the mass.
- *Close (general knowledge, and the two abstracts cited):* in real physics most of the proton's mass is not the mass of
  its quarks but the energy of the quark and gluon fields that bind them. Wilczek's review says most of the mass of
  ordinary matter comes from the dynamics of the strong force, the back-reaction of the gluon fields ([Wil12],
  arXiv:1206.7114, *Central European Journal of Physics*, abstract only); lattice calculations reproduce the proton and
  neutron masses from that theory ([Durr08], Science 322, 1224 (2008), arXiv:0906.3599, abstract only: "More than 99%
  of the mass of the visible universe is made up of protons and neutrons"). So her statement is standard physics.
- *Differs:* the twist is in a pion field on ordinary space, not a curl of space itself. And a Skyrmion meeting its
  opposite gives many mesons, not two photons. *General knowledge:* an electron and a positron at rest give two photons
  (or three, depending on how their spins are aligned), because at least two are needed to carry away zero total
  momentum; a proton and an antiproton give mostly pions. So "two loops make two photons" fits the electron, not the
  proton.

## 2. Knotted solitons and Kelvin's vortex atoms

**[FN97]** L. Faddeev and A. J. Niemi, "Knots and particles" (the arXiv title; the Nature title is "Stable knot-like
structures in classical field theory"), Nature 387, 58 (1997), arXiv:hep-th/9610193. **Read in full** (arXiv v1).
What it says:
- The field is an arrow of fixed length at every point (a unit three-vector). A knotted configuration carries a whole
  number, the Hopf invariant Q_H (Eq. 1). The energy has two terms (Eq. 2), and it is bounded below by a constant times
  |Q_H|^(3/4) (quoted from their ref. [14], Vakulenko and Kapitanskii, not read).
- The construction, in their words: "When embedded in three dimensions, a pointlike two dimensional soliton becomes a
  line vortex. For a finite energy its length must be finite which is possible if its core forms a knot."
- Each knot has an antiknot "with equal energy but opposite Hopf invariant", and the energy is "concentrated in a
  relatively narrow tubular neighborhood around the core".
- They simulated the unknot (a twisted ring, Q_H = 1) and the trefoil (Q_H = 3), and suggest all torus knots exist.
- Their first paragraph names the lineage: Kelvin's 1867 proposal that atoms "are knotted vortex tubes in ether", and
  Jehle (1972) for particles as knots.

**[BS98]** R. A. Battye and P. M. Sutcliffe, "To be or knot to be?", Phys. Rev. Lett. 81, 4798 (1998),
arXiv:hep-th/9808129. **Read:** abstract only. Stable solutions for Hopf charge 1 to 8: twisted rings, linked loops and
knots, produced by "the reconnection of string-like segments".

**[Buniy03]** R. V. Buniy and T. W. Kephart, "A model of glueballs", Phys. Lett. B 576, 127-134 (2003),
arXiv:hep-ph/0209339. **Read:** abstract only. The masses of glueballs (particles made only of the strong force's field)
modeled as the energies of "tightly knotted and linked QCD flux tubes", fitted with one parameter, with further masses
predicted. The closest thing we found to "the energy of a full curl" read as the length of a closed tube.

**[Kelvin1867]** W. Thomson (Lord Kelvin), "On vortex atoms", Proc. R. Soc. Edinburgh 6, 94-105 (1867); reprinted in
Phil. Mag. 34, 15-24 (1867) (details from search summaries). **Read:** nothing of the original (paywalled); passages
quoted in [Kragh02].

**[Kragh02]** H. Kragh, "The vortex atom: a Victorian theory of everything", Centaurus 44, 32-114 (2002). **Read in
part:** Sec. 1, the passages on Helmholtz's theorems and Thomson's 1867 address, the vortex-spectra passages, the opening
of Sec. 5 ("The enigma of gravitation"), the passage on J. J. Thomson's stability calculation, and the account of the
theory's end. What it says: Helmholtz showed that vortex tubes in a frictionless fluid "form closed rings" and "cannot
be either created or annihilated"; Thomson wrote that two linked rings "never could come into collision, or break one
another, they would form an indestructible atom"; their "definite modes of vibration" were to explain spectra, and the
sodium atom might "consist of two approximately equal vortex rings passing through one another like two links of a
chain"; Hicks's difficulty was how atoms made of a very thin fluid could have the density of ordinary matter; "At about
1890, the theory had run out of steam and was abandoned by most researchers, including its founder."

**How close, and where it differs.**
- *Close:* a closed loop whose energy sits in a tube around it, stable because it is closed or knotted, with an
  opposite of equal energy. Faddeev and Niemi's construction is, literally, a two-dimensional object made into a closed
  loop in three dimensions, which is the nearest published shape to "a loop from 2d" (but in their paper it is the
  candidate for ordinary particles, not dark ones).
- *Close, and a piece of exact mathematics (general knowledge, standard topology):* a closed curve can be knotted only
  in three dimensions. In a plane it cannot cross itself to make a knot, and in four or more dimensions every knotted
  loop can be untied. That is why every model in this section is three-dimensional, and it is one concrete sense in
  which "a loop from 3d" can carry something a loop from 2d cannot.
- *Close (ours, unverified, from [FN97]'s bound):* if the least energy grows like Q^(3/4), then one loop of charge 2
  costs less than two loops of charge 1, so loops tend to bind together; that is a published place where "the binding
  energy" of loops can be computed.
- *Differs:* the loop is in a field (or, for Kelvin, a fluid) on a fixed space, not a curl of space. Kelvin's version
  failed for stated reasons and was dropped by its author.

## 3. Braided ribbons of the network: Bilson-Thompson, Markopoulou and Smolin

**[BT05]** S. O. Bilson-Thompson, "A topological model of composite preons", arXiv:hep-ph/0503213 (v2, 27 October 2006;
no journal reference on the arXiv page). **Read in full** (figures not seen). What it says:
- Each particle of the first generation is three ribbons ("helons") braided together, joined at top and bottom; each
  ribbon carries a full twist one way (+2π), the other way (−2π) or none, and "A twist through ±2π is interpreted as
  an electric charge of ±e/3" (Sec. II, assumption 1). "Three helons seems
  to be the minimum number from which a stable, non-trivial structure can be formed", because two strands "can always
  be untwisted" (Sec. II).
- The three quark colors are the three positions of the odd ribbon out: H+H+H0, H+H0H+, H0H+H+ and so on (Sec. II).
- The antiparticle is "the top-to-bottom mirror image of that braid"; joining a braid with its mirror gives "a structure
  which can be deformed into the trivial braid", and braids and antibraids "are created and destroyed together, from
  trivial braids (i.e. bosons). This allows for pair annihilation and creation of fermions" (Sec. III). The photon is
  "a triplet of untwisted helons" (Sec. III).
- On mass, only a guess: "the more non-trivial the crossings in a fermion's substructure, the greater its mass"; the
  origin of mass is one of three "unresolved issues" (Sec. IV). Gravity and dark matter are not treated.

**[BTMS07]** S. O. Bilson-Thompson, F. Markopoulou and L. Smolin, "Quantum gravity and the standard model", Class.
Quantum Grav. 24, 3975-3994 (2007), arXiv:hep-th/0603022. **Read in part:** abstract, Sec. I, Sec. VIII B to D and
Sec. IX; Secs. II to VII skimmed. What it says: in theories where space is a network of ribbons (a framed graph in three
dimensions) changing by local moves, the braids of [BT05] appear as states that survive the network's own changes
("noiseless subsystems"), so the particles would come out of the geometry itself. Open, in their words: mass ("we have
had to make two assumptions about the mass matrix"; their hypotheses are that mass grows with the number of crossings),
how interactions arise (as defined, the network's moves "cannot tell us anything about the Standard Model dynamics"),
quantum statistics, and "How may CP violation enter the theory?" (Sec. IX).

**How close, and where it differs.**
- *Close:* particles are features of the relationships that space is made of, not things placed in space; the
  antiparticle is the mirror shape; particle and antiparticle join into a trivial shape that is a boson, the photon
  among them; three strands is a minimum; quark color is an ordering of strands. Among everything read, this is the
  nearest to "a curl of space completes into a loop" in a network.
- *Differs:* the objects are braids (strands crossing), not closed curls; mass is assumed, not derived, so her "mass is
  the cost of a full curl" has no counterpart computed there; nothing in it gives a dark copy. *General knowledge:* later
  papers by Bilson-Thompson, Hackett, Kauffman and Smolin extended the braids to interactions; not read.

## 4. Geons: mass without mass

**[Wheeler55]** J. A. Wheeler, "Geons", Phys. Rev. 97, 511-536 (1955), doi:10.1103/PhysRev.97.511. **Read in part:** the
abstract, the list of topics, the caption of Fig. 1 and the order-of-magnitude passage of Sec. 1, from an OCR'd scan of
the journal pages (the scan garbles the exponents of the abstract's mass range). What it says: "Associated with an
electromagnetic disturbance is a mass, the gravitational attraction of which under appropriate circumstances is capable
of holding the disturbance together"; Fig. 1 shows "a simple toroidal geon of zero angular momentum. Two waves of equal
strength run around the torus in opposite directions to produce a standing wave"; the simplest such geons have "a
number of standing waves around the circumference equal to an integer"; Sec. 4 (not read) covers "photon-photon
collision processes" and "violent transmutation processes in closer encounters"; the conclusion leaves open "the
relation between very small geons and elementary particles".

**[MW57]** C. W. Misner and J. A. Wheeler, "Classical physics as geometry: gravitation, electromagnetism, unquantized
charge, and mass as properties of curved empty space", Ann. Phys. (N.Y.) 2, 525-603 (1957) (pages to verify). **Read:**
nothing; a fragment of the abstract was seen in a bookseller's listing. *General knowledge, to verify:* this is the
"charge without charge, mass without mass" paper, in which electric field lines threading a handle in space (a
wormhole) look from outside like a charge at one mouth and its opposite at the other.

*Search summaries, not read:* classical geons were later found to be unstable, leaking their waves and to a collective
radial motion (Wikipedia's "Geon (physics)"; an APS 2020 talk, "Rise and Fall of the Wheeler Geon"). *General knowledge,
to verify:* Friedman and Sorkin (1980) showed that "topological geons", particles made of the shape of space itself,
can carry half-integer spin.

**How close, and where it differs.**
- *Close:* the only relative read here in which the mass is made of nothing but geometry and energy going round a
  ring: "a circle which stores energy like mass". Misner and Wheeler's handle gives a charge and its opposite from
  one feature of space, which is the published form of "same type points canceling".
- *Differs:* a geon is light held by its own gravity; nothing curls into a loop, and it is not stable. In her picture
  the loop is a curl of space with a fixed cost.

## 5. Flux loops: Jehle

**[Jehle72]** H. Jehle, "Flux quantization and particle physics", Phys. Rev. D 6, 441-457 (1972). **Read:** abstract
only, through the INSPIRE record (as summarized by the fetch tool; the opening sentence, "Quantized flux has provided an
interesting model for muons and for electrons", is quoted from it). What it says: a lepton is one closed loop of
quantized flux; a quark is a flux loop linked with another loop (a meson) or with two others (a baryon); weak
interactions come from loops that must cross themselves or each other. Close to "a closed loop is a particle" and to
"quarks group in specific ways"; to be read in full before anything is said about how it treats mass or antiparticles.

## 6. Closed strings (general knowledge only)

*General knowledge, to verify; no source read in this session.* In string theory the basic object is a tiny string;
a closed string is a loop, and each way it can vibrate is a different particle, the graviton among the closed-string
modes. A closed string wound round a curled direction carries a conserved winding number, and a winding and an
opposite winding can meet and cancel. The record already holds the published use of this: Brandenberger and Vafa's
proposal that only three space directions grow large because windings can find each other and cancel only in three
or fewer ([BV89], not read; its restatement read in `docs/reading/notes/decompactification_2026-09-25.md`), and
Alexander, Brandenberger and Easson's finding that "the winding modes of p-branes can interact in at most 2p+1 large
spatial dimensions" ([ABE00], read in full there). *Ours, unverified:* that last result is the one place in the record
where objects of different dimension interact in different numbers of large directions, which is the shape of "a loop
from 3d and a loop from 2d don't interact"; it is about which windings can cancel, not about dark matter.

## 7. Dark versions of everything: mirror matter and its relatives

**[LY56]** T. D. Lee and C. N. Yang, "Question of parity conservation in weak interactions", Phys. Rev. 104, 254 (1956).
**Read:** nothing; known through [Foot14]'s account.

**[Foot14]** R. Foot, "Mirror dark matter: cosmology, galaxy structure and direct detection", Int. J. Mod. Phys. A 29,
1430013 (2014), arXiv:1401.3965. **Read in part:** abstract, Sec. 1.1, the history passage, the passage on halos, and the
Bullet Cluster passage of Sec. 4.6. What it says: "Perhaps the simplest way to accommodate dark matter is via a hidden
sector"; in 1956 Lee and Yang "pointed out that even if the interactions of the known particles were to violate parity,
the symmetry could be restored if a set of mirror particles existed"; after 1964 Pomeranchuk and collaborators argued
"that a complete doubling of the known particles and forces (except gravity) was necessary"; mirror particles "don't
interact with any of the known particles except via gravity" and a possible weak mixing of the two kinds of light, whose
strength (about 10⁻⁹) is the one new number (abstract). Because mirror matter can radiate and cool, a halo would
collapse unless heated; Foot proposes that ordinary supernovae heat the mirror plasma. On colliding clusters he argues
the limits "might instead imply constraints on the distribution of dark matter", if most of it is in compact objects.

**[Berezhiani04]** Z. Berezhiani, "Mirror world and its cosmological consequences", Int. J. Mod. Phys. A 19,
3775-3806 (2004), arXiv:hep-ph/0312335. **Read:** abstract only. A mirror world with our particle physics, linked to ours
mainly by gravity; the formation of the light elements requires it to be cooler than ours, so its early history
differs; in some scenarios the mirror world ends with a larger matter excess than ours, and mirror baryons could be the
dominant dark matter. *Ours, unverified:* that is a published route to dark matter outweighing ordinary matter several
times over while being "the same kind of thing", which bears on the 5.36 of the record (VISION Update 42).

**[ADDK00]** N. Arkani-Hamed, S. Dimopoulos, G. Dvali and N. Kaloper, "Manyfold universe", JHEP 0012, 010 (2000),
arXiv:hep-ph/9911386. **Read:** abstract only. Our space as a sheet (a brane) folded many times inside tiny extra
dimensions; matter on the other folds is seen only by its gravity, as dark matter, because its light has a long way to
travel; this dark matter is "microphysically identical" to ordinary matter. The nearest published idea in which dark
matter is ordinary matter made dark by the *geometry* of how space is arranged.

**[ST03]** G. Servant and T. M. P. Tait, "Is the lightest Kaluza-Klein particle a viable dark matter candidate?",
Nucl. Phys. B 650, 391-419 (2003), arXiv:hep-ph/0206071. **Read:** abstract only. All particles move also in tiny curled
extra dimensions; the lightest particle with motion round the curled direction (a mode of the photon or of a neutrino)
is stable and could be the dark matter. The nearest published idea in which dark matter is a particle "of a curled
direction".

**How close, and where it differs.** *Close:* "dark versions of everything but they don't interact" is the mirror-matter
idea, in print since 1956 to 1966 and developed by Foot and Berezhiani. *Differs:* in mirror matter the dark copy is a
copy, with the same dimensions and forces; no paper we read derives it from a loop of lower dimension. The
manyfold universe and Kaluza-Klein dark matter make dark matter from the arrangement of space, but neither uses a
dimension count of the loop.

## 8. What observation already says about dark matter and antimatter

- **Dark matter barely touches itself.** In the Bullet Cluster the dark matter passed through the collision with the
  galaxies while the gas was slowed: "sigma/m<1 cm^2/g" ([Markevitch04]: M. Markevitch et al., Astrophys. J. 606,
  819-824 (2004), arXiv:astro-ph/0309303, abstract only). Combining 72 cluster collisions: "sigma_DM/m < 0.47 cm2/g (95%
  CL)" ([Harvey15]: D. Harvey et al., Science 347, 1462-1465 (2015), arXiv:1503.07675, abstract only). A full dark copy
  of our forces with dark atoms and dark gas would push on itself, which these limit (Foot's answer is above).
- **Dark matter does not seem to have flattened into a disk** as ordinary gas did: "If a component of the dark matter
  has dissipative interactions, it could collapse to form a thin dark disk"; Gaia's stellar motions "disfavor the
  presence of a thin dark matter disk" ([Schutz18]: K. Schutz, T. Lin, B. R. Safdi and C.-L. Wu, Phys. Rev. Lett. 121,
  081101 (2018), arXiv:1711.03103, abstract only; the number is in the paper, not read).
- **A dark copy must be cooler than ours** at the formation of the light elements, or its extra light would have
  changed them ([Berezhiani04], abstract).
- **Dark matter gravitates as ordinary three-dimensional mass** wherever it is measured: rotation of galaxies, lensing,
  the microwave background (*general knowledge, to verify*; the record says the same in VISION Update 36). So "a loop
  from 2d" would have to weigh like a 3D thing.
- **Antimatter regions.** If parts of the universe were antimatter, annihilation at their borders after the first atoms
  formed could not be avoided, and the gamma rays it makes would exceed what is seen unless our matter region is
  "virtually the entire visible universe" ([CDG98]: A. G. Cohen, A. De Rujula and S. L. Glashow, "A matter-antimatter
  universe?", Astrophys. J. 495, 539-549 (1998), arXiv:astro-ph/9707087, abstract only). *Ours, unverified:* for the
  owner's question whether antimatter could have dispersed while matter was trapped, the same argument applies with
  more force: antimatter spread thinly among matter would meet it sooner than antimatter kept in separate regions. So
  the antimatter is not hiding, spread out or in patches, within the visible universe; the standard account is that it
  annihilated early and the excess of matter is what was left (see `2026-10-19_cp_violation_and_baryogenesis.md`).

## 9. What none of them does, in what we read

- In what we read, no paper makes a particle out of **a completed curl of space** with a fixed cost per curl. The
  nearest are the geon (energy going round a ring, mass from geometry, but no curl and not stable) and the braids of
  [BTMS07] (particles from the network's relationships, but braids rather than curls, and mass not derived).
- In what we read, no paper derives dark matter from **a loop of a different dimension** than ordinary matter. Dark
  copies exist (mirror matter), dark matter from the arrangement of space exists (folds of a brane, motion round a
  curled direction), and the published result closest to "different dimensions do not interact" is about windings
  cancelling ([ABE00]), not about dark matter.
- In what we read, no paper ties **two photons** to the meeting of two loops of space. Annihilation into radiation is
  standard; into exactly two photons is the electron's case (general knowledge), and in the Skyrmion it is many mesons.
- These are statements about what we read on one day, not about the literature. Under rule 15 a write-up may not say
  "not found" from this note alone; a search note on the template is owed first.

## 10. What this gives the owner (ours, unverified)

Her points line up with published mathematics that can be applied rather than reinvented (the record's view is that
already-published math is good news): the winding number of a Skyrmion or the Hopf charge of a knotted soliton is a
worked example of "a loop that cannot undo and cancels against its opposite"; Faddeev and Niemi's energy bound and
Battye and Sutcliffe's solutions are a worked example of "the energy of a loop and of loops bound together"; and the
proton's mass as field energy is measured physics behind "most of the mass is the connecting energy". What the
hypothesis would add is the claim that the loop is a curl of space itself, and the toy's own closed scraps (the curled
four-point column and the knots of O107) are the objects on which such a claim could be tested. The toy result of
21 October (sandbox: curl and uncurl are not priced as mirror images) is a reason to test bound objects, not raw
defects, as candidate particle and antiparticle.
