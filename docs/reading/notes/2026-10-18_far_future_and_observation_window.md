## The eras of the standard universe [search summary; Adams and Laughlin, "A dying universe", Reviews of Modern Physics 1997; their book *The Five Ages of the Universe*, 1999]

| Era | When (years after the beginning) | What is there |
|---|---|---|
| Starlight (stelliferous) | now to about 10^14 | stars; star formation past its peak (about 10^10 years ago, general knowledge) and nearly over by about 10^11, the last small red stars burning to about 10^14 |
| Degenerate | about 10^15 to 10^39 | white dwarfs, brown dwarfs, neutron stars; protons decaying if they do |
| Black hole | to about 10^100 | black holes evaporating by Hawking radiation, the largest last |
| Dark | after | electrons, neutrinos, low-energy light |

hole dissolving) sits at the end of the black hole era, about 10^100 years. No published milestone sits near 10^1000.

## How long observation stays possible [search summary; Krauss and Scherrer, "The return of a static universe and the end of cosmology", General Relativity and Gravitation 2007, arXiv:0704.0221; Gravity Research Foundation prize 2007]

In the standard model with a constant dark energy, galaxies outside the Local Group pass beyond the horizon, the cosmic
microwave background redshifts below detectability and the light elements' abundances are polluted by stars, so a future
observer sees one island of stars (the merged Milky Way and Andromeda), infers a static universe, and cannot infer a Big
Bang. The abstract's timescale is of order 100 billion years; one secondary source says trillions. Not read in full.

*Ours:* this is the published form of the owner's "only 10 to 15 billion years left of good observational science"; her
number is about ten times earlier than theirs.

## Endings, and what the data say now

- **Heat death** (a constant dark energy, w = −1) [general knowledge]: expansion forever, the eras above.
- **Big rip** (phantom dark energy, w < −1) [general knowledge; Caldwell, Kamionkowski and Weinberg 2003, not read]:
  the dark energy density grows, and bound structures (clusters, galaxies, solar systems, atoms) are torn apart in a
  finite time.
- **Vacuum decay** [general knowledge; the [C77] picture]: a lower state of the Higgs field reached at a random time, a
  bubble spreading at nearly light speed; nothing measured dates it.
- **What is measured** [search summary; analyses of DESI DR2 baryon acoustic oscillations with supernova samples,
  2025 to 2026]: a weak preference, one to two sigma, for an evolving dark energy with w0 > −1 and wa < 0, phantom in the
  past and weakening now (a "phantom crossing" near redshift 0.4 to 0.5); several papers warn it rests on particular
  tracers and on extrapolating a two-parameter form; DESI DR3 is expected in 2027. A weakening dark energy avoids a rip.
