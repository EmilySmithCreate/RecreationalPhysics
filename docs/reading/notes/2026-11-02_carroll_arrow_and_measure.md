## 1. [CC04]: spontaneous inflation and the arrow of time

**The claim.** The abstract: "spontaneous eternal inflation can provide a natural explanation for the thermodynamic arrow
of time". The argument in order:

- **What the natural state is.** Sec. 4 concludes that with zero vacuum energy "the closest thing to a maximal-entropy,
  thermal-equilibrium state is flat empty space": gravitationally coupled systems empty out, because expansion can always
  dilute matter. With a small positive vacuum energy (Sec. 5) "most states tend to empty out to empty space, but 'empty
  space' will correspond to de Sitter rather than Minkowski spacetime". So the generic late state is de Sitter.
- **De Sitter is not an equilibrium.** Sec. 3.2.3: "de Sitter is not an equilibrium state about which we are
  fluctuating"; it is a long-lived state that can go somewhere with more entropy. This is the hinge of the paper, and it
  is also where the paper says it leaves the finite-Hilbert-space view of de Sitter (Dyson, Kleban and Susskind) behind:
  Sec. 6, "Our assumption has been that the number of degrees of freedom is infinite but fixed, not increasing during
  inflation", and "we imagine there are truly an infinite number of degrees of freedom". The paper calls the clash with
  causal-patch counting "this apparent contradiction between causal-patch physics and thermodynamics" and concedes that
  "the issues concerning the number and evolution of degrees of freedom clearly warrant further research".
- **The mechanism.** The inflaton fluctuates. Sec. 5.2: "the field can spontaneously fluctuate all the way up to phi_e in
  a patch of radius H_e^-1"; the calculation takes "an harmonic oscillator potential without any false vacua; in such a
  potential we can simply fluctuate up without any tunneling" (Sec. 5, introduction). The patch then inflates at the higher scale, and "pocket
  universes drop out of the inflating background and reheat". The paper's word is "pocket universe" (and "proto-
  inflationary universe"), not "baby universe", and it never uses "pinch off". What the parent sees is in fn. 3 of
  Sec. 5.2: "the process of spontaneous inflation presumably looks like the creation of a small black hole", citing Farhi
  and Guth [29]; the same footnote notes that half the time the fluctuated region's Hubble parameter is negative, "leading
  to a rapid collapse to a singularity inside a black hole". [FGG90] is listed among the tunneling-creation references in
  Sec. 5, not singled out.
- **The number.** Sec. 5.2, Eq. (45): the probability of the field jumping up the potential over one proto-inflationary Hubble volume is about 10^(-10^(10^56)) (evaluated with m ~ 10^13 GeV, M_pl ~ 10^19 GeV, T ~ 10^-43 GeV), with
  fn. 4, "We suspect that this may be smallest positive number in the history of physics"; the authors call it "a sensible
  minimum value" and say "The true answer may very well be bigger" (Sec. 5, introduction). Inputs: T_dS of about 10^-43 GeV from rho_Lambda of
  about 10^-47 GeV^4; the entropy of the proto-inflationary patch about 10^12 (Sec. 3.2.1, S_I ~ (L_I/L_pl)^2).
- **Why it happens anyway.** Sec. 5.3: with de Sitter lasting for ever, "the transition into our proto-inflationary universe
  is guaranteed eventually to occur. Indeed, it will eventually occur an infinite number of times", the only condition
  being that "the decay rate per Hubble volume be substantially less than unity" so that "the physical volume of
  spacetime in the de Sitter phase will continue to increase". Elsewhere "inflation continues forever".
- **The arrow of time.** Sec. 5.3: "the same set of events will naturally occur to the past as well as to the future";
  the whole is "statistically time-symmetric about some Cauchy surface of minimum entropy" (Introduction; Sec. 6, "a slice
  of spacetime on which the entropy is minimized"), and Sec. 5.3, after Fig. 9: "Observers in the very far past of our universe will also
  detect an arrow of time, but one that will be reversed from ours". The parent is high entropy (de Sitter, with the
  largest entropy any state of that vacuum energy can have), the child is low entropy relative to it (the proto-inflationary
  patch at about 10^12), and the arrow in each child points away from its own birth. The paper does not use the words
  "parent" and "child".
- **Conservation, and the parent.** The paper has no conservation statement for the birth and says nothing about the
  parent being changed by it; the calculation is "for a fixed de Sitter background" and "ignores many important subtleties,
  most obviously the back-reaction of the metric" (Sec. 5). The nearest thing to a conserved quantity is the fixed count of
  degrees of freedom (Sec. 6). *Ours, general knowledge, to verify:* energy is not globally conserved in de Sitter space in
  any case, which is why the paper has no ledger to keep.
- **Boltzmann brains.** Sec. 2: a fluctuation that explains our observations need only be "large enough to allow for a
  single conscious observer, not an entire universe", and the authors are "skeptical that any theory of fluctuations around
  equilibrium would be able to resolve this puzzle". Sec. 3.2.3: "It seems very difficult to derive a true arrow of time
  from fluctuations about an equilibrium background". The escape the paper offers is that de Sitter is not an equilibrium,
  so the question is not "which fluctuation about equilibrium is likeliest" but which observers exist in the whole
  time-symmetric multiverse; Sec. 5.3 says "the measure on what is more likely should come from observers in the
  post-fluctuation universe", and that using the volume factor this needs is "extremely difficult at best". The paper does
  not claim to have solved the Boltzmann-brain problem; [Car17], thirteen years later, is where Carroll says what it
  would take.
- **What it says it cannot show.** Sec. 5: the back-reaction; Sec. 6: "A more rigorous calculation would have to involve
  quantum gravity from the start, at least at a semiclassical level", and "how the 'initial' state is chosen" is open;
  Sec. 5: "it is quite possible that a similar tunneling into inflation may occur even in a Minkowski background", which
  would remove the need for a positive vacuum energy and is left open. [Car05]'s own summary of the proposal: "extremely
  speculative", with baby-universe creation "beyond established physics" and allowed by string theory in some settings and
  forbidden in others.
- **Relation to [BGG87].** [BGG87] (unread; in the bib from general knowledge as a bubble that pinches off as a child
  universe) is a classical false-vacuum bubble that disconnects from the parent through a wormhole and looks like a black
  hole from outside. [CC04] uses the same picture of what the parent sees (fn. 3, through Farhi and Guth) but a different
  cause: no false vacuum and no bubble wall, a field fluctuating up a smooth well. [CC04] does not cite [BGG87]: the full reference list (PDF) contains no Blau or Guendelman; it does cite Farhi and
  Guth [29], Farhi, Guth and Guven [31], and Fischler, Morgan and Polchinski [32, 33] (checked by the second agent).

## 2. [Car17]: why Boltzmann brains are bad

Sections: 1 Introduction; 2 The Origin of the Problem; 3 What Are Boltzmann Brains?; 4 What Theories Predict Boltzmann
Brains?; 5 Why Are Boltzmann Brains Bad? (5.1 The standard argument; 5.2 Typicality and likelihoods; 5.3 Cognitive
instability); 6 Conclusions.

- **The object.** Sec. 3: a Boltzmann brain (BB) is the configuration "as close as possible to thermal equilibrium, while
  still qualifying as an intelligent observer"; the principle behind the counts is that "the most common fluctuation will
  be the one with the highest entropy overall". The comparison is between two model universes: A, which ends after a
  finite time, and B, which "lasts for a recurrence time" and so "is overwhelmingly dominated by BBs" among its observers.
  Sec. 3 estimates N_OO and N_BB explicitly.
- **Which theories predict them (Sec. 4).** A de Sitter phase that lasts and in which "Boltzmann fluctuations" occur. He
  separates three kinds of fluctuation and says only the third makes BBs: vacuum fluctuations (which are not events),
  measurement-induced fluctuations, and Boltzmann fluctuations (dynamical transitions between states). The BCP argument
  (Boddy, Carroll, Pollack, not read here): "BCP therefore argued that there aren't true dynamical processes in the de
  Sitter quantum vacuum state", so "BBs don't appear in the true de Sitter vacuum", with the caveat "It's possible, however,
  that the situation changes dramatically in true quantum gravity". Lloyd's decoherent-histories reading would instead make
  BBs "inevitable in almost any cosmological model". So in Carroll's own account the answer depends on an unsettled
  question in quantum de Sitter ("an issue that is unfortunately murky in the current state of the art", Sec. 6).
- **The standard argument and its flaw (Secs. 5, 5.1).** The standard argument, "fairly straightforward" but "not quite
  correct": if BBs dominated I would probably be one, I am not, so the theory is falsified. The flaw: "we don't quite have
  data that we are not BBs, or at least not that we aren't Boltzmann Observers" and "We can't claim to have empirical
  evidence against this disturbing possibility", because a fluctuated brain's evidence is fluctuated too.
- **Typicality (Sec. 5.2).** A theory is a physical model plus a xerographic distribution (Hartle and Srednicki [SH10])
  that "specifies the probability distribution for us to actually be any of the observers in the model". Concentrating that
  distribution on ordinary observers by fiat would rescue any BB-dominated model, and he calls that "cheating": the
  distribution has to be one a reasoner could defend without already knowing the answer.
- **Cognitive instability (Sec. 5.3), the exact form.** A BB-dominated model is one in which the reasoner's own grounds
  for believing the model (memories, records, the physics learned from them) are themselves most likely fluctuations and
  so carry no evidential weight. "You cannot simultaneously conclude that you live in a randomly-fluctuating universe"
  and rely on having good reasons for the conclusion; "The randomly-fluctuating universe scenario is therefore
  self-undermining", or, in Albert's term, cognitively unstable: theories that "cannot simultaneously be true and
  justifiably believed" (abstract). The remedy is at the level of priors: the best option is "setting our prior for such a
  possibility to zero (or at least very close to it)". It is a norm of reasoning, not an observation.
- **What it demands of a cosmology (Sec. 6).** "the right strategy is to reject cosmological models that would be dominated by Boltzmann
  Brains", and "our job as cosmologists is to modify it until this problem is solved, or search for a better theory". The
  options he lists are scattered over Secs. 4 and 6: a universe that ends before BBs form (vacuum decay, which must
  percolate, needing a nucleation rate "of order one per Hubble spacetime volume or greater"); a de Sitter vacuum that is
  stationary, in an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space, so that no Boltzmann fluctuations occur (the BCP route); and the
  loophole that if there is at least one Lambda = 0 vacuum "the Hilbert space of the full theory will be infinite-
  dimensional". **The demand is therefore not "no measure" but "no domination": a model passes if its dynamics make BBs
  rare among observers, and fails if it needs the observer distribution to be chosen so as to exclude them.** The 2004
  baby-universe proposal is not named in this paper (the page reader found no reference to it).
- **What he says is uncertain.** "I don't know of a theorem" for the local ergodicity the fluctuation estimates assume;
  the conclusion "is not uncontroversial"; the Bennett and Riedel purification loophole is "worthy of further study".

## 3. [SC18]: the Born rule from self-locating uncertainty

- **Self-locating uncertainty.** Abstract: "observers are in a position of self-locating uncertainty during the period
  between the branches" forming and the observer registering the result; Sec. 2.2 names that interval and says "Actually,
  self-locating uncertainty is generic in quantum measurement". Alice "isn't uncertain about the way the universe is" but
  about "where she is in the quantum multiverse". The authors reject branch counting (Sec. 2.4): under Indifference an
  agent's credences would change when a distant part of the environment changes while nothing about the agent and the
  detector changes, which is the intuition the principle below formalizes.
- **The principle.** ESP (Sec. 3.1, Eq. 14): the credence to assign to being any one of several observers with identical
  experiences depends only on the state of the subsystem containing the observer and the detector, P(X|U) = P(X|S), and so
  must be "identical in any possible universe which also contains subsystems S in the same exact states". ESP-QM
  (Eq. 15): P(O_i|Psi) = P(O_i|rho_AD), the probability "only depends on the reduced density matrix of" the agent and
  detector. Strong ESP (Sec. 4.1, Eq. 33) adds that moving the subsystems in space and time changes nothing.
- **The derivation (Sec. 3.2).** Equal amplitudes: two states differing only in which display shows which symbol have the
  same reduced density matrix for Alice and her detector (Eq. 19), so by ESP-QM the two branches are equiprobable
  (Eq. 20 and the steps after it). Unequal amplitudes (1/3 and 2/3): an environment transformation rewrites the state as
  a sum of three equal-amplitude orthogonal terms (Eq. 26), and ESP-QM across displays gives P(down) = 1/3 and
  P(up) = 2/3 (Eq. 32). The recipe in their words: "write the state vector as a sum of orthogonal vectors with equal
  amplitudes by unitarily transforming the environment. Then (and only then), ESP justifies according equal credence to
  each such basis vector." Appendix C extends to N branches with arbitrary phases and rational squared amplitudes, and
  reaches irrational ones by continuity ("the probabilities vary continuously with small changes in the amplitudes").
  Appendix B defends the use of reduced density matrices against circularity, and notes the derivation can be done with
  state vectors alone (Carroll and Sebens 2014, arXiv:1405.7907, not read).
- **Quantum Sleeping Beauty (Sec. 4.2).** Where an observer is duplicated within a branch, Strong ESP gives 1/3 to each of
  three alternatives (Eqs. 35, 36), so the "thirder" answer in Two-Branch-Beauty and the Born values (1/4, 1/4, 1/2) in
  Three-Branch-Beauty; the extra uncertainty "arising from the duplication of Beauty's experiences on the up branch" is
  what separates self-locating credence from Born weight.
- **The cosmological multiverse (Sec. 4.3).** Observers are weighted by w_i = |alpha_i|^2 (Eq. 38) and
  P(O_i|U) = w_i / sum_j w_j (Eq. 39), which is Indifference within a branch and the Born rule across branches. For an
  observer count growing as e^(omega t) the sum is finite only if the branch amplitude decays faster than omega/2;
  otherwise the rule "fails to give a well-defined probability". They say this bears on the measure problem and do not
  pursue it. Boltzmann brains are not mentioned.
- **Against the alternatives (Sec. 6).** Zurek's envariance derivation shares the formal moves but does not explain "how
  probabilities arise at all in this deterministic theory"; the decision-theoretic axioms (Measurement Neutrality,
  Equivalence) "will be inconsistent with a branch-counting rule" and the argument for them "seems to beg the question
  against the defender of Indifference". Appendix A: branch counting is also hard to apply and diachronically inconsistent,
  not treated as decisive.
- **What it assumes and cannot show.** "We believe the principle to be well-motivated but not established beyond any
  doubt, and thus our derivation is provisional." Branching is assumed global and the branch number well defined
  ("Branch number may not be well-defined in realistic cases"); environments are assumed orthogonal; the step from the
  pre-measurement state to the branched one "could conceivably turn out to be relevant"; subsystems are not rigorously
  characterized "applicable to any conceivable theory"; personal identity is "a tricky metaphysical question upon which we
  will not speculate". Kent's reply [Kent14] (in the bib, read on 2026-10-12) disputes whether the copies have a principled
  ontology at all.

## 4. [ACJ11]: how a system fluctuates to a lower-entropy state

- **The result.** Abstract: the most likely history of a fluctuation from near-equilibrium to a lower-entropy state is
  the CPT conjugate of the most likely relaxation from that state back to equilibrium. Sec. III.2: the probability
  distribution over histories leading to a low-entropy macrostate "is the time-reverse of the PD leading to equilibrium
  from the time-reversed macrostate"; a second argument from Poincare recurrence and time-reversal gives the same thing.
  Sec. III.4, Eq. (23), is the quantum form. Sec. III.1 names Onsager's regression hypothesis as the classical ancestor
  (the words Onsager-Machlup do not appear). Sec. VI states the prescription: "choose a non-equilibrium state A0, take its
  CPT conjugate, and let it evolve back to equilibrium", with assumptions (a) a fixed set of states and a fixed evolution,
  which "requires additional assumptions when gravity is included"; (b) unitary evolution with CPT "or some other
  involution"; (c) a flat distribution over microstates in the specified macrostate. Sec. IV.3 notes the prescription
  "singles out a time foliation".
- **Which paths dominate.** Sec. III.2: of the histories that reach a low-entropy state, "the vast majority would simply
  appear to be the detailed time-reverse of standard evolution" toward equilibrium, and "A tiny fraction might contain
  sudden downward jumps in entropy"; Sec. II: "most such trajectories consist of many fluctuations that are individually
  unlikely". So the fluctuation does not pass through a hot intermediate and jump; it climbs the way the decay would fall,
  run backwards.
- **Over a barrier.** Three cases, all read as the reverse of a relaxation. The thermalon (Sec. V.1, Figs. 2, 3): a static
  field configuration whose formation is the reverse of its decay, in which "ingoing scalar waves emerge from the thermal
  bath and converge repeatedly on the same point". Coleman-de Luccia (Sec. V.2, Fig. 4): the authors adopt Brown and
  Weinberg's inhomogeneous pre-tunneling configuration. Hawking-Moss (Sec. V.3, Fig. 5): "The thermal fluctuation is the
  time-reverse of this relaxation process", with the caution that "there might be a most likely history, the variance
  around it will be large". The paper does not say the field climbs along a reversed tunneling trajectory; the climb is the
  reverse of classical relaxation, with the tunneling step added where the barrier is crossed.
- **Cosmology.** Sec. IV.2 gives the de Sitter entropy S = pi m_p^2 H^-2; Sec. V.5 treats empty de Sitter as an
  equilibrium (a black hole placed in it always lowers total horizon area), which is the opposite reading from [CC04]
  Sec. 3.2.3, and the paper says of the two readings of de Sitter, "It is not clear how to reconcile these interpretations,
  and we have little to add to the discussion" (Sec. IV.2). The baby-universe case treated is Dutta and Vachaspati's island
  universe (Sec. V.F); Farhi-Guth-Guven is named only in passing in Sec. V.G, as a bubble separated from the parent by a
  wormhole: the most probable history is a reversed collapse, a white hole that "expels a
  highly inhomogeneous ball that expands and smooths out", "quite gradual, rather than sudden"; a small island is
  "exponentially more likely" than one of horizon size, recent fluctuations are favored over ancient ones, and the
  scenario "probably would not be observationally viable" (Fig. 10 caption). Sec. V.8 applies the prescription to a
  Boltzmann brain: assembled "through a series of unlikely tunneling and baryon-production events", briefly aware, then
  disintegrating; whether the psychological arrow must follow the thermodynamic one is "a controversial one".
- **What it cannot show.** "Our prescription does not really address the denucleation process" (fn. 13); "we cannot apply
  our prescription to determine how the white hole itself formed from equilibrium" when evaporation is non-unitary
  (Sec. V.5); in de Sitter "it is unclear which observer to choose"; "how well does the macrostate A0 have to be specified
  to ensure a functioning brain" is open and the likeliest trajectories "will not be precisely the time-reverse" of decay;
  the prescription is "a first step" and has not been applied to terrestrial systems; and to explain our universe's
  low-entropy origin one of the four assumptions "should be violated": "that of a fixed set of states, or attainment of
  equilibrium, or democracy of microstates, or unitarity" (Sec. VI).

## 5. [Car08] and [Car05], read in part

- **[Car08].** The essay takes "the opposite tack" to time-denial: "time is real, it needs to be dealt with"; "What if
  time exists, and is eternal, and the state of the universe evolves with time" by something like the Schrodinger
  equation. Then "there is no boundary to time", and a beginning with "special low-entropy boundary conditions" is "closed
  to us", so the low entropy of the past "is not natural at all" and must be explained dynamically. The Boltzmann-Lucretius
  box-of-gas picture is rejected by Eddington's argument ("a single mathematical physicist" is likelier than the observed
  universe), a prediction that "manifestly, has been falsified" in his wording here, which is the form [Car17] later
  calls not quite correct. "Worlds Without End" gives the alternative: entropy "can always grow", the multiverse "can be
  completely symmetric with respect to time", the minimum-entropy moment "need not be an especially low entropy"; it cites
  [CC04] as ref. [13] and Garriga and Vilenkin's recycling universe. Recurrence: in a finite Hilbert space "recurrences are
  inevitable"; with an accumulation point in the spectrum recurrence times "get pushed off to infinity", the "Heraclitean"
  universe; the Hilbert space "needs to be infinite-dimensional", against the finite local description of de Sitter
  (fn. 3, Dyson, Kleban, Susskind). His own caveats: "We are a long way from understanding the details of such pictures,
  or indeed whether they make much physical sense at all"; "None of these assumptions is beyond questioning".
- **[Car05].** Naturalness: dimensionless parameters "of order unity, not too large nor too small", and for
  configurations, high entropy. Unnatural features: the vacuum energy near 10^-3 eV against the Planck scale, the early
  universe "in a state of incredibly low entropy", and the hierarchies. Entropy numbers: about 10^88 in matter and
  radiation in the observable universe, at least about 10^99 in black holes today, about 10^120 if all matter were one
  black hole. The Boltzmann-brain argument is not discussed in this essay. On the multiverse: "the multiverse is not a
  theory; it is a consequence of certain theories", "Every theory makes untestable predictions", and theories "should be
  judged on the basis of the testable ones".

## 6. Against the record (ours, unverified throughout)

### (a) The owner's loop measure against the cognitive-instability test

**Her measure as decided** (VISION Updates 23, 26, 43, 44; `docs/papers/loop_measure/paper.tex`, Sec. 1): universes of
one setting counted by their own age; each civilization counted once at its birth; the causal diamond's comoving volume
as the weight on ages; an observer a civilization in which at least one being asks this question; a turn ends when its last
black hole has dissolved (Update 44: when all matter is in black holes nothing moves and there is no time); and, from
Update 43, "Brains that assemble by chance in space are not real observers: they cannot evolve."

**Does it pass [Car17]?** Not as written, and for a reason the paper states in advance. The exclusion of chance-assembled
brains is a choice of xerographic distribution (Sec. 5.2): it concentrates the distribution on observers that evolved. That
is exactly the move Carroll calls "cheating" if the model's own dynamics would make Boltzmann brains dominate; the test is
not whether the measure counts them but whether the model produces them. Her reason for the exclusion (a chance brain
cannot evolve, and a reality "has stable rules of order") is a reason to doubt such brains would reason well, which is the
same fact Carroll turns against the theory: fluctuated reasoners have no reliable evidence, so if the model says most
reasoners are fluctuated, the model's own believers have none either. Her exclusion rule and his instability argument rest
on the same premise and reach opposite verdicts, because his is about the theory's believability and hers about the
count. So the measure cannot settle the question; the dynamics of the late phase of a turn must.

**What she would have to add.** One of Carroll's own escape routes, stated for her picture. His list (Sec. 6): the
universe ends before fluctuations have time to assemble a brain; or the late state has no dynamics (a stationary vacuum).
Her Update 44 conjecture is the second route in her words: a turn ends when all matter is in black holes and "nothing
moves through space, and so there is no time". If that is a claim that the late state of a turn is stationary (no
thermal fluctuations, no de Sitter vacuum sitting for a recurrence time), then her picture passes without the exclusion
rule, and the rule becomes a consequence rather than a stipulation. What that costs: (i) the claim has to be stated as a
property of the dynamics (what the late state is, why nothing fluctuates in it, and in particular whether a de Sitter
phase with today's vacuum energy persists after the last star, which standard cosmology says it does and which is
precisely universe B of [Car17] Sec. 3); (ii) it has to be squared with O101's requirement that a turn's yield be finite,
which the same end supplies; (iii) [CC04] Sec. 5.3's point applies to her tree as to his: births occur in the parent
"an infinite number of times" if the parent lasts for ever, and then the count of evolved observers per turn is set by
the births, not by the parent's own observers, which is her per-turn count. The toy cannot test any of this; it has no
observers and no late phase. What it can supply is already on the record: whether a sealed system with nothing to spare

### (b) [CC04]'s nucleation against her black-hole birth

**The same.** A parent that is stable for now and not the lowest state (his metastable de Sitter, "not an equilibrium
state about which we are fluctuating"; her curled X, a dip above flat space, O13). A child born low in entropy relative
to its parent and carrying its own arrow pointing away from the birth (his proto-inflationary patch at about 10^12
against the parent's de Sitter entropy; her tube or slab opening into a sheet, with the lump as the hot beginning). No
effect on the parent: his calculation is on a fixed background with back-reaction ignored and "inflation continues
forever" elsewhere; her Update 59 decision that "the black hole might remain present in the parent" and no whole-universe
reset is required. Births that repeat: his "an infinite number of times"; her twins and the next set "when there are
enough curls again" (Update 149). And one point of contact worth a sentence in any letter: in his picture the birth
*looks like* a small black hole from the parent's side (fn. 3, through Farhi and Guth), while in hers the black hole is the
*cause* of the birth (Updates 42, 59, 149): the same object at the junction, with the arrow of explanation reversed.
The 1 November lineage note (`2026-11-01_universe_inside_black_hole_lineage.md`) already places her beside Frolov,
Markov and Mukhanov and Poplawski, where the daughter is inside the horizon; [CC04] is the other family, where the
daughter is a fluctuation that forms a horizon.

**What differs.** (1) *Fluctuation against a wall paid by accumulated energy.* His birth is a thermal fluctuation of a
field up a smooth well, with no barrier and a probability of 10^(-10^(10^56)) per Hubble volume per Hubble time; hers is
an activation over a wall (12 units in two dimensions, 24 to 25.6 for the rod under her tie, T88) paid by energy

child, dense, and ordered (stacked one-sided loops, Update 149). His birth raises the entropy of the whole; whether hers
does is not computed anywhere on the record, and it is a benchmark (below). (3) *Time symmetry.* His multiverse is

### (c) [ACJ11]'s fluctuation-path result against the toy's escape paths

**The published statement, in the toy's terms.** A metastable state in a bath gets over its wall, most probably, along
the time-reverse of the path by which the same state would relax back from the top. The toy's chain satisfies detailed
balance by construction (Metropolis or Glauber; the module docstrings), and for a stationary reversible Markov chain the
statement is a theorem (*ours, general knowledge, to verify*: the reversed stationary chain has the same law, so the
distribution of paths from basin to barrier top equals the reversed distribution of paths from top to basin). So in the
toy this is not a test of the hypothesis; it is an exact check of the sampler against a published prescription, and a
measurement of how large the "variance around" the most likely path is, which [ACJ11] Sec. V.3 says can be large.

**What the record has.** T38 (TWO POPULATIONS; O84, reread in O109 as mostly the detector's watch) saved per-decay rows
(`waiting`, `f_200`, `sweep_25`, the local-dimension histograms at quarter, half and three-quarter conversion) and the
final wirings (`results/t38_*_adj/*.npz`, which hold `adj`, `part`, `lam`, `g`, `h0` only); it did not save the move
sequence. T22 (`graphity.exits.run_until_through`) counts every accepted move and so sees every exit and every fall-back
(FALL-BACKS, kappa about 0.6 exits per decay), but it stores counts, not the moves. T54 priced the barrier exactly as a
function of patch size (CRITICAL PATCH before the wrap under the tie at lambda = 1.10 to 1.20, k* of 5, 3 and 2 columns;
FIXED WALL at tied 1.25 and 1.40; O118). So the toy has the two things the published result needs, a relaxation path
(the fall-back) and an escape path, and has never recorded either as a path.

**Candidate registration (proposed, not written into PREREGISTRATION.md).** *Setting:* the case in which the wall is
several moves, not one, since a one-move wall makes the statement empty: the one-curled slab under her tie at
lambda = 1.15 or 1.20 (critical patch 3 or 2 columns, T54), in a bath at the couplings T56 used; a two-dimensional control
at lambda = 1.25, where the wall is the single move. *Recorded:* every accepted move in order (the two edges removed, the
two added, delta S, delta X, delta H, the sweep), from the last visit to the perfect curled state to the first moment the
committor passes one half (defined as in T54 by patch size: k above k*), and likewise for every fall-back from the first
exit back to the perfect state. *Observables:* (1) the distribution over escape paths of the sequence of (delta S,
delta X) steps, against the reversed distribution over fall-back paths; (2) the number of moves from first exit to
commitment against the number from exit to return; (3) the share of escapes whose step sequence is the exact reverse of
some observed fall-back; (4) the entropy excursion along the path (the energy, as the toy's stand-in). *Predictions to
write before the run:* ours, that (1) and (2) agree within sampling error because the chain is reversible, and that
(3) is small because the number of distinct paths of three or more moves is large (the variance [ACJ11] warns of); the
owner's to be asked. *What would count against:* a systematic difference between the escape distribution and the reversed
fall-back distribution, which would mean the chain or the recorder breaks detailed balance (a bug, not physics). *Clock:*
the fair clock of O90 for the comparison across sizes, with a held-out size under rule 14 for any claim that the
path-length ratio is fixed with N.

### (d) The Born-rule derivation against the record's quantum leg

The record's quantum leg went through three steps: counting versions (renamings) as the weights (Update 17); the exact
finding that a renaming of a fixed arrangement is a complete instruction set, so counting cannot violate Bell (O29,
T15 rung 3a, CLASSICAL); and the adoption of the published amplitude rule (Update 57 and the 30 October addendum: a
complex amplitude on each point of a small loop, evolving by the loop's Laplacian, "probability its square", a detector
projecting onto the classes its attachment leaves; quantum mechanics put in, not derived, Hardy's fifth axiom as a
postulate).

[SC18] bears on each step. (1) Counting versions is branch counting (Indifference) in their terms, and Sec. 2.4 is the
published argument that it cannot be the probability rule, independent of Bell: it makes an agent's credence change when
only the environment changes. O29 ruled the counting out for correlations; [SC18] rules it out for single-outcome
probabilities too. (2) Their derivation does not start from counting; it starts from a state with amplitudes and derives
the weights from ESP. So it is not a route from the toy's renamings to the Born rule, and nothing in it rescues Update 17
as stated. (3) For the adopted rule it offers one thing: "probability its square" need not be a separate postulate. If the
toy's loop with its amplitudes is a subsystem whose reduced state is what a detector sees, ESP-QM gives the squared
amplitudes as the only credence consistent with the environment not mattering. The cost is the subsystem split that
ESP-QM assumes (Appendix B): a loop whose state is its own. T15 rung 0b found that the loop's vibrations "dissolve into
the sheet's modes" (O30), which is a finding that the natural split does not hold for the four-point loop; so before the
derivation can be borrowed the toy owes a subsystem, which is the same thing the amplitude rule's interference test
needs. (4) Sec. 4.3's weighting of observers across a cosmological multiverse by |alpha_i|^2 is a published measure that
combines branch weight with an observer count, with a convergence condition (amplitude decay faster than omega/2 for a
count growing as e^(omega t)). Her measure is classical (a tree of turns, no amplitudes) and is not touched by it unless
the hypothesis takes a quantum form, in which case this is the published form it would have to meet.

### Benchmarks the owner's picture must meet (rule 13)

1. **No Boltzmann-brain domination from the dynamics, not from the count** ([Car17] Secs. 5.2, 6). The late state of a
   turn must be shown stationary, or the turn must end before fluctuations assemble a brain; the exclusion rule of
   Update 43 does not count.
2. **The entropy balance of a birth** ([CC04] Secs. 3.2.1, 5.2, 5.3; [Car05]'s 10^88, 10^99, 10^120). Her birth should be
   shown to raise the entropy of the whole (parent plus child) or be stated as an exception with a reason; nothing on the
   record computes it, in the toy or in words.
3. **The rate** ([CC04] Eq. 45). His is 10^(-10^(10^56)) per proto-inflationary Hubble volume with the inputs stated. Hers
   has no rate; what it has is a supply (the curl energy) and a wall (T88). A rate per black hole per unit of accumulated
   energy, however rough, is the number a comparison needs.
4. **The arrow of time without time symmetry** ([CC04] Sec. 5.3, after Fig. 9). If her turns are one-way, what breaks the
   symmetry has to be named; "the one-way infall" is her answer and Poplawski's, and it is a claim about dynamics that
   the toy, having no time, cannot check.
5. **The most likely escape path is the reversed relaxation** ([ACJ11] Secs. III.2, VI). Exact for the toy's reversible
   chain; registration (c) above would show it on saved moves, and its variance is the measurable part.
6. **Branch counting is not a probability rule** ([SC18] Sec. 2.4), and **a subsystem split is owed before the Born rule
   can be borrowed** (Appendix B; O30).
7. **De Sitter as equilibrium or not** ([CC04] Sec. 3.2.3 against [ACJ11] Sec. V.5). Carroll's own papers read it both
   ways and say so; her picture should say which it assumes for the parent's late phase, since benchmark 1 turns on it.
