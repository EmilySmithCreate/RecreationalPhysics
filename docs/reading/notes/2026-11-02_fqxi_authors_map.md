## Five first contacts, ranked, and why

The ranking weighs closeness to a piece, a 2024 to 2026 paper a narrow question can be asked about, and reachability. A caution that applies to every line: being featured by FQxI shows that a person is active and visible, not that they answer letters from hobbyists. Several of these people are very senior and very busy, and FQxI membership is by invitation, so the feature is a sign of standing, not of openness. The owner's rules (one narrow question from the recipient's own work, disclosure, no pitch) are the only lever.

1. Sean M. Carroll (Johns Hopkins). His May 2026 paper 2605.30405 is a cyclic universe in which the Big Bang is one excursion of an exactly periodic state and ordinary observers outnumber fluctuations; that is the owner's piece 5 in a published form, and he is in Baltimore. The question about the period and the sharpness of the excursion is answerable from that one paper. He has a very large public following, which lowers the odds of a reply.
2. Hiranya V. Peiris (UCL per the member list). 2607.06680 and 2504.02829 are real-time lattice simulations of vacuum decay with bubble profiles and rates read off and compared with the instanton prediction, which is the method nearest to the toy's sealed decay runs (piece 2); the lattice-size question is one she will have faced. She is in the United Kingdom, so the only route is a letter.
3. Niayesh Afshordi (Waterloo and Perimeter). Active on FQxI through 2026 (the causal sets seminar, the dark energy podcast, the Sorkin tribute), with a 2026 one-parameter alternative to dynamical dark energy and a 2026 bounce paper; touches pieces 1, 2 and 4. He also ran a survey of physicists' views (2605.11058), which suggests an interest in how people reason about these questions. Canada.
4. Daniele Oriti (LMU Munich). 2609.04328 derives both dark matter and dark energy from the collective behavior of a quantum-gravity condensate with a bounce, which is piece 4 and piece 2 together; the question about whether the dark matter thins like a fixed number of leftovers is exactly the leftover question of VISION Updates 46 to 48. The FQxI link is only membership and a 2019 grant, and he is in Germany.
5. Laura Mersini-Houghton (UNC Chapel Hill). Three hours from Charlottesville, an FQxI member, and her 2025 time-crystal papers make dark energy a property of space with a stated dynamics (piece 4); the birth-versus-today question matches the owner's decision of 5 October 2026 (VISION Update 41). She was not featured by FQxI in the window, so the FQxI signal is weak for her.

Alternates: Yasaman Yazdi (DIAS; early career, causal-set fluctuations, piece 1) and Sumati Surya (Raman; sequential growth and the energy question in VISION claim 3) are the two causal-set people whose 2024 to 2026 papers carry a question the owner can ask cleanly; Jenann Ismael (Johns Hopkins; Wheeler's participatory universe, piece 8) is the local choice for the loop theme, with no arXiv record to anchor a question, so her talk itself would be the hook.
