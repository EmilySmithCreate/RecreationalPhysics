# The loop, counting per turn, and comparing infinities: reading notes (5 October 2026)

The owner asked whether a theory of maximizing, as in economics, can be applied to the sizes of infinities, and what
research exists on loops and on comparing infinite outcomes (VISION Updates 23, 26, 42). **Read by an assistant agent
on 5 October 2026; the owner has not read these passages.** Two sources were read from text the agent extracted itself
(Bostrom, Askell). The others came through a page reader that can paraphrase, so their quotes should be checked before
they are cited. Read status is given per source. Keys refer to `REFERENCES.bib`.

## In one paragraph

Her "count per turn" matches a standard criterion: the long-run average, called the limit of means in economics, with
the turn as the period. Its probability form is the renewal-reward theorem. It is defensible on three conditions,
listed below, one of which her picture does not yet meet. Economics has proved that no criterion ranks every pair of
infinite streams fairly, so any such measure works only on a restricted class. Physicists' measures for an infinite
multiverse are a different kind of thing, and depend on how the infinity is cut off; two published papers find that
leading measures favor universes that cycle. Not found in what was read: a measure defined per turn of a loop from
black hole to new spacetime, or any formal use of Hofstadter's strange loop in cosmology.

## Economics and "infinite ethics": how to compare streams whose totals are infinite

- **The criteria** (an encyclopedia page, *read through the page reader*): compare running totals (the overtaking and
  catching-up criteria); discount the future; or compare **long-run averages per period (the limit of means)**. "Some
  sequences may be incomparable by the overtaking criterion."
- **[Askell18] "Pareto principles in infinite ethics"**, PhD thesis, New York University, 2018. *Abstract, contents and
  passages of chapter 1 read from extracted text.* Four mild axioms imply "ubiquitous incomparability between infinite
  worlds". It reports the classic results: "there is no social welfare function that can satisfy Pareto and Finite
  Anonymity" (Diamond; Basu and Mitra), and a complete fair ranking cannot be constructed explicitly (Lauwers; Zame).
  The originals were not opened.
- **[Bostrom11] "Infinite ethics"**, Analysis and Metaphysics 10 (2011). *Sec. 2.3 in full and parts of 2.2, 3.1, from
  extracted text.* Value-density, the average value per region, needs the assumption "that the cosmos is, at a
  sufficiently large scale, approximately homogeneous"; it "at least provides a cardinal measure. Yet it is woefully
  incomplete".
- **Jonsson and Voorneveld** (two working papers, 2014; *abstracts only*): letting the discount vanish compares more
  pairs than overtaking does; "one axiom suffices to distinguish streams with different long-run averages."

## Renewal theory: why "per cycle" is a natural unit

- **The renewal-reward theorem** (an encyclopedia page, *read through the page reader*; it cites Cox 1970 and Feller
  1971, not opened): for a process that repeats in cycles that are alike, the long-run reward per unit time equals the
  expected reward in one cycle divided by the expected length of a cycle.

## The measure problem in cosmology

- **[Freivogel11] "Making predictions in the multiverse"**, Class. Quantum Grav. 28, 204007; arXiv:1105.0244.
  *Abstract and Secs. 1, 3, 5, 6.* When a multiverse is infinite, "both the numerator and the denominator… are
  infinite", and the answer "is highly regulator-dependent". Known traps: a cutoff in time makes us "incredibly
  unlikely to live at such a late time" (the youngness problem); chance observers can outnumber real ones (Boltzmann
  brains); and with any geometric cutoff "we could run into the cutoff, and time would end."
- **[BHKP07] Bousso, Harnik, Kribs, Perez, "Predicting the cosmological constant from the causal entropic principle"**,
  Phys. Rev. D 76, 043513 (2007); arXiv:hep-th/0702115. *Abstract only.* **An explicit maximizing principle:** the
  likeliest values of the constants are those "for which the total entropy production within a causally connected
  region is maximized". Agreement with the observed cosmological constant is claimed.
- **[Lehners12] "Eternal inflation with non-inflationary pocket universes"**, Phys. Rev. D 86, 043518 (2012);
  arXiv:1206.1081. *Abstract only.* "Pocket universes which undergo cycles are further preferred, because they produce
  habitable conditions repeatedly inside each pocket."
- **Johnson and Lehners, "Cycles in the multiverse"**, arXiv:1112.3360. *Abstract and body.* With cycling regions "the
  youngness and Boltzmann Brain problems, are substantially alleviated."

## Universes that reproduce

  raised to 2.0, beside measured stars of 2.08 ± 0.07 and 2.35 ± 0.17. No formal assessment after 2013 was found.

## Strange loops made formal

- **Yanofsky**, Bull. Symbolic Logic 9 (2003); arXiv:math/0305282. *Abstract only.* Following Lawvere, "many
  self-referential paradoxes, incompleteness theorems and fixed point theorems fall out of the same simple scheme". A
  real formalism, in logic and computability. No use of it in cosmology was found.

## What this says about "count per turn" (*ours, from the agent's reading; all of it inference*)

1. **It has a name and a pedigree.** Counting per turn is the long-run average criterion with the turn as the period.
   It is well defined, and ranks every pair, on the class of processes for which the average exists.
2. **Three conditions.** (i) Turns must be alike, so that an average per turn exists; her rule that the rules do not
   change from one turn to the next (Update 23) supplies this. (ii) **What one turn yields must be finite.** Update 26
   says our stable phase "really never ends"; if so the count inside one turn is itself infinite and the problem
   returns. It needs a stated end: the last star, or the entropy produced, which is what [BHKP07] counts. (iii) A
   common unit across different loops. The renewal theorem gives yield per unit time, which is the yield per turn
   divided by how long a turn lasts; counting per turn drops the length. Update 23 already flagged that choice.
3. **A trap.** If one turn makes many black holes, each starting its own turn, the process branches, and then the
   rate of multiplying, not the yield of a turn, decides what dominates. That is Smolin's fitness, and it has the same
   structure as the youngness problem.
4. **Economics' impossibility results do not bite here**: they concern ranking all infinite streams impartially, and
   loops with alike turns are a restricted class.
5. **Not found in what was read:** a measure per turn of a loop from black hole to new spacetime; the renewal-reward
   theorem used as a cosmological measure; a formal use of the strange loop in cosmology.
