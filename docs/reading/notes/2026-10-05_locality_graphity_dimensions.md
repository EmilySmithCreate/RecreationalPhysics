# Disordered locality, quantum graphity, and dimensions that open with time: reading notes (5 October 2026)

The owner asked what the people nearest to two of her ideas have published most recently, and how it meets her
hypothesis. **Read by an assistant agent on 5 October 2026 from ar5iv and arXiv HTML texts, searched locally; the
owner has not read these passages.** Quotes are from the HTML conversion and should be checked against the PDF before
they are used in a paper. Read status is given per paper. Keys refer to `REFERENCES.bib`; papers without a key are
cited by arXiv number.

## In one paragraph

Not found in what was read: a sharp change between two ordered arrangements that releases energy, or a count of long
links left when a space opens from several seeds. The nearest published items are a 2012 paper on quantum graphity in
which domains that nucleate independently leave a defect frozen where they meet, and Stojkovic's "vanishing
dimensions", in which dimensions open one after another as the universe cools.

## Part 1. Disordered locality and quantum graphity

- **[MS07] Markopoulou and Smolin, "Disordered locality in loop quantum gravity states"**, Class. Quantum Grav. 24
  (2007) 3813; arXiv:gr-qc/0702044. *Secs. 3.1 to 3.3 read.* Local moves can leave non-local links; in early
  simulations "no evidence that non-local links were eliminated by the dynamics". Up to 10⁸⁰ such links would go
  unnoticed locally. No distribution of lengths is given.
- **[PWS09]** (already in the bibliography; *full text read again*). It names a transition as the origin in one sentence
  and gives no mechanism: "We do not address how this suppression is accomplished in this paper". For dark energy the
  number of links has to grow with the comoving volume, and the two ends of a link are uncorrelated anywhere in it.
- **Caravelli and Markopoulou**, arXiv:1201.3206, Phys. Rev. D 86, 024019 (2012). *Parts read.* Non-local links of a
  given density give a propagating field a mass. One suggested origin: "a non-zero temperature bath in Quantum
  Graphity".
- **[CM11]** (already in the bibliography; *parts read*). Mean field puts the graphity transition at zero temperature:
  "the model has no phase transition and at T=0 the system goes to the ground state as expected".
- **[QSMG12] Quach, Su, Martin, Greentree, "Domain structures in quantum graphity"**, Phys. Rev. D 86, 044001 (2012);
  arXiv:1203.5367. *Secs. I, III, V read.* **The closest relative of what this project sees.** "fluctuations may seed
  the nucleation of space-like separated local energy minimum regions"; "it is not inevitable that the domains will
  coalesce to a global ground state"; "Two domains form independently. Where they meet is an unstable antiphase
  boundary defect … After the quenching process the boundary defect is frozen into a metastable amorphous state." A
  quench at zero temperature on a torus 100 by 9. Proposed consequences: scattering and lensing-like effects. It counts
  nothing against volume.
- **[WG15]** (already in the bibliography; *full text read*). With the original graphity energy "disjoint graphs are
  inevitably lower in energy than connected graphs"; at 36 points "a small K4 forms from the 'leftover' vertices".
  The same pathology this project measured at λ = 0 (shattering into knots, with a leftover piece), at 24 and 36
  points.
- **[H13] Hossenfelder, "Phenomenology of space-time imperfection I: nonlocal defects"**, arXiv:1309.0311. *Secs. 1,
  5.4, 6 read.* "This expectation that space-time is left with defects is generic and independent of the details of
  the underlying theory"; "nonlocal defects cannot be denser than one per femtometer"; existing bounds are about ten
  orders of magnitude too weak to reach a density comparable to the cosmological constant.
- **Newest work citing these** (*read as stated*): Javed and Wilson-Ewing, arXiv:2512.02156 (*full text*), bounding
  wormhole-like non-local links with the spectrum of hydrogen; Smolin's recent "views" papers, which cite disordered
  locality but do not use it as a mechanism (*abstract and one list*). No graphity simulation paper after 2018 was
  found in the lists opened.

## Part 2. The number of large dimensions changing with time

- **[St14] Stojkovic, "Vanishing dimensions: review"**, arXiv:1406.2696. *Secs. 1, 5 to 7, 11 to 16 read.* "the
  dimensions open up as the universe expands and temperature drops": one to two below about 100 TeV, two to three near
  1 TeV, and a fourth space dimension at about the size of the horizon today, in which case "an effective cosmological
  constant of the correct magnitude is induced". The next opening "would happen if and when the total energy density
  drops below some critical value".
- **Mureika and Stojkovic**, Phys. Rev. Lett. 106, 101101 (2011); arXiv:1102.3434. *Full text read.* An era with too
  few dimensions for gravitational waves gives a highest primordial frequency, near 10⁻⁴ Hz for a change at 1 TeV. On
  the change itself: "the dimensional crossover may perhaps be a violent highly non-adiabatic process with huge
  entropy production". A published comment disputes the claim (arXiv:1104.1223, *abstract only*).
- **Anchordoqui, Dai, Fairbairn, Landsberg, Stojkovic**, arXiv:1003.5914. *Read.* The model is an ordered lattice,
  "analogous to that of dimensional crossover in layered strongly correlated metals".
- **Tests** (*abstracts only*): planar events in cosmic rays, where a 2026 paper says its two-dimensional model cannot
  reproduce the data; no gravitational-wave test of the cut-off was found in the citing records looked at.
- **Recent nucleation of dimension changes** (*introductions and conclusions read*): de Sitter vacua decaying toward a
  higher-dimensional, decompactified theory (Draper, Garcia Garcia, Lillard, arXiv:2105.10507); universes created by
  "bubbles of something" whose wall is where the internal space shrinks to nothing (arXiv:2310.06021). Neither
  discusses energy released to matter or anything left behind in the passages read.

## How it meets this project (*ours, from the agent's reading*)

- **The far-reaching links (O95).** [QSMG12] has the same cause the project sees: several seeds, a quench, a defect
  frozen where they meet. Its object is a boundary line, not a link whose ends are otherwise far apart, and it gives
  no count, length distribution or scaling with volume. Those three measurements were not found in what was read.
- **Not dark energy.** [PWS09] needs the number of links to grow with the comoving volume and their ends to be
  uncorrelated across it. Links frozen in once at an opening are fixed in number and short, so by that paper's own
  derivation they do not give dark energy. This confirms O95's reading.
- **A density to respect.** [H13] bounds non-local defects at one per femtometer. A few links per thousand points,
  scaled naively, is far denser; the number that would matter is links per seed against volume.
- **Dimensions opening in order.** [St14] agrees with the owner's picture that dimensions open one after another as
  things cool, and ties an opening to the cosmological constant. Two differences: there it is the latest opening, not
  the first, and the mechanism is geometric, not a release of energy. Its change is called a crossover, and whether it
  is sharp has not been calculated.
- **Already done more deeply here.** [WG15]'s result at 24 and 36 points is this project's λ = 0 shattering and ribbon,
  measured at larger sizes with exact checks.
- **Not found in what was read:** a change between two ordered arrangements with an activation energy that does not
  grow with size and an exact release per point.

## One narrow question each

- Edward Wilson-Ewing: does the hydrogen bound constrain a fixed population of non-local links of the
  Markopoulou–Smolin kind that are not tied to entanglement, and what number density does it correspond to?
- Dejan Stojkovic: in the model of the 2014 review, is the opening from two to three dimensions a smooth crossover or
  a change with latent heat? The 2011 letter says it "may perhaps be a violent highly non-adiabatic process"; has that
  been calculated?
