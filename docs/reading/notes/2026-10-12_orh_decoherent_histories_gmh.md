## 0. Plain-language glossary (ours; the papers' meanings, in everyday words)

- **History:** a sequence of yes/no questions about the universe at a sequence of times, with one answer picked for each. Written as a chain of projection operators.
- **Coarse graining:** asking fewer questions, asking them at fewer times, or accepting ranges rather than exact values. Only coarse-grained histories can be given probabilities.
- **Decoherence functional D:** a complex number for every pair of histories; its off-diagonal entries measure the quantum interference between two histories. When they are negligible, the diagonal entries are probabilities.
- **Record (generalized):** a projection at a later time that is perfectly correlated with a history so far. It need not be readable by anyone.
- **Branch dependence:** which questions can sensibly be asked later depends on what happened earlier (there are no sensible questions about the Earth's weather on a branch where the Earth never formed).
- **Quasiclassical domain / realm:** a decoherent family of histories in which things mostly follow classical equations, as fine-grained as decoherence allows. "Realm" replaced "domain" in 1995.
- **IGUS:** information gathering and utilizing system; the papers' word for an observer, defined as a complex adaptive system that uses the probability rules, however crudely.

### 1.2 Key definitions and equations (numbered as in the paper)
- Heisenberg evolution of a projection, Eq. (3): $P(t) = e^{iHt/\hbar} P(0) e^{-iHt/\hbar}$.
- Exhaustive, exclusive alternatives at one time, Eq. (4): $\sum_\alpha P^k_\alpha(t) = 1$, $P^k_\alpha(t) P^k_\beta(t) = \delta_{\alpha\beta} P^k_\alpha(t)$. A history is a sequence $[P_\alpha] = (P^1_{\alpha_1}(t_1), \ldots, P^n_{\alpha_n}(t_n))$. "The completely coarse-grained history is one with no projections whatever, just the unit operator!" (Sec. IVb).
- Decoherence functional, sum-over-histories form Eqs. (6)-(7), and Heisenberg form Eq. (8):
  $$D([P_{\alpha'}],[P_\alpha]) = \mathrm{Tr}\big[P^n_{\alpha'_n}(t_n)\cdots P^1_{\alpha'_1}(t_1)\,\rho\,P^1_{\alpha_1}(t_1)\cdots P^n_{\alpha_n}(t_n)\big].$$
  Projections are time ordered with the earliest innermost. By the cyclic property of the trace, "D is always diagonal in the final indices"; "Decoherence is automatic for 'histories' that consist of alternatives at but one time" (Sec. IVc).
- Coarse graining sums D over what is not fixed, Eq. (9).
- Decoherence condition, Eq. (10): $D([P_{\alpha'}],[P_\alpha]) \approx 0$ for any $\alpha'_k \neq \alpha_k$. Called simply "decoherence" here; the later papers call it *medium* decoherence.
- Probabilities, Eq. (12): $p([P_\alpha]) = D([P_\alpha],[P_\alpha])$. Sum rule, Eq. (13): $p([P_\beta]) \approx \sum_{\text{all } P_\alpha \text{ not fixed by } [P_\beta]} p([P_\alpha])$; the one-time version is Eq. (14). "The fundamental formula", Eq. (15): $D([P_{\alpha'}],[P_\alpha]) \approx \delta_{\alpha'_1\alpha_1}\cdots\delta_{\alpha'_n\alpha_n}\, p([P_\alpha])$.
- The weaker condition Eqs. (16)-(17), $\mathrm{Re}\,D([P_\alpha],[P_{\alpha'}]) \approx 0$, "is the condition used by Griffiths as the requirement for 'consistent histories'"; the authors prefer (10) because "it is hard to think of a general mechanism that suppresses only their real parts" (Sec. IVc). *(The attribution to Griffiths is corrected in the 1993 paper, footnote 4: his condition is weaker still.)*
- Conditional probabilities, Eq. (18); prediction Eq. (19); retrodiction Eq. (20):
  $$p(\alpha_{n-1}t_{n-1},\cdots,\alpha_1 t_1 \mid \alpha_n t_n) = \frac{p(\alpha_n t_n,\cdots,\alpha_1 t_1)}{p(\alpha_n t_n)}.$$
  Effective density matrix for prediction, Eq. (21): $\rho_{\rm eff} = P^k_{\alpha_k}(t_k)\cdots P^1_{\alpha_1}(t_1)\,\rho\,P^1_{\alpha_1}(t_1)\cdots P^k_{\alpha_k}(t_k) / \mathrm{Tr}[\cdot]$, with Eq. (22) giving future probabilities from it. "By contrast, there is no effective density matrix representing present information from which probabilities for the past can be derived. As (20) shows, history requires knowledge of both present data and the initial condition of the universe" (Sec. IVd). The "reduction of the wave packet" is "no different from the classical reassessment of probabilities that occurs after new information is obtained" (the horse-race example).
- Branches for a pure state, Eqs. (23)-(25): $|\Psi\rangle = \sum_{\alpha_1\cdots\alpha_n} P^n_{\alpha_n}(t_n)\cdots P^1_{\alpha_1}(t_1)|\Psi\rangle \equiv \sum |[P_\alpha],\Psi\rangle$, with $\langle [P_{\alpha'}],\Psi \mid [P_\alpha],\Psi\rangle \approx 0$ for $\alpha'_k \neq \alpha_k$. "Decohering sets of alternative histories give a definite meaning to Everett's 'branches'" (Sec. IVe).
- Sets with the same probabilities, Eqs. (26)-(27): constant unitary transformations fixing $\rho$ give sets with identical D but "very different descriptions in terms of fundamental fields"; "the solution of the mathematical problem of enumerating the sets of decohering histories of a given Hilbert space has no physical content by itself" (Sec. IVf).
- Quasiclassical domain (abstract and Sec. VI): "a set of alternative decohering histories, maximally refined consistent with decoherence, with its individual histories exhibiting as much as possible patterns of classical correlation in time". "No such coarse graining is determined by pure quantum theory alone." "There are no classical domains, only quasiclassical ones."
- Entropy of a set of histories, Eqs. (31)-(32) (Sec. VII): the Jaynes density matrix $\tilde\rho$ maximizing $S(\tilde\rho) = -\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde\rho \log\tilde\rho)$ subject to reproducing every $\mathrm{Tr}[P^n_{\alpha_n}(t_n)\cdots P^1_{\alpha_1}(t_1)\tilde\rho P^1_{\alpha_1}(t_1)\cdots P^n_{\alpha_n}(t_n)]$. "In a certain sense, $S(\tilde\rho)$ for histories can be regarded as decreasing with time" as projections at later times are adjoined; a note adds that such a quantity "can be converted into an increasing quantity by adding an algorithmic complexity term".
- Branch dependence (Sec. X): "There are no variables that are expected to decohere universally." A record is defined: "A record is a present alternative that is, with high probability, correlated with an alternative in the past." "The subject of history is most honestly described as the construction of probabilities for the past, given such records." The decohering set "must refer both to the records of the condensation and to hydrodynamic variables".
- Measurement situation (Sec. XI): "When a correlation exists between the ranges of values of two operators of a quasiclassical domain, there is a measurement situation." Irreversibility is "the cost ... of tracking down the phases specifying coherence and restoring them", or "the negative of the logarithm of the probability of doing so"; in many cases "the probability of recovering them is truly zero".
- IGUS (Sec. XII): complex adaptive systems "considered within quantum mechanics as portions of the universe, making observations"; "the foremost characteristic of an IGUS is that, in some form of approximation, however crude or classical, it employs the fundamental formula, with what amounts to a rudimentary theory of $\rho$, $H$, and quantum mechanics. Probabilities of interest to the IGUS include those for correlations between its memory and the external world. (Typically these are assumed perfect; not always such a good approximation!)" "An IGUS uses decohering sets of alternative histories and therefore performs further coarse graining on a quasiclassical domain." "IGUSes, including human beings, occupy no special place and play no preferred role in the laws of physics."
- On Everett (Secs. III and XIII): the program is "an attempt at extension, clarification, and completion of the Everett interpretation"; "the Everett analysis was not complete. It did not adequately explain the origin of the classical domain or the meaning of the 'branching'"; Everett "suggests that a probability formula is somehow not needed ... even though a 'measure' is introduced that, in the end, amounts to the same thing." They prefer "many histories" to "many worlds" and read "equally real" as "meaning only that quantum mechanics prefers none over another except via probabilities"; "one cannot assign 'reality' simultaneously to different alternatives because they are contradictory."

### 1.3 Stated assumptions and limitations
- Fixed background spacetime: "We neglect gross quantum variations in the structure of spacetime. This approximation, excellent for times later than $10^{-43}$ sec after the beginning, permits us to use any of the familiar formulations of quantum mechanics with a preferred time" (Sec. IVa). In the sum-over-histories form, spin-zero fields only.
- Probabilities are approximate: they "need obey the rules of the probability calculus only up to some standard of accuracy sufficient for all practical purposes" (Sec. II).
- No measure of classicity: "We have not solved this problem to our satisfaction" (Sec. VI); "We are searching for such a measure" (Sec. VIII).
- No preferred family is claimed: "It is an important question whether all the quasiclassical domains are roughly equivalent or whether there are various essentially inequivalent ones" (abstract); "It would be a striking and deeply important fact of the universe if ... there were one roughly equivalent group with much higher classicities than all the others" (Sec. XIII). If there are many, "It would be better ... to say that the IGUS evolves to exploit a particular quasiclassical domain" (Sec. XII).
- "Our research is not complete" (Sec. III).

### 1.4 Criticisms cited or responded to
- Positions the work against Copenhagen (Bohr, Landau-Lifshitz, Heisenberg) as "inadequate for cosmology" (Sec. III), and as a completion of Everett (above). Acknowledges Griffiths and Omnès as independent and essentially equivalent on the probability rule. Griffiths is thanked for "a critical reading of the manuscript". No critique of decoherent histories itself is cited; this is the 1989 text.

### 2.2 Key definitions and equations
- Terminology (Sec. I, footnote 1): a decoherent set of coarse-grained histories is now a **realm**, not a "domain" and not a "world" ("that word connotes a single history and not a set of alternative ones").
- Quasiclassical realm (Sec. I): "an exhaustive set of mutually exclusive coarse-grained alternative histories that obey a realistic principle of decoherence, that consist largely of similar but branch-dependent alternatives at a succession of times, with individual histories exhibiting patterns of correlations implied by effective classical equations of motion subject to frequent small fluctuations and occasional major ones, the whole set being maximally refined given these properties. The theory may exhibit essentially inequivalent quasiclassical realms, but there is certainly at least one that includes familiar experience."
- Branch-dependent projections, Eq. (2.1): $P^k_{\alpha_k\alpha_{k-1}\cdots\alpha_1} P^k_{\alpha'_k\alpha_{k-1}\cdots\alpha_1} = \delta_{\alpha_k\alpha'_k} P^k_{\alpha_k\alpha_{k-1}\cdots\alpha_1}$, $\sum_{\alpha_k} P^k_{\alpha_k\alpha_{k-1}\cdots\alpha_1} = I$. "Useful sets of alternative histories of the universe (such as those constituting a quasiclassical realm) will be branch-dependent because the efficacy of physical mechanisms of decoherence depends on particular present circumstances and past history."
- Chains (homogeneous histories), Eq. (2.3): $H_{\alpha_k\cdots\alpha_1} = P^k_{\alpha_k\cdots\alpha_1}(t_k; t_{k-1},\ldots,t_1)\cdots P^1_{\alpha_1}(t_1)$. Sums of chains, Eq. (2.4): $C_\beta = \sum_{(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k)\in\beta} H_{\alpha_k\cdots\alpha_1}$; Eq. (2.5): $\sum_\beta C_\beta = I$.
- The ladder of conditions (Sec. II), for $\rho$ and for pure $\rho = |\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|$:
  - Linearly positive (Goldstein-Page), Eq. (2.6): $p(\alpha) = \mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Tr}(C_\alpha\rho) \geq 0$.
  - Consistent histories (Griffiths, Omnès), Eq. (2.7): $\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Tr}(C_{\alpha'}\rho C^\dagger_\alpha) = \delta_{\alpha'\alpha}\,p(\alpha)$ "provided $C_\alpha + C_{\alpha'}$ is a chain as well".
  - Weak decoherence, Eq. (2.8): the same with no restriction on the sums.
  - Medium decoherence, Eq. (2.9): $\mathrm{Tr}(C_{\alpha'}\rho C^\dagger_\alpha) = \delta_{\alpha'\alpha}\,p(\alpha)$; pure case $\langle\Psi|C^\dagger_\alpha C_{\alpha'}|\Psi\rangle = \delta_{\alpha'\alpha}\,p(\alpha)$.
  - Strong decoherence, Eqs. (2.10)-(2.11): $\langle\Psi|C^\dagger_\alpha M^\dagger M' C_{\alpha'}|\Psi\rangle = 0$ for $\alpha \neq \alpha'$, for every $M \in \{M\}_\alpha$ and $M' \in \{M\}_{\alpha'}$, both sets containing $I$. Probabilities, Eq. (2.12): $p(\alpha) = \langle\Psi|C^\dagger_\alpha C_\alpha|\Psi\rangle$.
- Generalized records (Sec. III), Eqs. (3.1)-(3.2): medium decoherence with a pure state implies orthogonal projections $R_\alpha$ with $C_\alpha|\Psi\rangle = R_\alpha|\Psi\rangle$ and $R_\alpha R_{\alpha'} = \delta_{\alpha\alpha'}R_\alpha$; and conversely. "We call them 'generalized' records because it does not follow from this definition alone that these operators have any of the further properties that might normally be required of records --- being quasiclassical operators, persisting over a period of time, being accessible to an observer, etc." Unless the branches form a basis there are "many different possible choices of the $\{R_\alpha\}$".
- The pool $\{M\}_\alpha$ of operators effectively commuting with the records, Eq. (3.3): $[M, R_\alpha]\,C_\alpha|\Psi\rangle = 0$; equivalently Eq. (3.4): $R_\alpha M R_\alpha|\Psi\rangle = M R_\alpha|\Psi\rangle$. Eq. (3.6): $R_\alpha = \mathrm{Proj}(\{M\}_\alpha C_\alpha|\Psi\rangle)$.
- Partial histories, Eq. (3.8): $H^\ell_{\alpha_\ell\cdots\alpha_1} = P^\ell_{\alpha_\ell\cdots\alpha_1}\cdots P^1_{\alpha_1}$.
- **Condition I**, Eq. (3.9): for each $\ell < k \leq n$, $P^k_{\alpha_k\cdots\alpha_1}\cdots P^{\ell+1}_{\alpha_{\ell+1}\alpha_\ell\cdots\alpha_1} \in U_{k\ell}\{M\}^\ell_{\alpha_\ell\cdots\alpha_1}$, with $U_{k\ell}$ a unitary adjusting the pool to the later time, Eq. (3.10): $U_{k\ell}U_{\ell j} = U_{kj}$. "The pool of operators for extending each history depends on that history."
- Nesting of records, Eq. (3.15): $R^k_{\alpha_k\cdots\alpha_1} \subseteq R^k_{\alpha_\ell\cdots\alpha_1}$, $\ell < k \leq n$. "Thus the records nest. ... the subspaces corresponding to the records narrow with each extension into the future." Memory-slot picture, Eq. (3.19): $R^k_{\alpha_k\cdots\alpha_1} = \prod_{j=1}^k A^{(j)}_{\alpha_j}$ with commuting projection sets ("c-numbers" registering "q-numbers").
- **Condition II**, Eq. (3.22): $R^\ell_{\alpha_\ell\cdots\alpha_1}|\Psi\rangle \approx R^k_{\alpha_\ell\cdots\alpha_1}|\Psi\rangle$, $k > \ell$. "This relation expresses the permanence of the record of the history $(\alpha_1,\cdots,\alpha_\ell)$ ... We take it to be the second defining property of strong decoherence."
- What strong decoherence adds (abstract and Sec. III): conditions I and II "guarantee what we call 'permanence of the past' --- in particular the continued decoherence of past alternatives as the chains of projections are extended into the future." Strong implies medium, "but not every set of consistent or even medium decoherent histories is strongly decoherent."
- Reduced density matrix for a branch (Sec. III; equation numbers (3.23)-(3.28) reconstructed, to verify): for the history $(\alpha_1,\alpha_2)$, a common subalgebra $\{F\}^3_{\alpha_2\alpha_1}$ of the pools gives, by the Jaynes construction, $\tilde\rho^{\alpha_2\alpha_1} = \sum_i \langle\Psi_{\alpha_2\alpha_1}|A^{\alpha_2\alpha_1}_i|\Psi_{\alpha_2\alpha_1}\rangle A^{\alpha_2\alpha_1}_i$, where $|\Psi^2_{\alpha_2\alpha_1}\rangle \propto H^2_{\alpha_2\alpha_1}|\Psi\rangle = R^2_{\alpha_2\alpha_1}|\Psi\rangle$ is the normalized **branch state vector**; strong decoherence makes it diagonal in $\alpha_3$. In the Sec. IV model this is "nothing more than the usual reduced density matrix for the 'system'".
- Sec. IV model: Hamiltonian Eq. (4.1) $H = H_x + H_0 + H_I$; wave function Eq. (4.3) with record slots $\chi^{k\alpha_j\cdots\alpha_1}(Q^j)$, orthogonal by Eq. (4.4); independent evolution of a slot after registration, Eq. (4.5); record projections Eq. (4.8); nesting Eq. (4.14); condition II as Eq. (4.16); condition I as Eq. (4.17) (numbers to verify).
- Classicality (Sec. V): entropy of coarse-grained histories, augmented by the algorithmic information content of the ensemble, $\Sigma \equiv K_{U'}(\tilde E) + S$ ("total information"; Eq. (5.14), to verify), "a negative measure of classicality". Thermodynamic depth $D = S_{\max} - S$. "Evidently the smallness of $\Sigma$ is not by itself a sufficient criterion" (the pathological set of projections onto $|\Psi\rangle$ and its complement); strong decoherence with restricted pools $\{M\}_\alpha$ is proposed to rule such sets out.

### 2.3 Stated assumptions and limitations
- Pure state throughout ("For the rest of the article we assume that $\rho = |\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|$", Sec. I); the earlier (1993) use of "strong decoherence" for $C_\alpha\rho = R_\alpha\rho$ is abandoned as "too restrictive when the state is not pure and ... redundant otherwise".
- Fixed spacetime geometry with a time variable $t$ (Sec. II), "an excellent approximation for many accessible coarse grainings".
- Chains of projections only (homogeneous histories), with coarse grainings of them allowed.
- Condition II is approximate by nature: "Eq. (3.22) cannot be exactly satisfied because it holds only for times $t_k$ later than $t_\ell$. ... [it] cannot be strictly true in quantum mechanics, where evolution is continuous and amplitudes analytic, but it can be true to an excellent approximation, which improves rapidly as the interval increases. Indeed, physically one cannot expect it to hold until a time after $t_\ell$ when the record is created."
- "Strong decoherence is an idealization" (abstract, Sec. III). The Sec. IV model "is a souped-up version of the usual ideal measurement model" and "subject to all the caveats associated with the ideal measurement model. In particular, as was shown by Wigner and Araki and Yanase, there are no realistic Hamiltonians $H$ that will effect the evolution (4.3) exactly."
- Realistic quasiclassical coarse grainings (hydrodynamic variables) "do not correspond to a distinction between two kinds of coordinates", so the system/environment model "is too restrictive for realistic application".
- Set selection left open: the theory "presumably exhibits a great many essentially different strongly decohering realms, but only some of those are quasiclassical"; whether "the usual quasiclassical realm is essentially unique" is a question for the completed program. "We are continuing our efforts to complete, refine, and investigate the consequences of this definition of classicality."

### 2.4 Criticisms cited or responded to
- **Dowker and Kent** are cited once, in footnote 2 of Sec. I, as the reason persistence of the past is not automatic: "Indeed, Dowker and Kent [DKpp] have given examples with special final conditions where a quasiclassical realm cannot be extended at all." [DKpp] = H. F. Dowker and A. Kent, *On the Consistent Histories Approach to Quantum Mechanics*, gr-qc/9412067. The whole strong-decoherence construction is the authors' answer: a step-by-step rule for extending histories that keeps the past decoherent.
- Weaker rival conditions are listed and compared: Goldstein-Page linearly positive histories, Griffiths-Omnès consistency (Sec. II). Finkelstein's "PT-decoherence" is identified as a special case of strong decoherence (Secs. I, IV). Zurek's suggestion of augmenting entropy with algorithmic information is acknowledged (Sec. V).

### 3.2 Key definitions and equations
- Two meanings of "decoherence" (Sec. II, footnote 1): decoherence of *histories* (a property of a set, measured by D) and decoherence of *density matrices* (decay of off-diagonal elements of a reduced density matrix at one time); "These two notions are not exactly the same."
- Sum-over-histories decoherence functional, Eq. (3.1); influence phase $W$, Eq. (3.3); reduced density matrix $\tilde\rho(x'_0,x_0) = \int dQ_0\,\rho(x'_0,Q_0;x_0,Q_0)$, Eq. (3.4); $D$ in terms of $W$, Eq. (3.5); the factorization assumption $\rho = \bar\rho(x'_0,x_0)\rho_B(Q'_0,Q_0)$, Eq. (3.8).
- Chains, Eq. (4.1): $C_\alpha = P^n_{\alpha_n}(t_n)\cdots P^1_{\alpha_1}(t_1)$. "$\alpha$ or $\beta$", Eq. (4.2): $C_{\alpha\ {\rm or}\ \beta} = C_\alpha + C_\beta$. Coarser classes, Eq. (4.3): $C_{\bar\alpha} = \sum_{\alpha\in\bar\alpha} C_\alpha$. **Branch-dependent chains**, Eq. (4.4): $C_\alpha = P^n_{\alpha_n}(t_n;\alpha_{n-1},\ldots,\alpha_1)\,P^{n-1}_{\alpha_{n-1}}(t_{n-1};\alpha_{n-2},\ldots,\alpha_1)\cdots P^1_{\alpha_1}(t_1)$. Footnote 3: "In Ref. [2], Section X, we unnecessarily eliminated the possibility of branch-dependent chains of the form (4.4) ... In fact, that special derivation is unnecessary". (Ref. [2] is the 1989/2018 paper.) "In limiting ourselves to projections that depend only on previous alternatives rather than future ones we have incorporated a notion of causality consistent with the usual arrow of time in quantum mechanics."
- Decoherence functional, Eq. (4.5): $D(\alpha',\alpha) = \mathrm{Tr}[C_{\alpha'}\,\rho\,C^\dagger_\alpha]$.
- **Weak decoherence**, Eq. (4.6): $\mathrm{Re}\,D(\alpha',\alpha) = 0$, $\alpha' \neq \alpha$, "the necessary and sufficient condition for probability sum rules to be satisfied". Probabilities, Eq. (4.7): $p(\alpha) = D(\alpha,\alpha)$. Footnote 4 (Griffiths' correction): consistent histories require the real part to vanish "only if $C_\alpha + C_{\alpha'}$ is another chain", a weaker condition.
- **Medium decoherence**, Eq. (4.8): $D(\alpha',\alpha) = 0$, $\alpha' \neq \alpha$. "Medium decoherence is a consequence of realistic mechanisms that are widespread in the universe. ... It is not, by itself, sufficient to single out a quasiclassical domain."
- Pure state, Eq. (4.9): $D(\alpha',\alpha) = (\langle\Psi|C^\dagger_\alpha)(C_{\alpha'}|\Psi\rangle)$. Resolution into branches, Eq. (4.10): $|\Psi\rangle = \sum_\alpha C_\alpha|\Psi\rangle$. Records, Eqs. (4.11)-(4.12): $R_\alpha|\Psi\rangle = C_\alpha|\Psi\rangle$, $R_\alpha R_{\alpha'} = \delta_{\alpha\alpha'}R_\alpha$, $\sum_\alpha R_\alpha = 1$. For coarser histories the record is the sum of the corresponding $R_\alpha$; "progressive fine graining of the coarser-grained histories results in a sequence of *nested* $R$'s, projecting onto smaller and smaller subspaces". A set whose branches form a complete basis is "full". The records "may not represent records in the usual sense of being constructed from quasiclassical variables accessible to us, but the condition (4.11) means that at any time there is complete information somewhere in the universe about the histories $\{\alpha\}$."
- **Permanence of the past** (Sec. IVa): "what has happened in the past is independent of any information expressed by a future projection. Neither the decoherence of past alternatives nor the selection of a particular past alternative is threatened by new information." Effective density matrix for a history up to $t_k$, Eq. (4.13); for a pure state the "reduction of the state vector" Eq. (4.14): $|\Psi\rangle \to N^{-1/2}P^k_{\alpha_k}(t_k)\cdots P^1_{\alpha_1}(t_1)|\Psi\rangle$. Then the caveat: "Yet we know that such continued decoherence of the past is not guaranteed *in general* by quantum mechanics. Adjoining future alternatives to a set of histories is a fine graining of that set and in general a fine graining of a decoherent set of histories may no longer decohere. ... Yet we adjoin sets of projections onto ranges of quasiclassical operators without making this calculation, secure in the faith that previous alternatives will continue to decohere despite this fine graining. It is this assumption of continued decoherence of the past that permits the focus for future predictions on the one branch corresponding to our particular history and the discarding of all others." With a pure $\rho$, nested records for abbreviated histories guarantee permanence, "including not only the permanence of its decoherence, but also the permanence of the selection of particular past alternatives".
- Schrödinger-picture model of record formation, Eqs. (4.15)-(4.22): branch wave functions $\sum_{r_3\in\alpha_3}\phi^{3\alpha_2\alpha_1}_{r_3}(x)\chi^{\alpha_2\alpha_1}_{r_3}(Q)$; the decoherence functional as an overlap of the $\chi$'s, Eq. (4.20); footnote 7: "as more and more times are added to the histories ... we expect that eventually even the space ${\cal H}^Q$ will be exhausted and $\chi$'s no longer orthogonal." "The decoherence of histories is a much stronger requirement than the diagonality of density matrices." The Schmidt decomposition (Kübler-Zeh, Albrecht, Kochen) "does not guarantee the permanence of the past".
- The 1993 "strong decoherence" for density matrices, $C_\alpha\rho = R_\alpha\rho$ (the equation after (4.22); its published number is to verify, since the source reuses the tag), judged "too strong a condition" for impure states; "medium strong decoherence", $C_\alpha|\Psi^\mu\rangle = R^\mu_\alpha|\Psi^\mu\rangle$ for each eigenstate of $\rho$, is proposed instead (number to verify). *This is the definition the 1995 paper renames and replaces.*
- Permanence in the oscillator model (Sec. IVb): decoherence is "essentially local in time"; "Once dispersed among the continuum of oscillators ... [phases] cannot be recovered by finer grainings beyond $t_n$ that involve the $x^a$ alone. To recover the phases, one would need a much finer graining that involved the whole set of variables."
- Arrow of time and causality (Sec. VIIc): "It is by *convention* that we call the extremity of the histories next to the density matrix $\rho$ *the past* and consider time as increasing from it." Quantum-mechanical causality (needing only $\rho$ and the history to the present) implies the retardation of the effective force, hence classical causality. A generalization with both initial and final conditions $\rho_i$, $\rho_f$ is written (Eq. (7.26), label `sixtyfour`, number to verify); for $\rho_f \not\propto I$ "there would be neither a notion of quantum mechanical causality nor a notion of 'state of the system at a moment of time'."
- IGUS and set selection (Sec. X): "Observations by such complex adaptive systems, which we call IGUSes (information gathering and utilizing systems) when they are functioning as observers, are then considered to be actual measurements." "For the coarse graining defining a quasiclassical domain to be an emergent feature of the universe characterized by $H$ and $\rho$ rather than an artifact chosen by some IGUS, it should be as refined a description of the universe as possible consistent with the requirements of decoherence and quasiclassicality." "We have posed the question as to whether there could be various kinds of essentially inequivalent quasiclassical domains ... The former case poses some challenging intellectual puzzles, especially if we imagine IGUSes evolving in relation to each". "The distinguished variables cannot be defined once and for all, but depend on history."

### 3.3 Stated assumptions and limitations
- Fixed background spacetime with a well-defined time (Sec. III). Coarse grainings that distinguish a fixed subset $x^a$ of coordinates and ignore the rest $Q^A$; realistic (hydrodynamic, branch-dependent) coarse grainings are "not of this simple type" and only sketched formally in Sec. IX. "In the models that we treat in detail in this article, we do not make use of branch dependence ... It should be borne in mind, though, that realistic coarse-grained histories do involve branch dependence" (Sec. IVa).
- Factored initial density matrix, Eq. (3.8): "we do not necessarily expect initial density matrix of the whole universe to factor as in (3.8), but, for widespread mechanisms of decoherence that operate essentially locally in space and time ... (3.8) is an excellent approximation."
- Action of kinetic-minus-potential form with interaction local in time (Eqs. (3.6)-(3.7)); Gaussian (quadratic) truncation of the influence phase for the noise; neglect of the coarse-graining constraints on the difference variable $\xi$ "may result in small negative probabilities" (Sec. V).
- In these models momentum coarse grainings do not decohere (Sec. IXa): "Two coarse grainings that would be essentially indistinguishable classically may have very different properties quantum-mechanically."
- Densities (Sec. IXb): "we have not yet progressed beyond this formal sketch".

### 3.4 Criticisms cited or responded to
- Dowker and Kent are **not** cited (the paper predates them); Dowker and Halliwell (1992) are cited as the nearest related analysis of decoherence in linear models (footnotes 2 and 8). Griffiths' correction of the attribution of weak decoherence is recorded (footnote 4). The Schmidt-decomposition approach of other authors is argued not to give decoherence of histories (Sec. IVa). Bell's inequalities are traced to non-positive distributions (Sec. V).

## 5. What contradicts or complicates the handoff (ours, unverified)

1. **The observer-relative conditional measure is already in the papers; the handoff's nested-measure statement is a restatement.** The 2018 paper's Eq. (20) is exactly "the probability of the past given present data": $p(\alpha_{n-1}t_{n-1},\ldots,\alpha_1t_1\mid\alpha_nt_n) = p(\alpha_nt_n,\ldots,\alpha_1t_1)/p(\alpha_nt_n)$, where $\alpha_n$ is the present record; Eq. (18) is the general conditional; Eq. (21) is the effective density matrix of a branch; 1993 Eq. (4.13) is the same per history; 1995 Eqs. (3.24)-(3.25) define the normalized branch state vector and its reduced density matrix. In that language, the handoff's $\mu(\mathcal C(R_n))$ is $p(R_n)$, the probability of the coarse-grained history "the record reads $R_n$", and the handoff's normalized conditional measure is GMH's Eq. (18). What the handoff adds is only the name "surprisal" and the explicit statement that $p(R_n)$ is nonincreasing as the record extends, which is the sum rule. The papers also say the thing the handoff wants said: "The subject of history is most honestly described as the construction of probabilities for the past, given such records" (2018 Sec. X). Stage A should record this as overlap, not as a result.

2. **"Using a common decoherent history family" is precisely what the papers say is not guaranteed.** Three warnings apply. (a) Useful families are *branch-dependent* (1995 Eq. (2.1) and text; 1993 Eq. (4.4); 2018 Sec. X: "There are no variables that are expected to decohere universally"), so a single branch-independent family covering every observer's possible records is the special case, not the rule. (b) Extending a family forward in time to add the new record $R_{n+1}$ is a *fine graining* and "a fine graining of a decoherent set of histories may no longer decohere" (1993 Sec. IVa; 1995 Sec. I). The nesting $\mathcal C(R_{n+1}) \subseteq \mathcal C(R_n)$ is trivial at the level of labels, but the statement that both sets still carry probabilities needs the past alternatives to stay decoherent after the extension, which is what strong-decoherence Conditions I and II (1995 Eqs. (3.9), (3.22)) are designed to secure and which the Dowker-Kent examples show can fail "with special final conditions" (1995 footnote 2). (c) Because $D$ is automatically diagonal in the *last* index (2018 Sec. IVc), a record alternative taken at the latest time always decoheres by itself; the question is never the record, it is the past it points to. The handoff should either (i) restrict Stage C to a strongly decoherent family in the 1995 sense and say so, or (ii) state the nesting as conditional on the extended family remaining (medium) decoherent, and test that in the model rather than assume it.

3. **The handoff's "record" conflates two things the papers keep apart.** GMH's *generalized record* $R_\alpha$ (1995 Eq. (3.1); 1993 Eq. (4.11)) exists whenever a pure-state family is medium decoherent and is "complete information somewhere in the universe" that "may not be accessible to us". An IGUS's *memory* is a much coarser, imperfectly correlated alternative (2018 Sec. XII). The handoff's $\mathcal C(R_n)$ and its remarks on erased records belong to the second; its nesting theorem is proved for the first. Erasing an accessible memory does not destroy the generalized record (so the global branch structure is untouched, as the handoff wants), but it does re-widen the observer's compatible set, so $I(R_n)$ can fall, as the handoff already notes. Say which object each formula is about.

4. **A retracted statement sits uncorrected in the 2018 re-posting.** 2018 Sec. X says branch dependence "is not described by considering histories where the set of alternatives at one time ... depends on specific alternatives ... of sets of earlier times. Such dependence would destroy the derivation of the probability sum rules." The 1993 paper, footnote 3, withdraws this: branch-dependent chains (4.4) are fine, and weak decoherence is necessary and sufficient "whether or not the choice of the set $P^k_{\alpha_k}$ is branch-dependent". The preface confirms the 2018 text "has not been updated". A reader of the handoff's item 2 alone would inherit the retracted form; use 1993 Eq. (4.4) or 1995 Eq. (2.3).

5. **Two different things have been called "strong decoherence".** 1993 (Sec. IVa) uses it for $C_\alpha\rho = R_\alpha\rho$ with a general $\rho$; 1995 (Sec. I) abandons that name and defines strong decoherence by Conditions I and II for a pure state. The handoff's item 3 cites the 1995 sense, correctly; any future reference to the 1993 paper's "strong decoherence" must say which.

6. **"Negligible off-diagonal terms" is medium decoherence, and medium is not the minimum.** The papers are explicit that the sum rules need only $\mathrm{Re}\,D = 0$ (1993 Eq. (4.6)) and that Griffiths-Omnès consistency is weaker still (1995 Eq. (2.7)). Stage C's check "approximately vanishing off-diagonal terms" tests medium decoherence; if a small model happens to satisfy weak but not medium decoherence, the handoff's probabilities are still defined and the check would wrongly report failure. Name the condition and, if useful, report both.

7. **Set selection is left open by the authors themselves, so nothing in the handoff may lean on "the" quasiclassical realm.** 2018 abstract and Sec. XIII, 1995 Sec. V, 1993 Sec. X all pose uniqueness as an open question, and 2018 Sec. XII says an IGUS "evolves to exploit a particular quasiclassical domain or set of such domains". The handoff's commitment 3 (observers evolve from physical subsystems) matches this; commitment 6's shared environments and the Stage D agents should be modeled as further coarse grainings of one realm, as 2018 Sec. XII prescribes ("its coarse graining is very much coarser than that of the quasiclassical domain").

8. **No support here for the handoff's remarks on experienced time.** Commitment 4's "apparent temporal passage is associated with ordered observer states, memories, and the arrow of records" has no counterpart in the three papers, which treat the arrow of time as a property of $D$ and of $\rho$ (1993 Sec. VIIc; 2018 Sec. IVd) and say nothing about experience. Hartle's 2020 arrows-of-time paper (handoff item 10) is the place to look; mark the sentence as a claim awaiting a source until then.

9. **The approximations the papers carry should be carried by Stage B and C too.** Fixed background with a preferred time; a pure initial state for the record theorems (1995 Sec. I); probabilities that are approximate "to the accuracy to which they are used" (2018 Sec. II); and the warning that a finite environment is eventually exhausted so that record states stop being orthogonal (1993 footnote 7). The last is directly testable in a small Hilbert-space model and is a natural "clear reason any model fails" for Stage F.

## 6. Known criticisms of decoherent histories (what the papers cite, and general knowledge)

- **Cited in the papers:** Dowker and Kent, gr-qc/9412067, for realms that "cannot be extended at all" under special final conditions (1995 footnote 2); Griffiths' correction that his consistency condition is weaker than GMH's weak decoherence (1993 footnote 4; 1995 Eq. (2.7)); Goldstein and Page's linearly positive histories as a weaker alternative (1995 Eq. (2.6)); the Schmidt-decomposition approach as insufficient for histories (1993 Sec. IVa).
- **General knowledge, to verify before any citation:** Dowker and Kent (J. Stat. Phys. 82, 1575, 1996) argued that consistency alone is far too weak to select a quasiclassical set, that there are many consistent sets with no quasiclassical properties, and that quasiclassicality now does not imply quasiclassicality later; Kent (Phys. Rev. Lett. 78, 2874, 1997) argued that different consistent sets can yield contrary retrodictions; Griffiths and Omnès answered with a "single-framework" rule; Gell-Mann and Hartle's later answers include quasiclassical coarse grainings via conserved densities (2007) and a version with one real fine-grained history (2012). None of these was read for this note; the handoff's Stage A owes a prior-work line for each before it cites them (CLAUDE.md rule 15).
