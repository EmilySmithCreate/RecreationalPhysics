## What the paper claims

Not Koide's 2/3 for hadrons. The form is √m_g = v + s cos(δ + 2πg/3) (his Eq. 43) with the angle δ held at 2/9, or at
2/9 + π/12 for "neutrino-like" triplets, and v and s **both free** for each triplet of excitations with the same quark
content and quantum numbers (ω(782), ω(1420), ω(1960) and so on). For the leptons s/v = √2 gives Koide; for the hadrons
s/v comes out 0.02 to 0.6, so the hadron triplets have K ≈ 0.50 to 0.60 and Koide's relation is not claimed for them.
"We give 39 mass fits for 137 hadrons": 33 meson triplets, 6 baryon triplets, fitted by least squares to PDG 2008. The
nucleons "do not exhibit any convincing Koide formula"; nor do Σ and Ξ. His reason for 2/9 in hadrons: "the amplitude for
each soft gluon emission is 2i/9" (Eq. 68), a preon argument. His own caveat (p. 46): "Some of the fits are undoubtedly
incorrect; they are the result of random chance ... Even a collection of 41 × 3 = 123 random data points has an equation
that removes 41 degrees of freedom from it." No chi-squared or chance estimate is given; in 2008 he wrote "What I need is
a decent measure for goodness of fit".

**Count (rule 13):** three masses, two free constants, one angle fixed: one constraint per triplet, and the only thing
tested is whether the angle read from three masses lands on 2/9 or 2/9 + π/12, with free choice of which three of 4 to 14
same-quantum-number states to group, two allowed angles, and merging of close states.

Extracted angles with tight errors sit 0.05 to 0.08 rad from the nearest target, 25 to 70σ away. **Chance rate:** among the
30 lightest well-established mesons (4,060 triplets), a fixed-angle two-constant fit reproduces all three masses within
their errors for 6.9 % of triplets at 1σ, 14.4 % at 3σ, and 18.5 % with a 5 MeV floor; the angle lands within 0.05 rad of
one of his two targets for 10.5 % (uniform expectation 19 %). *Inference (the agent's, and ours):* 39 fits from 137 hadrons
with free grouping is about what chance delivers, and the author's text concedes it in words.

## Verdict for the record

**KILLED as a route for G4 (several loops).** Hadrons as their own Koide triplets with the lepton angle is one constraint
per triplet, fails on the best-measured triplets with current masses, and is matched by chance at the rate observed. The
2/9 angle gains no support from hadrons. What remains for G4 is a rule that is not a sum and not a per-triplet angle,
which no one has published that we have found (this note is the search behind that sentence, rule 15: brannenworks.com,
INSPIRE forward citations, Semantic Scholar, arXiv author listing, viXra, PhysicsForums, his blog; 30 October 2026).
