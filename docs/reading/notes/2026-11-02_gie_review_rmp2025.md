## 2. What the witness tests, and its classicality assumptions (read in full: Secs. I, III.A, IV.B, Appendix C)

**The theorem in one sentence (Sec. I, p. 4).** "This, in short, is the theorem: if a system M (e.g. gravity)
can entangle two quantum systems QA and QB (e.g. two masses) by local interactions (by QA coupling to M and M to
QB, but without QA and QB interacting directly) then M must be non-classical. The theorem, in this general form,
is a generalisation of the Local-Operation-and-Classical-Communication (LOCC) theorems of quantum information,
[18] (which assume quantum theory's formalism) to the domain where quantum theory may not apply."

**Non-classical, defined (Sec. I, p. 3).** "A system is 'non-classical' if it must have at least two distinct
physical variables that are necessary to describe its features, and yet cannot be measured to arbitrarily high
accuracy simultaneously (i.e. by the same measuring system). These variables in quantum theory correspond to
incompatible observables, as prescribed by Heisenberg's uncertainty principle, but the notion of non-classicality
is more general than quantum theory." And Sec. IV.B (p. 17): "One variable is the classical observable of the
physical system at hand - for instance, in the case of the quantum mass, its position. The additional
non-classical variable is necessary to enable the non-classical system to mediate entanglement between two other
quantum systems. However, unlike the classical variable, it may not be a fully-fledged observable in the sense
that its states may not be physically preparable or perfectly distinguishable."

**Classical, defined (Sec. IV.B, p. 16; Appendix C, p. 32).** The information variable "generalises the idea of a
classical observable, i.e., a set of states that are all distinguishable." In the proof: "one considers three
systems: QA and QB (two quantum probes), and M, the mediator, which has a 'classical' observable T, and no other
known degrees of freedom. In the case of gravity, the classical basis of the gravitational field would be the
number operator, or its energy." A mediator counts as classical when all it has is one such copiable,
distinguishable variable (Appendix C: "an information medium M, with maximal information observable T"); the
non-classical case is a second variable V "disjoint from T", with "The union of V with T is not a distinguishable
variable" (Appendix C, item 2, p. 32).

**Locality, the first assumption (Sec. IV.B, p. 16).** "The first assumption is Einstein's principle of locality,
or no action at a distance. This principle requires that physical variables of a given subsystem (whether or not
directly measurable on that subsystem) cannot be affected by operations acting solely on other subsystems. Note
that by the term 'locality' authors have indicated other concepts in the literature on this topic. For instance,
there is the no-signalling property of quantum theory ... There is also Lorentz-covariance ... The property of
locality as defined above is implied by Lorentz-covariance, as the latter rules out instantaneous action at a
distance. Locality also implies no-signalling. However, it is more general than either of those, as it is
formulated in a dynamics-independent way." In the proof (Appendix C, p. 31): "If a and b are attributes, the
attribute (a, b) of the composite system S1 ⊕ S2 is defined as the set of all states of the composite system where
S1 has attribute a and S2 has attribute b. I shall assume throughout the principle of locality (P1), which
requires that if a transformation operates only on substrate S1, then only the attribute a changes, not b." And
(p. 32): "Locality is here used one more, because we have taken into account that the descriptor of system B must
not have changed since t0, given that the interaction at t1 only involves A and M."

**Interoperability, the second assumption (Sec. IV.B, p. 16).** "First, one defines an information medium, as a
physical system with a set of disjoint states that can be copied to perfect accuracy. ... Then the principle is
stated as follows: If S1 and S2 are information media, respectively with information variables X1 and X2, their
composite system S1 ⊕ S2 is an information medium with information variable X1 × X2, where × denotes the
Cartesian product of sets." Appendix C (pp. 31 to 32) adds the entry condition: "information media must have a
variable X with the property that it is possible to perform all the permutation tasks on X, and that it is
possible to perform the task of copying all attributes in X from one substrate to its replica. Any variable X that
can be copied and permuted in all possible ways is called an information variable."

**Mediation, the third assumption (Sec. IV.B, p. 16).** "The third assumption of the GWT is that the interaction
between the quantum probes is mediated (by gravity in the specific case). This assumption is independently
testable via other experiments involving classical gravity interacting with a quantum probe, (e.g., via the COW
experiment)."

**What the theorem does not assume (Sec. IV.B, p. 17).** "It is also appealing that, aside from the assumption of
there being some topology on the set of states of the systems in question, the theorem does not assume the
presence of a probabilistic structure on the systems in question. Hence The GWT is proven in a setting that is
more general than other frameworks, such as Generalised Probabilistic Theories (GPTs), [72], where probabilities
are assumed."

**The LOCC argument, and why the review ranks it below the GWT (Sec. IV.A, p. 16).** "Bose and collaborators have
appealed to theorems from quantum information theory, stating that Local Operations and Classical Communication
(LOCC) cannot create entanglement between two quantum systems, in order to support the claim that GIE rules out
the possibility that gravity is a classical decohered channel, [13]. That theorem holds only once one has already
assumed that gravity obeys quantum theory, and it allows one to rule out classicalised, decohered versions of it,
such as [61]. ... Moreover, it could be argued that without assuming that the Newtonian gravity is an appropriate
limit of some quantum theory, the formula represents a non-local action-at-distance interaction, thus violating
the basic premise of the LOCC paradigm."

**The witness as a one-sided test (Sec. I, p. 4; Sec. IV.C, p. 20).** "successfully carrying out the protocol to
witness non-classicality, thereby measuring GIE, is sufficient evidence that gravity is non-classical. However,
failing to detect GIE does not immediately imply that gravity is classical". And: "Given the natural asymmetry of
the witness (GIE is only a sufficient, but not necessary condition for non-classicality), failing to observe GIE
does not demonstrate that gravity is classical."

## 3. The no-go the other way: which theories predict no entanglement (read in full: Secs. IV.A, IV.B.1)

Yes, the review states it, for general relativity by name and for semiclassical gravity with the calculation.

**General relativity (Sec. I, p. 4; Sec. IV, p. 14).** GIE "would constitute, if GIE were to be observed, the
first experimental refutation of Einstein's theory of general relativity (as a classical theory of gravity)."
And: "The General Witness Argument allows the detection of GIE to be elevated to conclusive evidence that no
classical model satisfying the assumptions of the theorem (which is a vast class of plausible physical models for
gravity, including general relativity) is suitable to describe gravity."

**Any classical local mediator (Sec. III.A, p. 11).** "if one were to use a classical mediator to couple the two
quantum probes locally, the most general state that one can achieve by local interactions would be separable at
all times, [13, 14, 16]."

**Semiclassical gravity, with the arithmetic (Sec. IV.B.1, pp. 17 to 18).** "Observing GIE would refute all those
theories which regard a semiclassical description of gravity as fundamental, such as quantum field theory in
curved spacetime [26]. ... This is because they resort to a field which is classical - in the sense that it has
no pair of non-commuting observables. The field strength at each point is determined by an average of the
stress-energy tensor in the quantum state of the masses. In the situation of the GIE experiment, each mass would
therefore be affected by the average of the gravitational field generated by the other superposed mass. ... Thus
the phase acquired would be a local phase, which cannot generate entanglement between the masses. Each mass would
be undergoing a separate, COW-type experiment: the state of the two masses would be at any time a product state.
Semiclassical theories would therefore be refuted as fundamental descriptions of gravity by observing
entanglement in the proposed experiment. The same prediction of no entanglement would be reached by models that
resort the (non-equivalent) procedure of averaging the linearised quantum gravity Hamiltonian in the quantum
state of the two masses."

**The list, as the review gives it.** Sec. IV.A (p. 15): "the detection of GIE rules out general relativity,
quantum field theory in curved spacetime, certain collapse-models that predict collapse at the relevant scale, and
a decohered version of linear quantum gravity where gravity is an entanglement-breaking channel, [59], as well as
other stochastic semiclassical hybrids such as [61]." Sec. IV.B.1 adds: collapse models ("According to Penrose's
collapse models, [69, 80], this time is of the order t = ℏ/(G m² / d) ≈ 10⁻¹³ s, well below 10⁻⁶ s, required for
our experiment", p. 18); "collapse-inducing hybrid stochastic quantum-classical models, such as [59, 61]"; the
Hall and Reginatto classical-quantum ensemble hybrid, which does produce entanglement but "conceals a hidden
non-locality in the configuration space dynamics, in contradiction with the locality assumption of the GWT" (p.
19); "recently proposed models such as [72, 73] all resort to the classical Newtonian potential and can generate
entanglement, but violate the assumption of locality, thus corroborating the witness of non-classicality" (p. 19);
and the Sherry and Sudarshan superselection hybrids, which "if they are able to generate entanglement, are in fact
fully non-classical" (p. 19). Theories with no mediator at all (Hoyle and Narlikar, Wheeler-Feynman absorber
theory) are outside the theorem: "if there is no mediator, there is nothing to quantise. In that sense, the
non-classicality witness we discussed does not apply to them" (p. 19), though "this theory is ruled out a-priori
by the principle of locality."

What the review refuses to conclude from a positive result (Sec. IV.A, pp. 15 to 16): not gravitons ("GIE only
tests the presence of non-commuting degrees of freedom in gravity, not the presence of gravitons"); not
superposed geometries ("spacetime geometry during the experiment is never prepared in a superposition of its
classical configurations. It is in a mixed state"); not discrete time ("discreteness of a variable is not
necessarily a proof of its non-classicality").

## 4. Discrete, lattice, emergent, non-perturbative and identical-particle treatments (searched in full text)

**Not found** for lattice, emergent, graph, network-as-gravity, spin network, causal set, triangulation,
entropic, automorphism, identical particles, exchange statistics or indistinguishability of the mediator's
constituents. Terms searched in the extracted text (case-insensitive): lattice, emergen, graph, spin network,
causal set, triangul, Verlinde, entropic, automorph, identical, indistinguish, boson, fermion, symmetr, permut,
exchange, statistics, ensemble, global, Gauss, superselection, discret, network, non-perturbative. The hits:

- "discret" (8): the qubit "z-component represents a discretised position of each mass" (Sec. III.B, p. 12); "a
  discrete sum over the relevant gravitational quantum modes" (III.B); and the Christodoulou and Rovelli
  discrete-time passage of Sec. IV.A (p. 15), quoted in section 3 above, with its verdict that "all observables
  (classical or quantum) are discrete (to allow for error-correction, see e.g. [182]), so that discreteness of a
  variable is not necessarily a proof of its non-classicality."
- "network" (6): all refer to the quantum gate ("quantum network") model of Appendix A that approximates the
  linear-quantum-gravity Hamiltonian by Hadamard, controlled-NOT and controlled-phase gates; not a network model
  of gravity.
- "non-perturbative" (8): "In the quasi-Newtonian regime where GIE is generated, existing non-perturbative
  proposals such as string theory and loop quantum gravity agree with the predictions of linearised quantum
  gravity, [2], hence it is impossible to claim that this experiment, should GIE be observed, discriminates any
  particular quantum gravity theory from another" (Sec. I, p. 4). Sec. VII (pp. 26 to 27): "it would be
  theoretically important to understand how a non-perturbative model would explain the GIE generation and
  detection. One could speculate that the metric may be one of the non-commuting degrees of freedom, but then it
  would be necessary to identify another degree of freedom that does not commute with it. To the best of our
  knowledge, there is at present no explicit calculation of the GIE effect in non-perturbative models. Hence, an
  interesting open question is finding the degrees of freedom of gravity that mediate GIE in non-perturbative
  models of quantum gravity, such as loop quantum gravity or string theory."
- "boson" (1): "a and a† can be interpreted as the bosonic annihilation and creation operators for gravitons"
  (Sec. III.B, p. 12). Nothing on symmetrization of a many-body mediator.
- "permut" (2): the information-variable definition of Appendix C quoted in section 2 (a variable on which "all
  the permutation tasks" are possible). This is a permutation of the mediator's states, not a permutation of its
  constituents.
- "symmetr" (7): none about the mediator's symmetry group; "symmetric quantum network" is the gate circuit of
  Fig. 6; "by symmetry, for the other mass" is the semiclassical calculation.
- "global" (6): the "global state of the hybrid ensemble" in the Hall-Reginatto model (Sec. IV.B.1, p. 18) and
  the conserved "global additive observable" of the temporal witness (Secs. V.D and App. C.A), the latter being an
  additive conservation law, [U, Z_M + Z_Q] = 0, not a global count.
- "Gauss" (7): all are Gaussianity of states (the Howl et al. single-probe variant); nothing on a Gauss law.
- "superselection": the Sherry-Sudarshan hybrids (Sec. IV.B.1, p. 19), a non-classical variable "which by the
  superselection rule is required to be unobservable"; generating entanglement "is equivalent to indirectly
  measuring the superselected variable."
- "ensemble": only the Hall-Reginatto "classical-quantum ensemble hybrid model" (Sec. IV.B.1).

So the review does not treat any model in which gravity's carrier is a discrete network, nor any in which the
carrier's constituents are identical particles, nor any in which an arrangement's weight is a function of the
whole. The one place where a mediator's "statistical" or "ensemble" character enters is the Hall-Reginatto model,
and the review's verdict on it is the locality verdict quoted in section 3: the entanglement it generates comes
from "a hidden non-locality in the configuration space dynamics", and such a model "validates the witness" rather
than evading it (the review's words, p. 19, citing Marletto and Marconato [71]).

## 6. Loopholes and caveats (read in full: Secs. IV.B, IV.C, IV.C.1)

- "Loopholes can always be found, as pointed out by A. Kent et al, just like for Bell-like experiments, [67].
  Indeed, it is of the essence to be able to independently argue or test the additional principles on which the
  argument rests, otherwise observing GIE may not prove that gravity is quantum. Moreover, there are classical
  models which could describe a GIE detection, by violating the assumptions. For instance, as we shall mention,
  models where gravity is not mediated (see Wheeler-Feynman absorber theory, [68]); and non-local models, such as
  Hall and Reginatto's." (Sec. IV.B, p. 17.)
- The gauge objection [75] and the replies (Sec. IV.B.1, p. 19): "the dynamical perturbation induced by the
  quantum probe cannot be gauged away, and ... the GIE effect is not a completely static one".
- A null result (Sec. IV.C, p. 20): "it could be that some of the assumptions of the witness are false
  (particularly, locality or interoperability of information). It could also be that gravity is quantum, but obeys
  a different model of quantum gravity, and hence the magnitude of GIE calculated with LQG is incorrect; for
  instance, one could have a model that is non-linear"; and "other fields being at play (the fifth force?)".
- Gravitational decoherence is not evidence of classicality (Sec. IV.C.1, pp. 20 to 21): entanglement with a
  quantized field that is undone when the arms recombine is "fake decoherence"; classical dephasing is reversible
  by acting on the system alone (spin echo), decoherence by entanglement is not; genuine collapse "can always be
  thought of as a form of decoherence" at the level of a larger Hilbert space, so telling them apart "would then
  require us to have the full knowledge and control of the environment".
- Caveat on linear quantum gravity (Sec. IV.A, p. 15): "Linear quantum gravity is known to be an approximation
  scheme, that is problematic in that it starts from a linearised version of general relativity. Moreover, that
  linear quantum gravity is compatible with the detection of GIE does not mean that it is verified by observing
  GIE."

## 7. The owner's question, answered

The question: does the review say anything about mediators whose state weights depend on a global symmetry
count, about discrete network models of the mediator, or about interchangeable (identical-particle) treatments of
the mediator, that would already settle whether such a mediator satisfies the locality principle and counts as
classical?

**Not found.** Searched for: symmetry count, automorphism, identical particles, indistinguishable, permutation
(of constituents), exchange statistics, bosonic or fermionic mediator, interchangeable, ensemble weight, global
constraint, Gauss law, network model of gravity, graph, lattice, discrete gravity, emergent gravity, spin network,
causal set, triangulation, entropic gravity, non-perturbative (found, but only loop quantum gravity and string
theory, with the GIE calculation in them named as open). The review contains no example of a mediator whose
dynamics or weights depend on a property of the whole arrangement, and no statement about whether such a mediator
meets Principle 1.

Three passages come nearest, and none answers it (ours, unverified, on what they imply):

1. **The Hall-Reginatto verdict (Sec. IV.B.1, p. 19)** is the review's only treatment of a mediator defined by an
   ensemble rather than a state, and the verdict is that its entanglement is bought by "a hidden non-locality in
   the configuration space dynamics, in contradiction with the locality assumption of the GWT", so the model is
   non-local and the theorem does not reach it (it "validates the witness"). The interchangeable-points chain
   (T10; [DQM25] Eq. 72) accepts a move in one region with a probability that multiplies by |Aut(G′)|/|Aut(G)|, a
   ratio set by the wiring of the whole network. By the review's own reading of Hall and Reginatto, a transition
   rule for region A that depends on the wiring of region B looks like the same kind of hidden non-locality in the
   dynamics, which would put the knob outside the theorem rather than inside its "classical" case. That is an
   inference from an analogous case, not a statement of the review, and it is exactly what the letter asks her to
   rule on.
2. **No probabilistic structure assumed (Sec. IV.B, p. 17).** The theorem is proved from possible and impossible
   tasks on states with "some topology", with no probabilities. A weight on an arrangement is a statistical
   feature, so the theorem as stated has no slot for it: the question "is a weight-by-symmetry mediator classical"
   has to be asked about the tasks the mediator makes possible, not about its weights. The review does not spell
   this out for any model.
3. **Discreteness is beside the point (Sec. IV.A, p. 15).** "all observables (classical or quantum) are
   discrete"; "a classical bit is discrete, but does not have any non-classical properties." So the toy's
   discreteness neither helps nor hurts its classicality in the theorem's sense; what decides it is whether the
   carrier has a second variable V not jointly distinguishable with its arrangement T. For a named-points carrier
   whose only variable is the wiring, that is the classical case (Appendix C: "a 'classical' observable T, and no
   other known degrees of freedom"), and the prediction is no entanglement, shared with general relativity.

**The no-go the other way** is stated, with the quotations in section 3: general relativity is named as refuted
by a positive result (Sec. I, p. 4; Sec. IV, p. 14), and semiclassical gravity is shown to give "a local phase,
which cannot generate entanglement between the masses ... the state of the two masses would be at any time a
product state" (Sec. IV.B.1, p. 18).

## 8. What this changes for the Marletto letter (ours)

The question still stands, and the review strengthens the case for sending it: the review's one treatment of a
non-state-based mediator (Hall-Reginatto) was decided by the locality principle, the review lists "how a
non-perturbative model would explain the GIE generation" as its first open problem and says no explicit GIE
calculation exists in any non-perturbative model, and no discrete or identical-particle carrier appears anywhere
in it. Rewordings the review suggests:

- Cite the review, not only the 2020 paper, and use its own wording for the principle: "physical variables of a
  given subsystem (whether or not directly measurable on that subsystem) cannot be affected by operations acting
  solely on other subsystems" (Sec. IV.B). The draft's paraphrase ("acting on one part leaves the other alone") is
  faithful to Appendix C's P1 and can stay.
- Say what "weight" means in the toy in one clause, since the theorem has no probabilities in it: the chain's
  acceptance rule for a move inside one region is multiplied by a ratio of symmetry counts of the whole network.
  That turns the question from one about weights, which the theorem does not speak about, into one about whether
  the dynamics is local in her sense, which it does. Suggested form: "the rule that decides whether a local
  rearrangement happens depends on a symmetry count of the whole network, not only on the region rearranged."
- Add the Hall-Reginatto comparison as the specific thing being asked, since it is her own precedent: "Your
  review reads the Hall-Reginatto hybrid as hiding a non-locality in its configuration-space dynamics. Is a
  carrier whose local transition rule depends on a global symmetry count of the same kind (non-local, so outside
  the theorem), or is it a classical mediator in your sense (one distinguishable variable, the arrangement), so
  that the theorem predicts no entanglement from it?" Either answer is still useful, as the earlier note said.
- Drop any suggestion that discreteness itself bears on the answer; the review says it does not.
- The outreach preference (check the recipient's most recent papers first) is now met for the review. Two later
  preprints by the same authors surfaced in the search and were not read: arXiv:2510.19969, "Classical Gravity
  Cannot Mediate Entanglement by Local Means" (October 2025), and arXiv:2511.07348 (title not read). The first,
  by its title, is on exactly the locality question; it should be read (at least the abstract and its definition
  of "local means") before the letter goes, since it may answer the question for classical mediators in general.
  Not read here; nothing is claimed about its content.
- Rule 13 stands: the review is a target, and a null GIE result is not evidence for the hypothesis ("failing to
  observe GIE does not demonstrate that gravity is classical", Sec. IV.C). A positive GIE result would refute any
  named-points classical carrier in the toy, as it would general relativity.
