**Terms.** *Equation of state, w:* dark energy's pressure divided by its energy density. A cosmological constant has w = −1 exactly and a density that never changes as space grows; w above −1 means the density falls as space grows; below −1 ("phantom") it rises. *w0 and wa:* the two numbers of the standard fit, w(a) = w0 + (1 − a) wa, with a the size of the universe relative to today ([Planck18] Eq. (49)); w0 is w now, wa how fast it was changing. *BAO:* baryon acoustic oscillations, a fixed ruler in the galaxy distribution left by early sound waves. *De Sitter space:* the empty universe with only a positive cosmological constant, expanding at a fixed rate forever. *Cosmic no-hair:* the theorem that such a universe forgets its starting shape and becomes de Sitter.

## 1. The standard assumption and the end it implies

Quoted, [Planck18] Sec. 7.4.1: "In ΛCDM, w0 = −1 and wa = 0." Paraphrased: dark energy is taken to be a constant of nature, the same density at every place and time. *Ours:* "ends at 0" is the reverse of this picture: everything else thins to zero as space grows, dark energy alone stays, and the end state is de Sitter space.

The no-hair result is Wald, Phys. Rev. D 28, 2118 (1983). Quoted (abstract, opening): "We examine the late-time behavior of intially expanding homogeneous cosmological models satisfying Einstein's equation" (the typo is the record's). Paraphrased from the same abstract: models of most Bianchi types (homogeneous but possibly lopsided universes) approach de Sitter expansion exponentially at late times. The proof covers homogeneous models only; the general case remains a conjecture (*ours, from secondary sources*).

## 2. What the measurements say

**Planck 2018.** Quoted (abstract): "the dark-energy equation of state parameter is measured to be w0=−1.03±0.03, consistent with a cosmological constant". Table 6, Planck+SNe+BAO, w0wa fit: w0 = −0.957 ± 0.080, wa = −0.29 (+0.32/−0.26), Δχ² = −1.4 against ΛCDM.

**Pantheon+ (Brout et al. 2022).** Quoted (abstract): "For a Flat w0CDM model, we measure w0=-0.90±0.14 from SNe Ia alone"; with CMB and BAO, w0 = −0.978 (+0.024/−0.031), and wa = −0.65 (+0.28/−0.32) in the w0wa fit (paraphrased); "both w0 values are consistent with a cosmological constant."

**DES-SN5YR (2024).** Quoted (abstract): supernovae alone give w = −0.80 (+0.14/−0.16) in flat wCDM and (w0, wa) = (−0.36 +0.36/−0.30, −8.8 +3.7/−4.5) in flat w0waCDM; with Planck, SDSS BAO and DES 3×2-point, w = −0.941 ± 0.026; "In all cases dark energy is consistent with a cosmological constant to within ∼2σ."

**DESI DR1 (2024).** Quoted (abstract): "DESI BAO alone require w=−0.99 +0.15/−0.13"; combinations with CMB or supernovae "individually prefer w0>−1 and wa<0", at "2.6σ for the DESI+CMB combination" and "2.5σ, 3.5σ or 3.9σ" with Pantheon+, Union3 or DES-SN5YR added. Sec. 5.2, Eqs. (29) to (32), Table 3: DESI+CMB w0 = −0.45 (+0.34/−0.21), wa = −1.79 (+0.48/−1.0); +Pantheon+ −0.827 ± 0.063, −0.75 (+0.29/−0.25); +Union3 −0.64 ± 0.11, −1.27 (+0.40/−0.34); +DES-SN5YR −0.727 ± 0.067, −1.05 (+0.31/−0.27).

**DESI DR2 (2025).** Quoted (abstract): BAO parameters "are in mild, 2.3σ tension" with the CMB; the evolving fit "is preferred over ΛCDM at 3.1σ for the combination of DESI BAO and CMB data"; with supernovae the preference "ranges from 2.8-4.2σ depending on which SNe sample is used"; "Unless there is an unknown systematic error associated with one or more datasets, it is clear that ΛCDM is being challenged". Table 5, Eqs. (25) to (28): DESI+CMB w0 = −0.42 ± 0.21, wa = −1.75 ± 0.58; +Pantheon+ −0.838 ± 0.055, −0.62 (+0.22/−0.19); +Union3 −0.667 ± 0.088, −1.09 (+0.31/−0.27); +DESY5 −0.752 ± 0.057, −0.86 (+0.23/−0.20). Sec. VII.1, quoted: "at the 2.8σ, 3.8σ, and 4.2σ levels" for Pantheon+, Union3, DESY5. Table 5, constant w for the same three: −0.995 ± 0.023, −0.997 ± 0.027, −0.971 ± 0.021. Lodha et al., quoted (abstract): "The current data indicate a clear preference for models that feature a phantom crossing".

*Ours, unverified:* a constant w stays within 3 % of −1 in every combination. The departure is in the drift: w0 above −1 with wa below zero means w was below −1 in the past and above it now, so the density rose, peaked near the present, and is now falling; that is the "phantom crossing". The significance moves by more than a sigma with the supernova sample. None of these papers says what the density does in the future: the w0wa form is a fit to the past.

## 3. Thawing, freezing, phantom, and the four ends

Caldwell and Linder 2005, quoted (thawing section): "the field has been frozen by Hubble damping at a value displaced from its minimum until recently, when it starts to roll down to the minimum. We call these 'thawing' models"; w then "grows less negative with time". Quoted (freezing section): "For these 'freezing' models, initially w>−1 and w′<0", ending with "w→−1, w′→0." Paraphrased (abstract): the field rolls toward a minimum at zero.

*Ours, unverified, the four ends.* (i) **De Sitter:** a true constant, or a freezing field settling at w = −1 above a nonzero floor. (ii) **Flat Minkowski end,** empty and no longer accelerating: a thawing field rolling to a minimum at zero. de Souza et al. 2025, quoted (abstract): a thawing form fits DESI-era data while "predicting cosmic acceleration as a transient phenomenon". (iii) **Big Rip:** Caldwell, Kamionkowski and Weinberg 2003, quoted: "The positive phantom-energy density becomes infinite in finite time"; for w = −3/2 "the time remaining before the Universe ends in this 'Big Rip' is 22 Gyr." (iv) **Recollapse:** Kallosh et al. 2003, quoted (abstract): "the dark energy density may change its sign during the evolution of the universe. This may lead to a global collapse of the universe within the time t_c ~ 10^{10}-10^{11} years"; present data say it "will collapse not earlier than t_c > 10 billion years from the present moment." A constraint on all four, quoted from [Planck18] Sec. 7.4.1: "a single minimally-coupled canonical scalar field (quintessence) cannot cross w = −1 (Vikman 2005)"; a crossing needs two fields or modified gravity.

## 4. Does anything mainstream say dark energy goes to zero?

*Ours:* no measurement does. The data allow it (w0 > −1 today is a falling density; the thawing class ends at zero) and equally allow a freezing end at a nonzero floor. Who proposes zero: thawing quintessence (de Souza et al.), and the model this record builds on. [T24], quoted (abstract): "Dark energy is due to the ground-state curvature at finite coupling"; Sec. III: "we have a transition from infinite curvature at the critical point to a flat Euclidean plane" at g = 0, and "When the coupling is reduced, both the ultraviolet scale and the curvature decrease"; Sec. IV: "it is possible to identify 1/g itself with universal time", "the curvature decreases from extremely high values towards zero", and the perceived acceleration "actually tends to zero as the ground state curvature becomes smaller and smaller at tiny couplings." Paraphrased: the paper never writes "dark energy vanishes"; the curvature goes to zero as the coupling runs, and the acceleration with it. **The arXiv number "2208.XXXX" could not be confirmed:** his 2024 dark energy paper is arXiv:2409.09385 ([T24]); his 2022 cosmological-constant paper is [T22], arXiv:2112.03778. (arXiv:2512.11712, "Cosmic Acceleration from Quantum Gravity", is by Marchetti, Ladstätter and Oriti, not him.)

## 5. What would settle the sign, and when

Quoted (DESI slides, December 2025, slide 10): "DESI Data Release 3 (DR3) will be available late 2026/early 2027. Main survey observations from May 2021 until April 2024." Euclid's Wide Survey covers "roughly 15,000 square degrees of extragalactic sky on six years" (quoted, Scaramella et al.); ESA's page (15 June 2026) puts a first release of about 1900 deg² in November 2026 and the complete DR1 with clustering and lensing products in "mid-2027" (quoted). Truong et al. 2026 propose a DESI follow-up of Rubin supernovae "capable of measuring dynamical dark energy at the >5σ level with just one year of data" (quoted), from 2027, if the current best fit persists. *Ours:* these test the sign of the drift; the far future is beyond all of them, since every probe looks backward.

## What this bears on in the record

*Ours, unverified.* The owner's first hypothesis, a background curvature settling from above toward a small nonzero constant, is the freezing shape: w > −1 now, the density falling to a floor. Bearing on it: w0 > −1 in every DESI combination (DR2: −0.84 to −0.42); nothing measured fixes the floor, and the preferred phantom past (w < −1 before now) fits a settling-from-above story only if the settling began recently. Her second hypothesis, a curvature growing slowly over a universe's life from antimatter shells left by dissolving black holes, is a rising density, w < −1. Bearing on it: the phantom past the data prefer (Lodha et al.'s crossing) and, against it, w0 > −1 today at 2 to 4σ in the supernova combinations, which says any rise has already stopped. The published model's curvature falling to zero ([T24]) is the thawing shape, w > −1 and falling; the same w0 > −1 bears on it, and the phantom past bears against it as against any single-field fall. All three face a two-parameter fit whose significance moves with the supernova sample, and none has been written as a w(z) that could be scored.
