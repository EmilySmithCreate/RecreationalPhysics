Terms for the owner: a **branch** is one term of a sum of orthogonal (mutually exclusive) states that no longer interfere; a **measure** here is a non-negative weight on branches; **first-person** probabilities are "what will *we* see", **third-person** probabilities are "what the universe is like"; a **reference class** is the set of things one imagines oneself drawn from.

## 1. Everett (1957)

**What the paper does.** Drops the collapse postulate ("Process 1": a jump to eigenstate \(\varphi_j\) with probability \(|(\psi,\varphi_j)|^2\)) and keeps only the wave equation ("Process 2"), Sec. 2. Postulates that "a wave function that obeys a linear wave equation everywhere and at all times supplies a complete mathematical model for every isolated physical system without exception" (Sec. 3). Observers are modeled as physical systems with memories (Sec. 5).

**Key definitions and equations (numbering as in the paper).**

- Composite state and relative state, Sec. 4:
  \[
  \psi^S=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}\,\xi^{S_1}_i\,\eta^{S_2}_j \quad (1), \qquad
  \psi(S_2;\mathrm{rel}\,\xi_k,S_1)=N_k\sum_j a_{kj}\,\eta^{S_2}_j \quad (2), \qquad
  \psi^S=\sum_i \frac{1}{N_i}\,\xi^{S_1}_i\,\psi(S_2;\mathrm{rel}\,\xi_i,S_1) \quad (3).
  \]
  "There does not, in general, exist anything like a single state for one subsystem of a composite system" (Sec. 4). The relative state is the conditional distribution of the conventional theory, now without a collapse.
- Memory sequence notation, Sec. 5, Eq. (9): \(\psi^O_{[A,B,\cdots,C]}\), the symbols "ordered time-wise", standing for "punches in a paper tape, impressions on a magnetic reel, configurations of a relay switching circuit, or even configurations of brain cells".
- A "good observation", Eqs. (10)-(11): \(\varphi_i\psi^O_{[\ldots]}\to\varphi_i\psi^O_{[\ldots\alpha_i]}\) (eigenstates left unchanged; the memory records \(\alpha_i\)). By linearity, Eq. (12): \(\psi^{S+O\prime}=\sum_i a_i\varphi_i\psi^O_{[\ldots\alpha_i]}\).
- Rule 1 (Eq. 15) and Rule 2 (Eq. 17): a measurement on \(S_1\) sends \(\psi^{S_1}\cdots\psi^O_{[\ldots]}\to\sum_i a_i\varphi^{S_1}_i\cdots\psi^O_{[\ldots\alpha_i]}\), \(a_i=(\varphi^{S_1}_i,\psi^{S_1})\); Rule 1 "may be applied separately to each element of a superposition", giving \(\sum_{i,j}a_ib_j\varphi^{S_1}_i\eta^{S_2}_j\cdots\psi^O_{[\ldots\alpha_i,\beta_j]}\).
- Repeatability, Eq. (19): \(\sum_i a_i\varphi_i\psi^O_{[\ldots\alpha_i,\alpha_i]}\); a redetermination always agrees with the earlier record "in which the earlier memory coincides with the later, i.e., the memory states are correlated".
- After \(r\) measurements on identically prepared systems, Eqs. (23)-(24): a superposition of elements \(\psi'_{ij\cdots k}=\varphi^{S_1}_i\varphi^{S_2}_j\cdots\varphi^{S_r}_k\,\psi^{S_{r+1}}\cdots\psi^{S_n}\,\psi^O_{[\alpha^1_i,\alpha^2_j,\cdots\alpha^r_k]}\), "each of which describes the observer with a definite memory sequence". The memory "trajectory" is "not a linear sequence of memory configurations, but a branching tree, with all possible outcomes existing simultaneously".
- **The measure and the argument for the squared amplitude**, Eqs. (25)-(31). He wants a positive function \(m\) of the coefficients of a superposition of normalized orthogonal states, and argues it can depend only on the amplitude, \(m(a_i)=m(|a_i|)\), because phases are not determinable. Then the **additivity requirement**: regarding a subset as one element,
  \[
  \alpha\varphi'=\sum_{i=1}^n a_i\varphi_i \quad (25),\qquad m(\alpha)=\sum_{i=1}^n m(a_i) \quad (26).
  \]
  With \(|\alpha|=(\sum a_i^*a_i)^{1/2}\) and \(u_i=|a_i|\), (26) becomes \(m\big((\sum u_i^2)^{1/2}\big)=\sum m(u_i)\) (27); defining \(g(x)=m(\sqrt x)\) (28) gives \(g(\sum u_i^2)=\sum g(u_i^2)\) (29), so \(g\) is linear, \(g(x)=cx\) (30), and
  \[
  m(a_i)=c\,u_i^2=c\,a_i^*a_i \quad (31).
  \]
  His justification for (26) is the classical "conservation of probability" (Liouville) analogy: "we demand that the measure assigned to a trajectory at one time shall equal the sum of the measures of its separate branches at a later time. This is precisely the additivity requirement". He says the procedure is "quite as justified as that of classical statistical mechanics", no more.
- The measure on a memory sequence is a **product**, Eqs. (33)-(34): \(M_{ij\cdots k}=(a_ia_j\cdots a_k)^*(a_ia_j\cdots a_k)=M_iM_j\cdots M_k\), \(M_l=a_l^*a_l\). Hence, by the law of large numbers, "except for a set of memory sequences of measure nearly zero, the averages of any functions over a memory sequence can be calculated approximately by the use of the independent probabilities given by Process 1"; exact in the infinite limit.
- Several observers, Sec. 5, points 1-3: communication is "an interaction which correlates parts of the memory configuration of one observer with another"; observers who measure the same quantity and communicate "find that they are in agreement" in every element of the final superposition; an intervening non-commuting measurement by a second observer destroys the one-to-one correlation (the uncertainty principle); EPR correlations are "easily investigated".
- Note added in proof: "there is no such transition [from possible to actual] ... all elements of a superposition (all 'branches') are 'actual,' none any more 'real' than the rest ... no observer will ever be aware of any 'splitting' process."

**Stated assumptions and limitations, in his words where short.** Measurements are ideal ("good") observations that leave eigenstates unchanged; the measure is derived for superpositions of normalized orthogonal states; "In any familiar memory device the branching does not continue indefinitely, but must stop at a point limited by the capacity of the memory"; the probabilistic conclusions hold "in almost all of the observer states" by his measure, and "all results should be translated back to measure theoretic language". He claims only that statistical assertions are "deducible (in the present sense)".

**Criticisms known to this note.** (i) The SEP entry (summary only) reports that it is "left to the reader" to assume one's own records are typical by Everett's measure, and that it is "typically false that most relative sequences by count will exhibit the standard quantum statistics", so the choice of measure does all the work; it also notes the typicality measure is not obviously a probability (SEP Secs. 5.4, 6). (ii) Kent (2014, Introduction) lists Everett-derived programs as "generally incompatible attempts" with "problems with all of these". (iii) General knowledge, to verify: the preferred-basis problem (which decomposition defines the branches) is not addressed in the 1957 paper; the environment-induced decoherence answer came later (Zurek, 1981 onward; cited by Sebens-Carroll Sec. 2.1). (iv) The 1957 paper has no decoherence functional and no coarse graining: branches are exact orthogonal terms produced by ideal measurements.

## 2. Sebens and Carroll (2014/2018)

**Claim.** Probability enters Everettian quantum mechanics as **self-locating uncertainty** in the "post-measurement pre-observation" period (after decoherence, before the observer registers the outcome), following Vaidman; branch counting is wrong; a single epistemic principle (ESP) yields the Born rule (abstract; Sec. 1).

**Key definitions and equations (numbering as in arXiv v3).**

- Measurement and decoherence, Eqs. (4)-(6): \(|\Psi(t_4)\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}|{\uparrow}\rangle_A|{\uparrow}\rangle_D|{\uparrow_z}\rangle_a|E_1\rangle+\frac{1}{\sqrt2}|{\downarrow}\rangle_A|{\downarrow}\rangle_D|{\downarrow_z}\rangle_a|E_2\rangle\); the reduced density matrix \(\hat\rho_{ADa}(t_4)\) has off-diagonal terms proportional to \(\langle E_1|E_2\rangle\), which they set to zero ("in realistic cases \(\langle E_1|E_2\rangle\) is very close to zero"). Branches of a *reduced* density matrix are distinguished from branches of the universal wave function (Sec. 2.1).
- Indifference (Elga; Manley's version), Eq. (10): \(P(H|U)=N_{UH}/N_U\). Applied naively it gives branch counting, Eq. (12): \(P_{t_3}(\mathrm{down})=\tfrac13\) against the Born value \(\tfrac12\), Eq. (11).
- **The step where branch counting is rejected**, Sec. 2.4: in "Once-or-Twice", between \(t_2\) and \(t_3\) only Bob's particle is measured; Alice's reduced state \(\hat\rho_{AD}(t_2)=\tfrac12|R\rangle_A|{\uparrow}\rangle_D\langle{\uparrow}|_D\langle R|_A+\tfrac12|R\rangle_A|{\downarrow}\rangle_D\langle{\downarrow}|_D\langle R|_A\) (13) is unchanged, yet Indifference moves her credence from 1/2 to 1/3. "Two intuitive constraints come into conflict: Indifference, and the belief that Alice's probabilities should be unaffected by changes in the state of her environment. We recommend rejecting the former in favor of the latter." Appendix A says diachronic inconsistency (Wallace) is *not* the right objection.
- **ESP**, Sec. 3.1: for a set \(S\) of subsystems containing every copy of the agent, \(P(X|U)=P(X|S)\) (14). **ESP-QM**, Eq. (15): \(P(O_i|\Psi)=P(O_i|\hat\rho_{AD})\): the credence for a recorded outcome depends only on the agent+detector reduced density matrix.
- Born rule, equal amplitudes, Sec. 3.2, Eqs. (16)-(22): two wirings of a second display (17), (18) with the same \(\hat\rho_{AD_1}\) (19) and the same \(\hat\rho_{AD_2}\); ESP-QM gives \(P(\downarrow|\Psi_1)=P(\downarrow|\Psi_2)\) (20), \(P(\heartsuit|\Psi_1)=P(\heartsuit|\Psi_2)\) (21), branch identities (22), hence \(P(\uparrow|\Psi_1)=P(\downarrow|\Psi_1)=\tfrac12\). Unequal case \(\sqrt{2/3},\sqrt{1/3}\), Eqs. (24)-(32): three displays wired two ways (26) give \(P(\downarrow|\Psi_\alpha)=\tfrac13\), \(P(\uparrow|\Psi_\alpha)=\tfrac23\) (32). General rational case, Appendix C, Eqs. (51)-(59): split each environment state into \(c_k^2\) orthogonal pieces, \(|E_k\rangle\to\frac{1}{c_k}\{|E'_{k,1}\rangle+\cdots+|E'_{k,c_k^2}\rangle\}\) (52), reaching an equal-amplitude form (53); irrational amplitudes by continuity. Their own gloss: "write the state vector as a sum of orthogonal vectors with equal amplitudes by unitarily transforming the environment. Then (and only then), ESP justifies according equal credence to each such basis vector. In that sense, the Born rule is simply a matter of counting."
- **Strong ESP**, Eq. (33): \(P(X|U)=P(M_X(X)|U')\) under spatial, rotational or temporal relocation of the subsystems; it entails Indifference for classical duplicates (34).
- Mixed uncertainty, Secs. 4.2-4.3: Two-Branch-Beauty gives \(P(M_\uparrow)=P(M_\downarrow)\) (35), \(P(M_\uparrow)=P(T_\uparrow)\) (36), so \(P(\uparrow)=\tfrac23\) although the branch weight is 1/2: "the Born rule is perfectly correct for quantifying the uncertainty brought about by quantum measurement. However, there is also uncertainty arising from the duplication of Beauty's experiences". General rule: for observers \(O_i\) with \(|\Psi(t_i)\rangle=\alpha_i|\phi_i\rangle+\beta_i|\phi_i^\perp\rangle\) (37),
  \[
  w_i=|\alpha_i|^2 \quad (38),\qquad P(O_i|U)=\frac{w_i}{\sum_j w_j} \quad (39),\qquad P(\uparrow_z|U)=\sum_i P(O_i|U)\,|\gamma_i|^2 \quad (41),
  \]
  and for a branch whose observer density grows as \(n(t)\propto e^{\omega t}\),
  \[
  P(B|U)\propto\int dt\,|\alpha(t)|^2\,n(t) \quad (42),
  \]
  finite only if the amplitude decays faster than \(\omega/2\). "The weights \(\{w_i\}\) will not in general sum to unity."

**Stated assumptions and limitations (their words where short).** Branching is taken to happen "throughout the wave function whenever it happens anywhere" (Sec. 2.2; footnote 6 says the proof needs it). Branch number is "not well-defined" in realistic cases (Sec. 2.3; footnote 12). "We believe the principle to be well-motivated but not established beyond any doubt, and thus our derivation is provisional" (end of Sec. 3.2). Reduced density matrices as subsystem states need further justification (Appendix B). Observers in Sec. 4.3 are treated "under the fiction that the observers can be easily distinguished, labeled, and counted" (footnote 26). Pre-measurement uncertainty is conceded absent (Sec. 5). The conclusion lists four open items, including "the assumption in Sec. 2.2 that branching happens globally could be further defended".

**Criticisms.**
- **Kent (2014), read in full.** Does not attack the Born-rule derivation directly; attacks whether there is anything to be uncertain about. (a) Whether the post-measurement pre-observation state "does represent two copies of an observer" rather than one observer who has not yet interacted (his Eqs. (2) vs (3)); (b) since branch choice is "not objective, but rather an arbitrary mathematical choice", there is "no fixed number of copies of observers or branches", so no fact to be uncertain of; (c) relativity: splitting "the instant a measurement interaction is completed ... with respect to which reference frame?", plus recoherence and incomplete decoherence; (d) on ESP-QM, the event "the detector D having registered \(O_i\)" must mean "on my branch", which returns to the problems above. Conclusion: "it is hard to know whether a consistent ontology of the form Sebens and Carroll require exists at all"; an objective branching rule (Riedel-Zurek-Zwolak) would make many worlds "superfluous".
- **Dawid and Thebault (2015), read in part.** It **does not mention Sebens and Carroll** (full-text search: no hit). Its target is the Deutsch-Wallace program. Charge I1 (Sec. 3.2): the Everettian "must simultaneously assume: (i) that the Born rule provides a probability measure valid with respect to the entire wave function in order to appeal to the decoherence effects necessary to establish the Everettian ontology; and (ii) that there is no probability measure available whose viability extends beyond semi-classical branches which contain (or potentially contain) agents." Charge I2: eliminating off-diagonal elements for low Born weight while keeping low-weight distinct states is "ontological prejudice". *Ours, unverified:* I1 and I2 apply in kind to any derivation that first uses decoherence to define branches, Sebens-Carroll's included; Sebens-Carroll's Appendix B is their reply to this circularity family (citing Zeh 1997; Baker 2007; Schlosshauer 2007 Sec. 8.2.2; Kent 2010 Sec. 5.4).
- General knowledge, to verify: McQueen and Vaidman (2019, Stud. Hist. Phil. Mod. Phys.) defend the self-location account against Kent and others; the Born-rule-from-symmetry family (Zurek's envariance, Deutsch-Wallace decision theory) is reviewed by Sebens-Carroll Sec. 6 with their own objections.

## 3. Srednicki and Hartle (2010)

**Claim.** In a very large universe our data \(D_0\) is "exactly replicated elsewhere many times", so a theory \(T\) (quantum state plus dynamics) yields only **third-person** probabilities; **first-person** predictions need a further assumed distribution over which copy we are, the **xerographic distribution** \(\xi_A\); what is tested is the framework \((T,\xi)\) (abstract; Secs. II-III).

**Key definitions and equations (numbering as in arXiv v3).**
- Sec. III: \(N\) copies of \(D_0\) at locations \(x_A\); \(\xi_A\) = probability that we are the copy at \(x_A\). "A natural assumption is that we are typical of the instances of \(D_0\); this implies a uniform xerographic distribution \(\xi_A=1/N\). But it is also possible to assume that we are atypical." Uniform-on-a-class distributions for wider classes (all observational situations, all IGUSes in the galaxy or universe) are also allowed: "Each of these typicality notions corresponds to a xerographic distribution that is uniform on the class involved." The xerographic distribution "does not constitute a modification of the laws of the quantum mechanics of closed systems. The usual laws ... (including Born's rule) ... apply to third-person probabilities. First-person probabilities are the new feature." It "operates at the classical level" (footnote 4).
- Bayes on frameworks, Eq. (4.1): \(P^{(1p)}(T,\xi|D_0)=\dfrac{P^{(1p)}(D_0|T,\xi)P(T,\xi)}{\sum_{(T,\xi)}P^{(1p)}(D_0|T,\xi)P(T,\xi)}\), with factorized priors \(P(T,\xi)=P_{xd}(\xi)P_{th}(T)\) (4.2).
- Red/blue model, Sec. V: third-person likelihood \(P(E,R|T)=1-(1-p_E)^{N_R(T)}\) (5.1); typical-in-our-data \(\xi^{typD}=1/n_R\) (5.2) gives the same likelihood (5.5); typical-among-all-observers \(\xi^{typO}_A=1/n_O\) (5.6) gives \(P^{(1p)}(R|E,\xi^{typO},T)=N_R(T)/N\) (5.7a) and \(P^{(1p)}(E,R|\xi^{typO},T)=\frac{N_R(T)}{N}[1-(1-p_E)^N]\) (5.8); likelihood ratio \(N/N_R(SR)>1\) (5.9); competing \(\xi\)'s for one theory (5.10); infinite case through fractions \(f_R(T)\) (5.11).
- **The first-person prediction formula**, Eq. (6.1): \(P^{(1p)}(q_1|T,\xi,D_0)=\sum_A \xi_A\,P(q_1@A\,|\,\xi,T,D_0@A)\). "Without an assumed xerographic distribution, no prediction whatsoever can be made about what 'we' will see in the future."
- Appendix B, Eqs. (B1)-(B5): the general recipe. Third-person probability of a configuration \(P(n_O,n_R|T)=\binom{N_R}{n_R}\binom{N-N_R}{n_O-n_R}p_E^{n_O}(1-p_E)^{N-n_O}\) (B1); \(\xi\) **normalized within each configuration**, \(\sum_{A=1}^{n_O}\xi^{typO}_A=1\) (B3); first-person likelihood \(=\sum_{\text{configs}}\sum_A \xi_A P(\text{config}|T)\) (B4). "Calculate the third-person probabilities for a configuration ...; multiply by the xerographic distribution; sum over the possible configurations."
- Note on notation: the handoff's \(p(D|H,\xi)\) is not this paper's notation; here it is \(P^{(1p)}(D_0|T,\xi)\). The authors' follow-up (arXiv:1004.3816, their ref. [4], not read) may use the other symbols (to verify).

**Stated assumptions and limitations.** A fixed quasiclassical coarse graining is assumed (footnote 2). The HSI "is assumed to exist at a unique location". Boltzmann brains (Sec. VII.A): a uniform \(\xi\) is not predictive; choosing \(\xi\) "nonzero only for locations sufficiently close to a Big Bang" makes the framework predictive; "The answer to the question 'How do we know that we are not BBs?' is this: we do not know." Measures (Sec. VIII): solving the cosmological measure problem "does not remove the need to choose a xerographic distribution". Appendix A: both Page's proposals and their own examples "rely on an assumption of classical spacetime to make notions of location meaningful. But classical spacetime is neither fundamental nor inevitable in a quantum theory of gravity." They disagree with Page that "Born's rule dies".

**Criticisms known to this note.** Page's work (their Appendix A; Page 2007 "Typicality Defended", cited by Sebens-Carroll) argues the quantum state alone is insufficient and proposes replacements for Born's rule; Srednicki-Hartle reply that Born's rule stands for third-person probabilities. Sebens-Carroll Sec. 4.3 claim their Strong ESP "resolves" Page's ambiguity by a specific rule (39), which fixes what Srednicki-Hartle leave as a free assumption (ours: that is a disagreement about whether \(\xi\) is free or derivable from a rationality principle).

## 4. Hartle and Srednicki (2007)

**Claim.** Assuming we are typical observers commits a **selection fallacy** when nothing has selected us; typicality may be enforced only through priors, and then only explicitly (abstract; Sec. IV).

**Key definitions and equations (numbering as in arXiv v3).**
- Bayes, Eq. (1.1): \(P(T_i|D)=P(D|T_i)P(T_i)/\sum_i P(D|T_i)P(T_i)\). "Data are the domain of facts, likelihoods are the domain of logical deduction, and the priors are the domain of theoretical prejudice" (Sec. VII).
- "We" is the information gathering and utilizing system (IGUS) doing the analysis, the human scientific IGUS (HSI) (Sec. III). "We are not a disembodied entity that was randomly selected to have a particular physical description; instead, we are the meaning of a transcription of 'we' into the language of physical theory" (Sec. IV).
- **The Jovian example**, Secs. II and IV. Theory A: intelligent Jovians exist in great numbers; theory B: none. Correct likelihood, Eq. (4.1): \(P(D|T_i)=\sum_{J=0}^\infty P(H^*,J|T_i)\), independent of \(J\) because we have not observed Jovians. The fallacious likelihood, Eq. (4.2): \(P(D|T_i)=\sum_J \frac{H^*}{H^*+J}P(H^*,J|T_i)\), which "disfavor[s] theories that predict \(H\ll J\)". The factor "would be correct if we (or some outside agency) had performed an experiment that had (somehow) selected a single intelligent being in the solar system"; it is refuted by noting that two human authors do not each contribute a factor, "because the probability that one of us is human is obviously not independent of the status of the other." Typicality may be put in priors instead, e.g. \(P(T_i)\propto\bar H_i/(\bar H_i+\bar J_i)\), but that "simply represents an unsupported (by data) personal preference".
- Red/blue model, Sec. V, Eqs. (5.1)-(5.5): \(P(E,R|T)=1-(1-p_E)^{N_R(T)}\); when \(p_E\ll1/N\), \(P(AR|E,R)=1/(1+f_R)\) (5.3); the number of observers seeing blue "must be summed over" (5.5). "Even though the typical observing system in the SR theory is observing blue, our data provides no evidence that we are typical."
- Conclusions listed in the Introduction include: "Cosmological models that predict that at least one instance of our data exists (with probability one) somewhere in spacetime are indistinguishable no matter how many other exact copies of these data exist", and "In a fundamental theory of quantum cosmology, there is no need for any assumption of typicality ... However typicality, and the measures to implement it, may be implied by the fundamental theory."

**Stated assumptions and limitations.** The paper "does not criticize such assumptions when they are clearly stated" (type-2 assumptions such as observers proportional to galaxies); models are "highly idealized"; "In absence of a manageable candidate for such a theory, it can be useful to try to guess such measures" (Sec. VI). Footnote 2: Page says his typicality factors arise from his own quantum mechanics of conscious perceptions, not discussed.

**Criticisms known to this note.** Page's reply "Typicality Defended" (arXiv:0707.4169; not read; general knowledge, to verify) argues typicality is needed to test theories at all. The 2010 paper (Sec. IV) itself concedes that pure third-person likelihoods "approach unity for any theory that is consistent with our data" in a large universe, so the 2007 position leaves theories undiscriminated there; the xerographic distribution is their repair. General knowledge, to verify: this is one side of the self-sampling (SSA) versus self-indication (SIA) debate (Bostrom 2002), which neither paper names in those terms.

## 6. What contradicts or complicates the handoff

**(a) Everett's relative-state formulation already contains the "nested compatible set with nonincreasing measure" idea.** Yes, in his setting. Eqs. (23)-(24) give the branching tree of memory sequences; Eqs. (33)-(34) make the measure of a sequence a product of per-record factors, each at most one, so a longer retained record has measure no greater than its prefix; Eq. (26) is the statement that the total measure is conserved under branching, derived from (and justifying) the squared-amplitude choice. The handoff's Sec. 2 therefore restates Everett's Sec. 5 in set language, generalized from ideal projective measurements with exact orthogonal branches to a decoherent family with approximate decoherence. The generalization is the decoherent-histories program's (Gell-Mann and Hartle), not the handoff's. Two differences remain real: Everett's measure is *derived* from additivity, while the handoff takes \(p_\alpha=D(\alpha,\alpha)\) as given by the formalism; and Everett's records are exact, so "erasure" and "redefinition of the family" (handoff Sec. 3) have no counterpart in 1957 beyond his remark that memory capacity bounds the branching. Under the handoff's ninth methodological check, Sec. 2 should cite Everett (1957) Sec. 5, Eqs. (26) and (33)-(34), as the origin.

**(b) Is \(F(t_0)\) exactly a xerographic distribution in Srednicki-Hartle's sense? Not exactly.** Srednicki-Hartle's \(\xi_A\) is normalized within each third-person configuration (Eq. B3) and then averaged with the third-person probabilities (Eq. B4): first-person probability \(=\sum_{\text{config}}P(\text{config}|T)\times(\text{fraction of copies in that config satisfying the claim})\). The handoff's \(F(t_0)=\sum_\alpha p_\alpha N_\alpha(\le t_0)\big/\sum_\alpha p_\alpha N_\alpha^{\rm tot}\) normalizes *globally*, across histories. The two coincide only when every history with nonzero \(p_\alpha\) holds the same total number of observer-moments; otherwise the handoff's rule gives histories with more observer-moments more weight. The handoff's form is instead Sebens-Carroll's Eq. (39) (weights "will not in general sum to unity", normalized by their sum) and Eq. (42). So: \(F(t_0)\) is a uniform-on-observer-moments self-location rule of the Sebens-Carroll kind; it is a Srednicki-Hartle xerographic distribution only after the two normalizations are reconciled, and the handoff should say which it uses and compute both. *Ours, unverified:* the difference is the one that in anthropic literature separates "average over histories, then sample within" from "sample over all observer-moments pooled across histories" (general knowledge, to verify; Bostrom's SSA versus SIA is the usual naming).

**(c) Hartle-Srednicki's selection fallacy bears on how \(F(t_0)\) may be used.** Computing today's percentile under a declared \(\xi\) is descriptive and allowed (Srednicki-Hartle Sec. III). Using "we are early" or "we are late" as *evidence* for a measure or a theory is Eq. (4.2) of Hartle-Srednicki unless the typicality assumption is placed in the prior and competed (Srednicki-Hartle Eqs. 4.1-4.2). The handoff's Stage F item (6) ("today's percentile under each explicit population/typicality scenario") is consistent with this; a write-up must not promote the percentile to a test.

**(d) Sebens-Carroll's Two-Branch-Beauty complicates "Born weight is the measure of an observer-moment".** When one branch contains two indistinguishable observer-moments of the same person, Strong ESP gives first-person credence 2/3 to that branch against its Born weight 1/2 (Sec. 4.2). The handoff's \(W(t)\) already multiplies \(p_\alpha\) by an observer-moment rate, which is the same move; it should be stated as the Sebens-Carroll rule (39), and the handoff's commitment 5 ("amplitudes matter") should be read as "amplitudes matter *and* within-branch multiplicity matters", which Kent disputes at the root (no well-defined copies to count).

**(e) Kent's objections transfer to the handoff's Stage B and D models.** A register-based model will exhibit "two copies" only by the modeler's choice of decomposition (Kent's Eqs. (2) vs (3)). Stage B should therefore report its results for more than one system/environment split, and Stage C's "small consistent family" is a choice, as the handoff's Sec. 3 already warns. Dawid-Thebault's I1 (Born weights presupposed as probabilities when decoherence is used to define branches) applies to any such model; the handoff's Sec. 3 ("Do not treat \(\sum c_i|h_i\rangle\) as a rigorous generic decomposition") is the right posture but does not escape I1.

**(f) Srednicki-Hartle's classical-spacetime caveat limits Stage E.** Locations, and therefore cosmic time \(t\), presuppose a quasiclassical spacetime (their App. A and footnote 2). The handoff's commitment 7 and its finite comoving region are then statements inside one quasiclassical realm, which is what the handoff's eighth methodological check says.

**(g) Two small wording corrections for the handoff.** "Do not count 'worlds'" should read "do not count decoherence-defined branches", since Sebens-Carroll's derivation is explicitly a count of equal-amplitude components after an environment unitary (end of Sec. 3.2; App. C). And the reading-list note calling Sebens-Carroll's derivation "a debated argument" is right; the debate recorded here is Kent (2014) plus the circularity family (Zeh, Baker, Schlosshauer, Kent 2010) that Sebens-Carroll answer in App. B; Dawid-Thebault do not address them.

**What else in this repository these papers bear on (ours, one line).** VISION Updates 26 and 43 (the author's strange-loop measure, counting observers who evolved per turn) is a typicality choice of the kind Srednicki-Hartle call a xerographic distribution and Hartle-Srednicki say must sit in the prior, declared and competed; it is not derivable from the toy or from Born's rule.
