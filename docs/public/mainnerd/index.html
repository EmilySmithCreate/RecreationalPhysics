The curled torus burps

A small push, a large release, and the question of what survives. By Emily Smith.

The curled torus burps is a computational physics paper about a hypothetical sharp change in which flat space emerges from something more curled up. It uses a toy model of space as a network of dots and links, a close cousin of the published model called combinatorial quantum gravity. In that toy, a tube (a torus curled to four steps around) sits above the flat sheet in energy and is stable for now. The paper counts what it costs to start the change (12 units at the main setting, for every size of tube tested), predicts how long the tube waits by counting its exits, watches the flat arrangement spread, seals the system to see where the released energy (the burp) goes, and counts what is left behind (about one four-dot scrap). A toy can show that a mechanism is possible or impossible within a class of models; that is the whole claim.
