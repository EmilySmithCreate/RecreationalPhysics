What the burp leaves behind

How many scraps a sharp change leaves, and which of them last. By Emily Smith.

What the burp leaves behind is the second paper in a series about a hypothetical sharp change in which space emerges from something more curled up. It works in a toy model of space as a network of dots and links. The toy is the paper's own: a close cousin of the published model called combinatorial quantum gravity, with one knob turned above the published value, and not that model itself. In the toy a curled tube changes all at once into a wider tube and gives off energy (the burp), and the change leaves small scraps. This paper counts them and reads what they are. One start leaves about one scrap, whatever the size of the tube. More starts leave more, but fewer than one each. The common scrap, a curled loop of four dots, heals away in warm surroundings and is frozen in when its surroundings cool. Cooled slowly enough, nearly all of them heal, yet most of the leftover energy stays, held by two rarer kinds of scrap that no cooling in these runs removed. Three predictions written down beforehand failed, and they are reported. A toy can show that a mechanism is possible or impossible within a class of models; that is the whole claim.

POST No. 01 · 10 OCTOBER 2026 · PHYSICS

The curled torus burps

A small push, a large release, and the question of what survives. By Emily Smith.

The curled torus burps is a computational physics paper about a hypothetical sharp change in which flat space emerges from something more curled up. It uses a toy model of space as a network of dots and links. The toy is the paper's own: a close cousin of the published model called combinatorial quantum gravity, with one knob turned above the published value, and not that model itself. In the paper's toy, a tube (a torus curled to four steps around) sits above the flat arrangement in energy and is stable for now. The paper counts what it costs to start the change (12 units at the main setting, for every size of tube tested), predicts how long the tube waits by counting its exits, watches the flat arrangement spread, seals the system to see where the released energy (the burp) goes, and counts what is left behind (about one four-dot scrap). It also reads what the tube turns into: the tight curl is gone and the energy is released, but the result is still a narrow tube, six steps around, and a second large direction does not appear. A toy can show that a mechanism is possible or impossible within a class of models; that is the whole claim.
