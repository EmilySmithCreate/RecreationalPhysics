The Curling Ladder

What it costs to open each curled direction, what each opening gives back, and where our experiments have been, for two to five directions.

A rungEach point has D directions. A direction curled into a loop of four points is closed; X is the top rung, every direction curled, and flat space is the bottom.
The releaseStepping down one rung gives back a = 4(λ − 1) units per point, the same at every rung. This is exact in the model.
The wallThe wall is the cheapest single rewiring out of a rung, in the model's energy units. A push that size starts the change. Below zero means the rung falls by itself.
The coupling gg acts as a temperature in runs held at fixed g. The waiting chart uses it; sealed runs have no g, only a push and a bath.

Where the numbers come from

The walls are exact. Every single rewiring out of each rung was listed by brute force (scripts/exact_ladder_d.py, 27 Sep; ASSUMPTIONS O41, O49, O50). A move's cost is −16 ΔS + 4λ ΔX, so each kind of move becomes one straight line in λ. The rung energies were computed on the built tori. A fully curled piece larger than one cube cannot be connected (O85), so the top rung is one cube: 16, 64, 256 or 1,024 points. Named points throughout; interchangeable points make the curled rungs stickier, by a factor of about 960 for the first move out of the six-link cube. Every three-, four- and five-direction number carries VISION Update 24's caveat: the reproduction gate is open.
