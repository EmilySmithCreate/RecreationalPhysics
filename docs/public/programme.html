A phase-changing reality

The programme, and where each piece stands.

Emily Smith · Charlottesville · analysis drafted with Claude (Anthropic)

The claim, in the author's words

Our spacetime is one settled arrangement of something deeper, X: a specific, relatively stable arrangement with its own dimensions, curled where ours are open. X was stable for now, not for ever. A push started the change in one small part of X; it spread as a front, X opened into space, and the change came out as a burp: a fixed release of energy. X's three curled directions are alike, so which one opens first is chance. The first push can come from any kind of energy, most likely the kind there is most of. The first direction to open, red, releases ordinary matter and radiation, the Big Bang's energy, and spends part of it pushing the second direction, blue, open; blue's release is dark matter. The third, green, then opens with no push at all, and its release is dark energy. What the burp does not deliver returns to the black hole, ready to start another burp. What did not convert remained as a scrap. Energy was conserved across the change. The rules of the two phases are their constants and do not change from one generation to the next. Inside the space that forms, black holes are regions that re-curl towards X; one that grows large enough gets the activation energy and burps a new space: the same burp, the same scrap. Every possible arrangement of what a Big Bang produces therefore gets to exist. The quantum world is the set of undetectably different versions of an arrangement, all standing at once, until an interaction makes them distinguishable.

Where each piece stands

The tests use one published model of emergent geometry, Trugenberger's combinatorial quantum gravity, as a toy. Space is a network; X's stand-in is a torus with one direction curled (spherical cows being unavailable); the model's local-term coefficient λ is a knob. Only λ = 1 is his model. The torus results are at λ > 1, where the energy is no longer a curvature, so they are about a close cousin of his model and are never called combinatorial quantum gravity. Anything about gravity is tested in three dimensions only: in two, even Einstein's gravity gives no pull between masses at rest (general knowledge, to verify), so no number measured in 2D is carried into a claim about gravity. A toy model can show that a mechanism is possible or impossible inside a class of models; on its own it cannot show that reality works this way. That is the author's claim, and the model is how she tests its pieces.

The model shows

At λ = 1.25 the curled torus decompactifies (its curled direction opens): it waits, starts at one place, runs as one front with the two orders side by side, releases exactly ε = 4(λ − 1) per point, and leaves about one small relic at every size tried. The wall it must climb is one move costing exactly 12, at every size.

The wall's height is a fact about the energy, like the height of a hill; the waiting and the front are how this simulation walks over it, one local swap at a time, and another way of walking would change them.

Ours, exact; not yet run In the author's view the points are interchangeable. That changes no energy, so the wall, the burp and the relic stay as they are, but it makes the torus stickier: a perfectly symmetric arrangement looks the same from every point, one move breaks that, and counting look-alike arrangements as one then favours staying put, the more so the bigger the space.

How it could be wrong

A slide instead of a switch; a push that grows with size. Neither happened.

From λ = 1.05 to 1.35 the change is stuck for now, runs as one front with the two orders side by side, and releases exactly the predicted burp at every size. It is cleanest near λ = 1 and leaves more defects as λ grows; above 1.35 the curled torus is not stuck. Waiting times follow a formula with nothing fitted over two decades; counted move by move, the first exit arrives when the formula says, and about a third of exits fall back before the front takes hold, at every λ (T22).

The first pre-registered verdict (T8) was inconclusive because one criterion (memoryless waits) was set too tight for thirty decays per setting. A second run with four times the decays and that check sized to the sample (T23) found the waits memoryless in every setting that could be read, like a radioactive nucleus: however long the torus has waited, its chance of going in the next moment is the same. Both of the author's predictions held: sharp across the window, and break-up beginning at the edge. It is still inconclusive by the letter, for a second stated reason: the energy check compared an average over the last stretch of a decay with the exact energy of its final state, and at coupling 1.30 the sheet's own warmth tripped it in a few decays per hundred. One oddity is unexplained: at λ = 1.05 a few waits are far longer than that picture expects.

The third run

T24 read the energy exactly from each decay's final wiring, and that check passed in every one of its 28 cells. Two orders side by side and one front held in every cell, the waits were memoryless in 24 of 28, and the break-up at the edge appeared for the third time, as the author predicted. It is still inconclusive by the letter, for a third reason: in four cells a single extreme wait, one of them 76 times the expected wait, fails the memoryless band, and the rule needs every size sharp. That rare very long wait, now seen at three settings and always at the smaller sizes, is a real feature to study, not a rule to rewrite after the fact. Whether to pre-register a fourth run with a rule sized for it is the author's call.

The author's position (25 September): this arrangement of reality does not require the same thing to happen at every size in every instance; it has to happen at one size and lead to the trillion new strange loops. The every-size rule was ours, a bar for a mechanism claim; a rule written her way (a majority of instances at some size go sharply, stragglers reported and not counted against) is proposed for any fourth run, not applied to the three already read.

The model shows

The author's rule is the published energy, the total curvature, unchanged, plus one term: every link curled tight costs 4(λ − 1) extra. The burp per point is exactly that cost, ε = 4(λ − 1). At λ = 1, the published model, curling is free and nothing is released; below 1 the change runs the wrong way. The author's value is about 1.25 in 2D; in 3D her fully curled X is stuck only for 1 < λ < 1.2 (piece 5). These are different models asking different questions, not rival values for one model.

The author's position: the curling cost is a fact of X's arrangement, a line in F, perhaps from the three directions being intertwined. It is a constant of our loop, and it still exists in spacetime, where it is what a black hole pays to re-curl space. Its value is simply whatever reality has: the one that gives the biggest loop with the longest cold period for matter (piece 12).

Open

Whether a rule that is curvature plus a curling cost gives back Einstein's gravity at large scale (claim 2).

Ours, unverified Where space is smooth nothing is curled tight, so the extra term is zero and the rule there is curvature alone; it acts only on tight curls, the scraps and black holes. The test is in 3D.

Three directions

The author's rule: the three directions are intertwined; they curl together and open together, so one activation releases the whole burp.

In 2D the rule cannot be tested: a gas of fully curled pieces is not stuck at any λ > 1 (exact, O40). In 3D, with six links per point (exact, O41), the fully curled state, the 6-cube, is stuck for now only for 1 < λ < 1.2, and each curled direction holds a third of the burp. Like the 2D torus, this window lies off λ = 1, so results there are about a close cousin of the published model.

Three: what was run

First, reproduce the published 3D curve (Trugenberger 2022, Fig. 3; 500 points; λ = 1) before any 3D result is read. The first attempt did not reproduce it: under either reading of the published axis our curve has a different shape, so no single rescaling fixes it, and by the project's own rule the six-link track is stopped until that is resolved. The likely causes are the published run's protocol, which the paper does not state, and the energy's form in three dimensions, which we derived rather than quoted. An exploratory look the same evening (O45) found that a fast heating leg from a slowly cooled state reproduces the published curve's steepness and plateau, which supports reading it as a heating curve that lags equilibrium, though with two free choices it is suggestive and not a match. The question goes to the model's author. Then: does one push open all three directions at once, and does a curved region pull a relic towards it?

The author's decision (24 September, night): proceed with the three-dimensional work now and return to the reproduction later, on the ground that the six-link code is validated on its own terms (draw for draw with the 2D code at four links, brute force, the exact ladder), while a guess at what the published figure's axis means is tested in parallel (Gate C′). Every 3D result carries that caveat. Measured (T30, T32): the push that starts the change is exactly 16 units at every size from 192 to 512 points, nothing below it ever starts and everything at or above it does, so in three dimensions too the activation does not grow with the space (the author's prediction and ours). Four, with time

The change could be four-dimensional, with time uncurled by it too. Two ways that could go: time as a singleton direction beside the three space directions that are tied together, so that one direction opens on its own and three open together; or all four tied, which the relativity of time suggests, since something moving fast through space has its time run more slowly, so time and space are not separate things.

Ours The model has no time: a fourth direction of a torus is a direction like the others. What it can test is the pattern in which curled directions open: all four together, one alone and then three together, or one at a time. Those are what her two variants and our expectation predict, and they are distinct. Which direction is time, the model cannot say.

Four: what was run

Running (T33): the eight-link model at the smallest size where a flat four-torus exists beside the curled one (2,304 points). Two starts, sealed and given exactly the push their wall costs: a gas of fully curled 8-cubes, no direction singled out; and a torus with three directions curled and one already open. The pattern is read from how many directions are open at each point as the run goes. Her prediction: a tied pattern, all four together or one then three. Ours: one at a time, each rung with its own wall, as in the three-direction case.

It fails, for her picture, if the directions open one at a time.

Read (T33, 25 Sep): in every replica, from the three-curled torus and from the gas of 8-cubes, the push, exactly the cost of the cheapest first move, bought that one move and nothing followed: the bath was left empty and the next step is uphill. So no pattern was seen, tied or one at a time, and the verdict by the letter (NO CASCADE) says more about the push than about the directions. The lesson: in four directions the true cost of starting the change is the height of the pass over several moves, not the first move. The next test gives larger pushes; T39's eight-link cells, written before this was read, carry the same design and are expected to stall too.

Launched (T40, 25 Sep, 12:54 ET): the push scanned from 20 to 160 from the three-curled torus and from a gas of four 8-cubes, to find the push that actually starts the change and read the pattern above it. The author's ledger

Today's shares are about 5% ordinary matter and energy, 27% dark matter and 68% dark energy. Each of X's three curled directions releases the same amount on opening, about 66 units, some 200 in all. The first opening costs 44 to start and nets 22; 12 of that pays the second push, leaving 10: ordinary matter and energy. The second costs 12 and nets 54: dark matter. The third costs nothing and delivers its whole release: dark energy, 132. Which direction goes first is chance; the order sets the kind.

The net energy sets how each kind vibrates. Different vibrations pass through one another and meet only through gravity, which acts on all three; with enough speed or enough of them they sometimes meet, which is how such particles could be found in accelerators and underground laboratories. Only red, the kind that ripped the opening, builds complex molecules and observers: dark energy never compacted into matter, and dark matter stays in clouds, with no dark suns or planets. Her questions: what must λ be for these shares, and does the number of points change it? Is c the same for all three kinds?

Ours, exact Her numbers add to 196 rather than 198; they balance exactly if every unit paid to a wall ends in the third share (10, 54, 134). Her 54 : 10 is 5.4, the measured ratio of dark to ordinary matter, because 44 and 12 were chosen to give it: two numbers fitted to two ratios.

In this model a wall is a hill, not a toll: the energy paid to climb it comes back on the far side, so an opening's net release does not depend on its wall. With every direction costing the same to curl, the three releases are equal at every λ, so no λ gives these shares. The number of points does matter: walls are fixed with size while releases grow with the region, so her 44 of 66 describes a patch of about one 6-cube at λ = 1.25, and over a large region the walls' share goes to nothing. What can make the shares unequal is strain held in the partly open states: the direction tie she decided on 25 September. Fitted to today's budget it keeps X stuck but its first push is never the largest (O70); in its simplest form the walls never fall in her order, and the nearest is 44, none, 4.

Ours, general knowledge, to verify Waves of different frequencies pass through each other anyway, as two colors of light do; what keeps dark matter dark in the standard account is that it has no electric charge. Dark matter stays in diffuse clouds because it cannot radiate energy away and cool, rather than from repulsion. And c is one number for everything in the same spacetime: gravitational waves and light from a 2017 neutron-star merger arrived together after 130 million years. So the energy is (mred + mgreen + mblue)c², with a wave's frequency entering through E = hf.

A charge per kind

Decided by the author (26 September): each kind carries its own kind of charge, which acts only on its own kind. That is why dark matter looks uncharged to us: its charge is blue, and red cannot feel it. All three kinds feel gravity.

Ours The model has no charge and no force at a distance yet, so the charge needs a field to carry it. Three forms are written up for her choice: a field per kind (recommended, together with the gravity field still awaiting her decision), a contact term between points of the same kind, or Kaluza–Klein charge from motion round a still-curled direction. Published relatives (general knowledge and search summaries; the full reading is still owed): dark photons, a hidden charge with its own light, and mirror matter, a hidden copy of our forces. Observations limit both: a strong dark charge with its own light would let dark matter radiate, cool and form disks, which the observed halos rule out.

Compared and contrasted across the triad: red, the kind that ripped the opening, has the charge we see, builds atoms, stars and observers; blue has a charge of its own but stays in clouds, since it cannot shed energy; green, dark energy, the energy of a whole direction held by space itself, does not thin as space grows.

Relationships to test

The author proposes testing a few relationships between the large and the hidden degrees of freedom. On the record so far: the costs in her first order (44, 12, 0) are not reachable with a tie that counts open directions per point; the order 44, none, 12 is (λ = 1.171, κ = 1.735); the counting of look-alike arrangements charges the first opening most, in her order; and her hypothesis that the third opening pays for time as well as for itself (piece 10), which would be an extra cost on the last opening, a knob not yet decided.

Ours, general knowledge Today's shares are not the shares at the beginning: ordinary and dark matter thin together, so their ratio of about 5.4 is as old as they are, but dark energy does not thin, and its share was tiny when the first atoms formed. The ledger's 68 % is today's.
