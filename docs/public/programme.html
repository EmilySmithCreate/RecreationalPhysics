A phase-changing reality

Where each piece of the theory stands, which paper carries it, and what comes next.

Emily Smith · Charlottesville · analysis drafted with Claude (Anthropic)

The claim, in the author's words

  1. X is stable for now.
    • Our spacetime is one settled arrangement of something deeper, X: a specific, relatively stable arrangement with its own dimensions, curled where ours are open.
    • X was stable for now, not for ever. Most likely it was a rod between two curled slabs, one curled right-handed and one left-handed.
    • The two curls are always made together, and curling two directions costs the same as curling one.
    • Underneath, the base has two degrees of freedom, curl and twist; points and links are a way of describing the three-dimensional space that emerges from them.
  2. Energy arrives, and the curl gives way: the burp.
    • Energy arriving in X, as a black hole's infall arrives, raises its excitation.
    • When the stores exceed the barrier, the first curl snaps: the burp, a fixed release of energy. The hold-back drops at once and the second curl follows, a cascade.
    • The opening front is held back by a neutral layer at its edge, like a membrane, curl facing in and uncurl facing out; its tension is why the curl needs a surplus to open.
  3. The burp becomes the Big Bang's hot lump.
    • The release is the hot lump: light, with matter and antimatter in almost equal amounts.
    • As it cooled, nearly every pair of matter and antimatter cancelled into more light, so light carried almost all of the energy.
    • Each burp makes twin daughters, one from each curled direction, alike except that one has a small surplus of matter and the other the matching surplus of antimatter; each gets half the curl energy, and the black hole stays neutral. The release is fast, a far bigger explosion than any bomb.
    • The matter we are made of is that small excess, about one part in a billion. The black hole's spin sorts which way each twin's poles point, so each twin is neutral overall. Every daughter calls her own surplus matter.
    • While the light has nowhere to go it is pressed back into matter, and matter is curved space: tiny loops that are only a relationship, no points, metastable, which build quarks and then protons; once there is room, the light leaves instead.
    • A particle is a twisted strand: matter strands carry signed thirds of a twist along a cube's body diagonals, dark-matter strands signed halves along its face diagonals. Three thirds close into an electron; electric charge is minus the net thirds over three; an antiparticle is the mirror image of its strand. Each third carries a 60° spin-twist of its own, and three make the half turn of spin one half.
  4. The release leaves one way, and X re-forms.
    • The release goes into the daughter; nothing passes from open space back into X.
    • The slab re-forms and goes on accepting energy, so the black hole is a converter, not a store.
  5. What did not convert is the scrap.
    • Small knots of fixed energy, made while the new space is still hot enough to make flaws, then fixed.
    • The scrap is the best current guess for the dark matter; whether it is the dark matter is open. Dark-matter strands carry halves, which cannot hold a photon, so dark matter carries no charge.
  6. Dark energy is the energy of space itself.
    • A candidate source, adopted as a hypothesis to test: the network's own thermal defect density, the few defects any network carries at a coupling above zero.
    • The coupling is the loop's, set by the loop's total energy once every daughter's energy has returned to the network at the end of a turn.
    • Empty space grows at no cost, by whole rows, on a steady clock, and only where space is empty. The overall rate slows while matter clumps and speeds up as empty space takes over; observers are likeliest in between.
  7. The books balance, and the rules hold.
    • Energy is conserved across the change.
    • Curl and uncurl always total zero: a region curled one way carries as much uncurl somewhere else; they appear and disappear in pairs, and the total is zero over one joined surface.
    • The rules of the two phases are their constants and do not change from one generation to the next.
  8. Black holes curl space back, and each one births many daughters.
    • Inside the space that forms, black holes are regions where the parent's own geometry re-curls, two directions still open and one curled tight.
    • Daughter spaces open while the black hole remains in the parent, and every black hole births many new ones, so the tree of universes multiplies each generation. A black hole is bigger inside than it looks: each daughter is the size its mother was at the same time since its own birth.
    • A turn is one universe's own life, from its burp until the last of its black holes has dissolved; the end is many black holes, carried apart by the cold, expanding void, each dissolving alone.
    • A rich daughter, detailed enough for observers to evolve, needs a big, well-fed black hole. The loop is a fractal: very simple rules make the whole pattern, as z → z² + c makes the Mandelbrot set, and how rich it is comes from the coupling and the amount of curl.
  9. Each universe keeps its own clock.
    • Every universe runs an exact copy of one clock, from its own birth to its own heat death; no clock runs across universes.
    • Universes of one setting that differ only by a quantum outcome are counted each as a whole.
  10. The quantum world is versions not yet told apart.
    • An arrangement's undetectably different versions all stand at once, until an interaction makes them distinguishable.
    • The versions are the branches of the many-worlds reading; the rule that lets them cancel is the published one: versions carry amplitudes, and a record in the surroundings keeps them apart.

With those terms it is a close cousin of his model, not his model. A toy can show a mechanism is possible or impossible in a class of models; that reality works this way is the author's claim. 3

The rule: curvature plus a curling cost

Exact · the author's readingPapers 6, 11Updated 26 Oct 2026
Where it stands

Exact The rule is the published model's total curvature plus one term, a cost for every link curled tight. The burp per point is that cost, and where space is smooth the cost is zero, so there the rule is curvature alone. Every direction costs the same to curl and stands behind its own wall.

The author The cost is a constant of our loop. Curl and uncurl must always total zero: a region curled one way carries as much uncurl somewhere else.

Exact Under that rule squares equal points, and the published model's main term is zero. Space built of whole cubes that close up on themselves keeps the rule automatically, so a tight curl breaks it unless uncurl somewhere balances it; the published count of curvature defects on a closed surface balances the same way (Gauss-Bonnet), though the toy supplies a surface only once its squares are read as faces. At 18 points in two directions the rule's moves still reach every allowed arrangement. At six links, clean flat space cannot move under it at all: no single switch keeps the count, and no pair within two links does. Damaged space moves, rarely: frozen order with mobile defects, the published behavior of systems whose moves must keep a total.

Measured The network's own curvature does not set the cost: a curled ring does pay a penalty in the published model's warm background, on curl itself, but the penalty does not grow with the curvature (T78, T79). The cost stays a constant set by hand.

Fable Three switches deep, the count is still zero: out of clean six-link space no sequence of three switches, each within one link of the last, keeps the count and leaves the start (every one of 715 million third switches tried, exact). In two directions three switches across one seam do keep the count and make curl and uncurl together, four of each; but a census of sizes with a held-out size (its prediction written from sides 6, 8 and 10 held 9 of 9 at side 12) shows these are wrap-around effects of a short side, needing (L − 4)/2 parallel switches, so at side 12 three local switches do nothing but rename, and the rule freezes clean two-direction space too once the torus is large. The same move lifted into three directions is legal at every step and loses 12 squares (corrected 27 Oct: the first lift aliased two rows and printed 17); every sequence of up to four switches across one seam, in a window of two or three planes, keeps the count on neither six-link torus. And the picture of the rod's curl as a cap with a balancing skirt cannot be read from the wiring: at a side of four every wrap-around square is also the face of a little cube, so telling a wrap from a face needs the coordinates, which the toy is meant to derive rather than assume. The flat torus is made of exactly one cube per point and keeps her rule; the rod carries two cubes per point, branches, and breaks it.

The author X is opened not by one spark but by energy that keeps arriving until the stores hold the whole cost of the curl. Her newest X is a twin pair: a rod between two curled slabs of opposite hand, curled together, the first opening releasing the hold-back on the second.

Measured Under the published move, which breaks squares, the slab never opens, from one push or from any feed tried (T65, T67, T68): its cheapest clean opening is two switches away (exact, T69). With points compacting by whole layers, as she decided, the slab is a converter: it burps, re-forms and burps again, each daughter exactly its curling cost while the chain keeps pace with the feed, and at the largest size every number written beforehand from the two smaller sizes held (T71); between meals it keeps or loses its energy as its surroundings are warmer or colder (T74).
