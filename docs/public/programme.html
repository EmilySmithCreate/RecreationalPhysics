A phase-changing reality

The programme, and where each piece stands.

Emily Smith · Charlottesville · analysis drafted with Claude (Anthropic)

The claim, in the author's words

Our spacetime is one settled arrangement of something deeper, X: a specific, relatively stable arrangement with its own dimensions, curled where ours are open. X was stable for now, not for ever. A push started the change in one small part of X; it spread as a front, X opened into space, and the change came out as a burp: a fixed release of energy that became the Big Bang's matter and radiation. What did not convert remained as a scrap: dark matter. Energy was conserved across the change. The rules of the two phases are their constants and do not change from one generation to the next. Inside the space that forms, black holes are regions that re-curl towards X; one that grows large enough gets the activation energy and burps a new space: the same burp, the same scrap. Every possible arrangement of what a Big Bang produces therefore gets to exist. The quantum world is the set of undetectably different versions of an arrangement, all standing at once, until an interaction makes them distinguishable.

Where each piece stands

The tests use one published model of emergent geometry, Trugenberger's combinatorial quantum gravity, as a toy. Space is a network; X's stand-in is a torus with one direction curled (spherical cows being unavailable); the model's local-term coefficient λ is a knob. Only λ = 1 is his model. The torus results are at λ > 1, where the energy is no longer a curvature, so they are about a close cousin of his model and are never called combinatorial quantum gravity. Anything about gravity is tested in three dimensions only: in two, even Einstein's gravity gives no pull between masses at rest (general knowledge, to verify), so no number measured in 2D is carried into a claim about gravity. A toy model can show that a mechanism is possible or impossible inside a class of models; on its own it cannot show that reality works this way. That is the author's claim, and the model is how she tests its pieces.

1

A stable-for-now arrangement opens sharply into space

MeasuredT7 · T9 · T10 · T11 · paper 1
The model shows

At λ = 1.25 the curled torus decompactifies (its curled direction opens): it waits, starts at one place, runs as one front with the two orders side by side, releases exactly ε = 4(λ − 1) per point, has an activation energy of exactly 12 at every size, and leaves one small relic.

How it could be wrong

A slide instead of a switch; a push that grows with size. Neither happened.

2

It is not a fluke of one setting

Measured · inconclusive by the letter, for a stated reasonT8
The model shows

From λ = 1.05 to 1.35 the change is stuck for now, runs as one front with the two orders side by side, and releases exactly the predicted burp at every size. It is cleanest near λ = 1 and leaves more defects as λ grows; above 1.35 the curled torus is not stuck. Waiting times follow a formula with nothing fitted over two decades; counted move by move, the first exit arrives exactly when the formula says, and about a third of exits fall back before the front takes hold, at every λ (T22).

The pre-registered verdict is inconclusive because one criterion (memoryless waits) was set too tight for thirty decays per setting.

How it could be wrong

A floor below which the change stops being sharp: none found down to 1.05.

3

The rule: curvature plus a curling cost

Exact arithmetic · the author's readingVISION Update 23
The model shows

The author's rule is the published energy, the total curvature, unchanged, plus one term: every link curled tight costs 4(λ − 1) extra. The burp per point is exactly that cost, ε = 4(λ − 1). At λ = 1, the published model, curling is free and nothing is released; below 1 the change runs the wrong way. The author's value is about 1.25 in 2D; in 3D her fully curled X is stuck only for 1 < λ < 1.2 (piece 11). These are different models asking different questions, not rival values for one model.

The author's position: the curling cost is a fact of X's arrangement, a line in F, perhaps from the three directions being intertwined. It is a constant of our loop, and it still exists in spacetime, where it is what a black hole pays to re-curl space. Its value is simply whatever reality has: the one that gives the biggest loop with the longest cold period for matter (piece 10).

Open

Whether a rule that is curvature plus a curling cost gives back Einstein's gravity at large scale (claim 2).

Ours, unverified Where space is smooth nothing is curled tight, so the extra term is zero and the rule there is curvature alone; it acts only on tight curls, the scraps and black holes. The test is in 3D.

4

The new space must have room for the burp

MeasuredT9 · T18
The model shows

Sealed, the change completes only if the surroundings can absorb the burp; otherwise the new space melts. The room needed grows with the size of the space, and faster than the burp as λ rises (12, 36, 72 stores at λ = 1.10, 1.25, 1.40): a bigger burp is a harder birth.

A condition to meet

In the author's theory melting is not real, so the burp must always have somewhere to go.

5

The scrap: fixed size, and more seeds make more

MeasuredT10 · T17
The model shows

One relic (the scrap) of fixed size per tube, however large, from one seed. With 1, 2, 4, 8 seeds, 1.10, 2.15, 2.50, 3.40 relics: more seeds, more scrap, but fewer than one each (pre-registered verdict: BETWEEN). At fixed temperature near λ = 1, the change leaves no scrap at all.

How it could be wrong

A scrap that grows with the space, or more seeds leaving no more scrap. Neither happened.

The two guesses at the exact count both missed: the author's one per seed held up to two seeds and fell short beyond; ours, one per tube, was wrong from two seeds. The truth lies between.

6

The scrap lasts

First version didn't hold · in progressT19
The model shows

Held at one fixed temperature, every relic heals away in the end: within 500 to 13,000 sweeps at coupling 1.25, and 4,000 to 80,500 at 1.0. In a very cold sealed box (T10) it lasted the whole run. The colder, the longer it lasts.

Next

Reality cools as it expands, so the right test is a race: does the scrap freeze in before it heals? Cosmology calls this freeze-out (ours, general knowledge). Cool the box on a schedule after the burp and count the survivors. It fails if the scrap heals at every cooling speed.

7

Gravity from counting what we cannot see: curve-first gravity

First version didn't hold · in progressO22 · O32
The model shows

Tested so far only with space frozen. With the wiring held fixed, two relics exert no pull on each other through energy (exact), and with points interchangeable, counting does not pull them together either (it slightly prefers them exactly opposite).

Ours Frozen space cannot bend, so nothing can carry a pull. In Einstein's gravity too, masses pull through the bending of the space between them.

The author's position: it is not a wish. Gravity is a mathematical result of some local degrees of freedom being interchangeable and unobserved from our large-scale view of reality. Cost and count can both act, as the two halves of one free energy: refolding costs energy, counting favours it, and it happens where enough energy is packed in to pay. In her framing, curved space moves matter: space curves using energy that matter provides, and the three space directions curl together, never one alone. Published relatives of gravity from counting: Jacobson (1995) and Verlinde (2011) (general knowledge, not read by us).

Curve-first gravity (the author's term): space has its own drive to curve, and matter supplies the energy that starts the curve rather than causing it. Gravity belongs to the spacetime phase, not to X's rules, and the barrier X sits behind is in F, not in gravitational energy. It differs from general relativity in the cause, and must agree with it wherever general relativity has been measured (claim 2).

Next

In her terms: does curved space move matter? The real case: space free to bend, relics free to move, and points interchangeable, measuring the average pull at each separation (the standard name is the potential of mean force). In the model that needs a sheet warm enough to rearrange but not so warm that it melts (a cold sheet accepts no moves at all), so the first job is to find that window.

Caution The model's space is two-dimensional, and in two space dimensions even Einstein's gravity gives no pull between masses at rest (general knowledge, not read by us, to verify). This model may be the wrong place to see a pull even if the idea is right. Decided: the pull is tested in three dimensions (piece 11), never read from 2D.

What would earn the name gravity: a pull that attracts, grows with the energy present, is the same for every kind of relic, and in 3D weakens with distance roughly as 1/r².

8

Black holes as re-curled regions

First version didn't hold · in progressO20 · T21
The model shows

Energy spread evenly through a sealed sheet melts it rather than folding it, with named points and, at 64 points where complete folds exist, with interchangeable points too; at 36 points, a fragile lean towards folding.

The author's position: the melt is not a real phase with any stability, so melting briefly is fine as long as the region folds soon after. The melt can be the barrier between a new space and the black hole that birthed it, closing until the next 12 is available.

Next

A black hole is not energy spread evenly; it is energy packed into one place. The untested case is a local push into cold surroundings (the local-spark protocol, still to be built). It fails if a local push also melts rather than folds.

Second test, in the author's terms: in the model a melt lasts as long as its energy stays, so let the energy leak away at several speeds and see whether the melt folds before it flattens. It fails if every melt ends flat.

9

Quantum behaviour from counting versions

First version didn't hold · in progressT15 rungs 0b · 1 · 3a
The model shows

The author's framing: counting was a probe, not the expected mechanism. It was tried because its mathematics was already in hand (the model's own renaming counts), not because she expected it to work.

Counting alone can never beat Bell's inequality, for any fixed arrangement (argument, rung 3a), and the model's small loop is not a resonator inside the sheet (rung 0b). The next rung passed: with interchangeable points, time spent in each arrangement equals its share of the count (rung 1, validation).

Ours Why counting falls short: a count gives every version a positive weight, and positive weights only add, so two versions can never cancel. Quantum mechanics needs cancelling (interference), which is what a phase supplies. Smolin's real-ensemble quantum mechanics, the closest published relative, has it: his versions are real copies that affect one another, and they carry a phase.

Next

The author's position: versions do not affect one another; they are not different in the first place. What is needed is a rule under which versions can cancel one another. The author expects it to come from the loop (piece 10) and has not yet chosen one. It fails if the rule cannot reproduce the Bell violations that experiments measure.

Ours The most concrete candidate we know of (general knowledge, not read by us) is the exchange phase. Swapping two identical things gives back the same state up to a sign: −1 for fermions, and Pauli exclusion is two swapped versions cancelling. In two dimensions a swap can give any phase at all (anyons), because the swap traces a loop that cannot be undone. The phase belongs to the swap, not to either version. Whether this gets past rung 3a's argument is not known.

10

The cycle: new spaces from black holes, every arrangement existing

The author's · untestable in the modela strange loop, after Hofstadter
The model shows

The network model used here (Trugenberger's, not Smolin's) has no black holes and no expansion. It can map the ingredients (how stuck X is, the push, the burp, the room, the scrap), not count offspring.

The author's position: versions are not copies, neither the renamings of one arrangement nor identical arrangements in other spacetimes. So Smolin's route to quantum behaviour is not hers. She expects the missing rule of piece 9 to come from the loop itself: something about a strange loop that his picture does not use.

How it could be wrong

Smolin's argument: if a large object had copies, it would behave quantum-mechanically. The author's answer is that other spacetimes' arrangements do not count as copies.

Ours The counting already makes large objects classical without copies: a complicated arrangement has exactly one version (rung 0: a melt, and a sheet with three different defects, each have one).

11

The three space directions curl together

Exact so far · reproduction gate runningO40 · O41 · Gate C
The model shows

The author's rule: the three directions are intertwined; they curl together and open together, so one activation releases the whole burp.

In 2D the rule cannot be tested: a gas of fully curled pieces is not stuck at any λ > 1 (exact, O40). In 3D, with six links per point (exact, O41), the fully curled state, the 6-cube, is stuck for now only for 1 < λ < 1.2, and each curled direction holds a third of the burp. Like the 2D torus, this window lies off λ = 1, so results there are about a close cousin of the published model.

Next

First, reproduce the published 3D curve (Trugenberger 2022, Fig. 3; 500 points; λ = 1) before any 3D result is read. Then: does one push open all three directions at once, and does a curved region pull a relic towards it?

It fails if the directions open one at a time, or if no pull appears.

Stuck arrangements inside the published model itself

Designed · waiting on the graphallotropes
The model shows

The model's author proposes dark matter as allotropes: regions of the network stuck in a different discrete arrangement, as diamond survives where graphite is more stable (Trugenberger 2024; the smallest example is Fig. 9 of his 2025 review, drawn by Eryk Kopczyński). These sit at λ = 1, inside his model, and they are the author's order-to-order question asked there.

Ours, exact If no edge carries three squares, the local term vanishes and the question does not depend on λ at all.

Next

Build the allotrope from its actual wiring (requested, not guessed from the drawing). Then ask whether it is stuck, how high its barrier is, and how its lifetime grows with its size.

Ours, unverified The curved background is favoured only at finite temperature, so whether the patch is stuck is partly a question of entropy. It fails, for the author's picture, if the region dissolves with no wait.

What is new here, as far as we have found

We have not found published work that varies the coefficient of the model's local term, or that studies one ordered arrangement of it turning into another (literature check of 24 September, recorded in the repository). That is the ground of paper 1. The one place where the author's question meets the published model at its own value of λ is the allotrope (piece 12).

What is claimed, and by whom

The author claims that reality works this way. The claim at the top is hers, stated to be argued with. The model's results are narrower: in the model, piece 1 is established, and the others are measured, in progress or untested, as marked. No physicist has checked any of it yet.
