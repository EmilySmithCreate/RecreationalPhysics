A phase-changing reality

Where each piece of the theory stands, what comes next, and when it last changed.

Emily Smith · Charlottesville · analysis drafted with Claude (Anthropic)

The claim, in the author's words

Our spacetime is one settled arrangement of something deeper, X: a specific arrangement that was stable for now, curled where ours is open. A push started a change in one small part of X. It spread as a front, X opened into space, and the change released a fixed lump of energy: the burp. X's three curled directions are alike, so which opens first is chance, and the order sets what each release becomes. The first becomes dark energy, the energy of space itself. The other two become ordinary matter and radiation, the Big Bang's energy, and dark matter. What did not convert stayed behind as scrap. Energy was conserved across the change, and what the burp does not deliver returns to the black hole it came from. Inside the new space, black holes are regions that curl back toward X; one that grows large enough gets its own push and burps a new space, by the same rules as before. So every arrangement a Big Bang can produce gets to exist. The quantum world is the set of undetectably different versions of an arrangement, all standing at once until an interaction tells them apart.

The author's hypothesis. What it has to match is the make-up of reality at its birth, not today's.

The tests use a toy: a network of links whose rule is a published model of emergent geometry (Trugenberger's) plus one added term, a cost for curling tight. With that term the toy is a close cousin of his model, not his model. A toy can show that a mechanism is possible or impossible in a class of models. It cannot show that reality works this way; that is the author's claim.

No physicist has reviewed any of this yet. Paper 1 is on hold at arXiv until a physicist has read it or a journal has it. A reader is being sought.

Where each piece stands

The burp: how X opens

1

A stable-for-now arrangement opens sharply into space

MeasuredPaper 1Updated 25 Sep 2026
Current read

A torus with one direction curled, the toy's stand-in for X, sits still and then opens from one spot. The opening runs as a front, curled on one side and flat on the other, like a flame moving through paper, and gives up the same energy per point every time.

The push that starts it is one small move of fixed cost, the same at every size tried: a match lights a campfire or a forest. The height of that wall is a fact about the energy. How long the wait lasts depends on how the simulation walks.

2

It is not a fluke of one setting

Measured · inconclusive by the letterPaper 1Updated 27 Sep 2026
Current read

Across a range of the curling cost the change stays sharp and releases exactly what the arithmetic says. Most tubes wait like a radioactive nucleus, with no memory of how long they have waited. Two smaller groups stand apart: a few wait far longer, and about one in six go almost at once.

Three registered runs of this map were each inconclusive by the letter, each for a stated reason: twice a rule we had written badly, once the rare long waits themselves. The author Her bar is different from ours: the change has to happen at some size, not at every size in every instance.

Read the long waiters and the fast starters from their saved wiring. A fourth run only if the author wants one, scored her way.

3

The rule: curvature plus a curling cost

Exact · the author's readingPaper 3Updated 24 Sep 2026
Current read

The rule is the published one, total curvature, plus one term: every link curled tight costs a little extra. The burp per point is exactly that cost. At the published setting curling is free and nothing is released. With the cost switched on, flat space is the floor and X sits above it.

Where space is smooth nothing is curled tight, so the extra term is silent and the rule there is curvature alone. The author The cost is a constant of our loop, and it is what a black hole pays to curl space back.

Next

Whether curvature plus a curling cost gives back Einstein's gravity at large scale. That rides on piece 8.

4

The new space must have room for the burp

MeasuredPaper 3Updated 5 Oct 2026
Current read

Sealed off, a new space finishes opening only if its surroundings can take in the burp. With too little room it melts instead. A bigger burp is a harder birth.

Size matters too. Read from their saved wiring, long tubes in warm surroundings melted where short ones healed, so a larger space melts at a lower temperature. The author Melting is not a real end state, so the burp always needs somewhere to go.

Next

The same question in three directions has not been reached, because the last direction did not open (piece 5).

5

How the curled directions open

Measured · first version didn't hold · in progressThe paper on three and four directionsUpdated 5 Oct 2026
Current read

The author The three directions are intertwined, and one activation opens all three. X may begin with one direction already open.

In three and four directions, one push has never opened all of X into one space. The directions open one at a time, each behind its own wall: a combination lock, not a zipper.

Exactly: in this toy a fully curled X bigger than one small cube is always a gas of separate cubes. Each cube opens on its own and they do not join. So an X that opens into one space has to be one connected piece with a direction already open, which the author allows.

Such a connected X, with the released energy kept at the front, opened its next direction and stopped. Where the next wall is low it opened all the way into one space in 5 runs of 16, with damage in most of the others. One cube alone opens part of the way, and fully only in a minority of runs. Treating its points as interchangeable holds a stuck cube shut, and helps a cube that is already rolling to open fully.

Next

Stop guessing pushes: price the true barrier exactly, the smallest opened patch that grows instead of shrinking. Then a warm bath on the connected space, in place of a single push.

Still owed: our three-direction code does not reproduce the published curve for the parent model. One question to its author.

6

The burp's three shares

The author's · exact arithmetic · needs a physicistPaper 3Updated 5 Oct 2026
Current read

The author The first opening releases dark energy. With the other directions still curled it cannot spread or clump, so it stays as the energy of space itself. The other two release ordinary matter and radiation, and dark matter; which is which is not decided. One problem is carried openly: the first opening has to net almost nothing, and nothing yet says why.

Published numbers, our use of them Early on nearly all the energy is the hot lump: light and fast particles. Matter and light were equal only when distances were about 3,400 times smaller than now (Planck 2018); before that, the earlier you look, the smaller cold matter's share. At the latest moment the hot era can have begun, dark matter that was already cold was under a millionth of the energy, and dark energy was too small to measure. The famous five-to-one of dark to ordinary matter is real and old, but it compares two slivers: the mass of ordinary matter is the crumb left when matter and antimatter cancelled. So matching birth asks one opening to carry nearly the whole burp. Two things are not known and keep this from being one number: how hot reality was at birth, and whether dark matter began hot, as one more ingredient of the lump, or cold.

Exact A wall is a hill, not a toll: what is spent climbing it comes back on the far side. So pushes cannot set the shares; only energy held in half-open states can. Two shapes would match birth with nothing to tune. In one, nothing is released until the last direction opens. In the other, the second opening releases everything and the last direction is free to stay curled. Neither has been run.

Settings tuned to today's five-to-one were run and did not open all the directions. That target is withdrawn.

The scrap

7

The scrap: small, one per seed or fewer, and it freezes in

Measured · a run in progressPaper 2Updated 5 Oct 2026
Current read

One seed leaves one small scrap, a loop of four points left curled, whatever the size of the space. When the change starts in several places of one X at once, there is more scrap: about one for every three starting points. The starts usually grow together into one space.

Kept warm, every scrap heals. Cooled, some freeze in, more of them the faster the cooling; cosmology calls this freeze-out. A frozen scrap is a fixed count of objects, so as space grows it thins out the way matter does.

A first look, not yet a finding: among the leftovers of spaces that opened from several seeds are a few links joining points that are otherwise far apart.

The author The scrap may be the dark matter. That reading is open again, beside dark matter as a release (piece 6).

Next

Running: how much scrap stays frozen as the cooling gets slower, in spaces that seeded themselves. A gentle fall would make a small leftover the ordinary result of slow cooling. A cliff would leave none. The author's prediction is owed before it is read.

Then: read the far-reaching links from their positions before saying what they are.

What it must give back

8

Curve-first gravity

First version didn't hold · in progressPaper 5Updated 5 Oct 2026
Current read

The author Space has its own drive to curve, and matter supplies the energy that starts the curve. Gravity belongs to space, not to X. Wherever Einstein's account has been measured, hers must give the same numbers.

With the wiring held still, leftovers do not pull on each other at a distance (exact). The reason is known. Flat space in this toy is stiff: every move out of it costs a fixed amount, and a stiff medium passes a pull along only a few steps. It also heals every damage it was given.

Two carriers are on the table. One needs nothing new: a pull from counting. It needs warmth, and it needs the count of what a boundary hides to grow with the boundary, as in Jacobson's and Verlinde's gravity. The other is new: a quantity living on the points of space, which the author has decided to add if it matches curve-first gravity.

Next

Count exactly what a boundary hides around a scrap. Does the count follow the boundary or the volume? Our guess is the volume, which would not give gravity.

Then the pull between two leftovers in a warm bath, in three directions only. And the design for the quantity on the points.

9

Quantum behavior from counting versions

First version didn't hold · in progressPaper 6Updated 25 Sep 2026
Current read

Counting look-alike versions cannot give quantum behavior by itself: for any fixed arrangement it can never beat Bell's test. A count gives every version a positive weight, and positive weights only add. Quantum behavior needs versions that can cancel.

The first rule tried, a sign for each swap, prunes a few arrangements and changes little. The author Versions are not copies and do not affect one another; the missing rule should come from the loop itself.

Next

The author's choice of a rule under which versions can cancel. It fails if it cannot reproduce what Bell experiments measure.

10

Time and the speed limit

The author's · its counterpart measuredOutlineUpdated 5 Oct 2026
Current read

The author Time as we experience it exists because matter moves through three-dimensional space. X has order but not time. The speed of light is the limit of that relationship.

The toy has no time; its only clock is the count of moves. Its counterpart of a speed limit holds: an opening's front keeps a steady pace set by the medium, once time is counted fairly, so that one small place gets its turn as often in a large space as in a small one.

A front speeds up where extra energy sits, as the author predicted. Read as a clock, that is the opposite of clocks running slow near mass.

Next

The author's mapping from a front's pace to a clock's rate. A direction that is truly time-like needs a different kind of model, parked for a physicist collaborator.

Black holes as re-curled regions

The author Inside a black hole gravity has won. The mass supplies the energy to curl space back, starting with one direction, and each fold makes the next easier.

Nothing folds. Energy packed into one place, or spread through a sealed space, shakes flat space and it heals. With points treated as interchangeable even the small scar rarely forms.

Exact Two facts fit her mechanism. Each fold lowers the cost of the next. And the drive to curl that comes from counting is real, but it wins only when the curling cost is nearly switched off.

The cycle: a strange loop

The author's · outside what the toy can testPaper 7Updated 25 Sep 2026
Current read

The author Black holes burp new spaces by the same rules, so reality is a strange loop, in Hofstadter's phrase. We should expect to find ourselves in the kind of loop that holds the most time, matter and energy in a stable phase.

The toy has no black holes and no expansion. It can map the ingredients: how stuck X is, the push, the burp, the room, the scrap. It cannot count offspring.

What is claimed, and by whom

The author claims that reality works this way. The claim at the top is hers, stated to be argued with. The toy's results are narrower: piece 1 is established in the toy, and the others are measured, in progress or untested, as marked. We have not found published work that studies one ordered arrangement of this model turning into another.
