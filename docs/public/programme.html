A phase-changing reality

Where each piece of the theory stands, which paper carries it, and what comes next.

Emily Smith · Charlottesville · analysis drafted with Claude (Anthropic)

The claim, in the author's words

Our spacetime is one settled arrangement of something deeper, X: a specific, relatively stable arrangement with its own dimensions, curled where ours are open. X was stable for now, not for ever. Most likely X was a slab, two directions already open and one still curled tight; each curled direction stands behind its own wall and releases its own share when it opens. Energy arriving in X, as a black hole's infall arrives, raised its excitation until, when the stores first held the whole cost of the curl, the curled direction gave way: the burp, a fixed release of energy, which became the Big Bang's energy. The release leaves one way, into the daughter; nothing passes from open space back into X; the slab re-forms and goes on accepting energy, so the black hole is a converter, not a store. Dark energy is the energy of space itself; its best current guess for a source is the network's own thermal roughness at the loop's fixed coupling, a coupling set by the loop's total energy once every daughter's energy has returned to the network at the end of a turn. What did not convert remained as scrap: small knots of fixed energy, their number set at the opening; the scrap is the best current guess for the dark matter, and whether it is the dark matter is open. Energy was conserved across the change. The rules of the two phases are their constants and do not change from one generation to the next. Inside the space that forms, black holes are regions where the parent's own geometry re-curls, two directions still open and one curled tight. A daughter space opens while the black hole remains in the parent; each universe ends when its last black hole has dissolved, and every black hole births many new ones, so the tree of universes multiplies each generation. Every universe runs an exact copy of one clock, from its own birth to its own heat death, and no clock runs across universes; universes of one setting that differ only by a quantum outcome are counted each as a whole. The quantum world is the set of undetectably different versions of an arrangement, all standing at once, until an interaction makes them distinguishable; the versions are the branches of the many-worlds reading, and the rule that lets them cancel is the published one: versions carry amplitudes and a record in the surroundings is what keeps them apart.

The author's hypothesis, her current best guess. The claim of 9 October was confirmed by her; this version carries her decisions since, each dated in the record: the directions curling separately (16 Oct); the black hole as the parent's own geometry curled, a daughter without a whole-universe reset, the branching count (16 Oct); the burp when the stores first hold the whole cost, the release leaving one way into the daughter (18 Oct); the measure counted per universe, by each universe's own clock (18 Oct); the versions as Everett's branches (18 Oct); the loop's coupling fixed by the loop's total energy, and dark energy as the network's thermal roughness at that coupling, a hypothesis to test (19 Oct); the measure's unit and weight and what an observer is (19 Oct). The sentences that changed are ours in wording and hers once she confirms them.

Where it stands

A torus with one direction curled, the toy's stand-in for X, sits still, then changes from one spot. The change runs as a front, like a flame through paper, and gives up the same energy per point every time: the burp. The push that starts it is a single small move of fixed cost at every size: a match lights a campfire or a forest.

The toy's rule cannot tell the two apart, so nothing pays for growing wider. With an added price on loops of six, the tube does come out mostly wide, though crumpled with flaws throughout.

The author's best guess · dark energy given a candidate source · needs a physicistPaper 7Updated 19 Oct 2026
Where it stands

The author The burp is the hot lump, light and ordinary matter; the scrap is the dark matter; dark energy is the energy space itself keeps.

Ours At reality's birth nearly all the energy was the hot lump, so one opening has to carry nearly the whole burp. The author, 19 Oct · decided At the end of a turn every daughter's energy has returned to the network, and the network's temperature is fixed by the loop's total energy alone; so the loop's coupling is a derived number, the same every turn. Ours At any coupling above zero the network carries a few defects, because there are vastly more defected arrangements than one perfect lattice; the published model reads that roughness as negative curvature and as dark energy. Hers freezes the coupling: fixed by the loop rather than by the matter's temperature, the roughness never thins as space grows and behaves as a cosmological constant. What is measured is a density, constant to about ten per cent over eight billion years; shares at any epoch follow from it. Not every point converts. Ours That is the shape cosmologists call freeze-out: durable objects whose number is fixed by how the space formed.

Where it stands

Counting look-alike versions cannot give quantum behavior by itself: positive counts only add, and quantum behavior needs versions that cancel. The missing rule is the published one (piece 13): versions carry amplitudes, and a record in the surroundings keeps them apart.

The author, 15 Oct "Piece 9 should adopt the published amplitude rule, why would it not?" Adopted. Ours, what it costs The rule is quantum mechanics put in, not derived, so the quantum sentence of the claim becomes a bar the loop has to meet rather than evidence that it does. The object the amplitudes live on is open: the loop of four is not a resonator inside a sheet. And it assumes the toy's versions are the branches of the many-worlds reading, which is untested. None of these is a reason not to adopt it as the working rule; each is what paper 13 now has to say.

The author, 19 Oct Where the amplitudes live: "in the loop there are sets of universes that are identical until each moment passes. From the macro perspective those versions are indistinguishable so we experience all of the set, until a measurement or some next moment locks us into one version." Ours That is decoherence in her words. The published rule adds that until they separate the versions can also cancel one another, and that a next moment locks a version in only where it leaves a record. What the toy still lacks is the arrangement that carries the versions.

The cycle: daughters from black holes, and the measure

The author's measure, fully specified · its first two calculations runPaper 16Updated 19 Oct 2026
Where it stands

The author, 16 Oct · confirmed A daughter space may open while the black hole remains in the parent; each universe ends when its last black hole has dissolved; every black hole births many new ones, "a larger infinity: the length of time just as long, but each universe producing a trillion more rather than just one more." We should expect to find ourselves in the setting that holds the most observer-friendly time, and the reward is observers who evolved.

Ours, 18 Oct Her measure is written as one function with her terms: the length of the era of starlight, the rate at which habitable planets form, how long one stays habitable, the chance a planet produces a society that asks about the universe, the window in which an observer can still learn its universe's history (about 1011 years in the standard picture), the black holes formed and when, and the daughters per black hole. The toy supplies exactly one term, the daughters per unit of energy fed, and never the rest. Every number in it is pasted from published work and marked as such.

The author, 18 Oct · decided Universes of one setting that differ only by a quantum outcome are counted each as a whole, the branching ignored. For when an observer is most likely to exist, each universe is counted by its own clock, lined up at its own birth: "it's an exact copy though, starts at big bang runs though heat death, every time ... clocks are only within a universe." Ours The first rule avoids the trap of letting the deepest generations dominate. The second removes the youngness pressure known from eternal inflation by the hypothesis's own structure: that pressure needs a common clock across universes, and there is none. Had the tree been counted at one common moment, with trillions of daughters born early, an observer at our age would have been weighted by about e−36, and no late observers could balance it; late births could, but the published infall into black holes is front-loaded.

The author, 19 Oct · decided The measure counts once per civilization; it weights the ages by the causal diamond, the region an observer at that age can be causally tied to, "because I believe in the strange loop and the version probability mattering"; and an observer is "a civilization where at least one intelligent being asks this particular question", because how long a civilization lasts depends on things the asking itself can affect, so the measure does not weight by it.

Measured, 19 Oct Two calculations in standard cosmology, with published inputs and the rules written first, asked when an observer like us is most likely to find itself by its universe's own age. The first ran four declared reference classes of stars under her once-per-civilization unit: every class comes out typical, and the stars do no work at all, since counting each civilization once at a fixed delay makes the answer the star-formation history shifted by the delay whatever the stars. The published "we are early by a thousandfold" lives entirely in counting planet-years, which weights each planet by how long it lives; her unit removes it. The one thing that moves the peak is Carter's hard-steps delay, which puts a typical observer at 17 billion years with a quarter before us. The second multiplied every class by the diamond's volume, which peaks at 4 billion years and is already half its peak at our age: every class but hers goes late, most observers before us, while her class, sun-like stars with the hard-steps delay, lands with us at its median. Her prediction that the diamond would move the peaks held; her prediction that no verdict word would change failed, and so did ours.

Ours That her class alone keeps our age typical under both weights is a declared assumption holding together, not a finding: the class was built from our own case, a dozen combinations were looked at, and the choices were made with the numbers known. So our own age is not scored under the measure from here. What the two calculations do establish is that the counting unit decides more than the stars do, and that the diamond and the observation cap are different weights with very different consequences.

Whether we are early or late in cosmic time depends on who is counted: the same physics gives answers from under a millionth to nine tenths. The author, 18 Oct The versions are Everett's branches, "as needed for amplitudes and interference to work correctly."

Ours, 18 Oct Under her per-universe reading the one issue left is the published count: with habitable planets round the smallest, longest-lived stars, the most likely observer lives near 1013 years, and the chance of one as early as now is at most about a thousandth (Loeb, Batista and Sloan 2016). Her own window term answers it: observers who can no longer learn their universe's history count less, so the peak moves inside 1011 years and our age is unremarkable. That makes the window the load-bearing term of her function, and it is not scored on our age, which was known when she chose it. Her other candidates for bringing the prediction forward were put through the population model as a look, not evidence: a rip would do it but the data lean the other way, and it cuts against her heat-death argument for many daughters; a constant dark energy leaves bound structures bound and shortens only the window; "no interaction between planets" does nothing as a factor and a great deal as a rule for who counts, each civilization once, which puts the peak near 5 billion years and makes us late rather than early. The registered calculations of 19 October (piece 12) made this exact: under that rule the stars drop out of the answer entirely.

Next

Hers: who counts, chosen on its own grounds and not on our age; whether this piece stays; whether a two-turn version of the toy is tested. A critic would attack the window term first, and the habitability of planets round the smallest stars is a question astronomy is working on.

Connecting the hypothesis to observations

The author's request · a handoff, nothing measuredPapers 7, 12Updated 15 Oct 2026
Where it stands

The author "I need another bot to begin work on connecting the theory to observations and explaining the unexplainable." She wants published mathematics she can apply for predictions and constants, not novelty.

A handoff was written on 14 October for a fresh assistant. For each observation that standard physics measures well and does not explain, it asks for five things: the measured numbers with their uncertainties, from sources read; what her hypothesis says; what the toy has shown and cannot show; the nearest published work and its limits; and the observation that would rule the reading out. A target is something the hypothesis must reproduce, never evidence that it does. The sheets build on the existing targets file; they do not start a second list.

The five

Dark matter, the best candidate. Piece 7's relics; paper 7, with paper 2 for what the knots are. Nearest published relatives, none read yet: black-hole relics as dark matter (MacGibbon 1987), primordial black holes and their mass windows, asymmetric dark matter. First deliverable: the target sheet of limits a heavy, rare, gravity-only relic must meet, and the birth share of about 0.67 eV over the temperature of birth.

Black holes from concentrated dark matter. Piece 11; paper 12. First deliverable: a reading note on how dark matter gets dense enough (direct collapse, dark-matter spikes around black holes, the seeds of the early supermassive ones). Benchmarks only, never evidence: Smolin, Pathria, Popławski, Fife.

The birth, and a signal it could leave. Pieces 1, 2 and 6; paper 7. The hot era began no later than about 4 to 5 MeV. A front-driven change makes gravitational waves at a frequency set by the temperature of birth, and pulsar-timing arrays reported a background in 2023. First deliverable: temperature of birth against frequency band against what has been seen or bounded, so she can see which births are testable.

Dark energy. Piece 6; paper 7. What each candidate source predicts for whether it changes with time, set against recent survey hints. Low priority until the first two sheets exist.

Matter over antimatter. Pieces 1 and 2; a pointer for later in paper 7. The best-studied route makes the excess at the walls of bubbles in a sharp transition, and a front-driven burp supplies such walls; the toy has nothing that comes in opposite kinds.
