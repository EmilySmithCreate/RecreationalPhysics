A phase-changing reality

The programme, and where each piece stands.

Emily Smith · Charlottesville · analysis drafted with Claude (Anthropic)

The claim, in the author's words

Our spacetime is one settled arrangement of something deeper, X: a specific, relatively stable arrangement with its own dimensions, curled where ours are open. X was stable for now, not for ever. A push started the change in one small part of X; it spread as a front, X opened into space, and the change came out as a burp: a fixed release of energy that became the Big Bang's matter and radiation. What did not convert remained as a scrap: dark matter. Energy was conserved across the change. The rules of the two phases are their constants and do not change from one generation to the next. Inside the space that forms, black holes are regions that re-curl towards X; one that grows large enough gets the activation energy and burps a new space: the same burp, the same scrap. Every possible arrangement of what a Big Bang produces therefore gets to exist. The quantum world is the set of undetectably different versions of an arrangement, all standing at once, until an interaction makes them distinguishable.

Where each piece stands

The tests use one published model of emergent geometry, Trugenberger's combinatorial quantum gravity, as a toy. Space is a network; X's stand-in is a torus with one direction curled (spherical cows being unavailable); the model's local-term coefficient λ is a knob. Only λ = 1 is his model. The torus results are at λ > 1, where the energy is no longer a curvature, so they are about a close cousin of his model and are never called combinatorial quantum gravity. Anything about gravity is tested in three dimensions only: in two, even Einstein's gravity gives no pull between masses at rest (general knowledge, to verify), so no number measured in 2D is carried into a claim about gravity. A toy model can show that a mechanism is possible or impossible inside a class of models; on its own it cannot show that reality works this way. That is the author's claim, and the model is how she tests its pieces.

A stable-for-now arrangement opens sharply into space

MeasuredT7 · T9 · T10 · T11 · paper 1updated 24 Sep 2026
The model shows

At λ = 1.25 the curled torus decompactifies (its curled direction opens): it waits, starts at one place, runs as one front with the two orders side by side, releases exactly ε = 4(λ − 1) per point, and leaves about one small relic at every size tried. The wall it must climb is one move costing exactly 12, at every size.

The wall's height is a fact about the energy, like the height of a hill; the waiting and the front are how this simulation walks over it, one local swap at a time, and another way of walking would change them.

Ours, exact; not yet run In the author's view the points are interchangeable. That changes no energy, so the wall, the burp and the relic stay as they are, but it makes the torus stickier: a perfectly symmetric arrangement looks the same from every point, one move breaks that, and counting look-alike arrangements as one then favours staying put, the more so the bigger the space.

How it could be wrong

A slide instead of a switch; a push that grows with size. Neither happened.

It is not a fluke of one setting

Measured · inconclusive by the letter, three times, each for a stated reason; the rare long wait now under testT8 · T22 · T23 · T24updated 25 Sep 2026, 14:30 ET
The model shows

From λ = 1.05 to 1.35 the change is stuck for now, runs as one front with the two orders side by side, and releases exactly the predicted burp at every size. It is cleanest near λ = 1 and leaves more defects as λ grows; above 1.35 the curled torus is not stuck. Waiting times follow a formula with nothing fitted over two decades; counted move by move, the first exit arrives when the formula says, and about a third of exits fall back before the front takes hold, at every λ (T22).

The first pre-registered verdict (T8) was inconclusive because one criterion (memoryless waits) was set too tight for thirty decays per setting. A second run with four times the decays and that check sized to the sample (T23) found the waits memoryless in every setting that could be read, like a radioactive nucleus: however long the torus has waited, its chance of going in the next moment is the same. Both of the author's predictions held: sharp across the window, and break-up beginning at the edge. It is still inconclusive by the letter, for a second stated reason: the energy check compared an average over the last stretch of a decay with the exact energy of its final state, and at coupling 1.30 the sheet's own warmth tripped it in a few decays per hundred. One oddity is unexplained: at λ = 1.05 a few waits are far longer than that picture expects.

The third run

T24 read the energy exactly from each decay's final wiring, and that check passed in every one of its 28 cells. Two orders side by side and one front held in every cell, the waits were memoryless in 24 of 28, and the break-up at the edge appeared for the third time, as the author predicted. It is still inconclusive by the letter, for a third reason: in four cells a single extreme wait, one of them 76 times the expected wait, fails the memoryless band, and the rule needs every size sharp. That rare very long wait, now seen at three settings and always at the smaller sizes, is a real feature to study, not a rule to rewrite after the fact. Whether to pre-register a fourth run with a rule sized for it is the author's call.

The author's position (25 September): this arrangement of reality does not require the same thing to happen at every size in every instance; it has to happen at one size and lead to the trillion new strange loops. The every-size rule was ours, a bar for a mechanism claim; a rule written her way (a majority of instances at some size go sharply, stragglers reported and not counted against) is proposed for any fourth run, not applied to the three already read.

How it could be wrong

A floor below which the change stops being sharp: none found down to 1.05.

Running (T38, 25 Sep, 12:40 ET): the rare very long waits, 4,000 decays per setting at 64 points and 1,000 at 192, with the tubes still waiting at 5,000 to 20,000 sweeps saved so their wiring can be read. Is there a second, slower population, and what is it? Her prediction (inferred): yes. Ours: yes at 64 points, a variant of the tube with a higher wall.

The rule: curvature plus a curling cost

Exact arithmetic · the author's readingVISION Update 23updated 24 Sep 2026
The model shows

The author's rule is the published energy, the total curvature, unchanged, plus one term: every link curled tight costs 4(λ − 1) extra. The burp per point is exactly that cost, ε = 4(λ − 1). At λ = 1, the published model, curling is free and nothing is released; below 1 the change runs the wrong way. The author's value is about 1.25 in 2D; in 3D her fully curled X is stuck only for 1 < λ < 1.2 (piece 11). These are different models asking different questions, not rival values for one model.

The author's position: the curling cost is a fact of X's arrangement, a line in F, perhaps from the three directions being intertwined. It is a constant of our loop, and it still exists in spacetime, where it is what a black hole pays to re-curl space. Its value is simply whatever reality has: the one that gives the biggest loop with the longest cold period for matter (piece 10).

Open

Whether a rule that is curvature plus a curling cost gives back Einstein's gravity at large scale (claim 2).

Ours, unverified Where space is smooth nothing is curled tight, so the extra term is zero and the rule there is curvature alone; it acts only on tight curls, the scraps and black holes. The test is in 3D.

The scrap lasts

MeasuredT19 · T25updated 25 Sep 2026
The model shows

Held at one fixed temperature, every relic heals away in the end: within 500 to 13,000 sweeps at coupling 1.25, and 4,000 to 80,500 at 1.0. In a very cold sealed box (T10) it lasted the whole run. The colder, the longer it lasts.

Cooled, it freezes in (T25, pre-registered verdict FREEZES IN). Reality cools as it expands, so the right test is a race between healing and cooling. Cooled from a warmth at which every scrap heals to one at which nothing moves, every scrap survives a fast cooling, about four in five survive a cooling that takes ten to thirty thousand sweeps, and about a third survive the slowest tried. Cosmology calls this freeze-out (ours, general knowledge). The author's prediction, inferred from her position, held; ours put the freeze-out too early.

How it could be wrong

A scrap that heals at every cooling speed. It did not: even the slowest cooling kept a third. What the model cannot say is how its cooling clock relates to a cosmological one.

Gravity from counting what we cannot see: curve-first gravity

First version didn't hold · the reason is now known; a new rule awaits the author's decisionO22 · O32 · O55 · O56updated 25 Sep 2026, 14:30 ET
The model shows

Tested so far only with space frozen. With the wiring held fixed, two relics exert no pull on each other through energy (exact), and with points interchangeable, counting does not pull them together either (it slightly prefers them exactly opposite).

Ours Frozen space cannot bend, so nothing can carry a pull. In Einstein's gravity too, masses pull through the bending of the space between them.

The author's position: it is not a wish. Gravity is a mathematical result of some local degrees of freedom being interchangeable and unobserved from our large-scale view of reality. Cost and count can both act, as the two halves of one free energy: refolding costs energy, counting favours it, and it happens where enough energy is packed in to pay. In her framing, curved space moves matter: space curves using energy that matter provides, and the three space directions curl together, never one alone. Published relatives of gravity from counting: Jacobson (1995) and Verlinde (2011) (general knowledge, not read by us).

Curve-first gravity (the author's term): space has its own drive to curve, and matter supplies the energy that starts the curve rather than causing it. Gravity belongs to the spacetime phase, not to X's rules, and the barrier X sits behind is in F, not in gravitational energy. It differs from general relativity in the cause, and must agree with it wherever general relativity has been measured (claim 2).

The drive, exact (25 September). With interchangeable points the more symmetric arrangement weighs more, and at 512 points the weights are counted: flat space and the partly curled states carry a few thousand symmetries each, and the fully curled state (a gas of 6-cubes) carries 10³⁹. So the drive to curve from counting exists, it is worth about a quarter of a unit per point at this size, and it appears only at full curling, not gradually. It beats the curling cost only when λ is within about 2% of 1; above that, space holds. In the author's words of the same day: the walls say one direction at a time, the counting says all together, and λ decides.

Ours, exact, 25 Sep afternoon In six links at fixed wiring the answer is the same as in four: two defects pull on each other neither by energy nor by counting except at contact and at symmetric placements. And the object a pull would act on, a stable curled relic inside flat six-link space, has not yet been shown to exist; the one-switch column costs 120 units and is not a dip. Finding that relic is the first step of the gravity test (design brief in the repository); the test itself, near λ = 1.02 with interchangeable points where the counting can pay, is the author's stated target.

Next

In her terms: does curved space move matter? The real case: space free to bend, relics free to move, and points interchangeable, measuring the average pull at each separation (the standard name is the potential of mean force). In the model that needs a sheet warm enough to rearrange but not so warm that it melts (a cold sheet accepts no moves at all), so the first job is to find that window.

Caution The model's space is two-dimensional, and in two space dimensions even Einstein's gravity gives no pull between masses at rest (general knowledge, not read by us, to verify). This model may be the wrong place to see a pull even if the idea is right. Decided: the pull is tested in three dimensions (piece 11), never read from 2D.

What would earn the name gravity: a pull that attracts, grows with the energy present, is the same for every kind of relic, and in 3D weakens with distance roughly as 1/r².

25 Sep, 14:30 ET: why every test so far found no pull, and what would give one. Flat space in this model has an energy gap: every move out of it costs 32, 64 or 128 units, whatever λ is. A medium with a gap carries a force only over a short, fixed range, so this energy cannot pull relics together at a distance, and the four negatives so far are one fact seen four times (literature read today). The search for a stable six-link relic also found none among one- and two-move objects. A pull that falls like Newton's needs three things together: a mode with no gap, each relic a source for it in proportion to its energy, and a mode of the kind that makes like sources attract (a scalar or a tensor; the vector kind makes like sources repel, as electric charges do).

Ours, exact The simplest such mode a graph carries for free is a field on its points. Its fluctuations alone make identical defects attract, but weakly and over a few steps (a count of spanning trees: hidden structure, counted). With relics as its sources, the pull falls like 1/r in three directions: Newton's shape, carried by the emergent wiring. That rule would put the pull in rather than derive it, and it is the author's to adopt or not. Recommended: adopt it, test two relics drifting together in two directions first, then the shape in three, then whether many relics clump and re-curl space, which would join this piece to piece 8.

The author's position: the melt is not a real phase with any stability, so melting briefly is fine as long as the region folds soon after. The melt can be the barrier between a new space and the black hole that birthed it, closing until the next 12 is available.

The second version

A black hole is not energy spread evenly; it is energy packed into one place. The local spark (T26): energy put into one place in a cold sheet, as spendable heat that only moves from one spot can use, and as disorder wired into one patch, with named points at 144 and 256 points and the author's interchangeable points at 64. In every one of the 22 cells the region melts (pre-registered verdict MELTS): no cell folds, and the largest folded piece anywhere is eleven points. The author's prediction, confirmed as hers before any cell was read as a verdict, was that it re-curls; it failed.

The leaking melt (T27, verdict STAYS MELTED): energy spread through a sealed sheet and let leak away at four speeds, from a slow drain to an instant quench. At every speed the melt stays melted: it neither folds nor flattens within the run.

The author's mechanism

Inside a black hole gravity has won and reality is in a symmetric state. The mass supplies the energy to be space and the energy to collapse it, and the collapse probably begins on one direction, as the six-link result suggests for opening. Once one direction has curled we are closer to the symmetric state, which catalyzes the drive, so the grouped directions collapse together, a fold accelerated by the change itself even if the activation hit one direction. Whether time collapses first, the model cannot say.

Ours, exact The first half is in the walls: the extra cost of nucleating each successive fold falls (36, 16, then none with six links; 80, 40, 20, 0 with eight, at λ = 1.25; the fall is exactly a halving only in the second case). The second half is in the counting, and it appears only at full curling (piece 7).

Quantum behaviour from counting versions

First version didn't hold · in progress; the next step needs a rule over historiesT15 rungs 0b · 1 · 3a · O57updated 25 Sep 2026, 14:30 ET
The model shows

The author's framing: counting was a probe, not the expected mechanism. It was tried because its mathematics was already in hand (the model's own renaming counts), not because she expected it to work.

Counting alone can never beat Bell's inequality, for any fixed arrangement (argument, rung 3a), and the model's small loop is not a resonator inside the sheet (rung 0b). The next rung passed: with interchangeable points, time spent in each arrangement equals its share of the count (rung 1, validation).

Ours Why counting falls short: a count gives every version a positive weight, and positive weights only add, so two versions can never cancel. Quantum mechanics needs cancelling (interference), which is what a phase supplies. Smolin's real-ensemble quantum mechanics, the closest published relative, has it: his versions are real copies that affect one another, and they carry a phase.

Next

The author's position: versions do not affect one another; they are not different in the first place. What is needed is a rule under which versions can cancel one another. The author expects it to come from the loop (piece 10) and has not yet chosen one. It fails if the rule cannot reproduce the Bell violations that experiments measure.

Ours The most concrete candidate we know of (general knowledge, not read by us) is the exchange phase. Swapping two identical things gives back the same state up to a sign: −1 for fermions, and Pauli exclusion is two swapped versions cancelling. In two dimensions a swap can give any phase at all (anyons), because the swap traces a loop that cannot be undone. The phase belongs to the swap, not to either version. Whether this gets past rung 3a's argument is not known.

Decided (25 September, late afternoon): the exchange phase is the first rule of the new model to be tested. Its simplest form gives every arrangement a signed weight, the count of its renamings with a sign for each, which is a selection rule: an arrangement with an odd renaming weighs exactly zero, the way two fermions cannot share a state. Ours, exact On saved arrangements the rule spares every basic one (flat space, the curled torus, the knot) and every product of the burp read so far, and forbids a few per cent of damaged sheets (8 of 120 at 144 points). Computed exactly over every arrangement at 16 and 18 points, it removes 1 of 5 and 4 of 26 kinds of arrangement, including one of the three flat ones at 18 points, and moves the averages by a few hundredths: a mild pruning, not yet a change of character. The next step we had named, a phase per swap, does not exist in that form (literature read 25 Sep): renamings allow only the plain count or the sign, so a phase that could cancel needs paths of exchanges, a rule over histories. And what the sign forbids depends on a convention (the sign on points or on pairs), which is the author's to choose.

The cycle: new spaces from black holes, every arrangement existing

The author's · untestable in the modela strange loop, after Hofstadterupdated 25 Sep 2026
The model shows

The network model used here (Trugenberger's, not Smolin's) has no black holes and no expansion. It can map the ingredients (how stuck X is, the push, the burp, the room, the scrap), not count offspring.

The author's position: versions are not copies, neither the renamings of one arrangement nor identical arrangements in other spacetimes. So Smolin's route to quantum behaviour is not hers. She expects the missing rule of piece 9 to come from the loop itself: something about a strange loop that his picture does not use.

The measure, in the author's words (25 September): we are most likely to observe, and be part of, the strange loop whose arrangement has one of the largest possible infinities, one of the largest amounts of time, matter and energy in a stable phase. Our stable phase never really ends, because space expands and time continues, so it is a very populous spacetime, ending only when the last star is eaten by the last black hole; and the chance of our being at that late point is a smaller infinity, because fewer observers could have evolved to the point of observation by then. Ours, general knowledge, to verify The nearest published relatives are self-sampling arguments (Carter, Bostrom) and the argument for why observers find themselves in the stellar era (Dicke). The model cannot count observers; its counterpart is the map of which settings yield the most stable phase, matter and room per turn.

How it could be wrong

Smolin's argument: if a large object had copies, it would behave quantum-mechanically. The author's answer is that other spacetimes' arrangements do not count as copies.

Ours The counting already makes large objects classical without copies: a complicated arrangement has exactly one version (rung 0: a melt, and a sheet with three different defects, each have one).

It failed on the first: in a torus the directions do not open together. What survives is the fixed push and the gas result, and the next question is whether the joined cubes' partial opening is the start of something at a size the gas can finish.

Stuck arrangements inside the published model itself

The model shows

The model's author proposes dark matter as allotropes: regions of the network stuck in a different discrete arrangement, as diamond survives where graphite is more stable (Trugenberger 2024; the smallest example is Fig. 9 of his 2025 review, drawn by Eryk Kopczyński). These sit at λ = 1, inside his model, and they are the author's order-to-order question asked there.

Ours, exact If no edge carries three squares, the local term vanishes and the question does not depend on λ at all.

The drawn allotrope lives on an infinite hyperbolic graph, so there is no wiring to copy. Time as a fourth curled direction: a singleton beside the tied three, or all four tied

Measured: the push that is enough in three directions stalls in four (T33)VISION Update 25 · T33updated 25 Sep 2026, 14:30 ET
The author's idea

The change could be four-dimensional, with time uncurled by it too. Two ways that could go: time as a singleton direction beside the three space directions that are tied together, so that one direction opens on its own and three open together; or all four tied, which the relativity of time suggests, since something moving fast through space has its time run more slowly, so time and space are not separate things.

Ours The model has no time: a fourth direction of a torus is a direction like the others. What it can test is the pattern in which curled directions open: all four together, one alone and then three together, or one at a time. Those are what her two variants and our expectation predict, and they are distinct. Which direction is time, the model cannot say.

Next

Running (T33): the eight-link model at the smallest size where a flat four-torus exists beside the curled one (2,304 points). Two starts, sealed and given exactly the push their wall costs: a gas of fully curled 8-cubes, no direction singled out; and a torus with three directions curled and one already open. The pattern is read from how many directions are open at each point as the run goes. Her prediction: a tied pattern, all four together or one then three. Ours: one at a time, each rung with its own wall, as in the three-direction case.

It fails, for her picture, if the directions open one at a time.

Read (T33, 25 Sep): in every replica, from the three-curled torus and from the gas of 8-cubes, the push, exactly the cost of the cheapest first move, bought that one move and nothing followed: the bath was left empty and the next step is uphill. So no pattern was seen, tied or one at a time, and the verdict by the letter (NO CASCADE) says more about the push than about the directions. The lesson: in four directions the true cost of starting the change is the height of the pass over several moves, not the first move. The next test gives larger pushes; T39's eight-link cells, written before this was read, carry the same design and are expected to stall too.

What is new here, as far as we have found

We have not found published work that varies the coefficient of the model's local term, or that studies one ordered arrangement of it turning into another (literature check of 24 September, recorded in the repository). That is the ground of paper 1. The one place where the author's question meets the published model at its own value of λ is the allotrope (piece 12).

What is claimed, and by whom

The author claims that reality works this way. The claim at the top is hers, stated to be argued with. The model's results are narrower: in the model, piece 1 is established, and the others are measured, in progress or untested, as marked. No physicist has checked any of it yet.
