## 1. Repairs that apply across the set

1. **"Omega framework" appears nowhere** in the repository, on sidenerdapps.com/physics, or in any
   public page. The page is titled "Did Space Snap Open?". A recipient who searches the term finds
   nothing. Delete it.
2. **sidenerdapps.com/physics is not a working paper.** It is a 7,000-word account whose own byline
   says "told without hedges" and "written as the account would read with the theory accepted". A
   physicist sent there expecting a paper will read it as a claim of established results, which is
   the one impression the project's rules exist to prevent. In a first touch, link the repository
   (public, confirmed today; README discloses AI drafting) and, if the page is linked at all, describe
   it as what it is: "a plain-language account written as if the idea were true; the tests, the
   pre-registered predictions and the results that went against me are in the repository".
3. **The GHZ construction does not exist yet.** PREREGISTRATION T15 rung 3a is headed "written before
   any construction exists", and the same morning found two obstructions (loops with three-square edges
   cannot be joined by an edge; a symmetric triangle of loops cannot be linked by edges), so its
   definitions were changed before anything was built. The Pfister letter's "I'm constructing ... and
   enumerating exactly" is defensible; the Trugenberger letter's "the allowed configurations are
   exhaustively enumerated" is not. Say "pre-registered" and "the construction search is the next step",
   or run the construction step first (it is exact and takes days, not months) and write with a result.
   Recommended: run it first for the quantum letters, since the first question any of them will ask is
   "what did you get?"
4. **The pre-registered prediction is misstated.** The Trugenberger draft says "my pre-registered
   expectation is that it will remain classical". The record (PREREGISTRATION T15 rung 3a, committed) has
   Emily's prediction as STRONGLY CONTEXTUAL, with the classical expectation recorded beside it as the
   assistant's. The repository is public; the letters must match it.
5. **The hard-core rule is local**, not global: no two vertices may share more than two neighbours.
   What is global is its consequence for which joint configurations survive. "Global hard-core
   constraint" will read as a misunderstanding of the model to anyone who opens the paper.
6. **No letter discloses the AI drafting.** The README, the public page and the note already sent to
   Trugenberger all do. A recipient who clicks through will see it and wonder why the email did not.
   One sentence, the same in every letter: "The code and documents, and my drafts of this note, were
   prepared with an AI assistant (Claude); I have checked what I could against the papers and against
   exhaustive enumeration at 16 and 18 vertices, and no physicist has reviewed any of it."
7. **Send the reply to Trugenberger before anything else.** `reply_draft_2026-09-22.md` answers his
   questions with pictures, carries the N = 196 result that confirms his own postscript, and keeps to
   his model as that draft explains. His "hybrid transition" at N = 1024 is unpublished (the review's

### 5.1 Marija Vucelja, UVA Physics (new)

**Subject:** A sampling problem on constrained regular graphs: replica exchange stops making round trips near 500 vertices

Dear Professor Vucelja,

I'm a software engineer in Charlottesville with a hobby project reproducing Trugenberger's
combinatorial quantum gravity model: 4-regular bipartite graphs under a hard-core rule (no two vertices
share more than two neighbours), sampled by degree-preserving double-edge switches with Metropolis or
Glauber acceptance, with an energy built from square counts that orders the graph into a 2D lattice as
the coupling is lowered. Everything is public and pre-registered:
https://github.com/EmilySmithCreate/RecreationalPhysics. I'm writing because your work on the physics
of sampling and non-reversible chains is the tool I'm missing.

The problem. Single chains freeze at low coupling (acceptance below 1%). Replica exchange across the
coupling ladder works at 196 vertices, 5 to 15 round trips per replica, but at 484 the first replica
returned zero round trips in the same wall time, and the question those runs exist to settle, whether
the transition shows a jump at the same coupling in both directions, needs 484 and 676. A Wang-Landau
walk over the two-dimensional (squares, surplus squares) energy made zero round trips at 36 vertices
across a window spanning both phases, so its weights never converged. Ergodicity of the switch is
proved by exhaustive enumeration up to 18 vertices, and I have exact averages there that any sampler
must reproduce; ours does, to 0.03%.

The question: for a constrained graph ensemble with a bottleneck between two phases, what would you
try first, a lifted or non-reversible version of the switch chain, a different ladder, or a different
move altogether? And what is the first diagnostic you would run to tell a mixing failure from a
reachability one?

The code and documents, and my drafts of this note, were prepared with an AI assistant (Claude); I
have checked what I could against the papers and against exhaustive enumeration at 16 and 18
vertices, and no physicist has reviewed any of it. I'd be glad of 20 to 30 minutes over coffee at UVA,
or a one-line reply if that's easier. [I've written to your colleague Israel Klich about a separate
piece of the same project.]

Best,
Emily Smith

### 5.2 Abdelmalek Abdesselam, UVA Mathematics

**Subject:** Labelled versus isomorphism-class ensembles of a constrained graph model, with exact counts at 16 and 18 vertices

Dear Professor Abdesselam,

I'm a software engineer in Charlottesville with a hobby project on a published statistical model of
graphs (Trugenberger's combinatorial quantum gravity): 4-regular bipartite graphs under a hard-core
rule, with an energy built from square counts. I'm writing to you because your recent papers on tuples
of commuting permutations do the bookkeeping I have been doing by hand, passing from labelled objects
to isomorphism classes with the automorphism symmetry factor.

What I have. At 16 and 18 vertices every valid graph is enumerated (1,785,021,235,200 labelled graphs
in 26 classes at 18), with each class's automorphism count, so the labelled ensemble (class weight
(n!)² over the automorphism count) and the class-counted ensemble (weight 1) can both be evaluated
exactly. They differ: at 16 vertices the most symmetric class, the 4-cube, goes from 1.3% to 20% of the
ensemble. At 160 vertices, measured by computer, a melted graph has trivial automorphism group, the
flat torus has 320, and a shattered state of ten 4-cubes about 10^29. The model's authors set the
factor aside in 2020 on the grounds that a typical graph has no automorphisms, which is true of the
random phase and false of every ordered state.

Two questions; either would help.

1. For a constrained ensemble like this (regular, bipartite, hard-core), is there a species or
   generating-function treatment that carries the 1/|Aut| weight and the constraint together, so that
   the class-counted ensemble can be studied beyond the sizes enumeration reaches?
2. Are there known cases where the labelled and class-counted ensembles of the same energy differ in
   the order of a phase transition? I know of Evnin and Krioukov (PRL 2025) for unlabelled networks and
   would be glad of others.

Everything is public: https://github.com/EmilySmithCreate/RecreationalPhysics (the counts are in
`results/ergodicity_small.csv`). The code and documents, and my drafts of this note, were prepared with
an AI assistant (Claude); I have checked what I could against the papers and against the enumeration,
and no physicist or mathematician has reviewed any of it. I'd be very happy if the outcome were that you
showed me where the counting is wrong. Twenty minutes in office hours, or a line by email, would be a
great help.

Best,
Emily Smith

### 5.3 Israel Klich, UVA Physics

**Subject:** A GHZ-type construction on constrained graphs: is the check I've written down enough?

Dear Professor Klich,

I'm a software engineer in Charlottesville doing a hobby project on Trugenberger's combinatorial
quantum gravity model: 4-regular bipartite graphs under a hard-core rule (no two vertices share more
than two neighbours), sampled by Monte Carlo, with everything pre-registered and public
(https://github.com/EmilySmithCreate/RecreationalPhysics). I'm writing to you because your Motzkin and
Fredkin work is the closest thing I know to my objects: uniform ensembles over a globally constrained
combinatorial set, except that mine carry no amplitudes at all.

The narrow piece. I've pre-registered a GHZ-style test (PREREGISTRATION.md, T15 rung 3a). Three
4-cycles sit pairwise linked in a flat sheet. A rigid defect attached by one edge at one of two points
of a loop is the setting; the outcome is which of the loop's two swappable points the defect landed on,
read from the automorphisms of the whole arrangement; each of Mermin's four contexts gives a support
of outcome triples. I know that if outcomes are functions of one configuration drawn from one
context-independent distribution, a joint distribution exists by construction, so the only route to a
GHZ pattern is that attaching all three detectors changes which configurations are valid under the
hard-core rule. My first check is no-disturbance at the level of supports: loop A's outcomes must not
depend on the settings at B and C.

The question I'd value your view on: is that check sufficient to separate a genuine effect from one
built into the outcome definition? I gather that ordinary classical data tables have been shown to
pass the sheaf test, which is why I want the check attacked before I attach importance to any result.
If the construction is circular, that is the most useful answer.

For the record, my own pre-registered prediction is the quantum pattern; the counter-expectation
recorded beside it is that a complete labelling is a hidden variable and the result stays classical.
[Either: "The construction step gave: ..." Or: "The construction search is my next step; I'm asking
before the result so that the check is judged on its own."]

The code and documents, and my drafts of this note, were prepared with an AI assistant (Claude); I have
checked what I could against the papers and against exhaustive enumeration at 16 and 18 vertices, and
no physicist has reviewed any of it. I'd be glad of 20 to 30 minutes over coffee at UVA, or a one-line
