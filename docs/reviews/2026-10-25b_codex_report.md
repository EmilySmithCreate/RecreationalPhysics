### E. Physics from memory and published sources

**15. Weak interaction and neutral-layer analogy need narrower wording — confidence: high.** **Where:** `VISION.md:3655–3688`. Today's massive W and Z bosons do limit ordinary weak interactions to approximately `10^-18` meters (CERN), but “spin-one carriers imply opposite charges attract” is not a general theorem for chiral, non-Abelian weak interactions. In the high-temperature symmetric electroweak plasma, screening depends on field sector and scale; “all weak fields are simply short ranged” is too strong without specifying static electric versus magnetic modes. A membrane-like neutral double layer *can* have surface tension, but its tension does not follow from total curl-minus-uncurl being zero. **Right:** Keep the owner's layer as an additional rule she accepted, and mark the analogy to real weak charge or plasma as unverified physics, not a derivation. **Evidence:** CERN's W/Z description and the separate hard-rule and L4 statements.

**16. CP, CPT and paired-universe physics are being conflated — confidence: high.** **Where:** `VISION.md:3721–3728,3809–3815,3847–3852`. CP (charge conjugation plus spatial reflection) is violated by some weak interactions, while CPT (which also reverses time) is a symmetry under the usual assumptions of local relativistic quantum field theory. But this does **not** imply that every antimatter-dominated daughter obeying the same laws must literally evolve backward according to its own clock. A local future-directed antimatter universe can obey the same equations without being the exact CPT transform of our entire cosmic history. Boyle, Finn and Turok's 2018 model specifically puts a CPT-reflected epoch **before** the same Big Bang, not two simultaneous daughters of a parent black hole. **Right:** Call BFT a published mathematical cousin, not the same mechanism or an inevitable consequence of CPT. **Evidence:** their 2018 *Physical Review Letters* abstract and the CPT/CP distinction. This matters to claim step 9's independent clocks.

**17. Other analogies should not be presented as proved consequences — confidence: high.** **Where:** Updates 113, 116, 118–120. Coleman's 1977 false-vacuum work supports a wall-cost versus bulk-gain nucleation barrier in a scalar field, not automatically a shell in this graph toy; the familiar three-dimensional classical analogy is `E(R)=4*pi*R²*sigma-(4*pi/3)*R³*Delta_rho`, while quantum tunneling needs a bounce calculation. Lieb–Robinson's 1972 finite-speed bounds assume local quantum interactions and do not apply to arbitrary nonlocal rewrites. Causal-set growth is a sequential birth of order-related elements, not a proof of growth by paired torus rows, and Wolfram-model causal graphs are event-dependency structures, not an established derived electromagnetic speed.
