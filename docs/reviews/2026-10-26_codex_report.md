### 2.3 Anticurl and breakage (Update 127)

**F14.** The identity is exact. With 3N links and four links per square, Σ_e (4 − S_e) = 12N − 4S = 4(3N − S), so 16(3N − S) = 4(anticurl − curl) and H = 4 anticurl + 4(λ − 1) curl. The rod's curl 576 (two curled axes, 288 links each at five squares) and anticurl 0 reproduce. Confidence: high.

**F15.** The anticurl-on-broken claim is overstated. See summary 4. Over the 95 end states with a broken point, the share of anticurl on links touching one is below 0.85 in 16 (minimum 0.27, pair tie λ = 1.20, E = 512); four states (pair tie, λ = 1.20, E = 64 and 128) carry anticurl of 8 to 16 with no broken point at all, on points at d = 2 and 3. So anticurl is not only where d > 3. Update 127's "(1)" should give the range over all 100 or restrict itself to the BREAKS runs. Confidence: high (recomputed over all 100).

**F16.** The curl-beside-broken comparison has the wrong baseline and a wrong adjective. The baseline is the share of all links within one link of a broken point: 0.63 to 0.99 in the BREAKS runs, so "near a broken point" covers nearly the whole rod and the test cannot discriminate. The right baseline would be the share of the curled-axis links (the only links that can carry curl at the start) near a broken point, or a comparison with the rod's own geometry. The ratios curl share / link share run 0.95 to 1.01 untied at λ = 1.40 (chance), 0.69 to 0.96 untied at 1.25, and 0.42 to 0.94 tied. "A little less often than chance" fits none of these groups. The LOG's two cited pairs (0.88/0.95; 0.28/0.68) exist (pair tie, λ = 1.40, E = 512 replica 0 and E = 256 replica 0). Confidence: high.

**F17.** "The total moves toward her rule" is a fair description and a weak one. curl − anticurl in the four most broken untied runs is −4, 8, 20, 16 and in the tied ones 40, −20 (reproduced). But curl − anticurl = 4(S − 3N) is a count of squares, and a push of 512 that turns curl into anticurl lowers S toward 3N on the way to a melt; the number says the square count passed near the flat value, not that the state resembles a rule-obeying X. "In the runs that broke least it stays at 200 to 430" is wrong as written: the runs that moved least stay at 552 (curl 560, anticurl 8); 432 is the least-broken STAYS replica of a cell that also broke. Confidence: high.

**F18.** "Breakage is opening past flat" is a fair reading of d > 3, but d is a count, not a geometry. dimension.py's own docstring says d is a square-count signature and the converses do not hold. A point with d = 4 has one more pair of links closing no square than a flat point; whether that is "space bent the wrong way" is an interpretation. Update 127 marks the physics "ours, unverified" correctly. Confidence: high.

**F19.** The claim that every rod look ran an X her hard rule forbids is right, and its consequence is not drawn. See summary 5. The rule was adopted in principle on 25 October (Update 105) with its form (hard or soft) left to her; both forms make a state with curl 576 and anticurl 0 either forbidden or costly. The pair tie's a = 4(λ − 1) is the untied curling cost; with anticurl = curl the energy is 4λ curl, so at λ = 1 curling is no longer free, and all the walls, patch energies and releases of Updates 124 and 125 are for the wrong family. Update 127 says the rod runs "cannot tell"; it should also say the numbers would change. Confidence: high on the arithmetic; "forbids" depends on the form she chooses.

### 2.4 The hard-rule looks (Update 128)

**F20.** The single-switch enumeration is complete for "from a point", and the tori are vertex-transitive on both sides. moves() fixes u1 = 0 and ranges over every v1, every same-side u2 and every v2; the counts 3,514 (flat, dS −8: 3,224, −7: 80, −6: 178, −4: 32) and 3,946 (rod, dS −10 to −4) reproduce. I repeated the survey from five random points on each torus, on both sides of the bipartition: identical counts and dS histograms. Both tori are Cayley graphs of abelian groups, so translations carry any point to any other and one step carries side 0 to side 1; the numerical check agrees. Confidence: high.

**F21.** The pair search's undo exclusion is wrong, and every "restoring pair" is the undo. second_moves excludes the undo only in the form (u1, v2, u2, v1) with the u's as the switch's first and third points. The same undo written from the other side, (v2, u1, v1, u2), is a legal second switch with both first points in the ball (v1 and v2 are in it by construction) and is not excluded. The script found exactly 3,514 pairs for 3,514 first switches, at radius 1 and at radius 2, every one with (Δcurl, Δanticurl) = (0, 0): one undo each. My version with both forms excluded finds 0 restoring pairs at either radius; my version with the original exclusion finds 3,514 and every one returns the starting edge set. So: (a) the committed script does not compare edge sets, so the LOG's "checked by edge set" is not from it; (b) Update 128's "every pair ... puts the space back exactly as it was" is the bug described as a result; (c) the corrected conclusion, "no second switch within two links of a first switch out of the flat 6 × 6 × 8 torus restores the square count, other than undoing it", is stronger and does not change the record's bottom line. Confidence: high (both versions run).

**F22.** The damaged-state counts come from code not in the repository. See summary 2 and section 3 for the recomputation. Confidence: high that the committed script does not produce them.

**F23.** "Clean space is frozen under the hard rule with the published move" follows for the flat torus to two switches; "or curled" does not. For the flat 6 × 6 × 8 torus: no single switch keeps S (complete), and no pair within two links does (complete after F21). Not shown: three or more switches, pairs farther apart than two links, and any rule-obeying curled state (none has been built; the rod is not one). Update 128's "Not shown: that no three-or-more-switch move exists; the search stopped at two" is stated clearly; "pairs farther than two links" is not, and the N = 18 two-dimensional listing in the repository (nine rule-keeping classes in one piece under single switches, three of them flat) shows that at small sizes rule-keeping single switches between clean states do exist, so "frozen" is a property of large perfect tori under this move, not of the rule. Confidence: high.

**F24.** The soft form is not "the hard rule with excursions of any length allowed". μ(S − 3N)² charges a single switch out of flat space 16μ to 64μ (dS = −4 to −8) and a k-switch excursion of order μ(4k)² at worst; with μ large those are exponentially suppressed, not allowed. What the soft form does is make the rule's violations costly rather than forbidden, so that a return path exists in principle at any length but at a cost that grows with the excursion's depth squared. The sentence should say that. Confidence: high (arithmetic).

## 4. Published work that bears on this

These are mathematical relatives to apply, not evidence that her picture is right; her aim is to use existing mathematics, so a close match is good news. "To verify" where I did not check the source for this audit.

**Constrained Monte Carlo moves that keep a global count, and their ergodicity.**

- Kawasaki dynamics (K. Kawasaki, Phys. Rev. 145, 224, 1966): the standard conserved-order-parameter dynamics; the analogue of "moves that keep S fixed". To verify.
- Kinetically constrained models: Fredrickson and Andersen (PRL 53, 1244, 1984); review by Ritort and Sollich, Adv. Phys. 52, 219 (2003). In these models a constraint on which moves are allowed freezes ordered regions and lets only defects move ("facilitation"), which is exactly Update 128's (3): damaged states move rarely, clean ones not at all. Their literature has the tools for asking whether the constrained dynamics is ergodic or breaks into frozen sectors. To verify.
- Exponential random graphs with a constrained subgraph count: Strauss (SIAM Rev. 28, 513, 1986); Park and Newman (PRE 70, 066146, 2004); Chatterjee and Diaconis (Ann. Stat. 41, 2428, 2013). Fixing or rewarding the count of a small subgraph (triangles, squares) is known to make the ensemble "degenerate": it condenses into very ordered or very disordered graphs, which is the known failure mode of a rule like S = 3N. To verify.
- Degree-preserving switches and their ergodicity are already in the record (Taylor 1981; [Swap17]); I did not find in the bibliography a result on switch ergodicity under an additional global count constraint, and did not find one from memory either. Where I looked: REFERENCES.bib at the pinned commit and my own knowledge.

**Paired curvature defects on lattices.** Disclinations (a point with too few or too many neighbours) and dislocations (a bound pair of opposite disclinations) in two-dimensional crystals: Kosterlitz and Thouless (J. Phys. C 6, 1181, 1973); Halperin and Nelson (PRL 41, 121, 1978); Young (PRB 19, 1855, 1979); Nelson, Defects and Geometry in Condensed Matter Physics (2002); Bowick and Giomi, Adv. Phys. 58, 449 (2009), "Two-dimensional matter: order, curvature and defects"; Seung and Nelson (PRA 38, 1005, 1988) on a disclination's energy in a curved membrane. The combinatorial Gauss-Bonnet theorem says that on a closed surface of Euler characteristic χ the total disclination charge is 6χ for a triangulation (0 on a torus): positive and negative defects must balance on a torus, which is the closest published form of her "curl and anticurl always come in pairs". Caution already in the record (O156): the toy's S_e is a count on an abstract graph, not the face count of an embedding, so the theorem applies only once a surface is defined. To verify.

**Universes in pairs or from black holes.** Boyle, Finn and Turok 2018 (in the bibliography); Popławski 2010 (in); Frolov, Markov and Mukhanov, "Through a black hole into a new universe?" (PLB 216, 272, 1989) and PRD 41, 383 (1990); Easson and Brandenberger, "Universe generation from black hole interiors" (JHEP 06, 024, 2001); Smolin, "Did the universe evolve?" (CQG 9, 173, 1992), cosmological natural selection (Update 33 cites Smolin from general knowledge; check the bibliography entry); Pathria, "The Universe as a Black Hole" (Nature 240, 298, 1972; named in TASKS as owed). On a matter-antimatter pair: Sakharov (JETP Lett. 5, 24, 1967) for the three conditions a matter surplus needs; Cohen, De Rújula and Glashow, "A matter-antimatter universe?" (ApJ 495, 539, 1998) for why antimatter domains cannot exist inside our universe, which her picture avoids by putting the anti-surplus in a separate daughter. To verify.

**Monopoles on lattices.** DeGrand and Toussaint 1980 (named in Update 122, not in the bibliography at this commit); Banks, Myerson and Kogut, "Phase transitions in Abelian lattice gauge theories" (NPB 129, 493, 1977); Polyakov, "Compact gauge fields and the infrared catastrophe" (PLB 59, 82, 1975); Guth, "Existence proof of a nonconfining phase in four-dimensional U(1) lattice gauge theory" (PRD 21, 2291, 1980). These define a monopole as the integer flux through a closed surface of plaquettes, the construction Update 122 describes, and show that in compact lattice electromagnetism monopoles drive a phase transition. To verify.

I did not find, in the bibliography at the pinned commit or from memory, a published model combining a direction tie of the pair form, a hard square-count rule and a bipartite switch dynamics; that is a statement about where I looked, not a novelty claim.
