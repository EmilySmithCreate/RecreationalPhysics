A switch exchanges the partners of two links. A square is a four-edge cycle. The *hard-core rule* limits common neighbors; the owner's additional *hard rule* fixes the total square count, S = N in four links or S = 3N in six. These are different requirements. A graph can pass the former and fail the latter.

### 3. The hard-rule counts as enumerations

**F11 — The four-switch prune is not justified, so the claimed enumeration is incomplete. High confidence on the defect; no claim that a real opening has been found.** Where: `hard_rule_quads_look.py:225–229`, `need3 <= gmax`, inside `run_first_q`. Here `gmax = 10` bounds the gain of **one** switch. After two switches, **two** remain, so that bound cannot by itself discard every prefix below −10. A concrete sequence on the 6×6×8 torus is:

```text
(0,48,2,50), (1,49,3,51), (0,50,2,48), (1,51,3,49)
ΔS:             −6,             −5,             +5,             +6
```

All four switches are legal. The second is not the first's undo, their u endpoints lie in the restricted ball, and the final graph is BACK. The −11 prefix is discarded by the committed search. Thus at least its claimed complete sequence/back counts omit admissible paths. This example returns the start and **does not refute ZERO REAL MOVES**; it shows why that conclusion is not certified by this enumeration. A tighter theorem excluding *all nontrivial* recoveries from discarded prefixes could rescue it, but none is supplied. A safe generic recovery bound at that point is two times the one-switch bound. The one-switch prune in the triples search is appropriate because only one switch remains there.

### 9. Physics and mathematics checked against published work

**F29 — The KCM analogy is useful; the eight-edge result is not a four-switch theorem for this toy. High confidence within the passages checked.** Where: constrained-moves note, handoff Task C, quads header, two-stories dictionary. Ritort and Sollich distinguish isolated frozen configurations from effective irreducibility of typical states; they describe Kawasaki exchange as conserving concentration. Those comparisons are appropriate, with the note's stated source limits. Nishimura's section 4 constructs degree/triangle-constrained graph spaces and defines a k-edge swap as one cyclic endpoint permutation. That is not k successive two-edge switches through hard-core-valid intermediate graphs. His body gives an eight-edge separation example; its bound does not predict the minimal move order for square-constrained bipartite six-link tori. The note mostly preserves the scope, but the shorthand “8 links, which is four switches” and “unless” in the dictionary do not. Sources: [Nishimura 2017, §4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.01951); [Ritort–Sollich 2003, §§3.1.1, 3.2, 4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0210382).

**F30 — The curvature/elasticity cautions are substantially correct. High confidence on the arithmetic; source-checked in part.** Where: paired-defects note and dictionary. A two-dimensional Gauss–Bonnet balance is a total topological-charge condition, not necessarily one-to-one adjacent pairs. Filling every four-cycle of a four-regular graph gives χ = S−N, but that filled complex need not be a surface. A closed cubical 3-manifold needs specified cells and incidence conditions; a count of cube subgraphs is not that structure. The note correctly distinguishes this from total scalar curvature in three dimensions and says the toy lacks long-range elastic strain. In ordinary elasticity a dislocation line's energy includes a long-range contribution depending on cutoff/system size; the toy's finite local endpoint cost alone is not a measured elastic tension. I checked relevant Bowick–Giomi passages on charge balance and defect energetics, not its entire 102 pages. Source: [Bowick–Giomi 2009](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0812.3064). A Burgers vector—closure failure of a circuit around the defect—would substantiate the geometric name; this look does not compute one.

**F31 — The ensemble note gives the right warning, not a blanket finite-penalty equivalence. High confidence.** Where: subgraph-count note and soft-form proposals. Costeniuc et al.'s Gaussian-ensemble equivalence is a thermodynamic result with supporting-parabola/entropy regularity conditions and appropriate scaling; corners can require an infinite parameter. It does not identify every finite μ(S−3N)² chain with the hard ensemble. At fixed finite graph, μ→∞ suppresses off-fiber states when the target is feasible; that is distinct from equivalence of large-system ensembles and from mixing between all allowed states. The note largely says this and correctly flags its N² penalty versus an extensive N-scaled one. Carry those limits into proposed runs; softening a count constraint also cannot prove the underlying hard-core switch space connected for arbitrary N. Source: [Costeniuc, Ellis, Touchette & Turkington 2006, §V](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0505218).

**F32 — Jaffe does not support the claimed comparison with the Lamb shift. High confidence.** Where: previous report F35; O159's accepted caution. His introduction states that Casimir gives no greater or lesser support for vacuum energy than other one-loop QED effects, explicitly naming vacuum polarization in the Lamb shift. His paper supports computing Casimir forces without assigning physical reality to zero-point energy. It does **not** make the Lamb shift stronger evidence for literal fleeting particle pairs. Correct the reviewer's comparison, and distinguish useful perturbative descriptions from observed short-lived objects. Source: [Jaffe 2005, introduction](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/0503158).

## Published work to apply or finish checking

These are mathematical relatives, not confirmations of the physical picture. The search for this audit used arXiv, the papers' primary PDFs, author-hosted material, and primary textbook pages with the terms “swap connectivity triangle constraints,” “multiple edge switches,” “kinetically constrained models,” “Gaussian ensemble equivalence,” “dislocation climb,” “prismatic loop,” and “integrate-and-fire threshold reset.” I did not conduct an exhaustive novelty search.

- **Nishimura (2017)**, *Swap connectivity … and disconnectivity for triangle constraints*, and **Tabourier, Roth and Cointet (2011)**, *Generating constrained random graphs using multiple edge switches*. These directly address disconnected constrained graph spaces and larger moves. Nishimura §4 checked; Tabourier abstract checked, full paper still to read. They do not settle square-count plus hard-core ergodicity here. [Nishimura](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.01951) · [Tabourier et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1012.3023).
- **Fredrickson–Andersen (1984), Kawasaki (1966), Ritort–Sollich (2003)**. Useful distinctions: an isolated ordered configuration, a connected typical sector, kinetic facilitation and a conserved state-space restriction. This audit checked the review's relevant sections; the two original papers remain indirectly checked through it, not newly read in full. [Review](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0210382).
- **Costeniuc, Ellis, Touchette and Turkington (2006)**. The Gaussian ensemble is the relevant published framework for a quadratic penalty, subject to its scaling and regularity conditions; equilibrium equivalence does not imply rapid sampling. §V checked. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0505218).
- **Bowick–Giomi (2009)** and **Seung–Nelson (1988)** for defects and elastic relaxation; **Hirth–Lothe, Theory of Dislocations (1982)** for Volterra constructions, Burgers circuits and plane insertion. Bowick–Giomi relevant passages checked; Seung–Nelson and the book are still to verify at their original sources for this audit. **A. F. Bower, Applied Mechanics of Solids, §5.8** supplies a directly checked treatment of dislocation-loop energetics and prismatic loops. This is the nearest mathematical setting for identifying the inserted strip's ends and understanding which elastic contributions this graph energy omits. [Bowick–Giomi](https://arxiv.org/abs/0812.3064) · [Bower §5.8](https://solidmechanics.org/Text/Chapter5_8/Chapter5_8.php).
- **Gerstner, Kistler, Naud and Paninski (2014), Neuronal Dynamics**, §1.3: a published example of accumulation, threshold, reset and a construction-controlled repetition period. This is a mathematical accounting comparison, not a claim that a black hole is a neuron. The section was checked directly. For a constant ideal feed, threshold minus reset divided by feed sets the ideal period; leakage/caps and stochastic proposals change the details. The repository's graph-free countermodel is the more directly matched control. A mechanism claim needs an observable that its accounting control cannot reproduce. [Primary online text](https://neuronaldynamics.epfl.ch/online/Ch1.S3.html).

I did not find, in this limited search, a theorem certifying the particular four-switch pruning rule or the claim that the row-segment family obeys S = N/3N. The explicit calculations above show why neither claim should currently be treated as established.
