## Where it agrees with the record, and where the record already goes further

**Section 1, the relational substrate.** Hers; VISION claims 1 and 3, and Update 12 (the names of the
points are a convenience). "An opening need not create new points": the toy's count is fixed and every run
so far keeps it; a varying count is parked by claim 3. Agrees.

**Section 2, the slab.** The slab 4 × L × L with one curled direction is the stand-in for X since Update 47
and stands untied since Update 58. The side of 4 is the toy's smallest curled side; the "L × L × 6" she
remembers is the product of the two-direction change, the tube six around (O116), not a form of X. **New in
her words:** the two open directions are the parent's own, not severed at the horizon; the black hole is the
parent's geometry curling in on itself, "whirlpool-like", and not a small slab contained inside. *Ours:* this
revises her knot-and-shell picture of the same morning (notebook: a 64-point knot with a 4 × L × L shell
inside the horizon). In the toy the parent's own geometry curling one direction is the fold, piece 11, and
every attempt to make it happen from energy alone has ended in damage that heals, not a curl (T21, T26, T27,
T34, T42, T57; O114). So this reading does not change what piece 11 waits on: something that gathers energy
in one place and a reason for a curl to be favoured there (a carrier, piece 8). Both the continuous slab and
the graph as descriptions: the toy has only the graph.

**Section 3, growth of the black hole as g rising.** Hers. *Exact, from the toy's own machinery:* in a
sealed box the temperature is not imposed but read off the stores (Q12; O108: `bath_T` is the mean store
energy). So "energy entering raises g" is what a sealed box does by construction: a feed that raises the
stores' energy raises the g the chain feels. That makes H2 (below) shorter than the document expects.
"The amount of curled space is not a fundamental quantity; a larger horizon need not mean more degrees of
freedom": hers, and it is her answer to her own question of the morning (how the parent can hold more than
640 points). The toy cannot represent it: its count is fixed and a black hole's inside is the set of points
that curled. See section 9.

**Section 4, λ.** Hers; Update 23 (a constant of our loop), now stated to hold across parent and daughter.
The formula, ε = 4(λ − 1), and "ε = 1 at 1.25" are exact (Q1; Update 7). "A complete conversion releases N
units provided no defect keeps part of it": measured in two directions, where 83 of 100 cold end states are
exactly flat (O116) and the rest keep a scrap of fixed energy (O107). In three directions the slab's one
opening releases the same a = 4(λ − 1) per point (Update 58). Agrees.

**Section 5, the waiting-time law.** The two-channel formula is paper 1's Eq. for τ_AB (its channel costs
32 − 16λ and 64 − 40λ, 12 and 14 at λ = 1.25, offered 3 and 2 times a sweep), checked by the outside review
of 9 October to within 0.9 % of the omitted moves (`curled_torus/author_feedback_2026-10-09.md`). It is
measured to hold with nothing fitted: first exits are one memoryless population at 1.005 ± 0.016 of the
count (T59, O112), κ between 0.56 and 0.68 across the window (T22). **But it is the law of the four-link
tube, where the single move is the whole barrier.** It is not the slab's. For the slab the single move
costs 36 (O41) and the patch that grows is about 7.5 columns at λ = 1.25, a completed-patch energy of
about 225 (T54, with O118's correction that these are completed-patch energies, not barriers along a path).
What the slab does as g rises has been run three ways: in a bath it stays until the bath is hot enough to
break flat space (T56's untied control; T53 for the second direction of the rod); given one spark of 128 it
opens a scatter of pairs and closes them again (T65, FALLS BACK 31 of 32); fed at a steady rate it opens
from many spots and breaks before a third is open (the sandbox look of 16 October, not evidence). So the
document's "increasing g decreases the expected waiting time" is measured for the tube and, for the slab, the
wait that shortens is the wait to damage. *Ours:* the hazard for the slab is not known in closed form; what
is known is that no g opens it cleanly in the protocols run.

**Section 6, probabilistic nucleation.** Hers: no hard threshold; the chance rises with energy. The toy
agrees at fixed g: first exits are memoryless (T58, T59), which is the Poisson hazard the survival law
needs. With g rising in time the integral form is untested; it is cheap to check on the tube (a feed into
the tube's stores, exits timed), and a sandbox look would say whether the hazard follows the instantaneous
g. "The first escape does not guarantee a front": measured. In two directions a spark of 12 always converted
(320 runs, Q12), and whether a first exit in a warm bath commits is not run (TASKS, 13 October, item 5); in three the first exits fall back (T65).
So the three outcomes of H3 are already distinct in the toy, and the registered words already separate
them (OPENS, PARTIAL, FALLS BACK, DAMAGED).

**Section 7, two ways to pay the front.** Mechanism A, a reservoir paying the local costs, is the bath and
the feed (T53, T56, the feed look). Mechanism B, the release paying the next step, is T48's protocol, the
released energy kept where it is released: in two directions it runs as one front (ONE SPACE at λ = 1.25);
in three the two-curled rod opened one direction and stopped at 1.25, and both into one space in 5 of 16 at
1.40 with damage in 7 (O91). The accounting she writes, H_curl + E_R + E_other constant, is the sealed box
of Q12, conserved to the last bit, with the leak as E_other. Her concern that release destabilises what has
opened is measured and bounded: too little room leaves flaws, never a melt (T9, T18; O121, O123). The three
timescales she names exist as measurements: the front's pace (T47, T50), healing (T66), and damage against
opening (T65's end graphs). "Stretching, loss of order and disconnection are distinct": they are the toy's
own reading classes (open, curled, damaged by the local count d; pieces by `pieces_of`). *Measured:* untied,
the tube never stretches by itself (T61); it widens only under a price on loops of six (T62, T64), and then
flawed nearly everywhere.

**Section 8, the sealed results.** All as recorded (Q12; T9; O14), with two corrections of wording. "Melts
into disorder" is withdrawn by O123: a sealed tube cannot melt at these curling costs; with too little room
it ends damaged, keeping fifteen squares in sixteen. And the release is exactly 4(λ − 1) per point where the
end state is flat, approximately so where it keeps a scrap.

**Section 9, points as bookkeeping.** Hers, and the four distinctions are useful. The toy has only the
first: fixed vertices. It has no object that plays "effective point", no definition of a fundamental degree
of freedom beyond a link, and seeds are places, not points. "Curled remnants might be incorporated": in the
toy they are not; separate curled pieces never join (O85, which her section 13 keeps), and the knots are
stuck at 11 a move (O107). *Ours:* her position makes the point count a property of the description, which
is the interchangeable-points knob's reading (Update 12: renamings that preserve every relation are not
different worlds); the toy's one place where "the same structure, two descriptions" already exists is the
renaming count (Q15). A count that varies is a decision not made (claim 3), and it would be a new kind of
state under S1.

**Section 10, daughter universes.** Hers. *What changes:* "the earlier whole-universe-reset model ... is no
longer a requirement." The loop (Updates 23, 26, 33, 43) counted per turn; a daughter that opens while the
black hole stays in the parent is Smolin's tree, not a turn. Both are hers to hold; the record notes that
her strange-loop measure (the turn as the unit) needs the reset, and the daughter picture needs a different
count (per generation, which O101's reading already flagged as the branching trap). The toy is one graph:
a region that curls and opens again is a healed patch of the parent unless something distinguishes a
daughter from it, and nothing in the toy does. H7 needs that definition before any run.

**Section 11, energy across generations.** The toy conserves energy exactly inside one box and has no
generations. *Exact, in the toy:* a wall is a hill, not a toll (O71), and a fold costs exactly what the burp
returns, a per point, so a cycle at fixed count nets zero; "cumulative energy unbounded" can only mean
unbounded turns, never net creation, which is what her last paragraph there says.

**Section 13, the constraints.** All consistent with the rules. Two of ours to add: never "melts" for the
burp's own heat (O123), and never the tube's waiting-time law for the slab (above).
