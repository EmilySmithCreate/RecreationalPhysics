## 3. The speed test in soft space (piece 10: time and the speed limit)

**What it asks.** Soft space (term 7 on the programme page) puts one number on each point that slows its clock and
stretches its links, and it moves in waves. Does a disturbance travel at one steady speed, the same in every direction
of flat space, and does a flaw in the wiring travel at that speed too?

**Known.**
- *Exact:* soft space's waves have one speed at long wavelengths, set by the field's stiffness κ (ω² = κ(2 − 2 cos k)).
- *Measured:* in the stiff toy (no soft space) no disturbance travels at a steady pace (T75).
- *Built:* soft space's first stage is built and checked; the test is not yet registered.
- *General knowledge, to verify:* on a regular grid, waves are one speed in every direction only at long wavelengths,
  so the test is scored on pulses several links wide.

**Predicted (ours, the brief).** Disturbances in the field travel at one speed, the same in every direction. The test
fails if field disturbances and wiring flaws travel at different speeds, or if the speed depends on direction.

**What you said before (piece 10).** "Light moves one link per tick; matter moves slower because its twists must
manoeuvre." Read literally, that predicts the field's waves at one speed and flaws slower, which is the case the brief
calls a failure. That tension is worth deciding before the run.

**Asked of you:**
1. Your prediction: (a) one speed for everything; (b) the field's waves at one speed, flaws slower (your piece 10 words);
   (c) no steady speed. *Answered 25 October: "One speed for all flat space. If flaws create curl or curve they can
   slow things." (Update 115).*

## 4. The pull test in soft space (piece 8: curve-first gravity)

**What it asks.** Two knots (relics) as sources of soft space's field in a sealed six-link space: do they pull on each
other, how does the pull fall with distance, and does it grow with their energy?

**Known.**
- *Exact:* in the stiff toy no pull crosses a distance (flat space is stiff and passes a pull only a few steps; O22,
  O56).
- *Exact (O61):* a massless field on the points, sourced by relics, gives a pull of Newton's shape on fixed wirings.
- *Ours, said plainly:* the field and its coupling are put in, and the 1/r² law with them. So an attraction is close to
  built in; what the test can really fail on is whether the toy's own rewiring **screens** the pull (a gap reappears) or
  whether it follows the wiring rather than the energy.
- *General knowledge, to verify:* with only two open directions the pull would go as −ln r, and in Einstein's gravity
  with two space directions masses at rest feel no pull; hence six links.

**Predicted (ours, the brief).** Like sources attract; the force falls as 1/r² and scales with the product of the two
energies. It fails if the rewiring screens it or it scales with the wiring.

**What you said before (piece 8).** Space has its own drive to curve; matter supplies the energy that starts it;
wherever Einstein's account has been measured, yours must give the same numbers.

**Asked of you:**
1. Your prediction: attract or not; falls as 1/r² or otherwise; grows with energy or with something else.
   *Answered 25 October: "Pull, only at very short distances. Distance at high g can be greater but still very
   short." It differs from ours (long-range 1/r²), so the test can separate them. Asked: about the toy, or about
   reality, where gravity reaches across galaxies? (Update 115).*
2. Agree to a held-out separation range or size (rule 14). # #
