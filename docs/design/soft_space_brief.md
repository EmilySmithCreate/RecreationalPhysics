# Soft space: the brief (draft, 20 October 2026)

*Written on the author's decision of 20 October (VISION Update 76): "Yes we need soft space. It's time." This is the
brief that VISION Update 40, ASSUMPTIONS O103 (f) and `docs/design/gravity_brief.md` (section 7) call
`gravity_field_brief.md`; it carries her name for it, since the ingredient is asked to do more than carry gravity. Ours
throughout, for her decisions under S1. Nothing is built until she has chosen. Physics stated from general knowledge is
marked "to verify". At λ ≠ 1 the toy with this ingredient is our family, never combinatorial quantum gravity.*

## Why now

Three tests on 20 October asked the toy's space to do something and found it stiff. Every move out of flat space costs 32
(exact, O22); the space a burp makes is softer, but its cheapest flaw still costs 20 (T75 Amendment 3).
- **No signal.** A disturbance does not spread at a steady pace: cold, flaws stay put; warmer, effects turn up far away
  by jumps through the toy's far-reaching switch (T75, NO SIGNAL, O140).
- **No pull across a void.** Space bends only right next to a knot (the halo look of 20 October; O22, O56).
- **No thin, steady dark energy.** Missing squares come in clusters that either stay rare or melt the sheet (O141).

The three failures have one cause: flat space has a gap, so nothing in it varies smoothly, and a medium with a gap carries
effects only a short distance (O60 (a)). Soft space removes the gap by adding a quantity that can vary a little, cheaply,
everywhere.

## What it must face first: light bending

A single number per point, sourced by energy, is Nordström's scalar gravity. It gives the right Newtonian pull, bends no
light, and gives −1/6 of Mercury's perihelion shift ([Giulini08], Secs. 1 to 3; O103 (f)). Light bending is measured, so
the author's condition for this ingredient (Update 40: the same numbers as Einstein's wherever those have been measured)
cannot be met by a plain scalar. The way out is in how the quantity acts, not in how many numbers it has, at least for
slow, weak, unchanging fields (general knowledge, to verify). Near a mass, Einstein's gravity slows clocks and stretches
rulers by the same amount, set by one potential. Light feels both, and that doubles its bend over the Newtonian guess. A
scalar that acts on both clocks and rulers, oppositely, reproduces the static measurements. What it still cannot give is
gravitational waves with their two polarizations, and the orbital decay of binary pulsars, which matches Einstein's
quadrupole formula to a fraction of a per cent (to verify). That needs a quantity on the links (a length per link), the
discrete cousin of a metric.

## The seven declarations (gravity_brief, section 7)

1. **What it represents.** One number φ on each point of space: how fast that point's clock runs, and, in the
   recommended form, how long its links are. It belongs to space, not to X (Update 40).
2. **Why it belongs in the model.** The toy has no time. In Einstein's account slow matter falls because clocks run at
   different rates in different places (the time part of the weak-field metric carries Newton's potential; to verify), so
   a quantity that sets each point's clock is the missing piece. It is also the local pace of the present that the author
   adopted on 26 September (Update 36: slower where matter sits).
3. **Its symmetry.** Shifting φ by the same amount everywhere changes nothing, since only differences of clock rate are
   observable. That shift symmetry is what keeps the field gapless (massless).
4. **Its dynamics.** Energy (κ/2) Σ over links (φ_p − φ_q)², plus the coupling to matter in item 5. Two choices, for
   her (question 3 below): φ updated by Metropolis moves, which is diffusive (a disturbance spreads as the square root of
   time), or φ with a momentum, which makes waves that travel at one fixed speed set by κ.
5. **How matter couples to it.** Each relic is a source in proportion to its energy: −Σ_p e_p φ_p, with e_p the curling
   energy at point p. Equal energy, equal pull, whatever the wiring: the equivalence principle, put in.
6. **What continuum equation should emerge.** On a large flat region the energy becomes ∫ (κ/2)(∇φ)² − eφ, whose minimum
   solves Poisson's equation, κ∇²φ = −e: Newton's potential, falling as 1/r with three open directions and as −ln r with
   two. With momentum added, the wave equation. **The 1/r law is put in by this choice, not derived, and every page that
   uses it must say so.**
7. **What falsifiable prediction distinguishes it.** It differs from a scalar on a fixed lattice in two ways: the pull
   travels through the toy's own wiring, which can rearrange; and the field acts back on the wiring, through the cost of
   its gradients across a rewired link. The tests below each have a way to fail.

## What is exact already (O61)

A massless field on the toy's points has been integrated out exactly on fixed wirings. With relics as sources, the pull
between two of them has Newton's shape (1/r in three directions, −ln r in two). The field's own fluctuations add a very
weak, very short Casimir-type attraction (about 10⁻³ g at contact, falling as r⁻⁴ to r⁻⁶). And it barely moves the
ladder: the favor it gives a curled torus over flat space is like lowering λ by about 0.004 g. So adopting it does not
spoil the burp.

**New, from writing this brief (ours, exact by construction).** Integrating the field out costs about N² operations per
accepted move, because its energy then depends on the whole wiring. Keeping φ as an explicit variable makes every move
local: a switch changes four links, so it changes four gradient terms, and a φ move at one point changes four. The cost
is the field's slow relaxation over long distances, which a momentum (item 4) or over-relaxation speeds up. Rule-6 checks
are straightforward: at small N the sampled correlations must match the exact g L⁺ (the graph's own Green's function).

**A caution on the third row (ours, general knowledge, to verify).** In standard physics a massless field's heat behaves
like radiation, not like a cosmological constant, because stretching space stretches its waves. Here space grows by
adding points (Update 72), and the energy is in the shortest waves the network allows, which do not stretch. So whether
the toy's dark energy is constant depends on her growth rule, not on the field. That is a real feature of her picture,
but it is put in by the rule, and it should not be counted as the field predicting w = −1.

## Questions for the author

1. **What the quantity sets.** (a) Clocks only: Newton for slow matter, no light bending (Nordström). (b) **Clocks and
   link lengths together, oppositely** (recommended): matches Newton, light bending and the delay of light passing the
   Sun for slow, unchanging fields; no gravitational waves. (c) A length on each link as its own variable: the nearest
   discrete cousin of Einstein's metric, with waves; far more to build, and best taken up after (b).
2. **What sources it.** (a) **The relics' curling energy** (recommended: the equivalence principle, with sources only
   where matter is). (b) All energy, flaws included: then the network's own flaws source it everywhere, which would be a
   uniform background, closer to dark energy gravitating.
3. **Its dynamics.** (a) Diffusive: simplest; answers the pull and dark-energy questions, not the signal one. (b) **Waves**
   (recommended): φ carries a momentum, so disturbances travel at one fixed speed, which is what T75 asked.
4. **Whose clock times a switch.** (a) **The same for both ends** (recommended first): the toy's equilibrium stays the
   familiar one, and φ changes only how fast things happen. (b) The clock of the point the move starts from: then things
   wander toward slow clocks and collect there, a pull from time dilation alone, with no force term. That is her Update 40
   candidate ("whatever wanders collects where clocks run slow"). It changes the steady state, not only the pace, so it is
   a second knob, named and tested against (a).
5. **Where first.** Four links (two directions) for the signal and dark-energy tests, which are cheap. Six links (three
   directions) for the pull, since with two open directions the pull goes as −ln r, and in Einstein's gravity with two
   space directions masses at rest feel no pull (to verify).

**Her answers, 20 October, 15:33 UTC by the clock:** question 1, (b) ("Clocks and link lengths I agree"); question 3,
(b) waves ("Let's go waves, baby"); question 5, two-direction tests wherever they teach something. Questions 2 and 4

## What it cannot show

That gravity, light or dark energy emerge on their own: the field and its coupling are put in, and the 1/r law with them.
What it can show is whether the toy's own wiring, burps and knots carry these effects consistently once a soft ingredient
is there, and whether its predictions (one signal speed; an attraction that scales with energy; a dark-energy density
fixed by the network's coupling) survive their tests.
