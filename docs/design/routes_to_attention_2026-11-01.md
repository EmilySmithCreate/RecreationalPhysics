# The hypothesis-side topics: what would make each one somebody's business (1 November 2026)

*Written at the author's request: "these topics are important, I want to find collaboration on them if I cannot come up
with a way to predict an observation or otherwise get attention." Ours, unverified. It takes the four topics the
session's reply called "yours, unverified" and asks, for each, three things: which published community already works on
the nearest question; what number or observation the topic would have to produce to be a prediction there; and what the
cheapest next step is. Rule 13 throughout: a published theory that resembles ours is a target it must reproduce, not
evidence for it. Nothing here is a decision.*

## The general picture first

Attention in physics comes through four doors, in order of weight: a confirmed prediction with a number that was written
down before the measurement; a reproducible numerical result in a model other people already study (what paper 1 does
for the published model); a well-posed question put to a specialist inside their own work (the notes to Kallivayalil and
Kelly); and a talk or poster at a workshop where the model's authors are (DICE, where Carlo spoke). The programme has
the second, third and fourth doors open. The first is the one she is asking about, and it has one honest route: pick the
topic whose rules already fix a number nobody fed in, write the number down, and let the data decide. Of the four
topics, two can reach that door in this toy, one needs an ingredient the toy lacks, and one cannot be observed at all.

## 1. The particle rules (twist in thirds, spin times curl one half, mass as a count)

- **Nearest community.** Preon and braid models of the standard model (Bilson-Thompson's helons, read 30 October);
  the Koide formula and its extensions (Koide, Brannen, Zenczykowski; `koide_gaps_2026-10-30.md`); loop and knot models
  of particles. Small, long-running, and used to outsiders.
- **What would be a prediction.** A mass or a mass ratio the rules were not built from. The rules as they stand give
  charge and spin for the electron, neutron, photon and charged pion (exact, O166's replacement) and say mass counts the
  looped diagonals. The first held-out number is therefore a mass: the rules must say how many diagonals a muon or a
  tau loops, and that count must come out as 206.8 and 3477 electrons (rule 13's checks: derived independently, inputs
  not containing the target, neighbouring values tested, the count of other combinations that would match). The
  neutrino sector is where new data arrive soonest (KATRIN's direct bound, the cosmological sum from Planck and DESI),
  so a rule that fixes the neutrino's count against the electron's would be scored within a few years.
- **Cheapest next step.** An exact look, predictions first: under the mass-as-a-count rule, what counts do the three
  charged leptons and the three neutrinos get, and do the ratios land anywhere near the measured ones. If the look
  kills the rule as a mass rule, that is the result, and the rules stay rules for charge and spin.
- **Collaboration.** Brannen and the Koide circle publish on arXiv and viXra and correspond by email; a note with one
  held-out mass that came out right would get read. One that came out wrong would not, and should not be sent.

## 2. The pole picture (X as a stacked one-sided slab; a big bang at one pole; twins)

- **Nearest community.** Early-universe phase transitions: the stochastic gravitational-wave background from a
  first-order transition (the LISA Cosmology Working Group; pulsar-timing arrays, whose 2023 signal is read by some as
  such a background), and baryogenesis at bubble walls (Update 42; `2026-10-19_cp_violation_and_baryogenesis.md`).
  Also the "universe from a black hole" lineage (Popławski; TASKS, the reviews of 6 October, item 6).
- **What would be a prediction.** A first-order birth predicts a gravitational-wave spectrum with a peak set by the
  temperature of the transition and by how fast it completes (β/H), and twin universes predict nothing observable from
  inside one. The toy has no temperature in kelvin and no clock in seconds, so it cannot give the peak; what it can give
  is the *shape* of the completion (one front from a seed, or several seeds that merge; T88's words) and the fraction of
  energy left in walls and knots (paper 2), which are the inputs such a spectrum needs. That is a contribution to a
  calculation someone else would do, not a prediction of ours.
- **Cheapest next step.** None in the toy beyond what T88 and paper 2 give. The step is a reading: the standard
  gravitational-wave formulas for a first-order transition, to see which of their inputs the toy's measurements stand in
  for and which it cannot supply.
- **Collaboration.** Gravitational-wave cosmology groups take model inputs from lattice and bubble simulations; the
  conversation would start from "here is a simulated first-order birth with these completion statistics", which is a
  paper-2 result, not the pole picture itself.

## 3. The shares at birth (ordinary against dark matter as two leftovers; dark energy as the energy of space)

- **Nearest community.** Asymmetric dark matter (Kaplan, Luty and Zurek 2009; reviews by Petraki and Volkas, Zurek;
  abstracts read 5 October, O100): dark matter as a leftover excess of the same kind as ordinary matter, so that the
  ratio of about 5 is a ratio of two leftovers and, if the number densities are equal, the dark particle weighs about
  5 GeV. That is her guess of 5 October in published form, with a number attached.
- **What would be a prediction.** Asymmetric dark matter already predicts the mass scale; a model that gives the *same*
  asymmetry mechanism to both would predict the ratio of the two number densities, and then the dark mass. Her picture
  (the front of a sharp change makes the excess, Update 42) would have to say what sets the two excesses. *Corrected
  the same evening, after the owner's objection:* the toy does have something that comes in opposite kinds. A curled
  loop's two ways round differ by which kind of point sits where, so every curl carries a colour sign (exact, T87
  SIGNED, O170), and her picture puts the two colours at the two poles of the shell (Updates 133, 149). What the record
  does not yet have is a measurement in which anything sorts the two colours apart: the shell has exact poles as a
  shape (Update 134's addenda), the sorting is her claim, and signed twist cannot yet be read from a wiring (G9). So the
  design question is narrower than first written: not "what comes in two kinds" but "what moves one kind toward one
  pole", and that is a run to design once twist can be read. Dark energy as "the energy of space" carries the
  cosmological-constant problem openly (Update 41) and predicts nothing until a size is derived.
- **Cheapest next step.** A reading, not a run: the asymmetric dark matter reviews in full, to list what a mechanism
  must specify and which of those the hypothesis already fixes.
- **Collaboration.** This is a theory community reachable by a note that asks one question: whether a single
  out-of-equilibrium front can source both asymmetries with a fixed ratio, and what that ratio depends on.

## 4. The strange-loop measure (observers per turn times the multiplying of turns)

- **Nearest community.** The measure problem of eternal inflation and anthropic reasoning (Bousso's causal entropic
  principle, Freivogel's review, Hartle and Srednicki; read 12 to 19 October, `2026-10-19_causal_entropic_principle.md`,
  `2026-10-19_observer_window_endpoints.md`); Smolin's cosmological natural selection.
- **What would be a prediction.** *Corrected the same evening, after the owner's objection; the first version said this
  topic cannot produce an observation, which overstated it.* The programme already has the loop-measure paper
  (`docs/papers/loop_measure/paper.tex`; T72, O138): her measure stated (per universe, by its own age, once per
  civilization, weighted by the comoving volume of the causal diamond), computed in standard cosmology with rules written
  before the numbers. What it found: under her counting unit every stellar class is typical and the published "we are
  early by a thousandfold" returns only under units that weight a planet by how long it lives; the one input that moves
  the peak is Carter's hard-steps delay. What it predicts that was not put in: the paper names candidates, and one is
  observational and independent of any observer. The diamond's far edge is the past light cone of the universe's end,
  so the weight depends on how dark energy behaves late. A true constant closes the diamond and keeps our age typical;
  the published model's dark energy, falling as its coupling runs, never closes it and would make our age early. The
  measured equation of state of dark energy (DESI's w, its drift with redshift) therefore separates the two pictures.
  That is a real observable, and it is the measure's one genuine test so far. What the paper also says, and what the
  "fudge" objection cuts both ways on: her own class (sun-like stars with the hard-steps delay) was built from our case
  with the numbers known, so "typical" there is a declared assumption holding together, not a finding.
- **Cheapest next step.** Write the dark-energy fork as a pre-registered prediction with a number: which range of w
  and dw/dz keeps the diamond closed, scored against the next DESI release. That turns the paper's last paragraph into
  the measure's first held-out test. The hard-steps delay and the dinosaur extinction's timing are inputs to the delay
  distribution, not to the fork.
- **Collaboration.** Philosophers of physics and the FQxI essay circuit read this kind of argument; that is attention of
  a different kind, and it does not need a run.

## What this says, in one line each

- Particles: the one topic where a number can be written down now and scored soon; do the exact look first.
- The pole: an input to other people's gravitational-wave calculations, through paper 2, not a prediction of its own.
- The shares: her guess exists in print as asymmetric dark matter; read it in full, then ask one question.
- The measure: no observation; philosophy, with its relatives named.

And the standing fact behind all four: every one of them is downstream of a model with no units. The first number with
units will come from the published model's own program (Eq. (45) of [T25], the free-energy-to-curvature bridge, read on
22 October), or not at all in this family.
