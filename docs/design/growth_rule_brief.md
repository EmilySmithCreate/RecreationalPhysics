# Where space grows: the design brief (draft, 20 October 2026)

*Written from the author's decision of 20 October (VISION Update 75): "add points only where the surrounding links are
flat or short a square, never next to curled ones - agreed, proceed". Under VISION S1 the form below is fixed before any
registered run; nothing is built until she answers the questions at the end. The model with it is our family around the
published one, never combinatorial quantum gravity. Each item says whose it is.*

## What she decided, in her words

Space grows by gaining points, not by stretching (Update 72); "growth can happen in any flat space not just edges"; "it
can only grow where space is flat so anything too close to matter can't grow freely but where there is enough space that
curve is at least a little negative there it grows"; and, of the rule as we wrote it, "add points only where the
surrounding links are flat or short a square, never next to curled ones - agreed, proceed".

## What the toy says about adding points (exact)

1. **Points come in linked pairs.** Every point has four links (two directions) and the two kinds are equal in number,
   so the smallest addition is a linked pair, one point of each kind: three links are cut, the new pair takes their six
   loose ends, and the two new points are linked to each other. It is the pair compaction of the layer form run backwards.
2. **A whole row is clean and free.** Adding a full row across flat space (a layer) leaves every link on two squares: no
   flaw and no energy, which is why an opened tube can be any width at the same energy (O116).
3. **A single pair, added in one place, leaves a flaw** (`sandbox/pair_insertion_2d.py`, 20 October, a look, exhaustive
   within reach). In flat two-direction space at λ = 1.25: with the three cut links all within two steps of one point,
   no addition is valid (the hard-core rule forbids it every time); within three steps, 2,540 of 7,140 are valid and the
   cheapest adds **42 units and leaves 8 points off flat**; within four steps (L = 12), the same 42 and 8. So a local
   addition is never free: new space made in one small place arrives with a flaw.

## The published relative (read 20 October; `docs/reading/notes/2026-10-20_backreaction_timescape.md`)

Backreaction and Wiltshire's timescape: voids, negatively curved, expand faster than the flat walls around them, and the
expansion decelerates and then appears to accelerate once voids fill most of the volume. Contested on two fronts: a
theorem (Green and Wald) that small inhomogeneities cannot mimic dark energy, disputed as to whether it applies; and the
data, where a 2025 supernova analysis favours timescape strongly overall but only moderately beyond the homogeneity
scale, and the Dark Energy Survey finds it in tension once other data are added. A target for the rule, never evidence.

## What follows (ours, unverified)

Growth in one place needs energy, about 42 units a pair, so in the toy it can happen only where something pays: a bath,
or the stores. That fits two of her positions at once: "no warm bath no flaws", and new points carrying the network's
defects (Update 72). It also means local growth makes flaws as it goes, so how much dark energy (her defect density) a
region has would be tied to how much it has grown.

## Decided (20 October): whole rows only

Her words: "A sounds right. Empty space, more through lines, growth approaches light speed." And of single pairs: "B
seems like maybe it can happen but it's got to be hot and concentrated and in that setting it's more likely to curl than
expand, so I think it's unlikely or a mechanism I don't understand." *Ours, exact by construction:* a row runs right
across the space (in three directions a whole plane), so a row can be added only where its whole length is clear of
curled links. Growth is then confined to empty corridors, and there are more of them as matter gathers into clumps: the
growth of space is tied to how clumped the matter is. A row is free, so questions 3 (what pays) and 4 (do growth flaws
heal) fall away; what is left is how far "clear" reaches round the row and how often a clear row is added.

## Questions for her (the brief cannot be finished without them)

1. ~~**Which kind of growth?**~~ Decided above: (a). The choices were: (a) whole rows only: clean and free, but a row runs right across the space, so it is not
   local; (b) single pairs: local, but each costs about 42 and leaves a small flaw, paid from the stores; (c) both.
2. **How far does "surrounding" reach?** The cut links of a valid addition span three steps, so the check that no link
   nearby is curled should reach at least three steps (ours: three).
3. **What pays for a pair, and how often is one tried?** From the stores of the points involved, at a rate per sweep that
   is a knob (scanned, never tuned); or from the bath's warmth, so that a cold space never grows.
4. **Do the flaws it leaves heal?** Whether a growth flaw can settle back to flat is an exact count not yet made; if it
   cannot, every pair added is a permanent flaw.

## What a first registered run would read (after her answers)

A flat sheet with a few knots in it, under the growth rule: whether new points gather in the empty regions and avoid the
knots; how fast the space grows against the share of it that is empty; and whether the growth speeds up as the empty share
rises. Registered with her prediction before it runs.
