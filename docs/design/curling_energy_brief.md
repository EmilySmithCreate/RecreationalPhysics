## Relics: what is known (exact and measured; the record's entries named)

**Two directions (the tube's product, paper 2).**
- Three kinds of knot, read from the wiring, each a dip (every single move out costs): the column (four points, 14 units in
  its common form, 4 in another; O16, O126), the double column (eight points, 8 units) and the knot of two kinds of point
  (eight points, 20 to 30 units; O107). None carries a far link; they are not seams (T11, O16; T55, O107).
- Their number is set at the opening: one per seed from one seed however large the space (T10, O15); about one per three
  seeds from many (T17, T37). Cooling removes columns off a cliff and never the other two (T51, O104; T55, O107).
- The column does not go around the new tube (O126); a scrap can sit as a joint between a stretch six around and a wider
  one (O116).
- Given energy beside it, a column moves or dissolves; it does not seed a fold, with ordinary energy or with curl-only energy
  under either rule (sandbox, 14 and 15 October). Curl-only energy under the strict rule buys new columns, 14 units each,
  near or far from the relic alike.

**Three directions.**
- In flat six-link space no stable curled relic has been found among one- and two-switch constructions (O58, exact within
  that search); the four-link column has no analog there. Three-switch constructions and the quenched end states of the
  six-link runs are the places left to look.
- Inside the slab (one direction curled) relics do exist: the rod's product holds two-point stubs of 14 units, cheapest move
  out 28 (sandbox, 15 October). The slab's own opening leaves a sprinkle of pairs, never a knot (T65, O130).
- A fully curled knot, the 6-cube of 64 points, uses all six links at every point inside itself, so it cannot be wired into a
  slab or into open space: it is always a separate piece (exact; the degree count, and Mulder's bound, O85). Any relic that
  sits *inside* connected three-direction space is therefore partly open, like the column in two directions, never a
  6-cube.

## The 64-point knot, priced (exact, O41, O55; the author's question of 17 October)

At λ = 1.25 the 6-cube holds 12(λ − 1) = 3 units per point above flat space, 192 in all, one third per direction. Opening
is downhill: it releases 192, not costs it. What stands in the way is the walls. Out of the 6-cube the cheapest move costs
96 − 80λ (16 as λ → 1, 8 at 1.10, 4 at 1.15, 0 at 1.20), so at 1.25 it falls apart with no wait; out of the two-curled
state the same; out of the one-curled slab 96 − 48λ = 36, the one wall that stands at 1.25 (O41). Measured (T49, O92): one
6-cube alone opens part of the way and fully in a minority (4 of 32 with named points, 14 of 32 with interchangeable ones
where it has a way downhill); the 8-cube never fully. A single cube cannot end as a flat torus, since the smallest flat
six-link torus has 216 points; "fully open" there means every point at local dimension 3 in some other arrangement.

**Her idea of 17 October, "not fully thought through":** a patch of the slab opening (4 wide becoming 6) releases energy
enough to open a net new knot into space. *Ours, exact:* a patch of 64 slab points opening releases 64 units at λ = 1.25,
against a knot's first wall of at most 16 (λ → 1) and nothing at 1.25; so the energy is there in principle. What the toy says
against it: a 6-cube cannot be in the slab (above), so the knot would be a separate piece that the release has to reach
across a gap, and no link carries energy between pieces (the stores are the only carrier, Q12); and the slab's own release
of 1 per point is 36 below its single-move wall, so one patch's release does not pay the next patch's opening either (O118
prices the completed patch at about 225). The relics that *can* sit in the slab, the stubs, are not knots to open but
pieces to heal. If she wants it run, the first exact question is whether any partly open curled piece larger than a stub
can sit inside a slab as a valid arrangement; that is the three-direction relic search O58 left open.

## Proposed: energy that travels to the curl edge, and a maximum curling speed (19 October; for the author's decision)

*Her words, 19 October, evening (`sandbox/NOTEBOOK.md`): on how infall could enter the toy, "Let's try one" (energy that
has to travel from store to store to reach the cut); her concern, "black holes eat more get bigger, my theory would say if
a black hole stops eating it gets smaller so as long as the energy can be on a smooth path, something has to slow it
down. My intuition says it's something about max curling speed." Nothing below is decided until she confirms the form
(S1); the form is ours, written to her words.*

1. **Flow between neighbouring stores.** Each sweep, every pair of linked points moves a fraction k of the difference of
   their stores from the fuller to the emptier, so energy spreads downhill and is conserved exactly. At k = 0 the kernel
   is the present one, draw for draw (a rule-6 test). k is a knob, scanned and never tuned.
2. **A maximum curling speed.** Across the curl edge, the boundary of the stores that pay the curl (the pool beside the
   cut), the flow per sweep is capped at c. Inside the cap the flow is as in 1. What it gives, by construction: a meal
   reaches the cut no faster than c (her intake limit); and when feeding stops, a black hole whose stores are fuller than
   its surroundings loses energy no faster than c, so it shrinks slowly, not at once.
3. **Open, hers:** does the cap slow eating, shrinking, or both? (a) one cap both ways; (b) a cap on the way out only,
   eating as fast as the flow allows; (c) two caps. *Ours, general knowledge, to verify:* real black holes are strongly
   one-sided: infall can be fast (limited near the Eddington rate) while evaporation is extremely slow, and a black hole
   colder than its surroundings does not lose energy at all; (b) or (c) is closer to that. *Ours, a candidate link:* the
   toy already has one speed set by the medium, the pace of an opening front (T47 part A, FIXED SPEED on the fair clock;
   O76); whether a maximum curling speed is that pace, for a curl instead of an opening, is a question the toy can ask.
4. **What the first run would read** (T74, after she confirms 1 to 3): the slab under the layer form, a meal dropped at
   its outer layers as a burst, with gaps; whether and when the burp fires; how much of each meal reaches the cut; and,
   with feeding stopped, how fast the stores beside the cut empty, against c.
