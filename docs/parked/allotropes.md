The model shows
The model's author describes allotropes : regions of the network stuck in a different discrete arrangement, as diamond survives where graphite is more stable (Trugenberger 2024; the smallest example is Fig. 9 of his 2025 review, drawn by Eryk Kopczyński). He reads them as dark matter; the author's hypothesis does not, since in hers dark matter is what a second curled direction releases. Here they are simply her order-to-order question asked inside his model, at λ = 1 .
Ours, exact If no edge carries three squares, the local term vanishes and the question does not depend on λ at all.
Next
The drawn allotrope lives on an infinite hyperbolic graph, so there is no wiring to copy. The model's author suggested another route (25 September): let a finite graph settle at a coupling where points touch about three squares, and look for points or groups of points that touch only two. Running (T36): at 196 points, where our sampler is at equilibrium at that coupling, we look for such groups and ask whether they stay put for 2,000 sweeps; at 484 points beside it, flagged, because there equilibrium is not certified. The author's prediction, inferred from her position: allotropes found. Ours: they come and go.
Ours, noticed on the way In the model's own setting, at 676 points and couplings between about 2.3 and 3.2, replicas started as the perfect lattice stay perfect while replicas started random settle at three squares per point: the lattice is stuck for now there, the order-to-order situation of piece 1, inside the published model.
Ours, unverified The curved background is favoured only at finite temperature, so whether the patch is stuck is partly a question of entropy. It fails, for the author's picture, if the region dissolves with no wait.
Read (T36, 25 Sep): the search the model's author suggested, in his own model. At 196 points, in equilibrium, a fifth to a quarter of the points touch two squares or fewer, but which points they are is forgotten within about a hundred sweeps, and no group of four or more stays that way: TRANSIENT. The same at 484 points. The author's prediction (inferred) was allotropes; ours was this. A torus in equilibrium is not his infinite hyperbolic graph, so the question he asked, how long a planted allotrope lasts, is the natural next run. Built, exact (25 Sep): his smallest allotrope as a finite graph: a background in which every point touches three squares and one hexagon, as in his Fig. 7, with the allotrope planted in it (three fused, as counting allows). A flat torus cannot hold that background, which is why T36 could not find it. Ours, exact At λ = 1 no energy barrier holds the allotrope: one free move opens a downhill one, and at zero temperature it is gone in eight moves. If it lasts, entropy holds it, which is what his phrase, a barrier in free energy, implies. Launched (T43, 25 Sep, 14:24 ET): how long it lasts at seven temperatures, the question he asked. Her prediction (inferred): it lasts at some temperature. Ours: it dissolves at every one (a timing trial saw it gone within twenty sweeps at the warmest).
