# Parked: the cosmological extension of 2026-09-22

The author's words, put in order. Parked under VISION rule 2 (a new idea goes to the parked list
unless it bears on steps 2 to 4). Two pieces of it bear on step 4 and are marked as such; they wait
behind T9 and are not run on the strength of this page.

## The extension, in the author's words put in order

1. X is a tube: a curled arrangement. The Big Bang is the tube opening out into the large
   spatial dimensions.
2. Dark energy has something to do with the extra energy: the energy the opening released.
3. Gravity is the force of the opened space wanting to knit back up and fold. It pulls things
   together according to their sizes.
4. Under the right conditions, and given time, that folding makes black holes, which have a
   tube, or a closed tube, at the singularity.
5. Large enough black holes colliding supply the activation energy for a new open tube: a new
   Big Bang. It opens into a new three-dimensional space, not into ours, so ours does not grow.

The author's own instruction: park it, and return to it if what we find keeps looking reasonable
along this line.

## What the model already says about each piece (*Ours, unverified*)

1. **The tube that opens.** This is the λ > 1 track the repository already runs: the tube is one
   curled dimension, 4(λ − 1) per point above the flat sheet, metastable, and it uncurls by a
   seed and a front (T7; VISION Updates 7 to 9). So the extension's starting point is the one
   the runs have. It does not agree with the illustration of 2026-09-20 in VISION ("What X
   is"), where X has *more* large dimensions than spacetime and one of them collapses. Which
   direction the vision means is the first question put to the author on 2026-09-22.
2. **Dark energy.** Not reachable in this model: it has no expansion and no cosmological
   constant. In [T25] dark energy is the curvature of the ground state, not released energy.
   Stays parked.
3. **Gravity as a wish to fold.** Above λ = 1 folding costs energy, so an empty sheet has no
   drive to fold; whatever plays gravity must come from what the opening left behind. Two
   things in the model could stand in, and both are cheap:
   - *Do the leftover rings attract one another?* T9 records, for every end state, the number
     of curled pieces, the size of the largest, and the energy. A sheet with one ring carries a
     fixed excess (14 units at λ = 1.25); two separated rings should carry twice that if they do
     not interact, less if they attract, more if they repel. Readable from T9's own files with
     no new run. **Bears on plan step 4** (do domains of a different arrangement persist).
   - *Does concentrated energy re-curl a patch of sheet, or melt it?* T9's small baths spread
     the released energy as heat and the sheet melts (vertices at d ≥ 3). A local injection has
     not been tried. This is the reverse of T7 and it is what piece 4 needs.
4. **Black holes as re-curled regions.** In [T25] a black hole is a bubble that has reverted to
   the random phase: a *melted* region, which is what T9's small baths make. The author's
   version is a *re-curled* region. Which one concentrated energy produces is the test in 3(ii),
   and it discriminates between the two pictures.
5. **A new space budding off.** Out of reach here on two counts: a new space needs the number
   of points to change, which claim 3 parks (a cost-per-element term would be a new knob under
   S1), and three large dimensions need the D = 3 model, which nobody has run past N = 500
   [T22]. Stays parked.
