### 2.5 Her four questions of the day, answered here

1. **What will starting with clocks and lengths teach?** Stage A (one number per point) can measure three things the stiff
   toy could not: whether a disturbance travels at one steady speed (piece 10), whether two knots drift together and how
   the pull grows with their energy (piece 8), and whether a thin steady density of defects can be held (piece 6). It
   cannot carry gravitational waves, and it has a preferred frame that measurement rules out for reality. Stage B
   (lengths on links and diagonals) adds the objects her particle needs (rings, a twist) and, with time in the lattice,
   could carry waves; in three directions alone it gives only conical-defect particles (task 1).
2. **A black hole's eating rate in sweeps.** The toy's sweep has no physical length until one toy quantity is tied to one
   measured number (rule 13). What can be converted now is a ratio: the feed per sweep as a share of the slab's curling
   cost, r / a per point per sweep. For a real black hole the matching ratio is its growth rate as a share of its mass:
   at the fastest steady rate usually quoted, about one e-folding per 50 million years, so about 6 × 10⁻¹⁶ per second.
   T71's slowest feed, r = 1/64 at a = 1, is 1/64 per sweep, so one sweep there plays about 3 × 10¹³ seconds, about a
   million years, if that rate is the anchor. A different anchor gives a different number; none is chosen.
3. **The 3 eV per photon.** It is a budget, not a claim about her loop. Today's dark-matter mass divided by today's photon
   count is about 3.1 eV per photon (from 6.1 × 10⁻¹⁰ baryons per photon, 938 MeV per baryon, and the measured 5.36). If
   dark loops were made as often as pairs (about one per photon) and all survived, each could weigh only about 3 eV,
   which is too light for the main dark matter. If each weighs about three protons, as she guessed, only about one per
   billion photons can survive, so the dark loops must mostly cancel too and leave their own excess. It does represent
   her idea; it adds one requirement to it.
4. **Has the toy progressed toward predicting, and what were the biggest steps?** Exact and measured facts have grown
   steadily; a prediction about the real universe that was not put in has not yet been made (rule 13), so by the owner's
   own bar the predictive toy does not exist yet. The biggest steps, in order: the burp (a specific arrangement opens
   sharply from a seed with an exact release; paper 1); the sealed spark threshold of 12 at every size; the scrap as three
   kinds of knot of fixed energy, their number set at the opening (paper 2); the slab as a converter under her layer form
   and gate (T71, T74); the light-cone measure computed under both pictures of dark energy (paper 16); the toy's energy
   written term by term; soft space's first stage built and checked; and, one day old, the particle track (a ring with a
   twist), with its first exact facts (diagonals join one kind; the tetrahedron; curl and uncurl are not mirrors). The
   nearest candidates for a first prediction: the λ-from-defect-density relation (task 5, with the published sign
   against it), and the conical-defect mass of a ring in three directions (task 1).
